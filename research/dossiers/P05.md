# P05 — Fine topology and compact balayage

The setting is an open subset of real dimension d ≥ 2, with ball-relative Green energy for the measure construction. Fine neighborhoods use canonical pointwise subharmonic representatives. Thinness is equivalent to a missing fine neighborhood, and finite level constraints combine into one witness. Every fine neighborhood has positive local Lebesgue measure. Polar sets are fine closed and relatively discrete; the explicit polar Cantor measure constructed in P04 supplies an uncountable compact polar set and disproves Lindelöf and second countability. In dimensions greater than two, Fubini expresses the lifted potential's ball means as weighted planar disc means.

The Green gradient identity is extended from bounded potentials to compactly supported finite-energy measures by continuous-potential restrictions and a Cauchy argument. Relative capacity then has its Green probability variational formula and domain monotonicity. Compact reduction is proved through bounded majorants and lower semicontinuous Poisson lifting. Sweeping is characterized by Green energy orthogonality; additivity and the finite-energy extension follow from that characterization. The regular annular complement example connects the reduced Green kernel to harmonic measure, without identifying a swept measure with a boundary measure by an unproved reciprocity theorem.

Fine quasi-Lindelöf is proved from Choquet selection and polar envelope discrepancy, keeping countably many local rational domains. Quasi-openness uses quasi-continuity and capacity domain monotonicity. The final complex-hyperplane example proves a concrete distinction from the complex-line-generated topology. No real fine connectedness theorem is imported into the plurifine theory.
