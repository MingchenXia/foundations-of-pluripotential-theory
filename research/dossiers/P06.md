# P06 — Fourier energy and complex-line incidence

The dependency sequence is Euclidean Schwartz transform → Gaussian inversion → Plancherel and finite-measure L2 density → Riesz energy identity → invariant probabilities → projective ball estimate → positive-circle selector → uniform star energy → hyperplane L2 densities → incidence → local upper bound for finite Borel line-subharmonic functions.

Fourier constants use exp(-2πi x·ξ). The Riesz kernel is |x−y|^(−s), without a Laplace fundamental-solution factor. Gaussian Tonelli integration proves the exact constant π^(s−d/2)Γ((d−s)/2)/Γ(s/2), including infinite energy. Only positive finite measures are used in this identity. A Gaussian probability example checks these constants and supplies the sphere area formula. Orthogonal projection onto a complex hyperplane is a real projection of codimension two, giving the |ξ|^(−2) weight and exact density norm factor Γ(n)/π^(n−1).

Invariant probabilities are constructed from the polar unitary factor of a Gaussian matrix, with left/right invariance and compact metric homogeneous-space uniqueness proved. Projective small-ball measure is (sin a)^(2n−2), derived from independent exponential Gaussian coordinate radii. Borel Grassmannian frames make all bundle integrals measurable.

The specific high-value circle sections are closed and have positive arc length by the submean inequality. Their arc-length medians give an explicit Borel selector. This proves the actual selection needed in the supplied PSHD argument and does not assert a general compact-fiber selection theorem. The star estimate uses a dyadic product-ball measure bound instead of an unproved Riemannian volume assertion. The closed ambient ball is explicitly required inside the function's domain so the outer-radius circle is available. Compact inner approximation makes the incidence sets compact. A second Cauchy–Schwarz estimate proves a positive uniform incidence measure without invoking Jensen. No automatic upper semicontinuity conclusion is used in this chapter; the final local-bound corollary retains Borel measurability.
