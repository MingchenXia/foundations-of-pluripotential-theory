# P04 — Energy, capacity and polar exceptional sets

The chapter treats dimensions d ≥ 2; dimension one has the earlier convex interface and no nonempty polar sets. Newtonian kernels are positive definite on finite-energy signed measures. The logarithmic kernel is handled on a fixed compact set with a scale D exceeding its diameter; its signed quadratic form is positive definite on the mass-zero subspace. Gaussian integration proves these distinctions without invoking Fourier analysis or an unproved duality theorem. Probability minimizers, physical kernel capacities and the ball-relative mass capacity have different numerical definitions.

Published source read scope: Hörmander 2007 reprint, Theorem 3.2.13 and proof, pp. 150–151 (continuous-potential testing); Theorem 3.4.2, pp. 203–204 (local-to-global polarity and countable unions); Theorems 3.4.5–3.4.7, pp. 206–208 (nonpolar compact criterion, continuity principle, Lusin restrictions); compact extremals/strong subadditivity, pp. 209–211; Theorem 3.4.9, pp. 212–213; Theorem 3.4.12 and Proposition 3.4.13 with proofs, pp. 214–216; the converse zero-capacity statement and Theorem 3.4.14, pp. 216–217; Proposition 3.4.19, p. 221, and Proposition 3.4.21, pp. 222–223. The book reconstructs these proofs and supplies Lusin approximation, explicit ball Green symmetry, Gaussian positivity, probability minimization, and the two-radius gradient Cauchy argument. No general-domain symmetry, Morse–Sard or unproved Sobolev duality is imported.

The capacity argument uses the lower semicontinuous pointwise compact extremal before regularizing it; the regularization's constraints need not survive at irregular points. Outer capacity is initially defined for sets with compact closure in a fixed ball. Countable subadditivity/union arguments keep a common compact neighborhood. Capacitability is proved, including Borel sets via compact Hausdorff products. Polar sets coincide with local zero outer capacity. Finite-energy measures charge no polar sets, and continuous-potential measures test L1 limits, giving the stronger envelope discrepancy conclusion assigned here from P03.

Dirichlet energy is first established for bounded zero-boundary Green potentials with compact Riesz support. Quasi-continuity is extended by local bounded truncations and a radial harmonic collar, with all exceptional neighborhoods intersected into one fixed compact neighborhood. Compact exhaustion of a Borel set means approximation of its capacity by increasing compact subsets, not a claim that every Borel set is a literal countable union of compact sets.
