\preface
A singular potential carries two kinds of information: its distributional curvature and its values at every point. The relation between them begins with the mean-value inequality. In several complex variables, positivity must hold along every complex direction, and the resulting singularities govern products of currents, envelopes and the geometry of positive classes.

This book develops potential theory before pluripotential theory. The classical theory supplies the representatives, convergence arguments, capacities and energy estimates used in the complex theory. The latter proceeds from local plurisubharmonic functions and weighted holomorphic approximation to global singular potentials on compact K\"ahler manifolds. The conventions agree with the supplied manuscript \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory}; the proofs are to be organized around the dependencies of its constructions.
