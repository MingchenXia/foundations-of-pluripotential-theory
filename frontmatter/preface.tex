\preface
A singular potential carries two kinds of information: its distributional curvature and its values at every point. Their relation begins with the mean-value inequality. In several complex variables, positivity must hold along every complex direction, and exceptional sets must be controlled before nonlinear products of currents can be defined.

This book develops those foundations from classical potential theory. Measures, distributions, harmonic and subharmonic functions lead to capacity and canonical representatives; complex differential calculus then leads to plurisubharmonic functions, weighted holomorphic estimates, multiplier ideals and Monge--Amp\`ere theory. Convex and geometric prerequisites enter where global and toric constructions require them. The arguments assume undergraduate analysis, linear algebra, topology and elementary one-variable complex analysis.

The intended continuation is the first part of \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory}. The conventions agree with that book. Results used there without complete proofs, together with their foundational dependencies, are developed here in a connected order. Its second and third parts are left to that continuation; overlap is limited to the definitions and arguments needed for a self-contained foundation.
