\chapter{Weighted \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} estimates and holomorphic extension}
\label{chap:q04}

Many constructions in the rest of the book need holomorphic functions with prescribed local behavior and a controlled weighted $L^2$ norm: a holomorphic function given near a point, or on a hyperplane section, is to be extended to a whole domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, with an estimate of $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV$ for a plurisubharmonic weight $\varphi$. Gluing by a smooth partition of unity does not preserve holomorphicity. If $f$ is holomorphic near the support of a smooth cutoff $\chi$, then $\chi f$ is a global smooth function, but $\bar\partial(\chi f)=f\,\bar\partial\chi$ need not vanish. If we can solve $\bar\partial u=f\,\bar\partial\chi$ with an estimate in a weighted $L^2$ norm, then $F=\chi f-u$ is holomorphic. The weight has two tasks. It must make the equation solvable with a bound on $u$, and, when values are prescribed, it must be so singular there that the finiteness of the weighted norm forces $u$ to vanish.

The basic estimate, due to H\"ormander, comes from an integration-by-parts identity, the Bochner--Kodaira--Morrey--Kohn identity, in which the complex Hessian of the weight appears as a curvature term. In \cref{sec:hilbert-operator-complexes} we prove the Hilbert space duality that turns an a priori estimate for an adjoint operator into an existence theorem. In \cref{sec:pseudoconvex-distance} we study pseudoconvex open sets, the open sets with a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function; they carry smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion functions, which allow us to work on interior regions and to avoid any regularity assumption on the boundary. In \cref{sec:weighted-dbar-estimate} we prove the identity for compactly supported forms, extend the resulting inequality to the domains of the operators by an approximation argument with three related weights, and obtain H\"ormander's estimate, first for smooth weights and then for arbitrary plurisubharmonic weights (\cref{thm:hormander-smooth-weight,thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}).

In \cref{sec:weighted-interpolation-extension} we apply these estimates. Adding the logarithmic pole $n\log|z-a|^2$ to the weight prescribes the value of a holomorphic function at the point $a$ (\cref{prop:logarithmic-point-interpolation}). For extension from a hyperplane, a bounded change of weight gives a stronger estimate in the normal direction, and an explicit one-variable weight of $\log\log$ type then yields the Ohsawa--Takegoshi extension theorem, with a constant that depends only on the width of the domain in the normal direction (\cref{thm:local-ot-extension}). Its consequences for point values and for integrable germs (\cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound,cor:integrable-germ-restriction}) are the analytic input for the multiplier ideals of \cref{chap:q05} and the Bergman kernel approximation of \cref{chap:q06}. Throughout, weights enter through the factor $\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$, and Hessian conditions are expressed in the normalization \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}.

\section{Hilbert spaces and closed operator complexes}
\label{sec:hilbert-operator-complexes}

A complex Hilbert space is a complex vector space with an inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for which it is complete; our inner products are linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second. The Hilbert spaces of this chapter are weighted $L^2$ spaces. For a positive measure $\mu$ on a measurable space, $L^2(\mu)$ carries the inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\int f\bar g\,\mathrm d\mu$, and it is complete. Indeed, let $(f_j)$ be a Cauchy sequence in $L^2(\mu)$, and choose $j_1<j_2<\cdots$ with $\|f_{j_{k+1}}-f_{j_k}\|\le2^{-k}$. By the triangle inequality and the monotone convergence theorem, $g=\sum_k|f_{j_{k+1}}-f_{j_k}|$ satisfies $\|g\|\le1$, so $g<\infty$ almost everywhere. Hence $(f_{j_k})$ converges almost everywhere to a function $f$, and the bound $|f-f_{j_k}|\le\sum_{l\ge k}|f_{j_{l+1}}-f_{j_l}|$ gives $\|f-f_{j_k}\|\le2^{1-k}$. A Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges, so $f_j\to f$ in $L^2(\mu)$. Applying this to each coefficient, we see that spaces of $L^2$ differential forms, whose elements are finite tuples of functions in $L^2(\mu)$, are complete as well.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}
Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space. Every nonempty closed convex subset of $H$ contains a unique point of smallest distance to a given point of $H$. If $M\subset H$ is a closed linear subspace, then $H=M\oplus M^\perp$. Every bounded linear functional $L$ on $H$ has the form $L(x)=\langle x,y\rangle$ for a unique $y\in H$, and the norm of $L$ is $\|y\|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $C$ be a nonempty closed convex set and $x\in H$. Replacing $C$ by $C-x$, we may assume that $x=0$. Let $d=\inf_{z\in C}\|z\|$, and let $(x_j)$ be a sequence in $C$ with $\|x_j\|\to d$. Since $(x_j+x_k)/2\in C$ by convexity, the parallelogram identity gives
\[
 \|x_j-x_k\|^2=2\|x_j\|^2+2\|x_k\|^2
                  -4\|(x_j+x_k)/2\|^2
 \le2\|x_j\|^2+2\|x_k\|^2-4d^2.
\]
The right side tends to zero as $j,k\to\infty$, so $(x_j)$ is a Cauchy sequence. Its limit lies in $C$, because $C$ is closed, and has norm $d$. If $x,x'\in C$ both have norm $d$, the same inequality with $x_j=x$ and $x_k=x'$ gives $\|x-x'\|^2\le0$, which proves uniqueness.

Now let $M$ be a closed linear subspace and $x\in H$. Let $p$ be the point of $M$ closest to $x$, and put $r=x-p$. For $v\in M$ and $t\in\mathbb C$ we have $p+tv\in M$, and hence
\[
 0\le\|r-tv\|^2-\|r\|^2=-2\Real\bigl(\bar t\langle r,v\rangle\bigr)+|t|^2\|v\|^2.
\]
Taking small real $t$ of both signs gives $\Real\langle r,v\rangle=0$, and taking $t\in\mathrm i\mathbb R$ gives $\Img\langle r,v\rangle=0$. Thus $r\in M^\perp$ and $x=p+r$. Since $M\cap M^\perp=\{0\}$, this decomposition is unique, so $H=M\oplus M^\perp$.

Finally, let $L$ be a bounded linear functional. If $L=0$ we take $y=0$. Otherwise $\ker L$ is a proper closed subspace. Choose $x_0\notin\ker L$, and let $e$ be its component in $(\ker L)^\perp$; then $e\ne0$ and $L(e)=L(x_0)\ne0$. For every $x\in H$ the vector $x-(L(x)/L(e))e$ lies in $\ker L$ and is therefore orthogonal to $e$, so that $\langle x,e\rangle=(L(x)/L(e))\|e\|^2$. With $y=\overline{L(e)}\,e/\|e\|^2$ this reads $L(x)=\langle x,y\rangle$. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality shows that $L$ has norm at most $\|y\|$, and $L(y)=\|y\|^2$ shows equality. If $y'$ also represents $L$, then $\langle x,y-y'\rangle=0$ for all $x$, and $x=y-y'$ gives $y=y'$. Applying these results to the linear functional $\overline\ell$, we see that every bounded conjugate-linear functional $\ell$ has the form $\ell(x)=\langle y,x\rangle$ for a unique $y\in H$, and its norm is $\|y\|$.
\end{proof}
\index{Hilbert space!projection}\index{Riesz representation!Hilbert space}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hilbert-weak-sequences}
Every bounded sequence in a Hilbert space $H$ has a weakly convergent subsequence, that is, a subsequence $(x_j)$ for which there is $x\in H$ with $\langle x_j,v\rangle\to\langle x,v\rangle$ for every $v\in H$. If $(x_j)$ converges weakly to $x$, then $\|x\|\le\liminf_j\|x_j\|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $(x_j)$ be a sequence with $\|x_j\|\le C$, and let $V$ be the closed linear span of its terms. The finite linear combinations of the $x_j$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Q+\mathrm i\mathbb Q$ form a countable dense subset of $V$. Applying the Gram--Schmidt process to it, and discarding the vectors that depend linearly on earlier ones, we obtain a finite or countable orthonormal family $(e_k)$ whose linear span is dense in $V$. For each $k$ the sequence $(\langle x_j,e_k\rangle)_j$ is bounded by $C$, so by a diagonal argument we may pass to a subsequence, still denoted $(x_j)$, such that $\langle x_j,e_k\rangle$ converges to a number $a_k$ for every $k$. For every $N$, Bessel's inequality gives $\sum_{k\le N}|\langle x_j,e_k\rangle|^2\le C^2$; hence $\sum_k|a_k|^2\le C^2$, the series $x=\sum_ka_ke_k$ converges in $V$, and $\|x\|\le C$.

By construction, $\langle x_j,v\rangle\to\langle x,v\rangle$ for every $v$ in the linear span of the $e_k$. If $v\in V$ and $v'$ lies in that span with $\|v-v'\|<\delta$, then $|\langle x_j-x,v\rangle|\le|\langle x_j-x,v'\rangle|+2C\delta$, so the convergence extends to all $v\in V$. For an arbitrary $v\in H$, write $v=v_1+v_2$ with $v_1\in V$ and $v_2\in V^\perp$ by \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}; since $x_j$ and $x$ lie in $V$, we get $\langle x_j-x,v\rangle=\langle x_j-x,v_1\rangle\to0$.

For the last assertion we may assume that $x\ne0$. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $\|x\|^2=\lim_j\langle x_j,x\rangle\le\liminf_j\|x_j\|\,\|x\|$, and we divide by $\|x\|$.
\end{proof}
\index{weak convergence!Hilbert space}

Let $H_0$ and $H_1$ be Hilbert spaces. An operator $T$ from $H_0$ to $H_1$ is a linear map $T\colon\Dom T\to H_1$ defined on a linear subspace $\Dom T\subset H_0$, its domain. We write $\ker T$ for its kernel, $\operatorname{Ran}T=T(\Dom T)$ for its range, and $\operatorname{Gr}T=\{(x,Tx):x\in\Dom T\}$ for its graph, a subspace of the Hilbert space $H_0\oplus H_1$ with the inner product $\langle(x,y),(x',y')\rangle=\langle x,x'\rangle+\langle y,y'\rangle$. The operator $T$ is densely defined if $\Dom T$ is dense in $H_0$, and closed if $\operatorname{Gr}T$ is closed; equivalently, $x_j\in\Dom T$, $x_j\to x$ and $Tx_j\to y$ imply $x\in\Dom T$ and $Tx=y$. In particular, the kernel of a closed operator is a closed subspace. If $T$ is densely defined, its Hilbert adjoint $T^*$, an operator from $H_1$ to $H_0$, is defined as follows: $y\in\Dom T^*$ if the linear functional $x\mapsto\langle Tx,y\rangle$ on $\Dom T$ is bounded with respect to the norm of $H_0$, and then $T^*y$ is the vector determined by
\[
 \langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^*y\rangle\qquad(x\in\Dom T).
\]
Indeed, since $\Dom T$ is dense, such a functional extends uniquely to a bounded linear functional on $H_0$, which \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation} represents by a unique vector.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:closed-operator-adjoint}
Let $T$ be a densely defined operator from $H_0$ to $H_1$. Then $T^*$ is closed and $\ker T^*=(\operatorname{Ran}T)^\perp$. If $T$ is also closed, then $T^*$ is densely defined and $T^{**}=T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $y_j\in\Dom T^*$, $y_j\to y$ and $T^*y_j\to w$, then for every $x\in\Dom T$
\[
 \langle Tx,y\rangle=\lim_j\langle Tx,y_j\rangle=\lim_j\langle x,T^*y_j\rangle=\langle x,w\rangle;
\]
hence $y\in\Dom T^*$ and $T^*y=w$, so $T^*$ is closed. A vector $y\in H_1$ is orthogonal to $\operatorname{Ran}T$ exactly when the functional $x\mapsto\langle Tx,y\rangle$ vanishes, that is, when $y\in\Dom T^*$ and $T^*y=0$. This proves $\ker T^*=(\operatorname{Ran}T)^\perp$. In the same way, $(a,b)\in H_0\oplus H_1$ is orthogonal to $\operatorname{Gr}T$ if and only if $\langle Tx,b\rangle=\langle x,-a\rangle$ for all $x\in\Dom T$, so
\[
 (\operatorname{Gr}T)^\perp
       =\{(-T^*y,y):y\in\Dom T^*\}.
\]

Now let $T$ be closed, and let $v\in H_1$ be orthogonal to $\Dom T^*$. Since $\operatorname{Gr}T$ is closed, \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation} and the description of $(\operatorname{Gr}T)^\perp$ decompose $(0,v)$ as $(x,Tx)+(-T^*y,y)$ with $x\in\Dom T$ and $y\in\Dom T^*$. Thus $x=T^*y$ and $v=Tx+y$. Taking the inner product of $v$ with $y\in\Dom T^*$, we obtain
\[
 0=\langle v,y\rangle=\langle Tx,y\rangle+\|y\|^2=\langle x,T^*y\rangle+\|y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2,
\]
so $x=y=0$ and $v=0$. Hence $\Dom T^*$ is dense, and $T^{**}$ is defined. Let $J\colon H_1\oplus H_0\to H_0\oplus H_1$ and $J'\colon H_0\oplus H_1\to H_1\oplus H_0$ be the unitary maps $J(b,a)=(-a,b)$ and $J'(a,b)=(-b,a)$. The description of the orthogonal complement of a graph, applied to $T$ and to the densely defined operator $T^*$, reads $(\operatorname{Gr}T)^\perp=J(\operatorname{Gr}T^*)$ and $(\operatorname{Gr}T^*)^\perp=J'(\operatorname{Gr}T^{**})$. For a closed subspace $M$ we have $(M^\perp)^\perp=M$ by \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}, and unitary maps carry orthogonal complements to orthogonal complements. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Gr}T=\bigl((\operatorname{Gr}T)^\perp\bigr)^\perp
 =J\bigl((\operatorname{Gr}T^*)^\perp\bigr)
 =JJ'(\operatorname{Gr}T^{**})=\operatorname{Gr}T^{**},
\]
because $JJ'=-\mathrm{id}$ and graphs are linear subspaces. Thus $T^{**}=T$.
\end{proof}
\index{operator!closed and adjoint}

The next theorem is the abstract form of H\"ormander's method. It produces a solution of $Tu=g$ from an inequality for the adjoint operator; the second operator $S$ allows us to test the inequality only on forms in the domain of $S$, which is where the integration by parts of \cref{sec:weighted-dbar-estimate} applies.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability}
Let $H_0,H_1,H_2$ be Hilbert spaces, and let $T$ from $H_0$ to $H_1$ and $S$ from $H_1$ to $H_2$ be closed densely defined operators with $\operatorname{Ran}T\subset\ker S$. Let $g\in\ker S$ and $0\le M<\infty$, and assume that
\[
 |\langle g,f\rangle|^2\le M\bigl(\|T^*f\|^2+\|Sf\|^2\bigr)
 \qquad(f\in\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S).
\]
Then the equation $Tu=g$ has a solution $u\in\Dom T$ with $\|u\|^2\le M$. Among all solutions there is a unique one of smallest norm, and it is orthogonal to $\ker T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $S$ is closed, $\ker S$ is a closed subspace, and $H_1=\ker S\oplus(\ker S)^\perp$ by \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}. Since $\operatorname{Ran}T\subset\ker S$, \cref{lem:closed-operator-adjoint} gives $(\ker S)^\perp\subset(\operatorname{Ran}T)^\perp=\ker T^*$. Let $f\in\Dom T^*$, and write $f=f_0+f_1$ with $f_0\in\ker S$ and $f_1\in(\ker S)^\perp$. Then $f_1\in\Dom T^*$ and $T^*f_1=0$, so $f_0\in\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S$, $T^*f_0=T^*f$ and $Sf_0=0$. Moreover $\langle g,f\rangle=\langle g,f_0\rangle$, because $g\in\ker S$. The hypothesis applied to $f_0$ therefore gives
\[
 |\langle g,f\rangle|\le\sqrt M\,\|T^*f\|\qquad(f\in\Dom T^*).
\]

Consequently the map $\lambda\colon T^*f\mapsto\langle g,f\rangle$ is well defined on $\operatorname{Ran}T^*$: if $T^*f=T^*f'$, then $|\langle g,f-f'\rangle|\le\sqrt M\,\|T^*(f-f')\|=0$. It is conjugate-linear and bounded by $\sqrt M$, hence uniformly continuous, so it extends uniquely to a bounded conjugate-linear functional on the closure of $\operatorname{Ran}T^*$; we extend it further by zero on the orthogonal complement of this closure, and obtain a conjugate-linear functional on $H_0$ of norm at most $\sqrt M$. By \cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}, applied to conjugate-linear functionals, there is $u\in H_0$ with $\|u\|\le\sqrt M$ and
\[
 \langle u,T^*f\rangle=\langle g,f\rangle\qquad(f\in\Dom T^*).
\]
Taking complex conjugates, we get $\langle T^*f,u\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle$ for all $f\in\Dom T^*$. Thus the functional $f\mapsto\langle T^*f,u\rangle$ is bounded on $\Dom T^*$, so $u\in\Dom T^{**}$ and $T^{**}u=g$. Since $T^{**}=T$ by \cref{lem:closed-operator-adjoint}, we conclude that $u\in\Dom T$ and $Tu=g$.

The solutions of $Tu=g$ form the affine subspace $u+\ker T$, which is closed because $\ker T$ is closed. If $P$ denotes the orthogonal projection onto $\ker T$, then $\|u+k\|^2=\|u-Pu\|^2+\|Pu+k\|^2$ for $k\in\ker T$. Hence $u-Pu$ is the unique solution of smallest norm; it is orthogonal to $\ker T$, and its norm is at most $\|u\|\le\sqrt M$.
\end{proof}

\section{Pseudoconvexity and boundary distance}
\label{sec:pseudoconvex-distance}

H\"ormander's estimate holds on open sets that are exhausted by sublevel sets of plurisubharmonic functions. These pseudoconvex open sets are characterized by the plurisubharmonicity of minus the logarithm of the boundary distance, and they carry smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion functions, which \cref{sec:weighted-dbar-estimate} uses to localize the estimate. For $r>0$ we write $\Delta_r=\{t\in\mathbb C:|t|<r\}$ and $\Delta=\Delta_1$, and $d(z,E)$ denotes the Euclidean distance from a point $z$ to a set $E$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:pseudoconvex-domain}
Let $n\ge1$ and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open. An \emph{exhaustion function} of $\Omega$ is a function $\rho\colon\Omega\to\mathbb R$ such that the sublevel set $\{z\in\Omega:\rho(z)\le c\}$ is compact for every $c\in\mathbb R$. The open set $\Omega$ is \emph{pseudoconvex} if it has a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function.
\end{definition}
\index{pseudoconvex domain}\index{exhaustion function}

A continuous exhaustion function attains its minimum on each nonempty sublevel set, so it is bounded below; adding a constant, we may take it nonnegative. The restriction of a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function of $\Omega$ to a connected component of $\Omega$ is one of that component. Conversely, if $\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\ldots$ are the (at most countably many) components of $\Omega$ and $\rho_k\ge0$ is a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function of $\Omega_k$, then the function equal to $\rho_k+k$ on $\Omega_k$ is one of $\Omega$, since its sublevel set $\{\le c\}$ meets only the components with $k\le c$. Thus an open set is pseudoconvex if and only if each of its components is. Recall from \cref{sec:psh-hessian-maps} that a function $u$ is strictly plurisubharmonic if every point has a neighborhood on which $u-\varepsilon|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic for some $\varepsilon>0$; for smooth $u$ this means that the complex Hessian matrix $(u_{j\bar k})$ is positive definite at every point.
\index{plurisubharmonic function!strict}

To recognize plurisubharmonic functions we use the following criterion, in which the harmonic majorants in the submean inequality are restricted to real parts of polynomials.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polynomial-disc-comparison}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C^N$ be open and let $v\colon U\to[-\infty,\infty)$ be upper semicontinuous. Assume that for all $z_0\in U$ and $a\in\mathbb C^N$ with $\{z_0+ta:|t|\le1\}\subset U$, and for every polynomial $P$ in one complex variable with $v(z_0+ta)\le\Real P(t)$ whenever $|t|=1$, we have $v(z_0)\le\Real P(0)$. Then
\[
 v(z_0)\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}v(z_0+\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}a)\,\mathrm d\theta
\]
for all such $z_0$ and $a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $z_0$ and $a$, and put $g(\theta)=v(z_0+\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}a)$, an upper semicontinuous function on the circle. By \cref{lem:usc-majorants} there are continuous real functions $g_m$ decreasing to $g$ whose integrals decrease to the integral of $g$, which may be $-\infty$. Fix $m$ and $\delta>0$. We approximate $g_m$ uniformly by real trigonometric polynomials, using the Fej\'er kernel
\[
 F_N(\theta)=\frac1N\Bigl|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathrm e^{\mathrm ik\theta}\Bigr|^2=\sum_{|k|<N}\Bigl(1-\frac{|k|}N\Bigr)\mathrm e^{\mathrm ik\theta},\qquad N\ge1.
\]
It is nonnegative, has integral one with respect to the normalized measure $\mathrm d\theta/(2\pi)$, and satisfies $F_N(\theta)\le1/(N\sin^2(\theta/2))$; so it tends to zero uniformly on $\{\theta:\delta'\le|\theta|\le\pi\}$ for every $\delta'>0$. Since $g_m$ is uniformly continuous, the convolutions $q_N=g_m*F_N$ with respect to $\mathrm d\theta/(2\pi)$ converge uniformly to $g_m$. Each $q_N$ is a real trigonometric polynomial $\sum_{|k|<N}c_k\mathrm e^{\mathrm ik\theta}$ with $c_{-k}=\bar c_k$, so $q_N(\theta)=\Real P(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta})$ for the polynomial $P(t)=c_0+2\sum_{0<k<N}c_kt^k$, and $\Real P(0)=c_0$ is the mean of $q_N$, which equals the mean of $g_m$. Choose $N$ with $|q_N-g_m|<\delta$. Then $\Real(P(t)+\delta)\ge g_m\ge g$ on the circle, and the hypothesis gives
\[
 v(z_0)\le c_0+\delta=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}g_m\,\mathrm d\theta+\delta.
\]
Letting $\delta\to0$ and then $m\to\infty$, we obtain the asserted inequality.
\end{proof}

Richberg's lemma smooths a continuous strictly plurisubharmonic function while keeping it plurisubharmonic, at the cost of half of its strictness. Convolution does this locally; the local pieces are glued by a regularized maximum.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Richberg]\label{lem:richberg-euclidean}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open, let $u$ be a continuous function on $\Omega$, and let $a>0$ be such that $u-a|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic. Then for every continuous function $\varepsilon\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)$ there is a smooth function $v$ on $\Omega$ with
\[
 u\le v\le u+\varepsilon,\qquad v-\tfrac a2|z|^2\in\PSH(\Omega).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: local approximations.} Choose compact sets $K_1\subset\Int K_2\subset K_2\subset\Int K_3\subset\cdots$ with union $\Omega$, and put $K_0=K_{-1}=\varnothing$. For each $m\ge1$, the compact set $K_m\setminus\Int K_{m-1}$ is covered by finitely many balls $B(c,r/3)$ with $c\in K_m\setminus\Int K_{m-1}$ and $B(c,r)\Subset\Int K_{m+1}\setminus K_{m-2}$. Collecting these balls for all $m$, we obtain a sequence of balls $B_i=B(c_i,r_i)\Subset\Omega$ whose inner balls $B(c_i,r_i/3)$ cover $\Omega$. The family $(B_i)$ is locally finite: a compact set lies in some $K_m$ and meets only balls chosen at the stages $m'\le m+1$.

The function $u=(u-a|z|^2)+a|z|^2$ is continuous and plurisubharmonic. Let $\rho_h$ be the radial mollifier of \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution}. By \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} we have $u*\rho_h\ge u$, and by \cref{lem:mollifier-density} $u*\rho_h\to u$ locally uniformly as $h\to0$. Moreover $u*\rho_h-a|z|^2*\rho_h=(u-a|z|^2)*\rho_h$ is smooth with positive semidefinite complex Hessian, and $|z|^2*\rho_h=|z|^2+\int|y|^2\rho_h(y)\,\mathrm dV(y)$ because $\rho_h$ is radial. Hence the complex Hessian matrix of $u*\rho_h$ is at least $aI$.

For each $i$ choose $\delta_i$ with $0<\delta_i<a/2$ and $\delta_ir_i^2<\min_{\overline B_i}\varepsilon$, put $\eta_i=\delta_ir_i^2/6$, and choose $h_i>0$ so small that $u*\rho_{h_i}$ is defined near $\overline B_i$ and satisfies $0\le u*\rho_{h_i}-u<\eta_i$ there. Define
\[
 u_i=u*\rho_{h_i}+\delta_i\bigl((2r_i/3)^2-|z-c_i|^2\bigr)
 \quad\text{on }B_i.
\]
The quadratic term is at least $\delta_ir_i^2/3=2\eta_i$ on the inner ball $B(c_i,r_i/3)$, is less than $-2\eta_i$ where $|z-c_i|>\sqrt7\,r_i/3$, and is at most $4\delta_ir_i^2/9$ everywhere. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &u_i>u+\eta_i&&\text{on }B(c_i,r_i/3),\\
 &u_i<u-\eta_i&&\text{where }\sqrt7\,r_i/3<|z-c_i|<r_i,\\
 &u_i+\eta_i<u+\varepsilon&&\text{on }B_i,
 \end{aligned}
\]
the last because $2\eta_i+4\delta_ir_i^2/9=7\delta_ir_i^2/9<\varepsilon$ on $B_i$. The complex Hessian matrix of $u_i$ is at least $(a-\delta_i)I\ge(a/2)I$.

\emph{Step 2: a regularized maximum.} Fix an even smooth function $\theta\ge0$ on $\mathbb R$ with support in $(-1,1)$ and integral one. For an integer $p\ge1$, positive numbers $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_p$ and $t=(t_1,\ldots,t_p)\in\mathbb R^p$, define
\[
 M_\eta(t)=\int_{\mathbb R^p}\max_i(t_i+\eta_i x_i)
                         \prod_i\theta(x_i)\,\mathrm dx.
\]
The substitution $y_i=\eta_ix_i$ and the evenness of $\theta$ show that $M_\eta$ is the convolution of $t\mapsto\max_it_i$ with a smooth compactly supported function, so $M_\eta$ is smooth. It has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M_\eta$ is convex and nondecreasing in each variable, since for fixed $x$ the function $t\mapsto\max_i(t_i+\eta_ix_i)$ is.
\item $\max_it_i\le M_\eta(t)\le\max_i(t_i+\eta_i)$. The upper bound holds because $|x_i|<1$ on the support of the integrand. For the lower bound, integrate $\max_j(t_j+\eta_jx_j)\ge t_i+\eta_ix_i$ and use $\int x_i\theta(x_i)\,\mathrm dx_i=0$.
\item $M_\eta(t_1+c,\ldots,t_p+c)=M_\eta(t)+c$ for $c\in\mathbb R$. Differentiating in $c$, together with the first property, gives $\partial M_\eta/\partial t_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i\partial M_\eta/\partial t_i=1$.
\item If $t_i+\eta_i\le\max_{j\ne i}(t_j-\eta_j)$ for some $i$, then deleting the variable $t_i$ and the parameter $\eta_i$ does not change the value of $M_\eta$. Indeed, for $|x_j|<1$ we have $t_i+\eta_ix_i<t_i+\eta_i\le\max_{j\ne i}(t_j-\eta_j)<\max_{j\ne i}(t_j+\eta_jx_j)$, so the $i$-th term never realizes the maximum, and integrating out $x_i$ removes it.
\end{enumerate}
For smooth real functions $w_1,\ldots,w_p$, the chain rule gives
\[
 \partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}M_\eta(w)=\sum_i\frac{\partial M_\eta}{\partial t_i}(w)\,\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}w_i+\sum_{i,l}\frac{\partial^2M_\eta}{\partial t_i\partial t_l}(w)\,\partial_{z_j}w_i\,\overline{\partial_{z_k}w_l}.
\]
The second matrix is positive semidefinite, since the real Hessian of $M_\eta$ is, by convexity. Hence, by the third property, if each $w_i$ has complex Hessian matrix at least $(a/2)I$, so does $M_\eta(w_1,\ldots,w_p)$.

\emph{Step 3: gluing.} For $z\in\Omega$ let $I(z)=\{i:z\in B_i\}$, a finite nonempty set, and define $v(z)=M_\eta\bigl((u_i(z))_{i\in I(z)}\bigr)$ with the parameters $(\eta_i)_{i\in I(z)}$. We show that $v$ is smooth near a fixed point $z_0$. Let $I_0=\{i:z_0\in B_i\}$ and $I_1=\{i:z_0\in\partial B_i\}$, finite sets by local finiteness, and choose $j$ with $z_0\in B(c_j,r_j/3)$. There is a neighborhood $W$ of $z_0$ that is contained in $B(c_j,r_j/3)$ and in $B_i$ for $i\in I_0$, that meets $\overline B_i$ only for $i\in I_0\cup I_1$, and whose intersection with $B_i$, for $i\in I_1$, lies in the shell $\sqrt7\,r_i/3<|z-c_i|<r_i$. For $z\in W$ we have $I_0\subset I(z)\subset I_0\cup I_1$, $j\in I_0$, and every $i\in I(z)\cap I_1$ satisfies
\[
 u_i(z)+\eta_i<u(z)<u_j(z)-\eta_j.
\]
By the fourth property of $M_\eta$, applied to these indices one at a time, $v=M_\eta\bigl((u_i)_{i\in I_0}\bigr)$ on $W$, which is smooth. Thus $v$ is smooth on $\Omega$, and by Step 2 its complex Hessian matrix is at least $(a/2)I$; hence $v-\tfrac a2|z|^2$ is smooth with positive semidefinite complex Hessian, and so it is plurisubharmonic by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. Finally, every $z$ lies in some inner ball $B(c_j,r_j/3)$, and the second property of $M_\eta$ gives $v(z)\ge u_j(z)>u(z)$ and $v(z)\le\max_{i\in I(z)}(u_i(z)+\eta_i)<u(z)+\varepsilon(z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:oka-boundary-distance}
Let $n\ge1$ and let $\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb C^n$ be a nonempty open set. If $\Omega$ is pseudoconvex, then $-\log d(z,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega)$ is a continuous plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. Conversely, if this function is plurisubharmonic, then $\Omega$ is pseudoconvex. Every pseudoconvex open set in $\mathbb C^n$ has a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: distances in a fixed direction.} Let $\Omega$ be pseudoconvex, with a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function $\rho$. For $z\in\Omega$ and $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ put
\[
 \delta(z,\xi)=\sup\{r>0:z+\xi\Delta_r\subset\Omega\}\in(0,\infty],
\]
so that $\delta(z,0)=\infty$. The function $\delta$ is lower semicontinuous on $\Omega\times\mathbb C^n$: if $r<\delta(z,\xi)$, then the compact disc $z+\xi\overline\Delta_r$ lies in $\Omega$, hence so does $z'+\xi'\overline\Delta_r$ for all $(z',\xi')$ close to $(z,\xi)$, and $\delta(z',\xi')\ge r$ for these points. Thus $V=-\log\delta$ is an upper semicontinuous function on $\Omega\times\mathbb C^n$ with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$. We verify the hypothesis of \cref{lem:polynomial-disc-comparison} for $V$ on the open set $\Omega\times\mathbb C^n\subset\mathbb C^{2n}$.

Let $(z_0+ta,\xi_0+tb)$, $|t|\le1$, be a closed disc in $\Omega\times\mathbb C^n$, and let $P$ be a polynomial with $V(z_0+ta,\xi_0+tb)\le\Real P(t)$ for $|t|=1$, that is, $\delta(z_0+ta,\xi_0+tb)\ge|\mathrm e^{-P(t)}|$. Consider the holomorphic map
\[
 h(t,w)=z_0+t a+w\mathrm e^{-P(t)}(\xi_0+t b),\qquad(t,w)\in\mathbb C^2.
\]
We have $h(t,0)=z_0+ta\in\Omega$ for $|t|\le1$; and $h(t,w)\in\Omega$ for $|t|=1$ and $|w|<1$, because then $|w\mathrm e^{-P(t)}|<\delta(z_0+ta,\xi_0+tb)$. Let $R$ be the supremum of the numbers $r\ge0$ with $h(\overline\Delta\times\overline\Delta_r)\subset\Omega$. Since $h(\overline\Delta\times\{0\})$ is a compact subset of $\Omega$ and $h$ is continuous, $R>0$. Suppose that $R<1$. Then $h(\partial\Delta\times\overline\Delta_R)$ is a compact subset of $\Omega$; let $c$ be the maximum of $\rho$ on it. For $|w|<R$ we have $h(\overline\Delta\times\{w\})\subset\Omega$, because every radius smaller than an admissible one is admissible; hence the map $t\mapsto h(t,w)$ sends a neighborhood of $\overline\Delta$ into $\Omega$, and $t\mapsto\rho(h(t,w))$ is subharmonic there by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback} (it is finite), and the maximum principle of \cref{prop:subharmonic-maximum} gives $\rho(h(t,w))\le c$ for $|t|\le1$. Thus $h(\overline\Delta\times\Delta_R)$ lies in the compact set $\{\rho\le c\}$, and so does its closure $h(\overline\Delta\times\overline\Delta_R)$. This compact subset of $\Omega$ has positive distance from $\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega$, and the uniform continuity of $h$ on compact sets gives $h(\overline\Delta\times\overline\Delta_{R'})\subset\Omega$ for some $R'>R$, contradicting the definition of $R$. Hence $R\ge1$. In particular $h(0,w)=z_0+w\mathrm e^{-P(0)}\xi_0\in\Omega$ for $|w|<1$, which means that $\delta(z_0,\xi_0)\ge\mathrm e^{-\Real P(0)}$, that is, $V(z_0,\xi_0)\le\Real P(0)$. By \cref{lem:polynomial-disc-comparison}, $V$ satisfies the circle submean inequality on every closed complex disc in $\Omega\times\mathbb C^n$.

\emph{Step 2: the boundary distance.} Now let $\Omega\ne\mathbb C^n$ and put $d(z)=d(z,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega)$. For a unit vector $\xi$ we have $\delta(z,\xi)\ge d(z)$, because $z+\xi\Delta_{d(z)}$ lies in the ball $B(z,d(z))\subset\Omega$. Equality holds if $\xi$ points from $z$ to a nearest point of the closed set $\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega$, since that point is $z+d(z)\xi$. Hence
\[
 -\log d(z)=\max_{|\xi|=1}V(z,\xi).
\]
For fixed $\xi$, the function $z\mapsto V(z,\xi)$ satisfies the circle submean inequality on every closed disc $\{z_0+ta:|t|\le1\}\subset\Omega$, by Step 1 with $b=0$, and it is bounded above by $-\log d$. Therefore
\[
 V(z_0,\xi)\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}V(z_0+\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}a,\xi)\,\mathrm d\theta\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}-\log d(z_0+\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}a)\,\mathrm d\theta,
\]
and taking the maximum over $\xi$ gives the submean inequality for $-\log d$. Since $d$ is positive and $1$-Lipschitz, $-\log d$ is continuous. Its restriction to each connected component of each affine complex-line section of $\Omega$ is therefore subharmonic (\cref{def:subharmonic}), and $-\log d$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ by \cref{def:plurisubharmonic}.

\emph{Step 3: smooth exhaustion functions.} Let $\Omega\ne\mathbb C^n$ be an open set on which $-\log d$ is plurisubharmonic, and put
\[
 u(z)=|z|^2+\max\{-\log d(z),0\}.
\]
Then $u$ is continuous, $u-|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and $u$ is an exhaustion function of $\Omega$: the set $\{u\le c\}$ is contained in the ball $\{|z|^2\le c\}$ and in $\{d\ge\mathrm e^{-c}\}$, so it is a closed and bounded subset of $\mathbb C^n$ contained in $\Omega$, hence compact. In particular, $\Omega$ is pseudoconvex, which proves the converse statement. By \cref{lem:richberg-euclidean} with $a=1$ and $\varepsilon=1$, there is a smooth function $s$ with $u\le s\le u+1$ such that $s-|z|^2/2$ is plurisubharmonic. Then $s\ge u\ge0$, the complex Hessian matrix of $s$ is at least $I/2$, so that $s$ is strictly plurisubharmonic, and $\{s\le c\}$ is a closed subset of the compact set $\{u\le c\}$. Thus $s$ is a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function. If $\Omega\ne\mathbb C^n$ is pseudoconvex, Step 2 shows that $-\log d$ is plurisubharmonic, so this construction applies; for $\Omega=\mathbb C^n$ we take $s=|z|^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:pseudoconvex-sublevels-products}
Products of pseudoconvex open sets are pseudoconvex. If $s\ge0$ is a smooth plurisubharmonic exhaustion function of an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, then each nonempty sublevel set $\{s<c\}$ is pseudoconvex and relatively compact in $\Omega$. In particular, every pseudoconvex open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ is the union of an increasing sequence of pseudoconvex open sets $\Omega_j\Subset\Omega$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ and $\Omega'\subset\mathbb C^m$ be pseudoconvex, with nonnegative continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion functions $\rho$ and $\rho'$. The function $(z,w)\mapsto\rho(z)+\rho'(w)$ is continuous and plurisubharmonic on $\Omega\times\Omega'$, by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback} applied to the two projections and by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}. Its sublevel set $\{\le c\}$ is a closed subset of the compact set $\{\rho\le c\}\times\{\rho'\le c\}$, so $\Omega\times\Omega'$ is pseudoconvex.

Next let $s$ be as in the statement, and let $\{s<c\}$ be nonempty. The function $x\mapsto1/(c-x)$ is convex and increasing on $(-\infty,c)$, so the complex Hessian matrix of $1/(c-s)$, namely $(c-s)^{-2}(s_{j\bar k})+2(c-s)^{-3}(s_j\overline{s_k})$, is positive semidefinite. Hence $|z|^2+1/(c-s)$ is a continuous plurisubharmonic function on $\{s<c\}$. For $C>0$ its sublevel set $\{\le C\}$ is closed in $\{s<c\}$ and contained in the set $\{s\le c-1/C\}$, which is a compact subset of $\{s<c\}$; hence it is compact, and $\{s<c\}$ is pseudoconvex. Moreover $\{s<c\}$ is contained in the compact set $\{s\le c\}$, so it is relatively compact in $\Omega$.

Finally, if $\Omega$ is pseudoconvex, \cref{thm:oka-boundary-distance} provides a nonnegative smooth plurisubharmonic exhaustion function $s$, and the sets $\Omega_j=\{s<j\}$, $j\ge1$, are pseudoconvex (trivially so when empty), relatively compact in $\Omega$, increasing, and have union $\Omega$. They need not be connected, which is harmless, since \cref{def:pseudoconvex-domain} applies to arbitrary open sets.
\end{proof}

With the boundary distance at hand, the invariant exhaustion function required in Kiselman's minimum principle exists automatically on pseudoconvex domains.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:kiselman-pseudoconvex-tube}
Let $m,k\ge1$, and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^m\times\mathbb C^k$ be a pseudoconvex domain that is invariant under imaginary translations and whose nonempty real fibres $V_z$ are convex, in the notation of \cref{sec:kiselman-minimum}. If $u\in\PSH(\Omega)$ is invariant under imaginary translations, then the function $v(z)=\inf_{t\in V_z}u(z,t)$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on $p(\Omega)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
We construct a function $\psi$ with the properties required in \cref{thm:kiselman-invariant-exhaustion}; the corollary then follows from that theorem. If $\Omega=\mathbb C^{m+k}$, let $\psi(z,w)=|z|^2+|\Real w|^2$. Otherwise let $d$ be the distance to $\mathbb C^{m+k}\setminus\Omega$ and put
\[
 \psi(z,w)=|z|^2+|\Real w|^2+
           \max\{-\log d(z,w),0\}.
\]
Imaginary translations are isometries of $\mathbb C^{m+k}$ that preserve $\Omega$ and its complement, so $d$ and $\psi$ are invariant under them. The function $\psi$ is continuous and nonnegative. It is plurisubharmonic: $-\log d$ is plurisubharmonic by \cref{thm:oka-boundary-distance}, and $(\Real w_a)^2=\tfrac12|w_a|^2+\tfrac12\Real(w_a^2)$ differs from a plurisubharmonic function by a pluriharmonic one. Finally, for $c\in\mathbb R$ the set $\{(z,t)\in\Omega_{\mathbb R}:\psi(z,t)\le c\}$ is contained in $\{|z|^2+|t|^2\le c\}$ and, if $\Omega\ne\mathbb C^{m+k}$, in $\{d\ge\mathrm e^{-c}\}$; it is therefore a closed and bounded subset of $\mathbb C^m\times\mathbb R^k$ contained in $\Omega$, hence compact.
\end{proof}

\section{Interior density and the weighted estimate}
\label{sec:weighted-dbar-estimate}

Throughout this section $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ is open, $n\ge1$, and $\mathrm dV=\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Lebesgue measure. For a real function $q$ of class $C^2$ we write $H_q=(q_{j\bar k})$ for its complex Hessian matrix, with $q_{j\bar k}=\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}q$ as in \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}. Our estimates are expressed through $H_q$ rather than through the coefficients of $\ddc q$. With $\beta$ as in \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}, the inequality $\ddc q\ge\varepsilon\beta$ is equivalent to $H_q\ge\pi\varepsilon I$ by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}.

We give $(0,1)$- and $(0,2)$-forms the pointwise norms
\[
 \Bigl|\sum_jf_j\,\mathrm d\bar z_j\Bigr|^2=\sum_j|f_j|^2,\qquad
 \Bigl|\sum_{j<k}g_{jk}\,\mathrm d\bar z_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k\Bigr|^2=\sum_{j<k}|g_{jk}|^2;
\]
for $n=1$ there are no nonzero $(0,2)$-forms. For a measurable function $\varphi\colon\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)$, which we call a weight, we write $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi})$ for the Hilbert space of measurable functions $u$ with $\int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$, modulo null functions, with the inner product $\langle u,v\rangle_\varphi=\int_\Omega u\bar v\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV$; similarly $L^2_{(0,q)}(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi})$ consists of the $(0,q)$-forms $f$ with measurable coefficients and $\int_\Omega|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. We write $L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)$ for the $(0,1)$-forms with coefficients in $L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$. The operator $\bar\partial$ is always taken in the sense of distributions.

Fix smooth real functions $\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\varphi_3$ on $\Omega$ and put
\[
 \mathcal H_0=L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_1}),\quad
 \mathcal H_1=L^2_{(0,1)}(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2}),\quad
 \mathcal H_2=L^2_{(0,2)}(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_3}).
\]
Let $T=\bar\partial$ from $\mathcal H_0$ to $\mathcal H_1$ and $S=\bar\partial$ from $\mathcal H_1$ to $\mathcal H_2$ be defined on their maximal domains
\[
 \Dom T=\{u\in \mathcal H_0:\bar\partial u\in \mathcal H_1\},\qquad \Dom S=\{f\in \mathcal H_1:\bar\partial f\in \mathcal H_2\}.
\]
Since the weights are continuous, convergence in $\mathcal H_0$, $\mathcal H_1$ or $\mathcal H_2$ implies convergence in $L^2$ on compact sets, and hence in the sense of distributions; therefore $T$ and $S$ are closed. Since the measures $\mathrm e^{-\varphi_k}\,\mathrm dV$ are Radon measures, \cref{lem:mollifier-density} shows that smooth compactly supported functions and forms are dense in $\mathcal H_0$ and in $\mathcal H_1$; they belong to $\Dom T$ and $\Dom S$, so $T$ and $S$ are densely defined. Finally $\bar\partial\bar\partial=0$ on distributions, so $\operatorname{Ran}T\subset\ker S$. Thus $T$ and $S$ satisfy the hypotheses on the operators in \cref{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability}, with $\mathcal H_k$ in place of $H_k$.

Let $f=\sum_jf_j\,\mathrm d\bar z_j\in\Dom T^*$. For every $u\in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$ we have
\[
 \sum_j\int_\Omega\partial_{\bar z_j}u\;\overline{f_j}\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2}\,\mathrm dV=\langle Tu,f\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}=\langle u,T^*f\rangle_{\mathcal H_0}=\int_\Omega u\,\overline{T^*f}\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_1}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Integrating by parts on the left in the sense of distributions, we obtain $\mathrm e^{-\varphi_1}T^*f=-\sum_j\partial_{z_j}(f_j\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2})$, that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-weight-adjoint}
 T^*f=-\mathrm e^{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}
       \sum_j\bigl(\partial_{z_j}f_j-(\partial_{z_j}\varphi_2)f_j\bigr)
\end{equation}
in the sense of distributions. Conversely, if $f$ is a smooth compactly supported $(0,1)$-form, the same computation is valid for every $u\in\Dom T$, since it only pairs the distribution $\bar\partial u$ with the test form $f\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2}$; hence $f\in\Dom T^*$, and $T^*f$ is given by \eqref{eq:three-weight-adjoint}. For a general $f$, however, \eqref{eq:three-weight-adjoint} describes $T^*f$ but does not characterize $\Dom T^*$: membership in $\Dom T^*$ also encodes a boundary condition, which compactly supported test functions cannot detect. The following lemma shows that this boundary condition does not matter for the estimate, provided the weights are suitably related.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:three-weight-density}
Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be smooth real functions on $\Omega$, and put $\varphi_k=\varphi+(k-3)\psi$ for $k=1,2,3$, so that $\varphi_1=\varphi-2\psi$, $\varphi_2=\varphi-\psi$ and $\varphi_3=\varphi$. Assume that there are functions $\chi_\nu\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, $\nu\in\mathbb N$, with $0\le\chi_\nu\le1$ and $|\bar\partial\chi_\nu|^2\le\mathrm e^\psi$, such that for every compact set $K\subset\Omega$ we have $\chi_\nu=1$ on a neighborhood of $K$ for all large $\nu$. Then, for the operators $T$ and $S$ defined by these weights, the smooth compactly supported $(0,1)$-forms are dense in $\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S$ for the graph norm
$\|f\|_{\mathcal H_1}+\|T^*f\|_{\mathcal H_0}+\|Sf\|_{\mathcal H_2}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: cutting off.} Let $f\in\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S$, and write $\chi=\chi_\nu$. Since $\chi$ is real, $|\partial_{z_j}\chi|=|\partial_{\bar z_j}\chi|$, so the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\chi)f_j|^2\le|\bar\partial\chi|^2|f|^2$. Lagrange's identity $\sum_{j<k}|a_jb_k-a_kb_j|^2=|a|^2|b|^2-|\sum_ja_j\bar b_j|^2$ gives likewise $|\bar\partial\chi\wedge f|^2\le|\bar\partial\chi|^2|f|^2$. Since $\varphi_1-2\varphi_2=-\varphi=-\varphi_3$ and $|\bar\partial\chi|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\le\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2}$, the form $\bar\partial\chi\wedge f$ lies in $\mathcal H_2$, the function $\mathrm e^{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\chi)f_j$ lies in $\mathcal H_0$, and both of their squared norms are at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-weight-cutoff-error}
 \int_\Omega|f|^2|\bar\partial\chi_\nu|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le\|f\|_{\mathcal H_1}^2.
\end{equation}

Let $u\in\Dom T$. Then $\chi u\in\Dom T$ and $T(\chi u)=\chi Tu+u\,\bar\partial\chi$, because $u\,\bar\partial\chi\in \mathcal H_1$, the weights being bounded on the support of $\chi$. As $\chi$ is real and $\overline{\partial_{\bar z_j}\chi}=\partial_{z_j}\chi$, we get
\[
 \langle Tu,\chi f\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}=\langle T(\chi u),f\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}-\langle u\,\bar\partial\chi,f\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}
 =\Bigl\langle u,\chi T^*f-\mathrm e^{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\chi)f_j\Bigr\rangle_{\mathcal H_0}.
\]
Hence $\chi f\in\Dom T^*$, and the distributional product rule shows that $\chi f\in\Dom S$, with
\[
 \begin{split}
 T^*(\chi f)-\chi T^*f
    &=-\mathrm e^{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\chi)f_j,\\
 S(\chi f)-\chi Sf&=\bar\partial\chi\wedge f.
 \end{split}
\]
By \eqref{eq:three-weight-cutoff-error}, the squared norms of both differences are bounded by $\int|f|^2|\bar\partial\chi_\nu|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV$. As $\nu\to\infty$, $\bar\partial\chi_\nu$ tends to zero pointwise, since it vanishes near each point for large $\nu$, and the integrand is dominated by the integrable function $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2}$. By dominated convergence, both differences tend to zero. Again by dominated convergence, $\chi_\nu f\to f$, $\chi_\nu T^*f\to T^*f$ and $\chi_\nu Sf\to Sf$. Therefore $\chi_\nu f\to f$ in the graph norm.

\emph{Step 2: smoothing.} It remains to approximate a form $f\in\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S$ with compact support $K\subset\Omega$. By \eqref{eq:three-weight-adjoint},
\[
 \sum_j\partial_{z_j}f_j=-\mathrm e^{\varphi_2-\varphi_1}T^*f+\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\varphi_2)f_j\in L^2_{\loc}(\Omega),
\]
and $\bar\partial f=Sf\in L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$. Let $f^\varepsilon=\sum_j(f_j*\rho_\varepsilon)\,\mathrm d\bar z_j$, with $\rho_\varepsilon$ the mollifier of \cref{lem:mollifier-density}; for small $\varepsilon$ these forms are smooth and supported in a fixed compact neighborhood $K'$ of $K$ in $\Omega$. Convolution commutes with derivatives with constant coefficients, so $\bar\partial f^\varepsilon=(\bar\partial f)*\rho_\varepsilon$ and $\sum_j\partial_{z_j}f^\varepsilon_j=(\sum_j\partial_{z_j}f_j)*\rho_\varepsilon$. By \cref{lem:mollifier-density}, $f^\varepsilon$, $\sum_j\partial_{z_j}f^\varepsilon_j$ and $\bar\partial f^\varepsilon$ converge in $L^2(K')$ to $f$, $\sum_j\partial_{z_j}f_j$ and $\bar\partial f$. Since \eqref{eq:three-weight-adjoint} applies to both $f^\varepsilon$ and $f$, and its remaining coefficients are smooth, $T^*f^\varepsilon\to T^*f$ in $L^2(K')$. The weights are bounded above and below on $K'$, so $f^\varepsilon\to f$ in the graph norm.
\end{proof}
\index{operator!graph norm density}

For compactly supported smooth forms, the inequality we need follows from an integration-by-parts identity, the Bochner--Kodaira--Morrey--Kohn identity in its flat weighted form.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:interior-bochner-identity}
Let $\varphi$ be a smooth real function on $\Omega$, and let $\delta_j$ be the differential operator $\delta_jg=-\partial_{z_j}g+(\partial_{z_j}\varphi)g$, $1\le j\le n$. Then every smooth compactly supported $(0,1)$-form $f=\sum_jf_j\,\mathrm d\bar z_j$ on $\Omega$ satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\int\Bigl|\sum_j\delta_j f_j\Bigr|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV
   +\int|\bar\partial f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\\
 &\qquad=\int\sum_{j,k}\varphi_{j\bar k}f_j\bar f_k\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV
   +\int\sum_{j,k}|\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV.
 \end{split}
\]
Consequently, if $\psi$ is a smooth real function and $T$ and $S$ are the operators defined by the weights $\varphi_k=\varphi+(k-3)\psi$ of \cref{lem:three-weight-density}, then for every $t>0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-weight-basic-estimate}
 \int\sum_{j,k}\varphi_{j\bar k}f_j\bar f_k\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV
 \le(1+t^{-1})\|T^*f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2+\|Sf\|_{\mathcal H_2}^2
      +(1+t)\int|f|^2|\bar\partial\psi|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For smooth compactly supported functions $g$ and $w$, integration by parts gives $\langle\partial_{\bar z_j}g,w\rangle_\varphi=\langle g,\delta_jw\rangle_\varphi$, so $\delta_j$ is the formal adjoint of $\partial_{\bar z_j}$ for the inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\varphi$. A direct computation gives the commutation relation
\[
 \partial_{\bar z_k}(\delta_jg)-\delta_j(\partial_{\bar z_k}g)=\varphi_{j\bar k}\,g.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Bigl\|\sum_j\delta_jf_j\Bigr\|_\varphi^2=\sum_{j,k}\langle\partial_{\bar z_k}\delta_jf_j,f_k\rangle_\varphi
 =\int\sum_{j,k}\varphi_{j\bar k}f_j\bar f_k\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV+\sum_{j,k}\langle\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j,\partial_{\bar z_j}f_k\rangle_\varphi.
\]
On the other hand, $\bar\partial f=\sum_{j<k}(\partial_{\bar z_j}f_k-\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j)\,\mathrm d\bar z_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$, and expanding the squares gives
\[
 \|\bar\partial f\|_\varphi^2=\sum_{j\ne k}\|\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j\|_\varphi^2-\sum_{j\ne k}\langle\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j,\partial_{\bar z_j}f_k\rangle_\varphi.
\]
When we add the two identities, the terms with $j\ne k$ in the last sums cancel, and the remaining terms $\|\partial_{\bar z_j}f_j\|_\varphi^2$ combine with $\sum_{j\ne k}\|\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j\|_\varphi^2$ to give $\sum_{j,k}\|\partial_{\bar z_k}f_j\|_\varphi^2$. This proves the identity.

For the estimate, we have $\varphi_1-\varphi_2=-\psi$ and $\partial_{z_j}\varphi_2=\partial_{z_j}\varphi-\partial_{z_j}\psi$, so \eqref{eq:three-weight-adjoint} reads
\[
 \sum_j\delta_jf_j=\mathrm e^\psi T^*f+\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\psi)f_j.
\]
By the elementary inequality $|A+B|^2\le(1+t^{-1})|A|^2+(1+t)|B|^2$,
\[
 \Bigl\|\sum_j\delta_jf_j\Bigr\|_\varphi^2\le(1+t^{-1})\int|T^*f|^2\mathrm e^{2\psi-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV+(1+t)\int\Bigl|\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\psi)f_j\Bigr|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
The first integral is $\|T^*f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2$, because $\varphi_1=\varphi-2\psi$, and $|\sum_j(\partial_{z_j}\psi)f_j|^2\le|\bar\partial\psi|^2|f|^2$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, since $|\partial_{z_j}\psi|=|\partial_{\bar z_j}\psi|$ for real $\psi$. Moreover $\|\bar\partial f\|_\varphi^2=\|Sf\|_{\mathcal H_2}^2$, because $\varphi_3=\varphi$. Inserting these bounds into the identity and dropping its last, nonnegative, term, we obtain \eqref{eq:three-weight-basic-estimate}.
\end{proof}
\index{Bochner--Kodaira identity!interior scalar form}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
The right-hand side of $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ is measured by a norm dual to the complex Hessian of the weight. Let $A=(A_{j\bar k})$ be a positive semidefinite Hermitian $n\times n$ matrix, and write $A[b]=\sum_{j,k}A_{j\bar k}b_j\bar b_k$ for $b\in\mathbb C^n$. For $\alpha\in\mathbb C^n$, identified with the $(0,1)$-covector $\sum_j\alpha_j\,\mathrm d\bar z_j$, the \emph{dual energy} of $\alpha$ with respect to $A$ is
\[
 |\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2
   =\sup_{b\ne0}\frac{|\sum_j\alpha_j\bar b_j|^2}
                            {\sum_{j,k}A_{j\bar k}b_j\bar b_k}\in[0,\infty],
\]
where a quotient with zero denominator is $0$ if its numerator vanishes and $+\infty$ otherwise. Directly from the definition, $|\sum_j\alpha_j\bar b_j|^2\le|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2A[b]$ whenever $|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}<\infty$. If $(e_l)$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb C^n$ in which the Hermitian form $A[\cdot]$ is diagonal, with $A[\sum_lc_le_l]=\sum_l\lambda_l|c_l|^2$, then $|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2=\sum_{\lambda_l>0}|\langle\alpha,e_l\rangle|^2/\lambda_l$ if $\langle\alpha,e_l\rangle=0$ for every $l$ with $\lambda_l=0$, and $|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2=\infty$ otherwise. In particular, the dual energy is a Hermitian quadratic form on the subspace of vectors where it is finite; it decreases when $A$ increases; and $|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2\le\lambda^{-1}|\alpha|^2$ if $A\ge\lambda I$ with $\lambda>0$. Defining the dual energy by a supremum covers singular matrices $A$ and avoids any ambiguity about transposed indices in the inverse matrix.
\index{dual energy}

\begin{theorem}[H\"ormander]\label{thm:hormander-smooth-weight}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a pseudoconvex open set, $n\ge1$, and let $\varphi$ be a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. Let $\alpha\in L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)$ satisfy $\bar\partial\alpha=0$ and
\[
 M=\int_\Omega|\alpha|_{H_\varphi^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\]
Then there is $u\in L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi})$ with $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ and $\int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The idea is to modify the weight far out in $\Omega$ so that \cref{lem:three-weight-density} applies and the error term in \eqref{eq:three-weight-basic-estimate} is absorbed by additional convexity, and then to let the modification recede to the boundary.

\emph{Step 1: auxiliary functions.} By \cref{thm:oka-boundary-distance}, $\Omega$ has a nonnegative smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function $s$. Fix an integer $t\ge1$. Let $\kappa$ be a smooth function on $\mathbb R$ with $0\le\kappa\le1$, $\kappa=1$ on $(-\infty,0]$, $\kappa=0$ on $[1,\infty)$ and $|\kappa'|\le2$, and for integers $\nu\ge t+2$ put $\chi_\nu=\kappa(s-\nu)$. Then $\chi_\nu\in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$, because $\{s\le\nu+1\}$ is compact; $\chi_\nu=1$ on $\{s\le\nu\}$, which contains any given compact set for large $\nu$; and $\bar\partial\chi_\nu=\kappa'(s-\nu)\,\bar\partial s$ vanishes on $\{s<t+2\}$ and satisfies $|\bar\partial\chi_\nu|^2\le4|\bar\partial s|^2$. Let $\lambda$ be a smooth function on $\mathbb R$ with $0\le\lambda\le1$, $\lambda=0$ on $(-\infty,t+1]$ and $\lambda=1$ on $[t+2,\infty)$, and put
\[
 \psi=\lambda(s)\log\bigl(1+4|\bar\partial s|^2\bigr).
\]
Then $\psi$ is smooth and nonnegative, $\psi$ and $\bar\partial\psi$ vanish on $\{s\le t+1\}$, and $|\bar\partial\chi_\nu|^2\le\mathrm e^\psi$ for every $\nu$: on $\{s\ge t+2\}$ we have $\mathrm e^\psi=1+4|\bar\partial s|^2$, and on $\{s<t+2\}$ we have $\bar\partial\chi_\nu=0$.

Next we choose a smooth convex nondecreasing function $\gamma\colon\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$ with $\gamma=0$ on $(-\infty,t]$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hormander-convex-increase}
 \gamma(s)\ge2\psi,\qquad
 H_{\gamma(s)}\ge(1+t)|\bar\partial\psi|^2 I,
 \qquad\int_\Omega|\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi-\gamma(s)+\psi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\end{equation}
For integers $j\ge1$ the sets $K_j=\{t+j\le s\le t+j+1\}$ are compact. Hence the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_j$ of $H_s$ on $K_j$ is positive, and the numbers $m_j=(1+t)\max_{K_j}|\bar\partial\psi|^2$, $P_j=2\max_{K_j}\psi$ and $I_j=\int_{K_j}|\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi+\psi}\,\mathrm dV$ are finite, since $\alpha\in L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)$. Choose a smooth nondecreasing function $g\colon\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$ that vanishes on $(-\infty,t]$ and satisfies, for every $j\ge1$,
\[
 g\ge m_j/\lambda_j\ \text{on }[t+j,t+j+1],\qquad
 \int_{t+j-1}^{t+j}g(x)\,\mathrm dx\ge\max\bigl\{P_j,\log^+(2^jI_j)\bigr\}.
\]
For instance, $g(x)=\sum_{j\ge1}c_j\kappa_1(x-t-j+1)$, where $\kappa_1$ is smooth and nondecreasing with $\kappa_1=0$ on $(-\infty,0]$ and $\kappa_1=1$ on $[1/2,\infty)$, and the constants $c_j\ge0$ are chosen successively large; the sum is locally finite, since only the terms with $j<x-t+1$ are nonzero at $x$. Put $\gamma(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xg(y)\,\mathrm dy$. Then $\gamma$ is smooth, nondecreasing and convex, because $\gamma'=g$ is nondecreasing, and $\gamma=0$ on $(-\infty,t]$. On $\{s\le t+1\}$ the first two conditions in \eqref{eq:hormander-convex-increase} hold because $\psi$ and $\bar\partial\psi$ vanish there and $H_{\gamma(s)}\ge0$. On $K_j$ we have $\gamma(s)\ge\gamma(t+j)\ge P_j\ge2\psi$, and the chain rule
\[
 H_{\gamma(s)}=\gamma'(s)H_s+\gamma''(s)\bigl(s_j\overline{s_k}\bigr)\ge\gamma'(s)H_s
\]
gives $H_{\gamma(s)}\ge(m_j/\lambda_j)\lambda_jI\ge(1+t)|\bar\partial\psi|^2I$. Finally, the integral in \eqref{eq:hormander-convex-increase} over the compact set $\{s\le t+1\}$ is finite, and its integral over $K_j$ is at most $\mathrm e^{-\gamma(t+j)}I_j\le2^{-j}$.

\emph{Step 2: the estimate.} Put $\varphi_0=\varphi+\gamma(s)$ and $\varphi_k=\varphi_0+(k-3)\psi$ for $k=1,2,3$, and let $T$ and $S$ be the corresponding operators. By the last condition in \eqref{eq:hormander-convex-increase}, $\alpha\in \mathcal H_1=L^2_{(0,1)}(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_2})$, and $\alpha\in\ker S$. Let $f$ be a smooth compactly supported $(0,1)$-form. We apply \eqref{eq:three-weight-basic-estimate} with $\varphi_0$ in place of $\varphi$. Since $H_{\varphi_0}=H_\varphi+H_{\gamma(s)}$, the second condition in \eqref{eq:hormander-convex-increase} absorbs the last term of \eqref{eq:three-weight-basic-estimate}, and we obtain
\[
 \int H_\varphi[f]\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_0}\,\mathrm dV
       \le(1+t^{-1})\|T^*f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2+\|Sf\|_{\mathcal H_2}^2.
\]
Moreover $\varphi-2\varphi_2=-\varphi_0-\gamma(s)+2\psi\le-\varphi_0$ by the first condition in \eqref{eq:hormander-convex-increase}. Since $M<\infty$, the dual energy of $\alpha$ is finite almost everywhere, and the inequality $|\sum_j\alpha_j\bar f_j|^2\le|\alpha|_{H_\varphi^{-1}}^2H_\varphi[f]$ together with the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 |\langle\alpha,f\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}|^2
 &\le\int|\alpha|_{H_\varphi^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\cdot\int H_\varphi[f]\,\mathrm e^{\varphi-2\varphi_2}\,\mathrm dV
 \le M\int H_\varphi[f]\,\mathrm e^{-\varphi_0}\,\mathrm dV\\
 &\le M(1+t^{-1})\bigl(\|T^*f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2+\|Sf\|_{\mathcal H_2}^2\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
Both sides are continuous for the graph norm of \cref{lem:three-weight-density}, whose hypotheses hold by Step 1; hence the inequality extends to all $f\in\Dom T^*\cap\Dom S$. By \cref{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability} there is $u_t\in \mathcal H_0$ with $\bar\partial u_t=\alpha$ and $\|u_t\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2\le M(1+t^{-1})$. On $\{s<t\}$ both $\gamma(s)$ and $\psi$ vanish, so $\varphi_1=\varphi$ there and
\[
 \int_{\{s<t\}}|u_t|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le M(1+t^{-1}).
\]

\emph{Step 3: passage to the limit.} For each integer $l\ge1$ the functions $u_t$ with $t\ge l$ are bounded by $2M$ in $L^2(\{s<l\},\mathrm e^{-\varphi})$. By \cref{prop:hilbert-weak-sequences} and a diagonal argument, there is a sequence $t_i\to\infty$ such that $u_{t_i}$ converges weakly in $L^2(\{s<l\},\mathrm e^{-\varphi})$ for every $l$. The limits on different sublevel sets agree on their intersections and define a function $u$ on $\Omega$. Weak convergence in these spaces implies convergence in the sense of distributions, so $\bar\partial u=\alpha$. By the lower semicontinuity of the norm in \cref{prop:hilbert-weak-sequences}, $\int_{\{s<l\}}|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le\liminf_iM(1+t_i^{-1})=M$ for every $l$, and the monotone convergence theorem gives $\int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le M$.
\end{proof}
\index{H\"ormander estimate!smooth weight}

We now allow arbitrary plurisubharmonic weights. The strict plurisubharmonicity is then supplied by a smooth function $q$, and only a lower bound for its complex Hessian enters the estimate.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a pseudoconvex open set, $n\ge1$, let $p\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and let $q$ be a smooth real function on $\Omega$ whose complex Hessian matrix $A=H_q$ is positive semidefinite. Let $\alpha\in L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)$ satisfy $\bar\partial\alpha=0$ and
\[
 M=\int_\Omega|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\]
Then the equation $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ has a solution with $\int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV\le M$. In particular, if $A\ge\lambda I$ for a positive continuous function $\lambda$ on $\Omega$, then $M$ may be replaced by $\int_\Omega\lambda^{-1}|\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:pseudoconvex-sublevels-products} there are pseudoconvex open sets $\Omega_1\subset\Omega_2\subset\cdots$ with $\Omega_j\Subset\Omega$ and $\bigcup_j\Omega_j=\Omega$. Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be the radial mollifier of \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution}, and choose $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon_j<d(\overline\Omega_j,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega)$. Then $p_j=p*\rho_{\varepsilon_j}$ is defined and smooth on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega_j$, with positive semidefinite complex Hessian, and $p_j\downarrow p$ pointwise by \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} and \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. Choose $\eta_j\downarrow0$ and put $\varphi_j=p_j+q+\eta_j|z|^2$. Then $\varphi_j$ is smooth and strictly plurisubharmonic near $\overline\Omega_j$, $H_{\varphi_j}\ge A+\eta_jI\ge A$, and $\varphi_j\ge p+q$. Since the dual energy decreases when the matrix increases,
\[
 \int_{\Omega_j}|\alpha|_{H_{\varphi_j}^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j}\,\mathrm dV
 \le\int_{\Omega_j}|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV\le M.
\]
By \cref{thm:hormander-smooth-weight} there is $u_j$ on $\Omega_j$ with $\bar\partial u_j=\alpha$ and $\int_{\Omega_j}|u_j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j}\,\mathrm dV\le M$.

Let $K\subset\Omega$ be compact. Then $K\subset\Omega_k$ for some $k$, and for $j\ge k$ we have $\varphi_j\le\varphi_k$ on $K$, because $p_j$ and $\eta_j$ decrease. Hence $\int_K|u_j|^2\,\mathrm dV\le\mathrm e^{\max_K\varphi_k}M$ for $j\ge k$, so the sequence $(u_j)$ is eventually bounded in $L^2(K)$ for every compact set $K$. Exhausting $\Omega$ by compact sets, using \cref{prop:hilbert-weak-sequences} and a diagonal argument, we find a subsequence that converges weakly in $L^2(K)$ for every compact $K\subset\Omega$, to a function $u\in L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$. Then $u_j\to u$ in the sense of distributions, and $\bar\partial u=\alpha$.

Fix $k$ and a compact set $K\subset\Omega_k$. On $L^2(K)$ the norm with weight $\mathrm e^{-\varphi_k}$ is equivalent to the unweighted norm, so the subsequence also converges weakly for it, and the lower semicontinuity in \cref{prop:hilbert-weak-sequences} gives
\[
 \int_K|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_k}\,\mathrm dV\le\liminf_j\int_K|u_j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_k}\,\mathrm dV\le\liminf_j\int_K|u_j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j}\,\mathrm dV\le M.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, $\mathrm e^{-\varphi_k}$ increases to $\mathrm e^{-p-q}$, so the monotone convergence theorem gives $\int_K|u|^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV\le M$, and exhausting $\Omega$ by compact sets proves the estimate. The last assertion follows from the pointwise inequality $|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2\le\lambda^{-1}|\alpha|^2$.
\end{proof}
\index{H\"ormander estimate!singular weight}

We record some properties of the weighted spaces in \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}. The density $\mathrm e^{-p-q}$ takes values in $(0,\infty]$; it is infinite exactly on the pole set $\{p=-\infty\}$, which is a Lebesgue null set because $p$ is locally integrable (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}), and it need not be locally integrable. Nevertheless, $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-q})$ is a Hilbert space, by the completeness argument at the beginning of \cref{sec:hilbert-operator-complexes}, which applies to every positive measure. Since $p+q$ is locally bounded above, $\mathrm e^{-p-q}$ is bounded below by a positive constant on every compact set; hence convergence in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-q})$ implies convergence in $L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$, and so in the sense of distributions. A distribution $h$ on an open subset of $\mathbb C^n$ with $\bar\partial h=0$ is a holomorphic function: its Laplacian $4\sum_j\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_j}h$ vanishes by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}, so Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}), applied to its real and imaginary parts, shows that $h$ is a smooth function, and then $\partial_{\bar z_j}h=0$ holds pointwise. We write $\mathcal O(\Omega)$ for the space of holomorphic functions on $\Omega$. It follows that the holomorphic functions in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-q})$ form a closed subspace $K$. If $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ has a solution $u_0$ in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-q})$, then all solutions in this space form the closed affine subspace $u_0+K$. As in the proof of \cref{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability}, it contains a unique solution of smallest norm, the \emph{minimal solution}, and a solution is minimal if and only if it is orthogonal to $K$: if $u\perp K$, then $\|u+k\|^2=\|u\|^2+\|k\|^2$ for $k\in K$, and two solutions orthogonal to $K$ differ by an element of $K\cap K^\perp=\{0\}$.
\index{dbar@$\bar\partial$-equation!minimal solution}

\section{Interpolation and extension across a hyperplane}
\label{sec:weighted-interpolation-extension}

We now construct holomorphic functions with prescribed values and controlled weighted norms. In both constructions of this section, a smooth function with the right values is corrected by a solution of a $\bar\partial$-equation, and a singular term in the weight forces the correction to vanish where the values are prescribed.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:logarithmic-point-interpolation}
Let $r,L>0$, let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\ge1$, be a bounded pseudoconvex domain of diameter at most $L$, let $a\in\Omega$ with $B(a,2r)\Subset\Omega$, and let $p\in\PSH(\Omega)$. If $h$ is holomorphic on $B(a,2r)$ and $\int_{B(a,2r)}|h|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$, then there is $F\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 F(a)=h(a),\qquad
 \int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le C(n,r,L)
                         \int_{B(a,2r)}|h|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV,
\]
where the constant $C(n,r,L)$ depends only on $n$, $r$ and $L$, and not on $\Omega$, $p$, $h$ or $a$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a smooth function $\kappa$ on $\mathbb R$ with $0\le\kappa\le1$, $\kappa=1$ on $(-\infty,1]$ and $\kappa=0$ on $[3/2,\infty)$, and put $\chi(z)=\kappa(|z-a|/r)$. Then $\chi$ is smooth with compact support in $B(a,2r)$, equals one on $B(a,r)$, and satisfies $|\bar\partial\chi|\le c_0/r$ with $c_0=\sup|\kappa'|/2$, because $|\bar\partial|z-a||=1/2$. Extend $\chi h$ by zero to $\Omega$. Its derivative $\alpha=h\,\bar\partial\chi$ is a smooth $\bar\partial$-closed form supported in the shell $r\le|z-a|\le2r$. We apply \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} with the plurisubharmonic function $p+n\log|z-a|^2$ in place of $p$ and with $q=|z-a|^2$, for which $A=I$. Since $\mathrm e^{-|z-a|^2}\le1$ and $|z-a|^{-2n}\le r^{-2n}$ on the support of $\alpha$, we obtain a solution of $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ with
\[
 \int_\Omega |u|^2|z-a|^{-2n}\mathrm e^{-p-|z-a|^2}\,\mathrm dV
    \le c_0^2r^{-2n-2}\int_{B(a,2r)}|h|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
On $B(a,r)$ we have $\bar\partial u=\alpha=0$, so $u$ is holomorphic there, by the discussion following \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}. Since $p$ is bounded above on $B(a,r)$, the factor $\mathrm e^{-p-|z-a|^2}$ is bounded below there by a positive constant. If $u(a)$ were nonzero, then $|u|$ would be bounded below by a positive constant near $a$, and the integral on the left would diverge, because $\int_{B(a,\rho)}|z-a|^{-2n}\,\mathrm dV$ is a positive multiple of $\int_0^\rho\tau^{-2n}\tau^{2n-1}\,\mathrm d\tau=\infty$. Thus $u(a)=0$. The function $F=\chi h-u$ satisfies $\bar\partial F=0$, so it is holomorphic, and $F(a)=h(a)$. Since $|z-a|<L$ on $\Omega$, we have $|z-a|^{2n}\mathrm e^{|z-a|^2}\le L^{2n}\mathrm e^{L^2}$, and therefore
\[
 \int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le c_0^2L^{2n}\mathrm e^{L^2}r^{-2n-2}\int_{B(a,2r)}|h|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Finally, $|F|^2\le2|\chi h|^2+2|u|^2$ and $|\chi h|\le|h|\mathbf 1_{B(a,2r)}$ give the assertion with $C(n,r,L)=2+2c_0^2L^{2n}\mathrm e^{L^2}r^{-2n-2}$.
\end{proof}
\index{interpolation!logarithmic pole}

For extension from a hyperplane we use the same method, with the pole $\log|z_n|^2$ along the hyperplane $\{z_n=0\}$. Now, however, the cutoff must be concentrated near the hyperplane, and with a fixed smooth function $q$ the bound of \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} deteriorates as the cutoff concentrates. The remedy is a bounded twist of the weight. For a bounded plurisubharmonic function $\psi$, the next lemma estimates the minimal solution with the weight $(1-h)\mathrm e^{\psi-p}$, while the right-hand side is measured by the dual energy with respect to the complex Hessian of $\psi$, which can be made large where the right-hand side lives.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-weight-twist}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded pseudoconvex open set, let $p\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and let $\psi$ be a smooth bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. Put $A=H_\psi$ and $h=|\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}^2$, where $\bar\partial\psi$ is identified with the vector $(\partial_{\bar z_j}\psi)_j$, and assume that $h<1$ everywhere. Let $\alpha\in L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)\cap L^2_{(0,1)}(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p})$ satisfy $\bar\partial\alpha=0$, let $0\le a<1$, and assume that $h\le a$ almost everywhere on $\{\alpha\ne0\}$ and that
$E=\int_\Omega|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$.
Then $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ has a solution with
\[
 \int_\Omega(1-h)|u|^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV
      \le\frac{1+\sqrt a}{1-\sqrt a}\,E.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} with $q=|z|^2$, for which $A=I$, the equation $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ has a solution with $\int|u|^2\mathrm e^{-p-|z|^2}\,\mathrm dV\le\int|\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{-p-|z|^2}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $|z|^2$ is bounded on $\Omega$, so this solution lies in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p})$. Let $u$ be the minimal solution in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p})$, in the sense of the discussion following \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}. Since $\psi$ is bounded, $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p})$ and $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi})$ consist of the same functions, with equivalent norms, and so contain the same holomorphic functions. The function $v=u\mathrm e^\psi$ belongs to $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi})$ and solves
\[
 \bar\partial v=\beta=(\alpha+u\,\bar\partial\psi)\mathrm e^\psi.
\]
For every holomorphic $g$ in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi})$ we have $\int v\bar g\,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi}\,\mathrm dV=\int u\bar g\,\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV=0$, so $v$ is the minimal solution of $\bar\partial v=\beta$ in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi})$.

We now estimate $v$ by \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}, with the plurisubharmonic function $p$ and the smooth function $q=\psi$, whose complex Hessian matrix $A$ is positive semidefinite. The form $\beta=\bar\partial v$ is $\bar\partial$-closed, and it lies in $L^2_{\loc,(0,1)}(\Omega)$ because $u\in L^2_{\loc}(\Omega)$ and $\psi$ is smooth. Since $E<\infty$ and $h<1$, the dual energies of $\alpha$ and $\bar\partial\psi$ are finite almost everywhere, where the dual energy is a Hermitian quadratic form; we write $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A^{-1}}$ for the associated Hermitian inner product. The triangle inequality $|\alpha+u\,\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}\le|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}+|u|h^{1/2}$ gives
\[
 \int|\beta|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{-p-\psi}\,\mathrm dV=\int|\alpha+u\,\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV\le2E+2\sup_\Omega\mathrm e^\psi\int|u|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\]
So \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} yields a solution of $\bar\partial w=\beta$ whose squared norm in $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-p-\psi})$ is at most this integral, and the minimal solution $v$ has no larger norm. As $\int|v|^2\mathrm e^{-p-\psi}\,\mathrm dV=\int|u|^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV$, we obtain
\[
 \int|u|^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV
 \le\int|\alpha+u\,\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV.
\]

Almost everywhere we can expand
\[
 |\alpha+u\,\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}^2=|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2+2\Real\bigl(\bar u\langle\alpha,\bar\partial\psi\rangle_{A^{-1}}\bigr)+h|u|^2,
 \qquad|\langle\alpha,\bar\partial\psi\rangle_{A^{-1}}|\le|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}h^{1/2},
\]
the second inequality being the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. The function $h|u|^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}$ is integrable, since $h<1$; moving its integral to the left, we find that $J=\int(1-h)|u|^2\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV$, which is finite, satisfies
\[
 J\le E+2\int|u|\,|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}h^{1/2}\mathrm e^{\psi-p}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
If $a=0$, then $h=0$ almost everywhere on $\{\alpha\ne0\}$, the last integral vanishes, and $J\le E$, as claimed. Let $a>0$. The last integrand vanishes outside $\{\alpha\ne0\}$, where $h\le a$ and hence $h/(1-h)\le a/(1-a)$. For $t>1$, the inequality $2xy\le t^{-1}x^2+ty^2$ with $x=(1-h)^{1/2}|u|$ and $y=(h/(1-h))^{1/2}|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}$ gives there
\[
 2|u|\,|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}h^{1/2}\le t^{-1}(1-h)|u|^2+\frac{ta}{1-a}|\alpha|_{A^{-1}}^2.
\]
Integrating, we obtain
\[
 J\le\left(1+\frac{ta}{1-a}\right)E+t^{-1}J.
\]
Choose $t=1+a^{-1/2}$. Then $1+ta/(1-a)=(1+\sqrt a)/(1-a)=1/(1-\sqrt a)$ and $t/(t-1)=1+\sqrt a$, so $J\le(1+\sqrt a)E/(1-\sqrt a)$.
\end{proof}

The twisting function used for extension depends only on the normal variable. The next lemma records the one-variable calculus behind it.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:extension-log-log-weight}
Let $0<\varepsilon<1/4$. For $\zeta\in\mathbb C$ with $|\zeta|<1/4$ put
\[
 s=-\log(|\zeta|^2+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 \eta=s+\log s,\qquad\psi_\varepsilon=-\log\eta.
\]
Then $\psi_\varepsilon$ is a smooth, bounded, strictly subharmonic function on the disc $\Delta_{1/4}$. Let $h=|\partial_{\bar\zeta}\psi_\varepsilon|^2/(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}$. Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 (1-h)\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}&\ge(6s^2)^{-1},\\
 h&\le\frac{2}{-\log(2\varepsilon^2)}\quad\text{for }|\zeta|\le\varepsilon,\\
 \frac{\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}}
 {|\zeta|^2(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}}
     &\le25/4\quad\text{for }\varepsilon/2\le|\zeta|\le\varepsilon.
 \end{split}
\]
In particular $h<1$ everywhere, and the bound for $h$ on the disc $|\zeta|\le\varepsilon$ tends to zero as $\varepsilon\to0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $|\zeta|^2+\varepsilon^2<1/8$, we have $s>\log8>2$, so $\eta>s>2$ and $\psi_\varepsilon<0$ is well defined. For fixed $\varepsilon$, $s$ ranges in a bounded interval, so $\psi_\varepsilon$ is bounded; and $\psi_\varepsilon=G(|\zeta|^2)$ for a function $G$ that is smooth near $[0,1/16)$, so $\psi_\varepsilon$ is smooth.

For $\zeta\ne0$ put $x=|\zeta|^2$, $t=\log x$ and $b=x/(x+\varepsilon^2)\in(0,1)$, and denote derivatives with respect to $t$ by primes. Then $s'=-b$ and $b'=b(1-b)$, so $s''=-b(1-b)$. With $D=1+1/s$ we have $\eta'=-bD$ and $D'=b/s^2$, and differentiation gives
\[
 \psi_\varepsilon'=\frac{bD}{\eta},\qquad
 \psi_\varepsilon''=A+B,\quad
 A=\frac{b^2D^2}{\eta^2},\quad
 B=\frac{b(1-b)D}{\eta}+\frac{b^2}{\eta s^2}>0.
\]
For a function $g$ of $t=\log|\zeta|^2$ we have $\partial_{\bar\zeta}g=g'/\bar\zeta$ and $\partial_\zeta\partial_{\bar\zeta}g=g''/|\zeta|^2$. Hence $(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}=\psi_\varepsilon''/x>0$ and $h=(\psi_\varepsilon')^2/\psi_\varepsilon''=A/(A+B)<1$ for $\zeta\ne0$. At $\zeta=0$ we have $\partial_{\bar\zeta}\psi_\varepsilon=G'(x)\zeta=0$ and $(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}=G'(0)$, where $G'(x)=\psi_\varepsilon'/x=D/(\eta(x+\varepsilon^2))>0$; so $(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}>0$ and $h=0$ there. Thus $\psi_\varepsilon$ is strictly subharmonic, and $h<1$ everywhere.

For the first estimate let $\zeta\ne0$. Since $B\ge b^2/(\eta s^2)$, we have $A/B\le D^2s^2/\eta=(s+1)^2/\eta$, and therefore
\[
 (1-h)\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}=\frac{B}{\eta(A+B)}
      \ge\frac1{\eta+(s+1)^2}\ge\frac1{6s^2},
\]
because $\eta\le2s\le2s^2$ and $(s+1)^2\le4s^2$ for $s\ge1$. At $\zeta=0$ we have $(1-h)\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}=1/\eta\ge1/(6s^2)$ as well. For the second estimate let $0<x\le\varepsilon^2$. Then $b\le1/2$, and $B\ge b(1-b)D/\eta$ gives
\[
 h\le\frac AB\le\frac{bD}{\eta(1-b)}\le\frac D\eta=\frac{s+1}{s\eta}\le\frac2s,
\]
since $s+1\le2s\le2\eta$; moreover $s\ge-\log(2\varepsilon^2)$, because $x+\varepsilon^2\le2\varepsilon^2$. Together with $h(0)=0$, this proves the second estimate. For the third, let $\varepsilon/2\le|\zeta|\le\varepsilon$. Then $1/5\le b\le1/2$, so $\eta\psi_\varepsilon''\ge\eta B\ge b(1-b)D\ge b(1-b)\ge4/25$, and $\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}/(|\zeta|^2(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta})=1/(\eta\psi_\varepsilon'')\le25/4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ohsawa--Takegoshi]\label{thm:local-ot-extension}
There is an absolute constant $C$ with the following property. Let $n\ge1$ and $R>0$, let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^{n-1}\times\Delta_R$ be a pseudoconvex open set, and let $p\in\PSH(\Omega)$. Put $\Omega_0=\Omega\cap\{z_n=0\}$, which we identify with an open subset of $\mathbb C^{n-1}$. Then every holomorphic function $f$ on $\Omega_0$ with
\[
 I=\int_{\Omega_0}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}<\infty
\]
has an extension $F\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 F|_{\Omega_0}=f,\qquad
 \int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n}\le C R^2 I.
\]
Here, for $n=1$, $\Omega_0$ is a point or empty, and $I$ is the weighted value of $|f|^2$ at that point, the integral with respect to the counting measure $\mathrm dV_0$. If $\Omega_0=\varnothing$ we take $F=0$. On a connected component of $\Omega_0$ on which the restriction of $p$ is identically $-\infty$, finiteness of $I$ forces $f=0$, and this component contributes zero to $I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Normalization.} The substitution $z_n=4R\zeta$ maps $\mathbb C^{n-1}\times\Delta_{1/4}$ onto $\mathbb C^{n-1}\times\Delta_R$, preserves the hyperplane and the integral $I$, and multiplies Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb C^n$ by $16R^2$. Hence it suffices to show the following: if $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^{n-1}\times\Delta_{1/4}$, then $f$ has an extension $F\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ with $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0I$ for an absolute constant $C_0$; then $C=16C_0$ works. From now on we write the points of $\Omega$ as $(z',\zeta)$ with $z'\in\mathbb C^{n-1}$ and $|\zeta|<1/4$; for $n=1$ the variable $z'$ is absent. We may assume $\Omega_0\ne\varnothing$.

\emph{Step 1: smooth weights on a relatively compact set.} Let $D\Subset\Omega$ be a pseudoconvex open set, let $U\subset\Omega$ be an open neighborhood of $\overline D$, and let $\varphi$ be a smooth plurisubharmonic function on $U$. We show that there are an open set $P\Subset\Omega_0$ with $\overline P\times\{0\}\subset U$ and a function $F\in\mathcal O(D)$ with $F(z',0)=f(z')$ whenever $(z',0)\in D$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ot-smooth-step}
 \int_D|F|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0\int_P|f(z')|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z'),
\end{equation}
where $C_0$ is an absolute constant. The set $K_0=\{z':(z',0)\in\overline D\}$ is a compact subset of the open set $U_0=\{z':(z',0)\in U\}\subset\Omega_0$; choose an open set $P$ with $K_0\subset P\Subset U_0$. There is $\varepsilon_0\in(0,1/4)$ such that every $(z',\zeta)\in D$ with $|\zeta|\le\varepsilon_0$ satisfies $z'\in P$: otherwise there would be points $(z'_k,\zeta_k)\in D$ with $\zeta_k\to0$ and $z'_k\notin P$, and a subsequence would converge, $D$ being bounded, to a point $(z',0)\in\overline D$ with $z'\notin P$, which is impossible. Decreasing $\varepsilon_0$, we may also assume that $\overline P\times\overline\Delta_{\varepsilon_0}\subset U$ and, by the uniform continuity of $\varphi$ on this compact set, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ot-weight-comparison}
 \mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',\zeta)}\le2\mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',0)}\qquad(z'\in\overline P,\ |\zeta|\le\varepsilon_0).
\end{equation}

Fix a smooth function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$ with $0\le\chi\le1$, $\chi=1$ on $(-\infty,1/4]$ and $\chi=0$ on $[1,\infty)$, and put $c_\chi=\sup|\chi'|^2$. For $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ define $g_\varepsilon$ on $D$ by $g_\varepsilon(z',\zeta)=f(z')\chi(|\zeta|^2/\varepsilon^2)$ if $z'\in P$, and $g_\varepsilon=0$ otherwise. The open sets $D\cap(P\times\mathbb C)$ and $D\cap\{|\zeta|>\varepsilon\}$ cover $D$ by the choice of $\varepsilon_0$, and $g_\varepsilon$ is smooth on each of them (on the second it vanishes); hence $g_\varepsilon$ is smooth on $D$, and $g_\varepsilon(z',0)=f(z')$ for $(z',0)\in D$. Since $f$ is holomorphic,
\[
 \alpha_\varepsilon=\bar\partial g_\varepsilon=f(z')\chi'(|\zeta|^2/\varepsilon^2)
                    \frac{\zeta}{\varepsilon^2}\,\mathrm d\bar\zeta
\]
is a $\bar\partial$-closed smooth form supported in $\{\varepsilon/2\le|\zeta|\le\varepsilon\}$.

We solve $\bar\partial u=\alpha_\varepsilon$ on $D$ by \cref{lem:bounded-weight-twist}, with the plurisubharmonic function $\widetilde\varphi=\varphi+\log|\zeta|^2$ in place of $p$ and with $\psi=\psi_\varepsilon(\zeta)$ from \cref{lem:extension-log-log-weight}, a smooth bounded plurisubharmonic function on $D$. The complex Hessian matrix $A=H_\psi$ has the single nonzero entry $(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}$, so $|\bar\partial\psi|_{A^{-1}}^2$ is the function $h$ of \cref{lem:extension-log-log-weight}; thus $h<1$, and $h\le a_\varepsilon=2/(-\log(2\varepsilon^2))$ on $\{\alpha_\varepsilon\ne0\}\subset\{|\zeta|\le\varepsilon\}$. The form $\alpha_\varepsilon$ belongs to $L^2_{(0,1)}(D,\mathrm e^{-\widetilde\varphi})$, because its support avoids $\{\zeta=0\}$ and lies in $\overline P\times\overline\Delta_{\varepsilon_0}$, where $f$ and $\varphi$ are bounded. Its dual energy is $|f|^2|\chi'|^2|\zeta|^2\varepsilon^{-4}/(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}$, so the third estimate of \cref{lem:extension-log-log-weight} and $|\zeta|\le\varepsilon$ give, on its support,
\[
 |\alpha_\varepsilon|_{A^{-1}}^2\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon-\widetilde\varphi}
 =|f(z')|^2|\chi'|^2\varepsilon^{-4}\frac{\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}}{(\psi_\varepsilon)_{\zeta\bar\zeta}}\mathrm e^{-\varphi}
 \le\frac{25}4c_\chi\varepsilon^{-2}|f(z')|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',\zeta)}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:ot-weight-comparison} and Fubini's theorem, since every point of the support has $z'\in P$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 E=\int_D|\alpha_\varepsilon|_{H_\psi^{-1}}^2
             \mathrm e^{\psi-\widetilde\varphi}\,\mathrm dV
 &\le\frac{25}4c_\chi\varepsilon^{-2}\cdot2\pi\varepsilon^2\int_P|f(z')|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z')\\
 &=\frac{25\pi}2c_\chi I_P,
 \end{split}
\]
where $I_P$ denotes the integral over $P$ on the right of \eqref{eq:ot-smooth-step}. If $\varepsilon$ is so small that $a_\varepsilon\le1/4$, then $(1+\sqrt{a_\varepsilon})/(1-\sqrt{a_\varepsilon})\le3$, and \cref{lem:bounded-weight-twist} gives $u_\varepsilon$ with $\bar\partial u_\varepsilon=\alpha_\varepsilon$ and $\int_D(1-h)|u_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon-\widetilde\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le3E$. By the first estimate of \cref{lem:extension-log-log-weight}, $(1-h)\mathrm e^{\psi_\varepsilon}\ge1/(6\log^2(|\zeta|^2+\varepsilon^2))$, and $\mathrm e^{-\widetilde\varphi}=\mathrm e^{-\varphi}|\zeta|^{-2}$. Hence, with $C_1=225\pi c_\chi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-extension-correction}
 \int_D\frac{|u_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}}
 { |\zeta|^2\log^2(|\zeta|^2+\varepsilon^2)}\,\mathrm dV
       \le18E\le C_1\int_P|f(z')|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z').
\end{equation}

On $D\cap\{|\zeta|<\varepsilon/2\}$ we have $\bar\partial u_\varepsilon=0$, so $u_\varepsilon$ is holomorphic there. Suppose that $u_\varepsilon(z'_0,0)\ne0$ for some $(z'_0,0)\in D$. On a small polydisc around this point, $|u_\varepsilon|$ is bounded below by a positive constant, $\varphi$ is bounded above, and $\log^2(|\zeta|^2+\varepsilon^2)\le\log^2(\varepsilon^2)$; so the integrand in \eqref{eq:normal-extension-correction} is at least a positive constant times $|\zeta|^{-2}$, whose integral over a disc around $\zeta=0$ diverges. This contradicts \eqref{eq:normal-extension-correction}. Hence $u_\varepsilon=0$ on $D\cap\{\zeta=0\}$, and $F_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$ is holomorphic on $D$, because $\bar\partial F_\varepsilon=0$, with $F_\varepsilon(z',0)=f(z')$.

It remains to estimate $F_\varepsilon$. For $0<x=|\zeta|^2<1/16$ we have $x<x+\varepsilon^2<1/8$, so $x\log^2(x+\varepsilon^2)\le x\log^2x\le4\mathrm e^{-2}<1$. Thus \eqref{eq:normal-extension-correction} gives $\int_D|u_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le C_1I_P$. By \eqref{eq:ot-weight-comparison} and Fubini's theorem, $\int_D|g_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le2\pi\varepsilon^2I_P\le I_P$. Therefore $\int_D|F_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\,\mathrm dV\le2(1+C_1)I_P$, which is \eqref{eq:ot-smooth-step} with $C_0=2(1+C_1)$.

\emph{Step 2: the general case.} By \cref{cor:pseudoconvex-sublevels-products} there are pseudoconvex open sets $D_1\subset D_2\subset\cdots$ with $D_j\Subset\Omega$ and $\bigcup_jD_j=\Omega$. As in the proof of \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}, choose $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon_j<d(\overline D_j,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega)$; then $p_j=p*\rho_{\varepsilon_j}$ is a smooth plurisubharmonic function on the open neighborhood $U_j=\{z\in\Omega:d(z,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega)>\varepsilon_j\}$ of $\overline D_j$, and $p_j\downarrow p$ pointwise. Step 1, applied to $D_j$, $U_j$ and $\varphi=p_j$, gives $F_j\in\mathcal O(D_j)$ with $F_j(z',0)=f(z')$ for $(z',0)\in D_j$ and
\[
 \int_{D_j}|F_j|^2\mathrm e^{-p_j}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0\int_{P_j}|f(z')|^2\mathrm e^{-p_j(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z')\le C_0I,
\]
because $p_j\ge p$. (On a component of $\Omega_0$ on which $p(\cdot,0)\equiv-\infty$ we have $f=0$, and the last inequality holds with the convention $0\cdot\infty=0$.)

Let $K\subset\Omega$ be compact. Then $K\subset D_k$ for some $k$, and for $j\ge k$ we have $p_j\le p_k$ on $K$, so $\int_K|F_j|^2\,\mathrm dV\le\mathrm e^{\max_Kp_k}C_0I$. The functions $|F_j|^2$ are plurisubharmonic, hence subharmonic, so the mean value inequality over balls in \cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison} shows that $(F_j)$ is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $\Omega$. The one-variable Cauchy estimates on polydiscs then bound the first derivatives of the $F_j$ uniformly on compact sets, so the family is equicontinuous on compact sets. By \cref{prop:arzela-ascoli} and a diagonal argument, a subsequence converges locally uniformly on $\Omega$ to a continuous function $F$. Since $\bar\partial F_j=0$ and $F_j\to F$ in the sense of distributions, $\bar\partial F=0$, and $F$ is holomorphic by the discussion following \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}. Moreover $F(z',0)=\lim_jF_j(z',0)=f(z')$ for $z'\in\Omega_0$.

Finally, fix $k$ and a compact set $K\subset D_k$. For $j\ge k$ we have $p_j\le p_k$ on $K$, so locally uniform convergence gives
\[
 \int_K|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p_k}\,\mathrm dV=\lim_j\int_K|F_j|^2\mathrm e^{-p_k}\,\mathrm dV\le\liminf_j\int_{D_j}|F_j|^2\mathrm e^{-p_j}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0I.
\]
Since $K\subset D_k$ for all large $k$ and $\mathrm e^{-p_k}$ increases to $\mathrm e^{-p}$, the monotone convergence theorem gives $\int_K|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0I$, and exhausting $\Omega$ by compact sets gives $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le C_0I$.
\end{proof}

Applying the theorem once for each normal direction, we obtain extension from affine subspaces of any codimension, in particular from points.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ot-point-value-bound}
Let $C$ be the constant of \cref{thm:local-ot-extension}. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded pseudoconvex domain of diameter at most $L>0$, and let $p\in\PSH(\Omega)$. Then for every $a\in\Omega$ with $p(a)>-\infty$ there is $F\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 F(a)=1,\qquad\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV
                       \le C^n L^{2n}\mathrm e^{-p(a)}.
\]
More generally, let $V\subset\mathbb C^n$ be an affine complex subspace of codimension $r$, $0\le r\le n$, and let $f$ be holomorphic on $\Omega\cap V$ with finite slice norm $\int_{\Omega\cap V}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2r}$, computed with Lebesgue measure on $V$. Then there is $F\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ with $F|_{\Omega\cap V}=f$ and $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV\le C^rL^{2r}\int_{\Omega\cap V}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2r}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
We prove the second assertion; the first is the case $V=\{a\}$, $r=n$ and $f=1$, whose slice norm with respect to the counting measure $\mathrm dV_0$ is $\mathrm e^{-p(a)}$. If $r=0$ we take $F=f$, and if $\Omega\cap V=\varnothing$ we take $F=0$. Otherwise choose $b\in\Omega\cap V$ and unitary affine coordinates $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)$ on $\mathbb C^n$ centered at $b$ with $V=\{w_1=\cdots=w_r=0\}$, and put $\Omega_k=\Omega\cap\{w_1=\cdots=w_k=0\}$ for $0\le k\le r$, an open subset of a space isomorphic to $\mathbb C^{n-k}$. Unitary coordinates preserve Lebesgue measure. Each $\Omega_k$ is pseudoconvex: the restriction of a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function of $\Omega$ to the closed subset $\Omega_k$ of $\Omega$ is continuous and plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, and its sublevel sets are compact. Since $|z-b|<L$ for $z\in\Omega$, the coordinate $w_k$ takes its values in $\Delta_L$ on $\Omega_{k-1}$. By \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, the restriction of $p$ to each component of $\Omega_{k-1}$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$; on a component of the second kind, functions with finite weighted norm vanish, and we extend by zero.

Put $f_r=f$. For $k=r,r-1,\ldots,1$ we apply \cref{thm:local-ot-extension} to $\Omega_{k-1}$, with normal coordinate $w_k$ and slice $\Omega_k$, and obtain $f_{k-1}\in\mathcal O(\Omega_{k-1})$ extending $f_k$ with
\[
 \int_{\Omega_{k-1}}|f_{k-1}|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2k+2}\le CL^2\int_{\Omega_k}|f_k|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2k}.
\]
Multiplying these $r$ estimates, we see that $F=f_0$ has the required properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:integrable-germ-restriction}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold, let $Y\subset X$ be a complex submanifold, let $a\in Y$, and let $p$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $a$ in $X$. Let $f$ be a holomorphic germ on $Y$ at $a$ such that $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p|_Y}$ is integrable on some neighborhood of $a$ in $Y$. Then there is a holomorphic germ $F$ on $X$ at $a$ with $F|_Y=f$ such that $|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}$ is integrable on some neighborhood of $a$ in $X$. Here integrability refers to smooth positive volume forms on $Y$ and $X$; since any two such forms are locally comparable, the notion does not depend on their choice.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $k=\dim Y$ and $n=\dim X$ near $a$. Near $a$, the submanifold $Y$ is the zero set of $n-k$ holomorphic functions with linearly independent differentials at $a$; completing them by $k$ further coordinate functions, we obtain, by the holomorphic inverse function theorem, holomorphic coordinates $w=(w',w'')\in\mathbb C^k\times\mathbb C^{n-k}$ centered at $a$ in which $Y=\{w''=0\}$. In these coordinates, $p$ is plurisubharmonic near $0$ by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}. Choose a ball $B$ around $0$ so small that $p$ is plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, that $f$ is defined on $B\cap Y$, and that $\int_{B\cap Y}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2k}<\infty$. On a smaller compact neighborhood of $a$, the Jacobians of the coordinates and the densities of smooth positive volume forms are bounded above and below by positive constants, so these comparisons preserve local integrability. The ball $B$ is a bounded pseudoconvex domain, and $B\cap Y$ is an affine subspace section of $B$ of codimension $n-k$. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound} there is $F\in\mathcal O(B)$ with $F|_{B\cap Y}=f$ and $\int_B|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. If $p|_Y$ is identically $-\infty$ near $a$, then $f=0$ and $F=0$; if $k=0$, the integral on $Y$ is the weighted value at $a$, as in \cref{thm:local-ot-extension}.
\end{proof}
