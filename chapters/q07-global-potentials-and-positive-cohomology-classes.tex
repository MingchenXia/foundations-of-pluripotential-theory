\chapter{Global potentials and positive cohomology classes}
\label{chap:global-potentials}

Let $X$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold, and let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. By \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential}, every point of $X$ has a coordinate neighborhood on which $T=\ddc u$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic. On the overlap of two such neighborhoods the two local potentials differ by a pluriharmonic function, and in general no choice of the local potentials makes them agree. For instance, if $X$ has positive dimension and $T=\omega$ is a K\"ahler form, a global potential $u$ would be plurisubharmonic on $X$; it would attain its maximum, since it is upper semicontinuous and $X$ is compact, and the set where it does would be closed and also open, because $u$ is subharmonic on coordinate balls (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}) and the maximum principle (\cref{prop:subharmonic-maximum}) applies there. Thus $u$ would be constant and $\omega=\ddc u=0$. The obstruction is the cohomology class of $T$: by the $\ddc$-lemma (\cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}), if $\theta$ is a smooth closed form in the class of $T$, then $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ for a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$, unique up to an additive constant. We prove this by solving a scalar Laplace equation for the trace of $T$, developing the necessary Hodge theory from the beginning; the same argument shows that the real $(1,1)$-cohomology of $X$ is finite dimensional. Closed positive currents in the class of $\theta$ therefore correspond, up to additive constants, to the functions in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ (\cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}), and the rest of the book works with such potentials.

Once the additive constant is fixed by normalizing the supremum, the local compactness and integrability theorems for plurisubharmonic functions become uniform statements on $X$, and the upper envelope $V_\theta$ of the normalized potentials has minimal singularities. With these tools and the Demailly approximation of \cref{chap:q06} we study the positivity of a class (pseudo-effective, nef, big or K\"ahler) and the places where it fails. Siu's decomposition (\cref{thm:siu-divisorial-decomposition}) splits off the divisorial part of a current, the minimal multiplicities of a class and its non-nef locus locate the points at which the positive currents in the class are forced to be singular, and the non-K\"ahler locus does the same for K\"ahler currents. A mixed version of the scalar equation gives the mixed Hodge index theorem (\cref{thm:mixed-hodge-index}) and inequalities between intersection numbers.

The second half of the chapter concerns a holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $X$. If $h_0$ is a smooth metric on $L$ with curvature form $c_1(L,h_0)$, a singular metric $h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,c_1(L,h_0))$ is the global form of a plurisubharmonic weight. We prove the $L^2$ estimate for $\bar\partial$ on line bundles over K\"ahler manifolds and combine it with the local extension theorems of \cref{chap:q04} to construct global sections with a prescribed value at a point (\cref{thm:compact-point-extension}) or a prescribed restriction to a compact submanifold (\cref{thm:compact-submanifold-extension}), with constants that depend only on the geometry. A related gluing construction extends strictly positive potentials from a submanifold (\cref{thm:holder-positive-potential-extension}). In the last section we leave the K\"ahler setting and prove Josefson's theorem on a compact complex manifold: every pluripolar set lies in the pole set of a single quasi-plurisubharmonic function (\cref{thm:compact-complex-global-josefson}).

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-global-potentials}.

\section{The trace equation and global potentials}
\label{sec:global-trace-equation}
Throughout this chapter, except in \cref{sec:compact-complex-josefson}, $(X,\omega)$ is a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. (In dimension zero all the notions below are trivial: $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ and all positivity cones are $\{0\}$, both positivity loci are empty and normalized potentials vanish. On a disconnected compact K\"ahler manifold the results apply to each of its finitely many components, with normalizations imposed on each component.) Let $J$ be the complex structure of $X$. The K\"ahler form defines the Riemannian metric $g(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$ and the volume form $\mathrm dV=\omega^n/n!$. Forms carry the pointwise inner product induced by $g$, and the $L^2$ inner product $\langle u,v\rangle=\int_X\langle u,v\rangle\,\mathrm dV$. The Hodge star operator is the complex-linear bundle map characterized by $u\wedge*\overline v=\langle u,v\rangle\,\mathrm dV$ for forms $u,v$ of the same degree. Explicitly, if $e_1,\ldots,e_{2n}$ is a positively oriented orthonormal basis of the real cotangent space at a point and $I'$ denotes the increasing multi-index complementary to $I$, then $*e_I=\varepsilon_Ie_{I'}$, where $e_I\wedge e_{I'}=\varepsilon_Ie_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{2n}$ and $\varepsilon_I=\pm1$; indeed $e_I\wedge\varepsilon_Je_{J'}$ is $\delta_{IJ}$ times the volume form. Hence $*$ is a real isometry, and since $e_{I'}\wedge e_I=(-1)^{k(2n-k)}e_I\wedge e_{I'}$ for $|I|=k$, we have $**=(-1)^{k(2n-k)}=(-1)^k$ on $k$-forms, the real dimension $2n$ being even. Now let $u$ be a $(k-1)$-form and $v$ a $k$-form, one of them with compact support. The form $\mathrm d*\overline v$ has degree $2n-k+1$, on which $**=(-1)^{k-1}$, so that $u\wedge\mathrm d*\overline v=(-1)^{k-1}u\wedge*\overline{(*\mathrm d*v)}=(-1)^{k-1}\langle u,*\mathrm d*v\rangle\,\mathrm dV$. Integrating $\mathrm d(u\wedge*\overline v)=\mathrm du\wedge*\overline v+(-1)^{k-1}u\wedge\mathrm d*\overline v$ by Stokes' formula (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}), we obtain $\int_X\langle\mathrm du,v\rangle\,\mathrm dV=-\int_X\langle u,*\mathrm d*v\rangle\,\mathrm dV$. Thus the formal adjoint of $\mathrm d$ is $\mathrm d^*=-*\mathrm d*$.

A \emph{distributional $k$-form} is a current of degree $k$ (\cref{sec:positive-currents}); it is paired with smooth $k$-forms $\phi$ by $\langle u,\phi\rangle=u(*\overline\phi)$, which equals $\int_X\langle u,\phi\rangle\,\mathrm dV$ when $u$ is a locally integrable form. A linear differential operator $P$ on forms with smooth coefficients has a formal adjoint $P^*$, a differential operator of the same kind characterized by $\langle P\alpha,\phi\rangle=\langle\alpha,P^*\phi\rangle$ for smooth forms $\alpha,\phi$; in a chart it is obtained by integrating by parts, and the local expressions agree on overlaps by this characterization. We let $P$ act on distributional forms by $\langle Pu,\phi\rangle=\langle u,P^*\phi\rangle$. For $P=\mathrm d$ this is the exterior derivative of currents, by the identity just proved, and for $*$, for the exterior product with a smooth form and for its pointwise adjoint it is the action on the distribution coefficients in local frames. Since $(PQ)^*=Q^*P^*$, and since an operator is determined by its formal adjoint, every identity between such operators that holds on smooth forms also holds on distributional forms. In particular, a scalar distribution $f$ is paired with smooth functions by $\langle f,\chi\rangle=f(\overline\chi\,\mathrm dV)$, so that a locally integrable $f$ gives $\langle f,\chi\rangle=\int_Xf\overline\chi\,\mathrm dV$; we say that $f$ has \emph{mean zero} if $\langle f,1\rangle=0$.

On functions we use the Laplace--Beltrami operator\index{Laplacian!Laplace--Beltrami}\index{Laplacian!metric} $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$. For the Euclidean metric on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ we have $L=-\sum_j\partial^2/\partial x_j^2$, so \emph{$L$ is the negative of the Laplacian $\Delta$ of Part~I}; we use this sign convention throughout the chapter because it makes $L$ a nonnegative operator: $\int_XfLf\,\mathrm dV=\int_X|\mathrm df|^2\,\mathrm dV$ for smooth real $f$. On forms we use the Hodge Laplacian\index{Laplacian!Hodge} $\mathcal L=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*+\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$, and a form or current $u$ is \emph{harmonic}\index{harmonic form} if $\mathcal Lu=0$. For smooth $u$, $\langle\mathcal Lu,u\rangle=\|\mathrm du\|^2+\|\mathrm d^*u\|^2$, so $u$ is harmonic if and only if $\mathrm du=0$ and $\mathrm d^*u=0$. On functions $\mathcal L=L$, so for the Euclidean metric a harmonic function in this sense is a harmonic function in the sense of \cref{sec:harmonic-mean-values}. For a general K\"ahler metric, however, $L$ has variable coefficients, and on the compact connected manifold $X$ the only solutions of $Lf=0$ are the constants (\cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}); the harmonic objects of interest here are forms.

We use the following Sobolev spaces\index{Sobolev space!on a compact manifold}. Choose a finite cover of $X$ by coordinate charts, frames of the bundle of differential forms over them, and smooth functions $\chi_k\geq0$ with compact supports in the charts and $\sum_k\chi_k=1$. Using the frames, $\chi_ku$ is a vector-valued function on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ for every form $u$. For an integer $r\geq0$, let $H^r$ be the completion of the space of smooth forms for the norm whose square is
\[
 \sum_k\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi|^2)^r
       |\widehat{\chi_k u}(\xi)|^2\,\mathrm d\xi,
\]
and let $H^{-r}$ be the space of distributional forms $u$ for which $\phi\mapsto\langle u,\phi\rangle$ is bounded on smooth forms for the norm of $H^r$, with the norm of this functional. We show in Step 1 of the next proof that other choices of the charts, frames and cutoffs give equivalent norms, and that the spaces $H^r$, $r\in\mathbb Z$, do not depend on the K\"ahler form $\omega$. The same notation is used for functions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}
On $X$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every distributional form $u$ with $\mathcal Lu=0$ is smooth, and the space of harmonic forms is finite dimensional.
\item For every scalar distribution $a$ of mean zero there is a unique distribution $f$ of mean zero with $Lf=a$. If $a$ is smooth, then $f$ is smooth, and if $a$ is real, then $f$ is real.
\item For every constant $c>0$, the operator $\mathcal L+c$ is a bijection of the space of distributional forms onto itself, and it maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^r$ for every integer $r$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: Sobolev spaces.} For a test function $v$ on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ we have $\widehat{\partial_jv}(\xi)=2\pi\mathrm i\xi_j\widehat v(\xi)$, and $(1+|\xi|^2)^r=\sum_{|\beta|\leq r}c_\beta|\xi^\beta|^2$ with positive multinomial coefficients $c_\beta$. Hence Plancherel's theorem (\cref{thm:plancherel}) shows that on smooth forms the norm of $H^r$, $r\geq0$, is equivalent to the norm whose square is the sum over $k$ and over $|\alpha|\leq r$ of $\|\partial^\alpha(\chi_ku)\|_{L^2}^2$. Let $\chi'_l$ be cutoffs subordinate to other charts, with other frames. Then $\chi'_lu=\sum_k\chi'_l\chi_ku$, and each term is supported in a compact subset of the intersection of two charts, on which the coordinate change and the change of frames have bounded derivatives of all orders and the Jacobian determinant is bounded below. By the chain rule, the Leibniz rule and the change of variables formula, the $L^2$ norms of the derivatives of order at most $r$ of $\chi'_l\chi_ku$, computed in the new chart and frame, are bounded by a constant times those of $\chi_ku$ in the old ones. Hence other choices of these data give equivalent norms. In the same way, multiplication by a smooth function, or by a smooth endomorphism of the bundle of forms, is bounded on $H^r$, and by duality on $H^{-r}$. For $r\geq0$, the pointwise bound $|u|\leq\sum_k|\chi_ku|$ shows that the norm of $H^r$ dominates a multiple of the $L^2$ norm. Moreover, if smooth forms $u_j$ form a Cauchy sequence in $H^r$ and $u_j\to0$ in $L^2$, then for each $k$ the functions $(1+|\xi|^2)^{r/2}\widehat{\chi_ku_j}$ converge in $L^2$, and their limit vanishes, because $\widehat{\chi_ku_j}\to0$ in $L^2$ and a subsequence converges almost everywhere; hence $u_j\to0$ in $H^r$. Therefore $H^r$ is a subspace of the space of $L^2$ forms, with $H^0$ the whole space. Since $\|\phi\|_{H^r}\leq C\|\phi\|_{C^r}$, where $\|\phi\|_{C^r}$ is the maximum over $k$ of the suprema on $\Supp\chi_k$ of the derivatives of order at most $r$ of the coefficients of $\phi$ in the $k$th chart and frame, every bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^r$ is given on smooth forms by a distributional form, which determines it because smooth forms are dense in $H^r$; thus $H^{-r}$ is the dual space of $H^r$, and $H^{-r}\supset H^{-r'}$ for $r\leq r'$.

The definition of $H^r$ for $r\geq0$ does not involve $\omega$. If the $L^2$ pairing is taken with respect to another K\"ahler form, the new pairing is $\langle u,Av\rangle$ for a smooth endomorphism $A$ of the bundle of forms that is positive definite at every point, so the identification of $H^{-r}$ with a space of distributional forms changes only by the invertible map $A$, which preserves $H^{-r}$. Hence no $H^r$ depends on $\omega$. If $r>l+n$, then $H^r\subset C^l$, and $\|u\|_{C^l}\leq C\|u\|_{H^r}$. Indeed, since $u=\sum_k\chi_ku$ and derivatives in two charts are related by the chain rule on compact overlaps, it suffices to bound the derivatives of the $\chi_ku$. For a smooth form $u$ and $|\alpha|\leq l$, Fourier inversion and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give
\[
 \sup|\partial^\alpha(\chi_ku)|
 \leq\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}|2\pi\xi|^{|\alpha|}|\widehat{\chi_ku}(\xi)|\,\mathrm d\xi
 \leq C\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi|^2)^{l-r}\,\mathrm d\xi\Bigr)^{1/2}\|u\|_{H^r},
\]
and the last integral is finite because $2r-2l>2n$; for a general $u\in H^r$, a sequence of smooth forms converging to $u$ in $H^r$ converges in $C^l$, and the limit agrees with $u$ almost everywhere. Finally, every distributional form $u$ on $X$ has finite order. Indeed, $\langle u,\phi\rangle=\sum_ku(\chi_k*\overline\phi)$, and the definition of currents (\cref{sec:positive-currents}), applied on the compact sets $\Supp\chi_k$, gives $|\langle u,\phi\rangle|\leq C\|\phi\|_{C^l}$ for some $l$ and all smooth $\phi$. By the embedding just proved, $u$ belongs to $H^{-r}$ for every $r>l+n$.

\emph{Step 2: the inclusion $H^1\subset L^2$ is compact.} Let $(u_j)$ be bounded in $H^1$, fix $k$, and put $v_j=\chi_ku_j$, which vanishes outside a fixed coordinate box $Q$. For $R>0$, the part of $\widehat{v_j}$ on $|\xi|>R$ has $L^2$ norm at most $R^{-1}\|v_j\|_{H^1}\leq C/R$. The inverse Fourier transforms $w_{j,R}$ of the parts on $|\xi|\leq R$ are uniformly bounded with uniformly bounded derivatives, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on the ball $|\xi|\leq R$, so by the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem a subsequence converges uniformly on $\overline Q$. Since $v_j$ vanishes outside $Q$,
\[
 \|v_j-v_i\|_{L^2}\leq\|w_{j,R}-w_{i,R}\|_{L^2(Q)}+2CR^{-1}.
\]
A diagonal subsequence over $R=1,2,\ldots$ is therefore Cauchy in $L^2$. Repeating this for the finitely many charts and using $u_j=\sum_k\chi_ku_j$ proves compactness.

\emph{Step 3: G\aa rding's inequality.}\index{Garding@G\aa rding's inequality} We first compute $\mathcal L$ in a chart with real coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_{2n}$ and the coordinate frame $\mathrm dx_I$ of the forms. Let $(g^{ij})$ be the inverse of the matrix $(g(\partial_i,\partial_j))$, so that $g^{ij}$ is the inner product of $\mathrm dx_i$ and $\mathrm dx_j$, write the pointwise inner product of forms as $\sum_{I,J}G_{IJ}u_I\overline{v_J}$ and the volume form as $\rho\,\mathrm dx$, and let $\epsilon_i$ be the exterior multiplication by $\mathrm dx_i$ and $\epsilon_i^\dagger$ its pointwise adjoint. If $e_1,\ldots,e_{2n}$ is an orthonormal basis of the cotangent space at a point, then exterior multiplication by $e_a$ and its adjoint $\epsilon(e_a)^\dagger$, which removes the factor $e_a$ from the basis forms $e_J$ containing it and kills the others, satisfy $\epsilon(e_a)\epsilon(e_b)^\dagger+\epsilon(e_b)^\dagger\epsilon(e_a)=\delta_{ab}$, as one checks on the basis forms $e_J$. Writing $\mathrm dx_i=\sum_aM_{ia}e_a$, so that $\sum_aM_{ia}M_{ja}=g^{ij}$, we obtain by bilinearity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exterior-contraction-anticommutator}
 \epsilon_i\epsilon_j^\dagger+\epsilon_j^\dagger\epsilon_i=g^{ij}.
\end{equation}
In the coordinate frame $\mathrm du=\sum_i\epsilon_i\partial_iu$, where $\partial_i$ acts on the coefficients. Integrating by parts in $\int\sum_{I,J}G_{IJ}(\epsilon_i\partial_iu)_I\overline{v_J}\,\rho\,\mathrm dx$, we find $\mathrm d^*v=-\sum_i\epsilon_i^\dagger\partial_iv+B_0v$, where $B_0$ is a matrix whose entries are linear combinations, with smooth coefficients, of first derivatives of the $G_{IJ}$ and of $\rho$. Since the $\epsilon_i$ have constant coefficients in the coordinate frame, \eqref{eq:exterior-contraction-anticommutator} gives
\[
 \mathcal L=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*+\mathrm d^*\mathrm d=-\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+B_1
\]
on each coefficient, where $B_1$ is a differential operator of order at most one with smooth coefficients. On functions $\mathrm d^*$ vanishes, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace-beltrami-local}
 Lf=\mathrm d^*\mathrm df=-\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\partial_i\partial_jf+B_0\Bigl(\sum_i\partial_if\,\mathrm dx_i\Bigr);
\end{equation}
at a point where the first derivatives of the metric coefficients vanish, $Lf=-\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\partial_i\partial_jf$ there.

Now let $u$ be smooth with compact support in the chart. Since $\langle\mathcal Lu,u\rangle=\|\mathrm du\|^2+\|\mathrm d^*u\|^2$, integrating the second-order term by parts once gives
\[
 \|\mathrm du\|^2+\|\mathrm d^*u\|^2
 =\int\sum_{i,j,I,J}g^{ij}G_{IJ}\,\partial_ju_I\,\overline{\partial_iu_J}\,\rho\,\mathrm dx+E(u),
\]
where $|E(u)|\leq C\|u\|_{L^2}\|u\|_{H^1}$, with $C$ depending on a compact set containing the support of $u$. For $\zeta=(\zeta_{jI})\in\mathbb C^{2n\times N}$, write $\zeta_j=(\zeta_{jI})_I$ and $G(\zeta_j,\zeta_i)=\sum_{I,J}G_{IJ}\zeta_{jI}\overline{\zeta_{iJ}}$. Diagonalizing the positive definite real symmetric matrix $(g^{ij})$ by an orthogonal matrix $O$, we get $\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}G(\zeta_j,\zeta_i)=\sum_ad_aG(\eta_a,\eta_a)$ with eigenvalues $d_a>0$ and $\eta_a=\sum_jO_{aj}\zeta_j$; hence this Hermitian form is positive definite, uniformly on compact subsets of the chart, and the integral is at least $\lambda\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2$ for some $\lambda>0$. Applying this to the $\chi_ku$, whose derivatives differ from $\chi_k\,\partial u$ by terms bounded by $C|u|$, summing over $k$, using $\sum_k\chi_k^2\leq1$ and absorbing the error terms by $ab\leq\varepsilon a^2+b^2/(4\varepsilon)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-garding}
 \|u\|_{H^1}^2\leq C\bigl(\|\mathrm du\|_{L^2}^2+
       \|\mathrm d^*u\|_{L^2}^2+\|u\|_{L^2}^2\bigr)
\end{equation}
for smooth forms $u$ on $X$, and by continuity for all $u\in H^1$.

Fix $c>0$ and put $P=\mathcal L+c$, with the quadratic form $Q(v,w)=\langle\mathrm dv,\mathrm dw\rangle+\langle\mathrm d^*v,\mathrm d^*w\rangle+c\langle v,w\rangle$. Since $\mathrm d$ and $\mathrm d^*$ are first-order operators with smooth coefficients, $Q$ extends continuously to $H^1$, and by \eqref{eq:compact-garding} the norm $Q(u,u)^{1/2}$ is equivalent to the norm of $H^1$. For $b\in H^{-1}$, the map $w\mapsto\langle b,w\rangle$ is a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^1$, so the Riesz representation theorem (\cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}) gives a unique $v\in H^1$ with $Q(v,w)=\langle b,w\rangle$ for all $w\in H^1$, and $\|v\|_{H^1}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{-1}}$. Since $Q(v,\phi)=\langle v,P\phi\rangle$ for smooth $\phi$, first for smooth $v$ and then by continuity, this says that $Pv=b$ as distributional forms. We write $v=P^{-1}b$.

\emph{Step 4: interior regularity.} We first prove a local regularity statement for second-order operators in divergence form; it applies to $P$ and to the scalar operators used later. Let $U\subset\mathbb R^{2n}$ be a bounded open set, let $N\geq1$, and for an integer $k\geq0$ let $H^k(U)$ be the space of $\mathbb C^N$-valued functions on $U$ whose distributional derivatives of order at most $k$ are square integrable on $U$. For $v\in H^1(U)$ and $\mathbb C^N$-valued $w\in C^\infty_c(U)$ consider
\[
 \mathcal A(v,w)=\int_U\Bigl(\sum a^{ij}_{IJ}\,\partial_jv_I\,\overline{\partial_iw_J}
   +\sum b^j_{IJ}\,\partial_jv_I\,\overline{w_J}+\sum c_{IJ}\,v_I\,\overline{w_J}\Bigr)\mathrm dx,
\]
where the sums run over $1\leq i,j\leq2n$ and $1\leq I,J\leq N$, the coefficients are smooth on a neighborhood of $\overline U$, and the principal coefficients satisfy, for some $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \sum a^{ij}_{IJ}(x)\,\zeta_{jI}\,\overline{\zeta_{iJ}}\geq\lambda|\zeta|^2
 \qquad\text{for all }x\in U\text{ and }\zeta\in\mathbb C^{2n\times N}.
\]
(A term of the form $\int e^i_{IJ}v_I\overline{\partial_iw_J}$ can be integrated by parts into terms of the last two kinds, so it is also allowed.) We claim: \emph{if $k\geq0$, $F\in H^k(U)$ and $\mathcal A(v,w)=\int_U\sum_JF_J\overline{w_J}\,\mathrm dx$ for all $w\in C^\infty_c(U)$, then $\chi v\in H^{k+2}$ for every $\chi\in C^\infty_c(U)$, and $\|\chi v\|_{H^{k+2}}\leq C(\|F\|_{H^k(U)}+\|v\|_{H^1(U)})$, where $C$ depends only on $\chi$, $k$, $\lambda$ and bounds for the coefficients and their derivatives.}

For $k=0$ we use difference quotients. Let $0\leq\chi\leq1$, let $h_0$ be half the distance from $\Supp\chi$ to $\partial U$, and for a coordinate direction $e_l$ and $0<|h|<h_0$ put $\delta_hv(x)=(v(x+he_l)-v(x))/h$. The function $w=-\delta_{-h}(\chi^2\delta_hv)$ lies in $H^1$ and has compact support in $U$. Its mollifications are test functions converging to $w$ in $H^1$ (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}), and both sides of the equation are continuous in $w$ for the norm of $H^1$, so the equation also holds for $w$. Discrete integration by parts, $\int f\,\overline{\delta_{-h}g}=-\int(\delta_hf)\,\overline g$, turns the principal term into $\int\sum\delta_h(a^{ij}_{IJ}\partial_jv_I)\,\overline{\partial_i(\chi^2\delta_hv_J)}$. Here $\delta_h(a\,\partial_jv)=a(\cdot+he_l)\,\partial_j\delta_hv+(\delta_ha)\,\partial_jv$ and $\partial_i(\chi^2\delta_hv)=\chi^2\partial_i\delta_hv+2\chi\,\partial_i\chi\,\delta_hv$. The ellipticity condition at $x+he_l$ bounds the main term $\int\sum a^{ij}_{IJ}(x+he_l)\chi^2\partial_j\delta_hv_I\overline{\partial_i\delta_hv_J}$ from below by $\lambda\|\chi\nabla\delta_hv\|_{L^2}^2$. The remaining terms, the lower-order terms and the right-hand side are estimated with $|\delta_ha|\leq C$, with $\|\delta_hv\|_{L^2(\Supp\chi)}\leq\|\partial_lv\|_{L^2(U)}$, and with $\|w\|_{L^2}\leq\|\partial_l(\chi^2\delta_hv)\|_{L^2}\leq\|\chi\nabla\delta_hv\|_{L^2}+C\|v\|_{H^1(U)}$. (For smooth $f$ we have $\delta_hf(x)=\int_0^1\partial_lf(x+the_l)\,\mathrm dt$, so Minkowski's integral inequality bounds the $L^2$ norm of a difference quotient by that of $\partial_lf$ on a slightly larger set; for $f\in H^1$ this follows by mollification.) This gives
\[
 \lambda\|\chi\nabla\delta_hv\|_{L^2}^2
 \leq C\bigl(\|F\|_{L^2(U)}+\|v\|_{H^1(U)}\bigr)
             \bigl(\|\chi\nabla\delta_hv\|_{L^2}+\|v\|_{H^1(U)}\bigr),
\]
and hence $\|\chi\nabla\delta_hv\|_{L^2}\leq C(\|F\|_{L^2(U)}+\|v\|_{H^1(U)})$, independently of $h$. If $\phi\in C^\infty_c$ is supported in the interior of $\{\chi=1\}$, then $-\int\partial_jv\,\partial_l\phi=\lim_{h\to0}\int(\delta_h\partial_jv)\,\phi$ is bounded by this constant times $\|\phi\|_{L^2}$. By the Riesz representation theorem in $L^2$ (\cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}), applied to the extension of $\phi\mapsto-\int\partial_jv\,\partial_l\phi$ to $L^2$, the distributional derivatives $\partial_l\partial_jv$ are square integrable there, with the same bound. Since $\chi$ may be chosen equal to one near the support of any given cutoff, this proves the claim for $k=0$.

Suppose the claim holds for some $k\geq0$, let $F\in H^{k+1}(U)$, and fix $\chi$ and an open set $U'\Subset U$ containing $\Supp\chi$. By the claim for $k$, $v\in H^{k+2}(U')$, with norm at most $C(\|F\|_{H^k(U)}+\|v\|_{H^1(U)})$. Fix $m$ and test the equation with $-\partial_mw$, $w\in C^\infty_c(U')$. Integrating by parts in $x_m$, we find that $u=\partial_mv\in H^1(U')$ satisfies $\mathcal A(u,w)=\int\sum_JF'_J\overline{w_J}\,\mathrm dx$ for all $w\in C^\infty_c(U')$, where, with the indices $I,J$ suppressed,
\[
 F'=\partial_mF+\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\bigl((\partial_ma^{ij})\partial_jv\bigr)-\sum_j(\partial_mb^j)\partial_jv-(\partial_mc)v.
\]
Since $v\in H^{k+2}(U')$, we have $F'\in H^k(U')$ with $\|F'\|_{H^k(U')}\leq C(\|F\|_{H^{k+1}(U)}+\|v\|_{H^{k+2}(U')})$. The claim for $k$ on $U'$ gives $\chi\,\partial_mv\in H^{k+2}$ for every $m$, hence $\chi v\in H^{k+3}$ with the asserted bound. This proves the claim.

We apply it to $P$. In a chart, with the coordinate frame $\mathrm dx_I$, the inner product $\sum G_{IJ}u_I\overline{v_J}$ and the volume $\rho\,\mathrm dx$, one integration by parts in the expression of $\mathcal L$ from Step 3 shows that $\langle Pv,w\rangle=\mathcal A(v,w)$ for smooth $v$ and $w$, the latter with compact support in the chart, with principal coefficients $a^{ij}_{IJ}=g^{ij}G_{IJ}\rho$ and smooth lower-order coefficients; these principal coefficients satisfy the ellipticity condition on compact subsets of the chart, as shown in Step 3. Both sides are continuous in $v\in H^1$, so the equation $Q(v,w)=\langle b,w\rangle$ of Step 3 reads $\mathcal A(v,w)=\int\sum_JF_J\overline{w_J}\,\mathrm dx$ with $F_J=\rho\sum_IG_{IJ}b_I$. Let $b\in H^r$, $r\geq0$, and $v=P^{-1}b$. Applying the claim with $\chi=\chi_k$ on relatively compact open subsets of the finitely many charts, we obtain $\|v\|_{H^{r+2}}\leq C(\|b\|_{H^r}+\|v\|_{H^1})$, and since $\|v\|_{H^1}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C\|b\|_{L^2}$,
\[
 \|P^{-1}b\|_{H^{r+2}}\leq C_r\|b\|_{H^r},\qquad r\geq0.
\]
In particular, if $b$ is smooth, then $P^{-1}b$ lies in every $H^r$ and is therefore smooth by Step 1, and $PP^{-1}b=b$; and if $u$ is smooth, then $P^{-1}Pu=u$, because $Q(u,w)=\langle Pu,w\rangle$ for $w\in H^1$ and the solution in Step 3 is unique. For smooth $b$ and $\phi$, with $\psi=P^{-1}\phi$, we get $\langle P^{-1}b,\phi\rangle=\langle P^{-1}b,P\psi\rangle=Q(P^{-1}b,\psi)=\langle b,P^{-1}\phi\rangle$. We therefore extend $P^{-1}$ to all distributional forms by $\langle P^{-1}u,\phi\rangle=\langle u,P^{-1}\phi\rangle$ for smooth $\phi$. If $u\in H^{-s}$ with $s\geq2$, then $|\langle u,P^{-1}\phi\rangle|\leq\|u\|_{H^{-s}}\|P^{-1}\phi\|_{H^s}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{-s}}\|\phi\|_{H^{s-2}}$, so $P^{-1}u\in H^{-s+2}$ and $P^{-1}$ is bounded from $H^r$ to $H^{r+2}$ for $r\leq-2$; for $u\in H^{-1}$ the new definition agrees with that of Step 3, by the same computation with $b=u$. Since $P$ is formally self-adjoint, $\langle PP^{-1}u,\phi\rangle=\langle u,P^{-1}P\phi\rangle=\langle u,\phi\rangle$ and $\langle P^{-1}Pu,\phi\rangle=\langle u,PP^{-1}\phi\rangle=\langle u,\phi\rangle$ for every distributional form $u$, so $P$ is a bijection of the space of distributional forms onto itself with inverse $P^{-1}$. Conversely, $P$ is bounded from $H^{r+2}$ to $H^r$ for every integer $r$: for $r\geq0$ because it is a differential operator of order two with smooth coefficients, for $r=-1$ because $|\langle Pv,\phi\rangle|=|Q(v,\phi)|\leq C\|v\|_{H^1}\|\phi\|_{H^1}$, and for $r\leq-2$ by transposition, since $|\langle Pu,\phi\rangle|=|\langle u,P\phi\rangle|\leq C\|u\|_{H^{r+2}}\|\phi\|_{H^{-r}}$. Hence $P$ maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^r$. As $c>0$ was arbitrary, this proves (3).

\emph{Step 5: harmonic forms.} If $\mathcal Lu=0$ distributionally, then $Pu=cu$, so $u=cP^{-1}u$. By Step 1, $u\in H^{-r}$ for some $r$; iterating this identity raises the Sobolev order by two at each step, so $u$ lies in every $H^r$ and is smooth by the embedding of Step 1. For smooth harmonic $u$, \eqref{eq:compact-garding} gives $\|u\|_{H^1}\leq C\|u\|_{L^2}$. An $L^2$ limit of harmonic forms is distributionally harmonic, hence smooth and harmonic, so the closed unit ball of the space of harmonic forms in $L^2$ is bounded in $H^1$ and therefore compact by Step 2. A normed space with compact closed unit ball is finite dimensional: an infinite orthonormal sequence would have no convergent subsequence. This proves (1).

\emph{Step 6: the scalar equation.} For scalar $v\in H^1$ of mean zero we have $\|v\|_{L^2}\leq C\|\mathrm dv\|_{L^2}$. Otherwise there are $v_j$ of mean zero with $\|v_j\|_{L^2}=1$ and $\mathrm dv_j\to0$ in $L^2$. Since $\mathrm d^*$ vanishes on functions, \eqref{eq:compact-garding} shows that $(v_j)$ is bounded in $H^1$, so by Step 2 a subsequence converges in $L^2$ to some $v$ of norm one and mean zero, and $\mathrm dv=\lim_j\mathrm dv_j=0$ in the sense of distributions. In every chart $v$ is locally constant by \cref{cor:zero-gradient-constant}, so $v$ is constant on the connected manifold $X$, which contradicts $\|v\|_{L^2}=1$ and mean zero. Consequently $\|\mathrm dv\|_{L^2}$ is an equivalent Hilbert norm on the closed subspace of $H^1$ of functions of mean zero. Let $a$ be smooth of mean zero. The Riesz representation theorem (\cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}) gives $f$ in this subspace with $\langle\mathrm df,\mathrm dw\rangle=\langle a,w\rangle$ for all $w$ of mean zero, hence for all $w\in H^1$, since both sides vanish for constant $w$. Testing with $w=f$ gives $\|\mathrm df\|^2=\langle a,f\rangle\leq\|a\|\,\|f\|\leq C\|a\|\,\|\mathrm df\|$, hence $\|f\|_{H^1}\leq C\|a\|_{L^2}$. With $c=1$ the function $f$ satisfies $Q(f,w)=\langle a+f,w\rangle$ for all $w\in H^1$, so $f=P^{-1}(a+f)$ by the uniqueness in Step 3; if $f\in H^r$, $r\geq0$, then Step 4 gives $f\in H^{r+2}$ with $\|f\|_{H^{r+2}}\leq C(\|a\|_{H^r}+\|f\|_{H^r})$. By induction $f$ lies in every $H^r$, so it is smooth, and $\|f\|_{H^{r+2}}\leq C_r\|a\|_{H^r}$ for every $r\geq0$.

Let $\Pi a=\langle a,1\rangle/\int_X\mathrm dV$ be the average of $a$, and let $G$ be the operator $a\mapsto f$ applied to $a-\Pi a$, for smooth $a$. Then $\|Ga\|_{H^{r+2}}\leq C_r\|a\|_{H^r}$ for $r\geq0$, and $LG=1-\Pi$ by construction. If $a$ is smooth, then $La$ has mean zero, because $\langle La,1\rangle=\langle a,L1\rangle=0$, and both $GLa$ and $a-\Pi a$ are smooth solutions $f$ of mean zero of $Lf=La$. The difference $f_0$ of two such solutions satisfies $\int_X|\mathrm df_0|^2\,\mathrm dV=\langle Lf_0,f_0\rangle=0$, so it is constant, and it vanishes since it has mean zero; hence $GL=1-\Pi$. For smooth $a$ and $b$, since $Ga$ has mean zero and $L$ is formally self-adjoint,
\[
 \langle Ga,b\rangle=\langle Ga,LGb+\Pi b\rangle=\langle LGa,Gb\rangle=\langle a-\Pi a,Gb\rangle=\langle a,Gb\rangle,
\]
so $G$ is formally self-adjoint, and so is $\Pi$. Extend $G$ to distributions by transposition, $\langle Ga,\chi\rangle=\langle a,G\chi\rangle$. This defines a distribution: if $a\in H^{-s}$ with $s\geq2$, then $|\langle a,G\chi\rangle|\leq C\|a\|_{H^{-s}}\|\chi\|_{H^{s-2}}$. The two identities $LG=GL=1-\Pi$ remain valid, by transposition of the identities $GL=LG=1-\Pi$ for smooth functions. Hence $f=Ga$ solves $Lf=a$ for every distribution $a$ of mean zero, and $\langle Ga,1\rangle=\langle a,G1\rangle=0$. If $Lf=0$ for a distribution $f$, then $\mathcal Lf=0$, so $f$ is smooth by Step 5, and $\int_X|\mathrm df|^2\,\mathrm dV=\langle Lf,f\rangle=0$ shows that $f$ is constant; this proves uniqueness. If $a$ is real, then $\overline f$ is another solution of mean zero, since $L$ has real coefficients, so $f$ is real.
\end{proof}

The next lemma solves a scalar equation in which the Laplacian is replaced by a mixed trace with respect to several K\"ahler forms. We use the real operator $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ of \eqref{eq:dc-operator}, for which $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\ddc$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mixed-scalar-poisson}
Let $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_{n-1}$ be K\"ahler forms on $X$ and put $A=\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1}$, with $A=1$ when $n=1$. For every smooth real $(n,n)$-form $\eta$ with $\int_X\eta=0$, there is a unique smooth real function $f$ of mean zero such that
\[
 \ddc f\wedge A=\eta.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For smooth real functions $f,g$ put $B(f,g)=\int_X\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}g\wedge A$. Only the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm df\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g$ contributes, and this part equals $\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}(\partial f\wedge\bar\partial g+\partial g\wedge\bar\partial f)$, which is symmetric in $f$ and $g$; in particular $\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}f$ has $(1,1)$-part $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial f\wedge\bar\partial f$. Thus $B$ is symmetric. Since $A$ is closed, Stokes' formula (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}) applied to $\mathrm d(g\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge A)$ gives
\[
 B(f,g)=B(g,f)=-\int_Xg\,\ddc f\wedge A.
\]
On the compact manifold there are constants $0<c\leq C$ with $c\,\omega\leq\omega_j\leq C\omega$ for all $j$. By the telescoping identity
\[
 A_1\wedge\cdots\wedge A_{n-1}-B_1\wedge\cdots\wedge B_{n-1}=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}A_1\wedge\cdots\wedge A_{m-1}\wedge(A_m-B_m)\wedge B_{m+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge B_{n-1},
\]
applied with $A_j=\omega_j$, $B_j=c\,\omega$ and with $A_j=C\omega$, $B_j=\omega_j$, the forms $A-c^{n-1}\omega^{n-1}$ and $C^{n-1}\omega^{n-1}-A$ are sums of products of semipositive $(1,1)$-forms. Such products, and their products with the semipositive form $\mathrm i\partial f\wedge\bar\partial f$, are strongly positive (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}). Hence $B(f,f)$ lies between $c^{n-1}$ and $C^{n-1}$ times $\int_X\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial f\wedge\bar\partial f\wedge\omega^{n-1}$. At a point, if $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n$ is a unitary coframe, that is, $\omega=\mathrm i\sum_j\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j$, and $\partial f=\sum_ja_j\theta_j$, then $\mathrm i\partial f\wedge\bar\partial f\wedge\omega^{n-1}/(n-1)!=\sum_j|a_j|^2\omega^n/n!$ and $|\mathrm df|^2=2\sum_j|a_j|^2$; so the last integral is $\frac{(n-1)!}{4\pi}\|\mathrm df\|_{L^2}^2$. By the inequality $\|v\|_{L^2}\leq C\|\mathrm dv\|_{L^2}$ for mean-zero $v$, proved in Step 6 of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, $B$ is an equivalent Hilbert inner product on the subspace of real functions of mean zero in $H^1$. The Riesz representation theorem (\cref{prop:hilbert-projection-representation}, for real Hilbert spaces) gives $f$ in that subspace with $B(f,g)=-\int_Xg\eta$ for every mean-zero $g$. Since $\int_X\eta=0$ and $B(f,1)=0$, the identity holds for all $g\in H^1$; this is the equation $\ddc f\wedge A=\eta$ in the weak sense.

It remains to prove that $f$ is smooth. In a chart, with $\eta=\tilde\eta\,\mathrm dx$, the weak equation reads $\mathcal A(f,w)=-\int\tilde\eta\,\overline w\,\mathrm dx$ for $w\in C^\infty_c$, where $\mathcal A(f,w)=B(f,\overline w)=\int\sum_{i,j}a^{ij}\partial_jf\,\overline{\partial_iw}\,\mathrm dx$ and the real symmetric matrix $(a^{ij})$ is the density of the quadratic form $\mathrm df\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge A$. By the bounds just proved, $(a^{ij})$ is uniformly positive definite on compact subsets of the chart. This is an operator of the kind considered in Step 4 of the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, with $N=1$, and since $\tilde\eta$ is smooth, the claim proved there shows that $f$ lies locally in every $H^k$; hence $f$ is smooth by the Sobolev embedding of Step 1. If $f$ and $f'$ are two solutions, then $B(f-f',f-f')=0$, so $f-f'$ is constant on the connected manifold $X$, and it vanishes since it has mean zero.
\end{proof}
\index{Poisson equation!mixed K\"ahler weights}

Let $\Lambda$ be the pointwise adjoint of the Lefschetz operator $u\mapsto\omega\wedge u$; thus $\Lambda\omega=|\omega|^2=n$. Let $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n$ be a unitary coframe of $(1,0)$-forms at a point, so that $\omega=\mathrm i\sum_j\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j$ there. A real $(1,1)$-form $T=\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}T_{j\bar k}\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_k$ corresponds to the Hermitian matrix $(T_{j\bar k})$, and $\Lambda T=\sum_jT_{j\bar j}$ is its trace. Indeed, the covectors $\theta_j,\bar\theta_k$ form an orthonormal basis of the complexified cotangent space, as one checks for $\theta_j=\mathrm dz_j/\sqrt2$ in coordinates in which $\omega$ is standard at the point, so the forms $\mathrm i\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_k$ are orthonormal, and $\Lambda T=\langle T,\omega\rangle$ for $2$-forms $T$. Since $\omega^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is the sum of the products of $n-1$ distinct forms $\mathrm i\theta_l\wedge\bar\theta_l$, we have $\mathrm i\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_k\wedge\omega^{n-1}/(n-1)!=\delta_{jk}\,\omega^n/n!$; equivalently,
\[
 T\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=(\Lambda T)\frac{\omega^n}{n!}.
\]
The last formula defines $\Lambda T$ for a current $T$, as a distribution; it is the action of $\Lambda$ on currents described at the beginning of this section.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kahler-trace-star}
For a scalar distribution $f$ and a real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kahler-trace-star}
 \Lambda\ddc f=-\frac{Lf}{4\pi},\qquad
 *T=(\Lambda T)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}
          -T\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}\quad(n\geq2).
\end{equation}
If $\mathrm dT=0$ and $\Lambda T$ is constant, then $\mathcal LT=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both sides of each identity are differential operators, or bundle maps, with smooth coefficients, acting on distributions and currents as explained at the beginning of this section. Hence it suffices to verify the identities for smooth $f$ and $T$, at an arbitrary point. Choose holomorphic coordinates centered at the point in which $\omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_j\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ at the point and the first derivatives of the coefficients of $\omega$ vanish there. Such coordinates exist. After a linear change of coordinates, $\omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_{k,l}g_{k\bar l}\,\mathrm dz_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_l$ with $g_{k\bar l}(0)=\delta_{kl}$, and $\partial\omega=0$ gives $\partial_mg_{k\bar l}=\partial_kg_{m\bar l}$. By the inverse function theorem, $z_l=w_l-\frac12\sum_{k,m}\partial_mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_kw_m$ defines holomorphic coordinates $w$ near $0$. Substituting $\mathrm dz_l=\mathrm dw_l-\sum_{k,m}\partial_mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_m\,\mathrm dw_k$, which uses the symmetry in $k$ and $m$, we find that the coefficient of $\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\mathrm dw_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_l$ in $\omega$ is $\delta_{kl}$ plus terms of order two in $w$: the terms $\sum_m\partial_mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_m$ of $g_{k\bar l}$ cancel, and so do their conjugates $\sum_m\partial_{\bar m}g_{k\bar l}(0)\bar w_m$, because $\overline{g_{l\bar k}}=g_{k\bar l}$. At the point, the first derivatives of the metric coefficients vanish, so \eqref{eq:laplace-beltrami-local} gives $Lf=-\sum_j(\partial_{x_j}^2+\partial_{y_j}^2)f=-4\sum_j\partial_j\partial_{\bar j}f$ there, while $\theta_j=\mathrm dz_j/\sqrt2$ is a unitary coframe and
\[
 \ddc f=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}f_{j\bar k}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k
       =\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}\frac{f_{j\bar k}}{\pi}\,\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_k .
\]
Hence $\Lambda\ddc f=\pi^{-1}\sum_jf_{j\bar j}=-Lf/(4\pi)$. For the second identity, diagonalize the Hermitian matrix of $T$ by a unitary change of coframe; by linearity it suffices to treat $T=\mathrm i\theta_1\wedge\bar\theta_1$. Writing $\omega_j=\mathrm i\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j$, the star of $\omega_1$ is $\omega_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$. On the other hand $\omega^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is the sum of all products of $n-1$ distinct $\omega_j$, while $\omega_1\wedge\omega^{n-2}/(n-2)!$ is the sum of those products that contain $\omega_1$; their difference is exactly $\omega_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$, and $\Lambda\omega_1=1$.

If $\mathrm dT=0$ and $\Lambda T$ is constant, the second identity and $\mathrm d\omega=0$ give $\mathrm d*T=0$, hence $\mathrm d^*T=-*\mathrm d*T=0$, and $\mathcal LT=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*T+\mathrm d^*\mathrm dT=0$. In dimension one a $(1,1)$-current is $T=(\Lambda T)\omega$, so $*T=\Lambda T$ is constant and the same conclusion holds.
\end{proof}

Since $\omega$ is an arbitrary K\"ahler form on $X$, \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations,lem:kahler-trace-star} hold for every K\"ahler form $\omega'$ on $X$ in place of $\omega$, with the same Sobolev spaces $H^r$; we write $L_{\omega'}$ and $\Lambda_{\omega'}$ for the corresponding operators. We record a consequence used later. Write $\omega'$ in local holomorphic coordinates as $\omega'=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}g_{j\bar k}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$, let $(g^{j\bar k})$ be the inverse matrix, normalized by $\sum_kg^{j\bar k}g_{l\bar k}=\delta_{jl}$, and put $D_gf=\sum_{j,k}g^{j\bar k}\partial_j\partial_{\bar k}f$. Since $\ddc f=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}f_{j\bar k}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$, the function $D_gf$ is the trace of $\ddc f$ relative to $\omega'$, and \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star} for $\omega'$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}
 D_gf=\Lambda_{\omega'}\ddc f=-\frac{L_{\omega'}f}{4\pi}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, by \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} for $\omega'$, for every constant $\beta>0$ the operator $D_g-\beta=-(4\pi)^{-1}(L_{\omega'}+4\pi\beta)$ is a bijection of the space of distributions onto itself and maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^r$ for every integer $r$. For $\beta=0$, the equation $D_gf=a$ for a distribution $a$ has a solution if and only if $a$ has mean zero with respect to the volume form $\omega'^n/n!$ (that is, $\int_Xa\,\omega'^n=0$ when $a$ is integrable); the solution is then unique up to an additive constant, and it is smooth if $a$ is smooth. (The condition is necessary because $\langle L_{\omega'}f,1\rangle=\langle f,L_{\omega'}1\rangle=0$, with the pairing through $\omega'^n/n!$.)

\begin{theorem}[The $\ddc$-lemma]\label{thm:global-ddc-lemma}
Every closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$ has a unique decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-current-decomposition}
 T=H+\ddc f,
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a smooth harmonic real $(1,1)$-form and $f$ is a real distribution of mean zero. If $T$ is smooth, then $f$ is smooth. If $T$ is positive, then $f$ has a canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative. Moreover, $T$ is exact as a current if and only if $H=0$.
\end{theorem}
\index{ddc@$\ddc$-lemma}\index{ddc@$\ddc$ operator!global potentials}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=\int_XT\wedge\omega^{n-1}/\int_X\omega^n$. Since $T\wedge\omega^{n-1}=(\Lambda T)\omega^n/n$, the distribution $\Lambda T-na$ has mean zero, and \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} gives a real distribution $f$ of mean zero with $Lf=-4\pi(\Lambda T-na)$. By \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}, $\Lambda\ddc f=\Lambda T-na$, so the closed current $U=T-a\omega-\ddc f$ has $\Lambda U=0$. By \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star} again, $U$ is harmonic, and by \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} it is smooth. The K\"ahler form is harmonic as well, since $\mathrm d\omega=0$ and $*\omega=\omega^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is closed. Hence $H=a\omega+U$ and $f$ give the decomposition \eqref{eq:global-current-decomposition}. If $T$ is smooth, then $\Lambda T$ is smooth, and so is $f$ by \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}.

An exact harmonic form $B$ vanishes: if $B=\mathrm dS$ for a current $S$, then
\[
 \|B\|_{L^2}^2=\int_X\mathrm dS\wedge*B=\pm\, S(\mathrm d*B)=0,
\]
since $\mathrm d*B=0$. If $T=H'+\ddc f'$ is a second decomposition, then $H-H'=\mathrm d\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(f'-f)$ is exact and harmonic, hence zero. Then $\ddc(f-f')=0$, so $L(f-f')=0$ by \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}; thus $f-f'$ is constant by \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, and it vanishes because both have mean zero. The same argument proves the exactness criterion: if $T$ is exact, so is $H=T-\mathrm d\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f$, whence $H=0$; conversely, if $H=0$, then $T=\mathrm d\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f$.

Suppose now that $T\geq0$. By \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential,lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}, every point of $X$ has a coordinate ball on which $T=\ddc u$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $H=\ddc h$ with $h$ smooth. Then $\ddc(f+h-u)=0$, so the scalar Laplacian of the distribution $f+h-u$ vanishes, and Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}) shows that it is a smooth pluriharmonic function $p$. Thus $f=u-h+p$ is, on the ball, the class of a plurisubharmonic function minus a smooth function. On the overlap of two balls, the two local representatives $u_1-h_1+p_1$ and $u_2-h_2+p_2$ agree almost everywhere; after adding $h_1-p_1$ to both, both become plurisubharmonic, because $h_1-p_1-h_2+p_2$ is smooth with $\ddc(h_1-p_1-h_2+p_2)=H-H=0$, and plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}). Hence the local representatives define a global quasi-plurisubharmonic representative of $f$. It is the only one: two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere, by the same argument applied after adding a local smooth function that makes both plurisubharmonic.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:real-one-one-cohomology}
The real $(1,1)$-cohomology space\index{cohomology!real $(1,1)$ classes} of $X$ is
\[
 H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)=
 \frac{\{\text{closed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms}\}}
      {\{\ddc f:f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)\}}.
\]
The class of a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ is denoted $\{\theta\}$. For a closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ we put $\{T\}=\{H\}$, where $H$ is the harmonic form in \eqref{eq:global-current-decomposition}.
\end{definition}
By \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}, every class contains exactly one harmonic form, so $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is isomorphic to the space of harmonic real $(1,1)$-forms; in particular it is finite dimensional, and we give it its vector-space topology. The real de Rham cohomology in degree two is the quotient of the closed smooth real $2$-forms by the forms $\mathrm d\beta$, with $\beta$ a smooth real $1$-form. A closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is exact in this sense has $H=0$ and is therefore of the form $\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth; hence $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is identified with the subspace of de Rham classes represented by $(1,1)$-forms. Choosing a basis of harmonic forms gives a linear choice $\alpha\mapsto\theta_\alpha$ of smooth representatives, and for every norm on $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ there is a constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-class-small-form}
 -C\|\alpha\|\omega\leq\theta_\alpha\leq C\|\alpha\|\omega.
\end{equation}
The class of a closed current does not change when $\ddc$ of a distribution is added to it, by the uniqueness in \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}. If $H_1,\ldots,H_N$ is an $L^2$-orthonormal basis of harmonic real $(1,1)$-forms, the coefficients of the harmonic part of $T$ are the pairings $\int_XT\wedge*H_k$, because $*H_k$ is closed and therefore $\int_X\ddc f\wedge*H_k=0$. Hence the map $T\mapsto\{T\}$ on closed currents is continuous for weak convergence.

Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form and $T\geq0$ a closed current with $\{T\}=\{\theta\}$. Write $T=H+\ddc f$ as in \eqref{eq:global-current-decomposition}; since $\{T\}=\{\theta\}$, also $\theta=H+\ddc g$ with $g$ smooth. Then \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma} gives $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, namely the canonical representative of $f-g$. This potential is unique up to an additive constant: if also $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi'$, then $\varphi-\varphi'$ is constant almost everywhere by the uniqueness part of the theorem, hence everywhere, since both functions are canonical representatives. If $\theta$ is replaced by $\theta+\ddc h$ with $h$ smooth, then $\varphi$ is replaced by $\varphi-h$, up to a constant. Consequently the pole set, the Lelong numbers and the comparison of singularities of potentials of currents in $\{\theta\}$ do not depend on the choice of the smooth representative $\theta$.

\section{Normalized potentials and minimal singularities}
\label{sec:global-minimal-potentials}
Fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$. A function $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is determined by its current $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ up to an additive constant, which we fix by normalizing the supremum; the local compactness theorem for plurisubharmonic functions then shows that normalized potentials form a compact family. Integrals over $X$ are taken with respect to $\mathrm dV$, or equivalently $\omega^n$, unless stated otherwise. A $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function is integrable on the compact manifold $X$, being locally integrable, and it is determined by its class in $L^1(X)$, since two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere (see the end of the proof of \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}). We may therefore regard $\PSH(X,\theta)$ as a subset of $L^1(X)$. More generally, if $\varphi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and $\psi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic or continuous, an inequality between $\varphi$ and $\psi$ that holds almost everywhere holds everywhere. Indeed, near each point choose a smooth function $h$ such that $\varphi+h$, and $\psi+h$ in the first case, are plurisubharmonic; the values of $\varphi+h$ and $\psi+h$ at the point are the limits of their averages over small coordinate balls, by \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} and by continuity, and the inequality holds between these averages.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}
Let $a\leq b$ be real numbers. The set of $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\leq\sup_X\varphi\leq b$ is compact in $L^1(X)$, and the map $\varphi\mapsto\sup_X\varphi$ is continuous on $\PSH(X,\theta)$ for the $L^1$ topology. In particular, the functions $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\sup_X\varphi=0$ are uniformly bounded in $L^1(X)$. The same statements hold for a sequence of backgrounds: if smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta_j$ converge smoothly to $\theta$ and $\varphi_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ satisfy $a\leq\sup_X\varphi_j\leq b$, then a subsequence converges in $L^1(X)$ to some $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\leq\sup_X\varphi\leq b$, and $\sup_X\varphi_j\to\sup_X\varphi$ whenever $\varphi_j\to\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ in $L^1(X)$.
\end{proposition}
\index{theta-plurisubharmonic function!compactness of normalized potentials}
\begin{proof}
Choose finitely many coordinate balls $B_k$ covering $X$, smaller concentric balls $B'_k$ still covering $X$, and smooth functions $h_k$ near $\overline{B_k}$ with $\ddc h_k=\theta$ (\cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}, applied on slightly larger concentric balls). For $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ the functions $\varphi+h_k$ are plurisubharmonic on $B_k$.

Let $(\varphi_j)$ be a sequence with $a\leq\sup_X\varphi_j\leq b$. The plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi_j+h_k$ are uniformly bounded above on each $B_k$. Since $\varphi_j$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $X$, it attains its supremum at a point $x_j$, and after passing to a subsequence $x_j\to x$, because $X$ is covered by finitely many closed coordinate balls. Choose $k$ with $x\in B'_k$. On $B_k$ the functions $\varphi_j+h_k$ cannot converge locally uniformly to $-\infty$, because their values at $x_j$ are bounded below by $a+\inf_{B'_k}h_k$ for large $j$. By \cref{thm:psh-compactness-hartogs}, a further subsequence converges in $L^1_{\loc}(B_k)$. If $B_l$ meets $B_k$, the functions $\varphi_j+h_l$ cannot tend to $-\infty$ locally uniformly on $B_l$, since on the nonempty open set $B_k\cap B_l$ they differ by the smooth function $h_l-h_k$ from a sequence that converges in $L^1_{\loc}$; so again a subsequence converges in $L^1_{\loc}(B_l)$. Since $X$ is connected, every ball is joined to $B_k$ by a finite chain of overlapping balls, and after finitely many extractions the sequence converges in $L^1(B'_l)$ for every $l$, hence in $L^1(X)$, to some $\varphi$. The limit satisfies $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0$ as a distributional limit of positive currents, and its canonical representative belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$.

Now let $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$, all in $\PSH(X,\theta)$. The submean inequality for $\varphi_j+h_k$ on small balls shows that the $\varphi_j$ are uniformly bounded above. Let $x_j\to x$; we claim that $\limsup_j\varphi_j(x_j)\leq\varphi(x)$. Indeed, choose $k$ with $x\in B_k$, let $c>\varphi(x)$ be real, and use upper semicontinuity to choose a closed ball $K\subset B_k$ around $x$ on which $\varphi<c$. The function $c+h_k$ is continuous near $K$ and $\varphi+h_k<c+h_k$ on $K$, so the second part of \cref{thm:psh-compactness-hartogs} gives $\varphi_j+h_k<c+h_k$ on $K$ for large $j$; hence $\varphi_j(x_j)<c$ for large $j$. Now choose a subsequence along which $\sup_X\varphi_j$ tends to $\limsup_j\sup_X\varphi_j$ and points $x_j$ with $\varphi_j(x_j)=\sup_X\varphi_j$ converge to some $x$; the claim gives $\limsup_j\sup_X\varphi_j\leq\varphi(x)\leq\sup_X\varphi$. Conversely, choose a subsequence along which $\sup_X\varphi_j$ tends to $\liminf_j\sup_X\varphi_j$ and $\sum_j\|\varphi_j-\varphi\|_{L^1}<\infty$; by monotone convergence $\sum_j|\varphi_j-\varphi|$ is integrable, hence finite almost everywhere, so $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ almost everywhere along it. We get $\varphi\leq\liminf_j\sup_X\varphi_j$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section. The two inequalities prove that the supremum is continuous, and in particular the limit in the previous paragraph satisfies $a\leq\sup_X\varphi\leq b$. Thus the set in question is sequentially compact, hence compact, in the metric space $L^1(X)$. The continuous function $\|\cdot\|_{L^1}$ is bounded on the compact set of normalized functions.

For smoothly convergent backgrounds, choose $C\geq0$ with $\theta_j\leq C\omega$ for all $j$. Then $C\omega+\ddc\varphi_j\geq0$, so all $\varphi_j$ lie in $\PSH(X,C\omega)$, and the first part, applied to the closed form $C\omega$, gives an $L^1$-convergent subsequence and the convergence of the suprema. Since $\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j\to\theta+\ddc\varphi$ as distributions, the limit belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{proof}
The proof uses neither the closedness of $\omega$ nor the particular volume form. It therefore applies verbatim on every connected compact complex manifold, with a Hermitian form in place of $\omega$ and $L^1$ taken with respect to any smooth positive volume form.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:normalized-global-psh-lp-bound}
For every $1\leq p<\infty$ there is a constant $M_p$, depending only on $X$, $\theta$, $\omega$ and $p$, such that
\[
 \|u\|_{L^p(X,\omega^n)}\leq M_p
           \quad\text{for all }u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\text{ with }\sup_Xu=0.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(If $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is empty, there is nothing to prove.) Choose finitely many coordinate polydiscs $D_k$ such that the polydiscs $\frac14D_k$ with the same centers and a quarter of the radii cover $X$, and smooth functions $h_k$ near $\overline{D_k}$ with $\ddc h_k=\theta$. For $u$ as in the statement, $v_k=u+h_k-\sup_{D_k}h_k$ is a nonpositive plurisubharmonic function on $D_k$. By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} and the boundedness of the $h_k$ and of the coordinate volume densities, the norms $\|v_k\|_{L^1(D_k)}$ are bounded independently of $u$. After rescaling $D_k$ to the unit polydisc, \eqref{eq:negative-psh-lp-lone-bound} bounds $\|v_k\|_{L^p(\frac14D_k)}$ independently of $u$. Since $u=v_k-h_k+\sup_{D_k}h_k$ on $D_k$, the bounded functions $h_k$ and the bounded volume densities give the assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!uniform global Lp bound}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound}
There are $\alpha,C>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta$ and $\omega$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound}
 \int_X\mathrm e^{-\alpha u}\omega^n\leq C
       \quad\text{for all }u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\text{ with }\sup_Xu=0.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(If $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is empty, any $\alpha,C>0$ will do.) Choose finitely many coordinate unit balls $B_j$ such that the balls $\frac14B_j$ with the same centers and a quarter of the radii cover $X$, and smooth functions $\rho_j$ near $\overline{B_j}$ with $\ddc\rho_j=\theta$. For $u$ as in the statement put $v_j=u+\rho_j-\sup_{B_j}\rho_j$ on $B_j$; then $v_j$ is plurisubharmonic and $v_j\leq u\leq0$. By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} and comparison of the coordinate volume densities, $\|v_j\|_{L^1(B_j)}\leq M_j$ for a constant $M_j\geq1$ independent of $u$. Applying \cref{prop:negative-psh-ball-exponential} to $v_j/M_j$, we see that $\int_{\frac14B_j}\mathrm e^{-\alpha_jv_j}$ is bounded independently of $u$, where $\alpha_j=\alpha_n/M_j$. Put $\alpha=\min_j\alpha_j>0$. Since $v_j\leq u\leq0$ and $\alpha\leq\alpha_j$, we have $\mathrm e^{-\alpha u}\leq\mathrm e^{-\alpha_ju}\leq\mathrm e^{-\alpha_jv_j}$ on $B_j$. Summing over $j$ and comparing the coordinate volume densities with $\omega^n$ gives \eqref{eq:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound}.
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!uniform exponential integrability}

Recall from \cref{def:pluripolar-set} that a subset of $X$ is pluripolar if it is locally contained in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonpluripolar-normalization}
Let $K\subset X$ be a compact set that is not pluripolar. Then there is a constant $C$ such that
\[
 \sup_X\varphi\leq\sup_K\varphi+C\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta).
\]
Consequently, every subset of $\PSH(X,\theta)$ on which $\sup_K\varphi$ is bounded above and below is relatively compact in $L^1(X)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that no such constant exists. Since $\sup_X\varphi-\sup_K\varphi$ does not change when a constant is added to $\varphi$, there are $\varphi_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\sup_X\varphi_j=0$ and $\sup_K\varphi_j\leq-2^j$. Choose $C\geq0$ with $\theta\leq C\omega$ and put $\psi_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}\varphi_j$. Since $\sum_j2^{-j}\leq1$, we have $C\omega+\ddc\psi_N\geq0$; the sequence $(\psi_N)$ is decreasing because $\varphi_j\leq0$; and $\|\psi_N\|_{L^1}\leq\sup_j\|\varphi_j\|_{L^1}<\infty$ by \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}. By monotone convergence the limit $\psi$ is integrable, so it is not identically $-\infty$, and \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}, applied on coordinate balls after adding local potentials of $C\omega$, shows that $\psi\in\PSH(X,C\omega)$. On $K$ every term $2^{-j}\varphi_j$ is at most $-1$, so $\psi=-\infty$ on $K$. Hence $K$ is pluripolar, a contradiction. The last assertion follows: bounds for $\sup_K\varphi$ give bounds for $\sup_X\varphi$, and \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:global-decreasing-nets}
Let $(\varphi_i)$ be a decreasing net in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ whose suprema $\sup_X\varphi_i$ are bounded below. Then its pointwise infimum $\varphi$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, $\varphi_i\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\sup_X\varphi_i\to\sup_X\varphi$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix an index $i_0$. For $i\geq i_0$ the suprema are bounded above by $\sup_X\varphi_{i_0}$, so the tail of the net lies in a compact subset of $L^1(X)$ by \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}, and a subnet converges in $L^1$ to some $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. For each fixed $i$, the members of the subnet are eventually below $\varphi_i$, so $\psi\leq\varphi_i$ almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section. Hence the pointwise infimum $\varphi$ is at least $\psi$ and is not identically $-\infty$. Applying \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets} on the coordinate balls of a finite cover, after adding local potentials of $\theta$, we find that $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\varphi_i\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$. The continuity of the supremum in \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:global-minimal-potential}
Let $\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Recall from \cref{sec:analytic-approximation-comparison} that $\varphi$ is \emph{more singular}\index{more singular potential} than $\psi$, written $\varphi\preceq\psi$, if $\varphi\leq\psi+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$; we then also say that $\psi$ is \emph{less singular}\index{less singular potential} than $\varphi$. A potential $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has \emph{minimal singularities}\index{minimal singularities} if every $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is more singular than $\psi$. If $\PSH(X,\theta)\ne\varnothing$, put
\[
 V_\theta=\bigl(\sup\{\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta):\varphi\leq0\}\bigr)^*,
\]
where ${}^*$ denotes the upper semicontinuous regularization.
\end{definition}
\index{minimal singularities!envelope $V_\theta$}
The family in this definition is nonempty, because $\varphi-\sup_X\varphi$ belongs to it for every $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and it is bounded above by $0$. \Cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, applied on coordinate balls after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta$, therefore gives $V_\theta\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $V_\theta\leq0$; and $\sup_XV_\theta=0$, because $V_\theta\geq\varphi-\sup_X\varphi$ for every $\varphi$. The same inequality reads $\varphi\leq V_\theta+\sup_X\varphi$, so $V_\theta$ has minimal singularities. Any two potentials with minimal singularities are each more singular than the other, so their difference is bounded on the set where both are finite. Replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc h$ with $h$ smooth replaces $\PSH(X,\theta)$ by $\PSH(X,\theta)-h$; since $h$ is bounded, the notion of minimal singularities depends only on the class $\{\theta\}$.

Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are defined pointwise as in \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}: if $\varphi=v+g$ near $y$, with $v$ plurisubharmonic and $g$ smooth, then $\nu(\varphi,y)=\nu(v,y)$, which does not depend on the decomposition. Hence the rules of \cref{prop:lelong-calculus}, the characterization of $\nu(\varphi,y)$ as the largest $c\geq0$ with $\varphi\leq c\log|z-z(y)|^2+O(1)$ near $y$ (\cref{prop:lelong-logarithmic-growth}), and the upper semicontinuity \eqref{eq:lelong-upper-semicontinuity} along sequences $\varphi_j=v_j-g$ with a common smooth $g$, all carry over. If $Z$ is an irreducible analytic subset of a complex manifold, for instance a \emph{prime divisor}, that is, an irreducible hypersurface, we call $\nu(\varphi,Z)=\inf_{y\in Z}\nu(\varphi,y)$ the \emph{generic Lelong number}\index{Lelong number!generic} of $\varphi$ along $Z$. The proof of \cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong} applies without change and shows that the infimum is attained at every point of $Z$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$; its only input, the analyticity of the sets $\{\nu(\varphi,\cdot)\geq c\}$, is a local property, which follows from \cref{thm:siu-lelong-level-sets} applied to the local plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi+g$. If $\pi\colon Y\to X$ is a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$ and $E\subset Y$ is a prime divisor, we put $\nu(\varphi,E)=\nu(\varphi\circ\pi,E)$ whenever $\varphi\circ\pi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, as in \cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-target-psh-modification}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$. Then $Y$ is compact and connected, and $\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\pi$ is a bijection from $\PSH(X,\theta)$ onto $\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$, which is continuous for convergence in $L^1(X)$ and $L^1(Y)$, the latter taken with respect to any smooth positive volume form. For every prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the generic Lelong numbers $\nu(\varphi,E)=\nu(\varphi\circ\pi,E)$ are bounded above uniformly for $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The manifold $Y=\pi^{-1}(X)$ is compact, because $\pi$ is proper. By the definition of bimeromorphic maps (\cref{sec:reduced-spaces-normalization}), there is a nowhere dense analytic subset $E_0\subset X$ such that $\pi^{-1}(E_0)$ is nowhere dense and $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism of $Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(E_0)$ onto $X\setminus E_0$. The set $X\setminus E_0$ is connected by \cref{lem:bounded-holomorphic-removal}, hence so is its dense preimage, and therefore $Y$ is connected. The image $\pi(Y)$ is closed, since proper maps are closed, and it contains the dense set $X\setminus E_0$; so $\pi$ is surjective. By \cref{prop:smooth-bimeromorphic-divisor-locus} there is an analytic subset $A\subset X$ of codimension at least two such that $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X\setminus A$. Then $\pi^{-1}(A)$ is an analytic subset of $Y$ different from $Y$, because $\pi$ is surjective. Since $Y$ is connected, $\pi^{-1}(A)$ is locally contained in the zero set of a holomorphic function that does not vanish identically, so it is nowhere dense and a null set (\cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}). The same holds for $A$ in $X$.

If $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $B$ be a coordinate ball and $h$ a smooth function on $B$ with $\ddc h=\theta$ (\cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}). On every connected coordinate neighborhood $W\subset\pi^{-1}(B)$ the function $(\varphi+h)\circ\pi$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$, by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, and the second case is excluded because $\varphi+h$ is finite almost everywhere on the image under the biholomorphism $\pi$ of the nonempty open set $W\setminus\pi^{-1}(A)$. Hence $\varphi\circ\pi=(\varphi+h)\circ\pi-h\circ\pi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, with $\pi^*\theta+\ddc(\varphi\circ\pi)=\ddc((\varphi+h)\circ\pi)\geq0$ on $\pi^{-1}(B)$. Thus $\varphi\circ\pi\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. If $\varphi\circ\pi=\varphi'\circ\pi$, then $\varphi=\varphi'$ on $X\setminus A$, hence almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section; this proves injectivity.

Conversely, let $\psi\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. Over $X\setminus A$ it descends to the function $\psi\circ\pi^{-1}$, which is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic there. It is bounded above, because $\psi$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $Y$. On a coordinate ball $B$ with $\ddc h=\theta$ on $B$, the plurisubharmonic function $\psi\circ\pi^{-1}+h$ on $B\setminus A$ is therefore locally bounded above near $A$, and it extends to a plurisubharmonic function on $B$ by \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension}. The extensions on two balls agree outside the null set $A$, hence everywhere, so they define $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. The pullback $\varphi\circ\pi$ equals $\psi$ outside the null set $\pi^{-1}(A)$, hence everywhere. This proves surjectivity.

For continuity, we use \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} on $X$ and on $Y$; by the remark following its proof, it holds on $Y$ with a Hermitian form in place of $\omega$. Let $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$. By the continuity of the supremum, it suffices to treat normalized functions, $\sup_X\varphi_j=\sup_X\varphi=0$, since subtracting the suprema preserves $L^1$ convergence. The pullbacks have the same suprema, because $\pi$ is surjective, so they are relatively compact in $L^1(Y)$, and every cluster value lies in $\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. On compact subsets of $Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(A)$, a change of variables shows that every $L^1$ cluster value of $(\varphi_j\circ\pi)$ coincides almost everywhere with $\varphi\circ\pi$; since $\pi^{-1}(A)$ is a null set, every cluster value is $\varphi\circ\pi$. A relatively compact sequence in a metric space with a single cluster value converges to it; hence $\varphi_j\circ\pi\to\varphi\circ\pi$ in $L^1(Y)$.

Finally, $\nu(\varphi\circ\pi,E)$ is the infimum over $y\in E$ of the point Lelong numbers $\nu(\varphi\circ\pi,y)$. Near $y$ choose a smooth $g$ with $\ddc g=\pi^*\theta$, so that the functions $\varphi\circ\pi+g$ are plurisubharmonic. By \eqref{eq:lelong-upper-semicontinuity}, applied to them, each $\nu(\varphi\circ\pi,y)$ is upper semicontinuous for $L^1$ convergence of $\varphi\circ\pi$, hence, by the continuity just proved, for $L^1$ convergence of $\varphi$; and an infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous. Since Lelong numbers do not change when a constant is added, we may restrict to the normalized functions, which form a compact set by \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}. An upper semicontinuous function with finite values attains its maximum on a compact set, which gives the uniform bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:global-psh-balayage}
Let $B\Subset B'$ be coordinate balls in $X$, and let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be bounded near $\overline{B'}$. There is $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\psi\geq\varphi$, $\psi=\varphi$ on $X\setminus B$, $\psi$ bounded near $\overline B$, and $(\theta+\ddc\psi)^n=0$ on $B$. It is the largest function in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ with these properties, and any two functions with these properties that are continuous on $\overline B$ coincide.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $\theta=\ddc h$ on $B'$, with $h$ smooth, and apply \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} to the locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\varphi+h$ on $B'$. Subtracting $h$ from the result on $B'$ and keeping $\varphi$ outside $B'$ gives $\psi$; here $(\theta+\ddc\psi)^n=(\ddc(\psi+h))^n$ is the Bedford--Taylor product of the bounded plurisubharmonic function $\psi+h$. If $\psi'$ is another function with the stated properties, then by upper semicontinuity $\limsup_{z\to\zeta,\,z\in B}\psi'(z)\leq\psi'(\zeta)=\varphi(\zeta)$ for every $\zeta\in\partial B$. Hence $\psi'+h$ belongs to the family whose upper envelope is $\psi+h$ in \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}, and $\psi'\leq\psi$ on $B$. If $\psi'$ is continuous on $\overline B$, then its boundary values $\varphi|_{\partial B}$ are continuous, and $\psi'+h$ is the solution of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem with the continuous boundary values $(\varphi+h)|_{\partial B}$; by the uniqueness in \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}, any two such continuous functions coincide.
\end{proof}

\section{Positivity cones and divisorial decomposition}
\label{sec:positive-cohomology-cones}
Let $\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ and fix a smooth representative $\theta\in\alpha$. By \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}, the closed currents in $\alpha$ are exactly the currents $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\varphi$ a real distribution, so positivity properties of $\alpha$ become inequalities for $\theta+\ddc\varphi$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:cohomology-positivity-cones}
A class $\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{pseudo-effective}\index{cohomology!pseudo-effective class} if it contains a closed positive current;
\item \emph{K\"ahler}\index{cohomology!K\"ahler class} if it contains a K\"ahler form;
\item \emph{nef}\index{cohomology!nef class} if for every $\varepsilon>0$ it contains a smooth form $\theta_\varepsilon$ with $\theta_\varepsilon\geq-\varepsilon\omega$;
\item \emph{big}\index{cohomology!big class} if it contains a closed current $T$ with $T\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$.
\end{itemize}
A closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ with $T\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$ is called a \emph{K\"ahler current}\index{K\"ahler current}. The cones of pseudo-effective, K\"ahler, nef and big classes are denoted $\mathcal E$, $\mathcal K$, $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal B$. The zero class belongs to the pseudo-effective cone and to the nef cone.
\end{definition}
These notions do not depend on $\omega$, because any two Hermitian forms on the compact manifold $X$ are bounded by constant multiples of each other. By the discussion after \cref{def:real-one-one-cohomology}, $\alpha$ is pseudo-effective if and only if $\PSH(X,\theta)\ne\varnothing$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cohomology-cone-topology}
The pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$ is closed and convex, and the K\"ahler cone $\mathcal K$ is open and convex. Moreover $\mathcal N=\overline{\mathcal K}\subset\mathcal E$, the K\"ahler cone $\mathcal K$ is the interior of $\mathcal N$, and the big cone $\mathcal B$ is the interior of $\mathcal E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sums and positive multiples of positive currents, K\"ahler forms or nef representatives are of the same kind, which proves convexity of all four cones. Let $\alpha_j\to\alpha$ be pseudo-effective classes, with positive currents $T_j\in\alpha_j$. Since the $\ddc$ of a distribution pairs to zero with the closed form $\omega^{n-1}$, the trace masses are
\[
 \int_XT_j\wedge\omega^{n-1}
       =\int_X\theta_{\alpha_j}\wedge\omega^{n-1},
\]
where $\theta_{\alpha_j}$ are the representatives of \eqref{eq:small-class-small-form}; they are bounded because the classes converge. Let $K$ be a compact subset of a chart, $\chi$ a test function on the chart with $0\leq\chi\leq1$ and $\chi=1$ on $K$, and $c'>0$ such that $\beta\leq c'\omega$ on $\Supp\chi$. By the telescoping identity used in the proof of \cref{lem:mixed-scalar-poisson}, $c'^{n-1}\omega^{n-1}-\chi\beta^{n-1}$ is a smooth strongly positive form on $X$, so the trace measures satisfy $\mu_{T_j}(K)\leq T_j(\chi\beta^{n-1})\leq c'^{n-1}\int_XT_j\wedge\omega^{n-1}$, which is bounded independently of $j$. By the compactness assertion of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, a subsequence of $(T_j)$ converges weakly to a closed positive current $T$, and $\{T\}=\alpha$ because the class map is weakly continuous. Hence $\mathcal E$ is closed.

If $\omega_0\in\alpha$ is a K\"ahler form, then $\omega_0\geq c\omega$ for some $c>0$, and by \eqref{eq:small-class-small-form} the form $\omega_0+\theta_{\alpha'-\alpha}\in\alpha'$ is K\"ahler whenever $C\|\alpha'-\alpha\|<c$; hence $\mathcal K$ is open. If $\alpha$ is nef and $\varepsilon>0$, a representative $\theta_\varepsilon\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ gives the K\"ahler form $\theta_\varepsilon+2\varepsilon\omega\in\alpha+2\varepsilon\{\omega\}$, so $\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal K}$. Conversely, let K\"ahler classes $\alpha_j$ with K\"ahler forms $\omega_j\in\alpha_j$ converge to $\alpha$. Then $\omega_j+\theta_{\alpha-\alpha_j}\in\alpha$ is at least $-C\|\alpha-\alpha_j\|\omega$, so $\alpha$ is nef; thus $\mathcal N=\overline{\mathcal K}$, and $\mathcal N\subset\mathcal E$ because $\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E$ is closed. Since $\mathcal K$ is open and contained in $\mathcal N$, it lies in the interior of $\mathcal N$. If $\alpha$ is nef and $t>0$, then $\alpha+t\{\omega\}$ is K\"ahler: take a representative $\theta_\varepsilon\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ with $\varepsilon<t$. If $\alpha$ lies in the interior of $\mathcal N$, then $\alpha-t\{\omega\}$ is nef for small $t>0$, and therefore $\alpha=(\alpha-t\{\omega\})+t\{\omega\}$ is K\"ahler. Thus $\mathcal K$ is the interior of $\mathcal N$.

If $T\in\alpha$ and $T\geq\delta\omega$, then $T+\theta_{\alpha'-\alpha}\in\alpha'$ is positive whenever $C\|\alpha'-\alpha\|\leq\delta$, so a neighborhood of $\alpha$ lies in $\mathcal E$. Conversely, if $\alpha$ lies in the interior of $\mathcal E$, then $\alpha-\delta\{\omega\}\in\mathcal E$ for some $\delta>0$, and adding $\delta\omega$ to a positive current in $\alpha-\delta\{\omega\}$ gives a K\"ahler current in $\alpha$. Hence $\mathcal B$ is the interior of $\mathcal E$.
\end{proof}

If $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n$ are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms, Stokes' formula shows that $\int_X\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_n$ is unchanged when one $\theta_i$ is replaced by $\theta_i+\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth. We therefore write $\int_X\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n=\int_X\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_n$ for the \emph{intersection number}\index{intersection number} of classes $\alpha_i=\{\theta_i\}$, and use the usual notation for powers, such as $\int_X\alpha^n$. In the mixed Hodge index theorem below, the mixed scalar equation of \cref{lem:mixed-scalar-poisson} turns a cohomological orthogonality condition into a pointwise one, to which the signature lemma for Hermitian matrices applies.

\begin{theorem}[The mixed Hodge index theorem]\label{thm:mixed-hodge-index}
Let $n\geq2$, let $\omega_3,\ldots,\omega_n$ be K\"ahler forms on $X$, and put $\Omega=\omega_3\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$, with $\Omega=1$ when $n=2$. The symmetric bilinear form
\[
 Q(\alpha,\beta)=\int_Xa\wedge b\wedge\Omega,
 \qquad a\in\alpha,\quad b\in\beta,
\]
on $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ has signature $(1,\dim H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)-1)$. For every K\"ahler class $\gamma$, it is negative definite on the hyperplane $\{\alpha:Q(\alpha,\gamma)=0\}$. For K\"ahler classes $\alpha,\beta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-intersection-cauchy}
 Q(\alpha,\beta)^2\geq Q(\alpha,\alpha)Q(\beta,\beta).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\gamma$ be a K\"ahler class with a K\"ahler form $h\in\gamma$, and let $\alpha$ satisfy $Q(\alpha,\gamma)=0$. Choose a smooth closed real form $a\in\alpha$; then $\int_Xa\wedge h\wedge\Omega=0$. Apply \cref{lem:mixed-scalar-poisson} with $A=h\wedge\Omega$, a product of $n-1$ K\"ahler forms, and $\eta=-a\wedge h\wedge\Omega$. The resulting $f$ makes $a'=a+\ddc f\in\alpha$ satisfy $a'\wedge h\wedge\Omega=0$ at every point. At a point, in holomorphic coordinates, the wedge products of real $(1,1)$-forms are expressed by mixed discriminants of their Hermitian matrices, so \cref{lem:mixed-discriminant-signature} gives $a'\wedge a'\wedge\Omega\leq0$, with equality only if $a'$ vanishes at that point. By Stokes' formula,
\[
 Q(\alpha,\alpha)=\int_Xa'\wedge a'\wedge\Omega\leq0.
\]
If this integral vanishes, the smooth nonpositive density $a'\wedge a'\wedge\Omega$ vanishes everywhere, so $a'=0$ and $\alpha=0$. Thus $Q$ is negative definite on the hyperplane $\{Q(\cdot,\gamma)=0\}$.

Since $Q(\gamma,\gamma)=\int_Xh^2\wedge\Omega>0$, every class splits uniquely as a multiple of $\gamma$ plus a class in that hyperplane. The space $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is finite dimensional by \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}, and $Q$ is positive on the line spanned by $\gamma$ and negative definite on its $Q$-orthogonal complement; this gives the signature. Finally, let $\alpha,\beta$ be K\"ahler classes and write $\beta=c\alpha+\delta$ with $Q(\delta,\alpha)=0$. Negative definiteness gives $Q(\beta,\beta)=c^2Q(\alpha,\alpha)+Q(\delta,\delta)\leq c^2Q(\alpha,\alpha)$, while $Q(\alpha,\beta)=cQ(\alpha,\alpha)$. Since $Q(\alpha,\alpha)>0$, we obtain $Q(\alpha,\beta)^2=c^2Q(\alpha,\alpha)^2\geq Q(\alpha,\alpha)Q(\beta,\beta)$.
\end{proof}
\index{Hodge index theorem!mixed}\index{intersection number!mixed}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nef-mixed-intersection-geometric-mean}
Let $r\geq1$, let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$ be nef classes, and let $k_1,\ldots,k_r\geq0$ be integers with $\sum_i k_i=n$. Then
\[
 \int_X\alpha_1^{k_1}\cdots\alpha_r^{k_r}
             \geq\prod_{i:k_i>0}\left(\int_X\alpha_i^n\right)^{k_i/n}.
\]
In particular, for nef classes $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$,
\[
 \int_X\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n
             \geq\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\int_X\alpha_i^n\right)^{1/n}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Assume first that all $\alpha_i$ are K\"ahler. Then all the intersection numbers $\int_X\prod_i\alpha_i^{k_i}$ are positive, and we put $b(k)=\log\int_X\prod_i\alpha_i^{k_i}$ for $k=(k_1,\ldots,k_r)$ with $\sum_ik_i=n$. If $n=1$, only one $k_i$ is positive and there is nothing to prove; let $n\geq2$. Let $i\neq j$ with $k_i,k_j\geq1$, let $\Omega$ be the wedge product of K\"ahler representatives of the remaining $n-2$ factors, counted with multiplicity, and let $e_i$ be the $i$th coordinate vector. Then \eqref{eq:mixed-intersection-cauchy} for $\alpha_i,\alpha_j$ reads
\[
 2b(k)\geq b(k+e_i-e_j)+b(k-e_i+e_j).
\]
Fix the coordinates other than $i,j$ and put $s=k_i+k_j$. The inequality says that the finite sequence $l\mapsto b(\ldots,l,\ldots,s-l,\ldots)$, with $l$ in the $i$th and $s-l$ in the $j$th place, $0\leq l\leq s$, is concave: its consecutive differences decrease. A concave sequence lies above the linear interpolation between its endpoints, since the average of its first $l$ differences is at least the average of all $s$ differences. Consequently
\[
 b(k)\geq\frac{k_i}{s}b(k^{(i)})+\frac{k_j}{s}b(k^{(j)}),
\]
where $k^{(i)}$ is obtained from $k$ by putting all $s$ factors in the $i$th place and $k^{(j)}$ by putting them in the $j$th place.

We now prove $b(k)\geq\sum_i(k_i/n)\log\int_X\alpha_i^n$ by induction on the number of positive coordinates of $k$. If only one coordinate is positive, this is an equality. Otherwise choose $i\ne j$ with $k_i,k_j\geq1$. The vectors $k^{(i)}$ and $k^{(j)}$ have one positive coordinate fewer, and inserting the induction hypothesis for them into the last display gives the claim: for $m\ne i,j$ the coefficient of $\log\int_X\alpha_m^n$ is $(k_i/s+k_j/s)\,k_m/n=k_m/n$, and the coefficients of $\log\int_X\alpha_i^n$ and $\log\int_X\alpha_j^n$ are $(k_i/s)(s/n)=k_i/n$ and $k_j/n$. Exponentiating proves the corollary for K\"ahler classes.

For nef classes, $\alpha_i+\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ is K\"ahler for every $\varepsilon>0$ by \cref{prop:cohomology-cone-topology}, so the inequality holds for these classes. Each intersection number involved is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ and tends to the corresponding number for the $\alpha_i$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; in particular the limits $\int_X\alpha_i^n$ are nonnegative. Since $t\mapsto t^{k_i/n}$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, the inequality passes to the limit.
\end{proof}
\index{intersection number!log-concavity}\index{nef class!mixed intersection inequality}

The next proposition uses the cutoff-sum approximations $G_m$ of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}: for $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and integers $m\geq1$, $G_m$ has gentle analytic singularities (\cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}) of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$ and satisfies $\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-(c/m)\omega$, where $c>0$ (the constant $c'$ of that lemma) depends only on $(X,\omega)$ and not on $\theta$, $\varphi$ or $m$. In particular $G_m$ is smooth near every point where $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$ is the unit ideal.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-potential-nef}
A class that contains a closed positive current with locally bounded potentials is nef. A class that contains a K\"ahler current with locally bounded potentials is K\"ahler. Every big class contains a K\"ahler current with gentle analytic singularities.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the given current as $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. If $\varphi$ is bounded, then every $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$ is the unit ideal, so the approximations $G_m$ are smooth and satisfy $\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-(c/m)\omega$; hence the class is nef. If moreover $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq\delta\omega$, then $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta-\delta\omega)$, and the approximations for the background $\theta-\delta\omega$ satisfy $\theta+\ddc G_m\geq(\delta-c/m)\omega$; for large $m$ the smooth form $\theta+\ddc G_m$ is a K\"ahler form in the class. For the last assertion, let $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq\delta\omega$ be a K\"ahler current in a big class, with $\varphi$ not necessarily bounded. The same inequality shows that $\theta+\ddc G_m$ is a K\"ahler current for large $m$, and $G_m$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$ by \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:strict-minimal-current-kahler}
If a closed positive current with minimal singularities is a K\"ahler current, then its class is K\"ahler. Moreover, in a K\"ahler class every potential with minimal singularities is bounded.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write the current as $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq\delta\omega$, where $\varphi$ has minimal singularities, and normalize $\varphi\leq0$. Choose $C'\geq0$ with $-\theta\leq C'\omega$. For $0<t\leq\delta/(\delta+C')$ the current
\[
 (1-t)T+t\theta=\theta+\ddc\bigl((1-t)\varphi\bigr)\geq\bigl((1-t)\delta-tC'\bigr)\omega\geq0
\]
is positive, so $(1-t)\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. By minimality, $(1-t)\varphi\leq\varphi+C$ for a constant $C$, so $\varphi\geq-C/t$ at every point where $\varphi$ is finite, hence almost everywhere. Since $\varphi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, the lower bound holds everywhere, as noted at the beginning of \cref{sec:global-minimal-potentials}. Thus $\varphi$ is bounded, and \cref{prop:bounded-potential-nef} shows that the class is K\"ahler. For the second assertion, suppose that the class contains a K\"ahler form $\theta+\ddc u$. Then $u$ is smooth and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, so a potential $\varphi$ with minimal singularities satisfies $u\leq\varphi+C$; hence $\varphi$ is bounded below, and it is bounded above as every quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$.
\end{proof}

Recall from \cref{def:lelong-number} that the Lelong number $\nu(T,x)$ of a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ at $x\in X$ is the Lelong number at $x$ of any local plurisubharmonic potential of $T$. If $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\varphi+h$ is such a potential wherever $h$ is smooth with $\ddc h=\theta$, so $\nu(T,x)=\nu(\varphi,x)$ in the sense of \cref{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}; in particular, these numbers do not depend on the background $\theta$. For a prime divisor $D\subset X$, the \emph{generic Lelong number}\index{Lelong number!generic} of $T$ along $D$ (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}) is therefore
\[
 \nu(T,D)=\inf_{x\in D}\nu(T,x)=\inf_{x\in D}\nu(\varphi,x)=\nu(\varphi,D),
\]
and by that proposition the infimum is attained at every point of $D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D$. We write $[D]$ for the current of integration along $D$.

\begin{theorem}[Siu's divisorial decomposition]\label{thm:siu-divisorial-decomposition}
Let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. Then $\nu(T,D)>0$ for at most countably many prime divisors $D$, the series below converges weakly, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:siu-divisorial-decomposition}
 T=R+\sum_D\nu(T,D)[D],
\end{equation}
where $R$ is a closed positive current with $\nu(R,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. In particular $T-\nu(T,D)[D]\geq0$ for every $D$. The decomposition is unique: if $T=R'+\sum_Db_D[D]$ for a weakly convergent series with coefficients $b_D\geq0$, at most countably many of them nonzero, and a closed positive current $R'$ with $\nu(R',D)=0$ for every $D$, then $b_D=\nu(T,D)$ for every $D$ and $R'=R$.
\end{theorem}
\index{Siu theorem!divisorial decomposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: one divisor.} Let $D$ be a prime divisor and $c=\nu(T,D)$; we show that $T-c[D]\geq0$, where $[D]$ is the current of integration along $D$ (\cref{thm:analytic-order-zero-support}); its coefficient measures are given by integration over $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and give no mass to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$. Near a regular point of $D$ choose coordinates with $D=\{z_1=0\}$ on a coordinate polydisc $W$ with $|z_1|<1$, and a plurisubharmonic potential $u$ of $T$ on $W$. Since the Lelong numbers of $u$ at the points of $D\cap W$ are at least $c$, \cref{prop:generic-lelong-uniform-growth} gives $u\leq c\log|z_1|^2+C$ near the point. Off $D$ the function $u-c\log|z_1|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and it is bounded above near $D$; by \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension} it extends to a plurisubharmonic function $v$. Since $v=u-c\log|z_1|^2$ almost everywhere and $[D]=\ddc\log|z_1|^2$ near the point (\cref{thm:poincare-lelong}), we get $\ddc v=T-c[D]$ there. Hence $T-c[D]$ is positive on a neighborhood of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and it equals $T$ on $X\setminus D$; so it is positive on the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$. Now let $\phi$ be a strongly positive test form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. Since $T$ and $[D]$ have measure coefficients (\cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}), there is a real measure $m_\phi$ on $X$, a combination of the coefficient measures with the coefficients of $\phi$ as densities, such that $(T-c[D])(\chi\phi)=\int\chi\,\mathrm dm_\phi$ for test functions $\chi$. The restriction of $m_\phi$ to the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is a positive measure, because $(T-c[D])(\chi\phi)\geq0$ for test functions $\chi\geq0$ supported there; and its restriction to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is that of the positive measure $\chi\mapsto T(\chi\phi)$, because $[D]$ gives no mass to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$. Hence $(T-c[D])(\phi)=m_\phi(X)\geq0$, that is, $T-c[D]\geq0$.

\emph{Step 2: the series.} By \cref{thm:siu-lelong-level-sets}, each set $E_j=\{x:\nu(T,x)\geq1/j\}$ is an analytic subset of $X$ different from $X$. Being compact, $E_j$ has finitely many irreducible components $Z$ by \cref{cor:analytic-global-components}. We first show:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-divisor-component}
 \text{a prime divisor $D$ contained in an analytic subset $E\neq X$ is an irreducible component of $E$.}
\end{equation}
An analytic subset of the connected manifold $X$ with an interior point is all of $X$, since the set of its interior points is open and, by the identity theorem applied to local defining functions, closed. Hence $E$ has no interior points, so its germs, and their branches, have dimension at most $n-1$, and $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$ has dimension at most $n-2$ at every point (\cref{prop:analytic-dimension-drop,cor:analytic-strata-hausdorff}). At a point $y\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ the germ of $D$ has dimension $n-1$, so by monotonicity of dimension (see \cref{prop:analytic-dimension-drop} and the remarks following it) it is not contained in the germ of $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$; thus there are points $y'\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap E_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Near such a point $E$ is a complex submanifold of dimension at most $n-1$ containing the $(n-1)$-dimensional submanifold $D$, so $D$ and $E$ coincide near $y'$. Let $S$ be the connected component of $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$ containing $y'$. The analytic subset $D\cap S$ of the connected manifold $S$ has an interior point, so it is all of $S$, and $D$ contains the closure $Z$ of $S$, an irreducible component of $E$ (\cref{cor:analytic-global-components}). Since $D$ is compact and the components of $E$ form a locally finite family, $D$ meets only finitely many of them and is the union of its intersections with these. By induction on the number of sets, an irreducible set is not a finite union of analytic subsets different from itself; hence $D=D\cap Z'$ for a component $Z'$. Then $Z\subset D\subset Z'$, and since no component is contained in another, $D=Z$. This proves \eqref{eq:prime-divisor-component}. In particular two distinct prime divisors are not contained in one another, and every prime divisor $D$ with $\nu(T,D)\geq1/j$, being contained in $E_j$, is one of its finitely many components. Therefore at most countably many prime divisors have $\nu(T,D)>0$. Let $D_1,D_2,\ldots$ be an enumeration of them and put $S_N=\sum_{k\leq N}\nu(T,D_k)[D_k]$.

We claim that $T-S_N\geq0$. Indeed, suppose that $T-S_{N-1}\geq0$. At a generic point $x$ of $D_N$, outside the proper analytic subsets $D_N\cap D_k$, $k<N$, and outside the exceptional sets of \cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong} for $T$ and $T-S_{N-1}$ along $D_N$, the currents $[D_k]$, $k<N$, vanish near $x$, so $T-S_{N-1}=T$ near $x$, and $\nu(T-S_{N-1},D_N)=\nu(T-S_{N-1},x)=\nu(T,x)=\nu(T,D_N)$. Step 1 applied to $T-S_{N-1}$ gives $T-S_N\geq0$. Consequently the trace measures of the $S_N$ in every chart are bounded by that of $T$, and the masses $\int_XS_N\wedge\omega^{n-1}$ are bounded by $\int_XT\wedge\omega^{n-1}$. The sequence $(S_N)$ converges weakly. Indeed, every test form is a finite linear combination of test forms $g\gamma$, where $g\geq0$ is a test function supported in a chart and $\gamma$ is a strongly positive form with constant coefficients in that chart: use a partition of unity, the fact that strongly positive forms span the real forms (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}), and write a real test function $g$ as $(g+M\psi)-M\psi$ with $\psi\geq0$ a test function equal to one on $\Supp g$ and $M\geq\sup|g|$. For such $g\gamma$ the numbers $S_N(g\gamma)$ increase with $N$, since $S_{N+1}-S_N\geq0$, and they are bounded by $T(g\gamma)$; hence they converge, and so does $S_N(\phi)$ for every test form $\phi$. By the compactness assertion of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, a subsequence converges weakly to a closed positive current $S$, so $S_N(\phi)\to S(\phi)$ for every $\phi$. Finally $R=T-S$ is closed and positive, as a weak limit of the closed positive currents $T-S_N$.

\emph{Step 3: properties of $R$ and uniqueness.} Fix a prime divisor $D$ and write $T=R+\nu(T,D)[D]+S_D$, where $S_D$ is the sum of the terms of the series for the other divisors. Lelong numbers are additive and nonnegative (\cref{prop:lelong-calculus}), and $\nu([D],x)=\nu(\log|f|^2,x)=1$ at regular points $x$ of $D$, where $f$ is a local coordinate defining $D$. Hence, at a point of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ outside the countable union of proper analytic subsets where one of the currents $T$, $R$, $S_D$ has a nongeneric Lelong number,
\[
 \nu(T,D)=\nu(R,D)+\nu(T,D)+\nu(S_D,D)\geq\nu(R,D)+\nu(T,D).
\]
Hence $\nu(R,D)=0$. Now consider any decomposition $T=R'+\sum_Db_D[D]$ as in the statement, fix $D$, and let $S'_D$ be the sum of the terms for the other divisors. If $D'\neq D$ is another prime divisor, then $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is an analytic subset of the connected manifold $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (\cref{cor:analytic-global-components}) which is not all of it, since otherwise $D'\subset D$, and then $D'=D$ by \eqref{eq:prime-divisor-component}. Hence $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a null set for the surface measure of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (\cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}, in local coordinates of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$), and $[D']$ gives no mass to $D$. The coefficient measures of $S'_D$, a weakly convergent series of such currents with nonnegative coefficients, therefore give no mass to $D$. By Step 1, $S'_D-\nu(S'_D,D)[D]\geq0$; comparing the trace measures of both sides on $D$, where $[D]$ has positive trace mass, forces $\nu(S'_D,D)=0$. Additivity of Lelong numbers at generic points of $D$, applied to $T=R'+b_D[D]+S'_D$, gives $\nu(T,D)=0+b_D+0$. Thus $b_D=\nu(T,D)$ for every $D$, and $R'=R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:modified-nef-class}
A class $\alpha$ is \emph{modified nef}\index{cohomology!modified nef class} if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a closed current $T_\varepsilon\in\alpha$ with $T_\varepsilon\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ and $\nu(T_\varepsilon+\varepsilon\omega,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. We write $\mathcal{MN}$ for the cone of modified nef classes. A closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ with $\nu(T,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$ is called \emph{non-divisorial}\index{current!non-divisorial}.
\end{definition}
The set $\mathcal{MN}$ is a convex cone. Indeed, multiplying $T_\varepsilon$ by $t>0$ shows that $t\alpha$ is modified nef, with $tT_{\varepsilon/t}$ for the allowance $\varepsilon$. If $\alpha,\alpha'$ are modified nef, with currents $T_\varepsilon,T'_\varepsilon$, then $T_{\varepsilon/2}+T'_{\varepsilon/2}\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ lies in $\alpha+\alpha'$, and at a point of a prime divisor $D$ at which the Lelong numbers of the positive currents $T_{\varepsilon/2}+\varepsilon\omega/2$ and $T'_{\varepsilon/2}+\varepsilon\omega/2$ attain their generic values $0$ (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}), the additivity of Lelong numbers (\cref{prop:lelong-calculus}) shows that their sum has Lelong number $0$; hence $\nu(T_{\varepsilon/2}+T'_{\varepsilon/2}+\varepsilon\omega,D)=0$. The cone $\mathcal{MN}$ contains $\mathcal N$, since smooth forms have zero Lelong numbers, and it is contained in $\mathcal E$: if $\alpha$ is modified nef, then $\alpha+\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ contains the positive current $T_\varepsilon+\varepsilon\omega$, and $\mathcal E$ is closed. It is also closed. Indeed, let $\alpha_j\to\alpha$ with $\alpha_j\in\mathcal{MN}$, and let $\varepsilon>0$. By \eqref{eq:small-class-small-form} we may choose $j$ such that $\alpha-\alpha_j$ has a smooth representative $\eta$ with $-\varepsilon\omega/2\leq\eta\leq\varepsilon\omega/2$. If $T'\in\alpha_j$ satisfies $T'\geq-\varepsilon\omega/2$ and $\nu(T'+\varepsilon\omega/2,D)=0$ for all $D$, then $T'+\eta\in\alpha$ satisfies $T'+\eta\geq-\varepsilon\omega$, and $\nu(T'+\eta+\varepsilon\omega,D)=0$ for all $D$, since adding smooth forms does not change Lelong numbers. As before, the definition does not depend on $\omega$.

In the definition of modified nef classes we may take $T_\varepsilon$ with gentle analytic singularities whose pole set has codimension at least two. Indeed, write $T_{\varepsilon/2}=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega/2)$ and apply \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum} to $\varphi$ with the background $\theta+\varepsilon\omega/2$, choosing $m$ with $c/m<\varepsilon/2$. Then $\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-\varepsilon\omega$. At a point $x$ of a prime divisor $D$ where $\nu(\varphi,x)=\nu(\varphi,D)=0$, the ideal $\mathcal I(m\varphi)_x$ is the unit ideal by \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}. Hence the pole set of $G_m$, which is the zero set of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, contains no prime divisor; its irreducible components of codimension one would be prime divisors, so it has codimension at least two. Moreover $G_m$ is smooth near such a point $x$, so the Lelong numbers of $\theta+\ddc G_m+\varepsilon\omega$ vanish there, and $\nu(\theta+\ddc G_m+\varepsilon\omega,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. Thus $T_\varepsilon=\theta+\ddc G_m$ has the required properties.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:divisorial-logarithmic-residual}
Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $D_1,\ldots,D_N$ be distinct prime divisors and $a_1,\ldots,a_N>0$ real numbers, and put $D=\sum_ia_iD_i$. Suppose that $\varphi$ has logarithmic singularities along $D$ with bounded remainder: near each point, $\varphi=\sum_ia_i\log|s_i|^2+v$ with $v$ bounded, where $s_i$ is a local equation of $D_i$, as in the proof of \cref{thm:analytic-order-zero-support}, and $s_i=1$ if the point does not lie on $D_i$. Then $\{\theta\}-\{D\}$ is nef, where $\{D\}=\sum_ia_i\{[D_i]\}$. If $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ is a K\"ahler current, then $\{\theta\}-\{D\}$ is K\"ahler.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Near a point, let $h$ be smooth with $\ddc h=\theta$. Off the support of $D$ the function $v+h=(\varphi+h)-\sum_ia_i\log|s_i|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, since $\log|s_i|^2$ is pluriharmonic there, and it is bounded above; as in Step 1 of the proof of \cref{thm:siu-divisorial-decomposition}, it extends to a plurisubharmonic function $\widetilde v$, which is bounded and equal to $v+h$ almost everywhere. Since $\ddc\log|s_i|^2=[D_i]$ (\cref{thm:poincare-lelong}), the current $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ satisfies $T-[D]=\ddc\widetilde v\geq0$ near the point, where $[D]=\sum_ia_i[D_i]$. Thus $T-[D]$ is a positive current in $\{\theta\}-\{D\}$ with locally bounded potentials. If $T\geq\delta\omega$, apply the same argument to $T-\delta\omega$, whose potential has the same logarithmic singularities; then $T-[D]\geq\delta\omega$. In both cases \cref{prop:bounded-potential-nef} gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Minimal multiplicities and positivity loci}
\label{sec:non-nef-non-kahler}
Let $\alpha$ be a class in the pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$ and $\theta\in\alpha$ a smooth representative. For every $\varepsilon>0$ the class $\alpha+\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ is big, since it contains $T+\varepsilon\omega$ for every positive current $T\in\alpha$, and its potential $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}$ with minimal singularities measures the positivity of $\alpha$ that remains after the curvature is allowed to drop by $\varepsilon\omega$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:minimal-class-multiplicities}
For $\alpha\in\mathcal E$, $x\in X$ and a prime divisor $D\subset X$ put
\[
 \nu(\alpha,x)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\nu(V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},x),
 \qquad
 \nu(\alpha,D)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\nu(V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},D).
\]
These are the \emph{minimal multiplicities}\index{minimal multiplicity} of $\alpha$. The \emph{non-nef locus}\index{non-nef locus} of $\alpha$ is
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)=\{x\in X:\nu(\alpha,x)>0\}.
\]
If $\alpha$ is big, its \emph{non-K\"ahler locus}\index{non-K\"ahler locus} is
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\bigcap_T\{\varphi_T=-\infty\},
\]
where $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi_T$ ranges over the K\"ahler currents in $\alpha$ for which $\varphi_T$ has analytic singularities. For a class $\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ that is not big we put $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=X$, and for a class that is not pseudo-effective we put $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)=X$.
\end{definition}
If $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon'$, then $\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega)\subset\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon'\omega)$, so $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}\leq V_{\theta+\varepsilon'\omega}$. Hence the minimal potentials decrease as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, their Lelong numbers increase, and the suprema in the definition may be taken along $\varepsilon=1/j$. Replacing $\theta$ by another smooth representative $\theta+\ddc g$ changes $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}$ only by a bounded function, because $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}-g$ and $V_{\theta+\ddc g+\varepsilon\omega}$ both have minimal singularities in $\PSH(X,\theta+\ddc g+\varepsilon\omega)$; and replacing $\omega$ by another K\"ahler form $\omega'$ with $C^{-1}\omega\leq\omega'\leq C\omega$ places $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega'}$ between $V_{\theta+C^{-1}\varepsilon\omega}$ and $V_{\theta+C\varepsilon\omega}$. Thus the minimal multiplicities and the two loci depend only on $\alpha$. If $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ is a positive current in $\alpha$, then $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, so $\varphi\leq V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}+\sup_X\varphi$; hence
\[
 \nu(\alpha,x)\leq\nu(T,x),\qquad\nu(\alpha,D)\leq\nu(T,D),
\]
and in particular both multiplicities are finite. For each $j$, the Lelong number $\nu(V_{\theta+\omega/j},x)$ equals its generic value $\nu(V_{\theta+\omega/j},D)$ at every point $x\in D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D$ (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}). At points of $D$ outside the union of these countably many exceptional sets we therefore have $\nu(\alpha,x)=\nu(\alpha,D)$, while $\nu(\alpha,x)\geq\nu(\alpha,D)$ at every point of $D$. Consequently
\[
 \nu(\alpha,D)=\inf_{x\in D}\nu(\alpha,x),
\]
and the infimum is attained at every point of $D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-class-minimal-multiplicity}
Let $\alpha\in\mathcal E$. Then $\nu(\alpha,x)\leq\nu(V_\theta,x)$ and $\nu(\alpha,D)\leq\nu(V_\theta,D)$ for every $x\in X$ and every prime divisor $D$, with equality if $\alpha$ is big. Moreover, $\alpha$ is modified nef if and only if $\nu(\alpha,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The positive current $T_0=\theta+\ddc V_\theta\in\alpha$ gives the inequalities, by the bound preceding the proposition. Suppose that $\alpha$ is big, and choose $S\in\alpha$ with $S\geq\delta\omega$, $\delta>0$. For $\varepsilon>0$ put $T_\varepsilon=\theta+\ddc V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ and $t=\varepsilon/(\delta+\varepsilon)$. Then $(1-t)T_\varepsilon+tS\geq\bigl(-(1-t)\varepsilon+t\delta\bigr)\omega=0$ is a positive current in $\alpha$, whose potential $(1-t)V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}+t\varphi_S$, with $S=\theta+\ddc\varphi_S$, is more singular than $V_\theta$. Since Lelong numbers are additive and positively homogeneous (\cref{prop:lelong-calculus}) and do not decrease when a function is replaced by a more singular one, we obtain
\[
 \nu(V_\theta,x)\leq(1-t)\nu(V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},x)+t\nu(S,x)\leq(1-t)\nu(\alpha,x)+t\nu(S,x).
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ we have $t\to0$, and since $\nu(S,x)$ is finite we obtain $\nu(V_\theta,x)\leq\nu(\alpha,x)$. For a prime divisor $D$, evaluate the same inequality at a point $x\in D$ at which the Lelong numbers of $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}$ and of $S$ attain their generic values along $D$ (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}); it gives $\nu(V_\theta,D)\leq\nu(V_\theta,x)\leq(1-t)\nu(\alpha,D)+t\nu(S,D)$, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the equality for $D$.

If $\alpha$ is modified nef and $T_\varepsilon$ is as in \cref{def:modified-nef-class}, then $T_\varepsilon=\theta+\ddc\varphi_\varepsilon$ with $\varphi_\varepsilon\in\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$, so $\nu(V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},D)\leq\nu(\varphi_\varepsilon,D)=0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$; hence $\nu(\alpha,D)=0$. Conversely, if $\nu(\alpha,D)=0$ for every $D$, then the currents $\theta+\ddc V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}$ satisfy the conditions of \cref{def:modified-nef-class}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:minimal-multiplicity-class-continuity}
For a fixed point $x\in X$ or a fixed prime divisor $D$, the minimal multiplicity $\alpha\mapsto\nu(\alpha,x)$, respectively $\alpha\mapsto\nu(\alpha,D)$, is positively homogeneous, convex and lower semicontinuous on the pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$, and continuous on the big cone $\mathcal B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We treat $\nu(\cdot,x)$; the proof for $D$ is identical. For $t>0$ we have $\PSH(X,t\theta+\varepsilon\omega)=t\PSH(X,\theta+(\varepsilon/t)\omega)$, hence $V_{t\theta+\varepsilon\omega}=tV_{\theta+(\varepsilon/t)\omega}$, and taking suprema over $\varepsilon$ gives $\nu(t\alpha,x)=t\nu(\alpha,x)$. If $\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in\mathcal E$ have representatives $\theta_1,\theta_2$, the sum of the minimal currents with allowance $\varepsilon$ in the two classes is a current $\theta_1+\theta_2+\ddc(V_{\theta_1+\varepsilon\omega}+V_{\theta_2+\varepsilon\omega})\geq-2\varepsilon\omega$ in $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$. By minimality and additivity of Lelong numbers,
\[
 \nu(V_{\theta_1+\theta_2+2\varepsilon\omega},x)\leq\nu(V_{\theta_1+\varepsilon\omega},x)+\nu(V_{\theta_2+\varepsilon\omega},x)\leq\nu(\alpha_1,x)+\nu(\alpha_2,x),
\]
and taking the supremum over $\varepsilon$ gives subadditivity. Together with homogeneity this proves convexity.

Let $\alpha_j\to\alpha$ in $\mathcal E$, with representatives $\theta_j$, and fix $\varepsilon>0$. For large $j$, by \eqref{eq:small-class-small-form}, the class $\alpha-\alpha_j$ has a smooth representative $\eta_j$ with $-\varepsilon\omega\leq\eta_j\leq\varepsilon\omega$. The current $\theta_j+\ddc V_{\theta_j+\varepsilon\omega}+\eta_j\in\alpha$ is at least $-2\varepsilon\omega$, and its potential relative to $\theta$ differs from $V_{\theta_j+\varepsilon\omega}$ by a smooth function. By minimality,
\[
 \nu(V_{\theta+2\varepsilon\omega},x)\leq\nu(V_{\theta_j+\varepsilon\omega},x)\leq\nu(\alpha_j,x)
\]
for large $j$, so $\nu(V_{\theta+2\varepsilon\omega},x)\leq\liminf_j\nu(\alpha_j,x)$. Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon$ proves lower semicontinuity.

Finally, a convex function $f$ with finite values on an open convex subset $U$ of a finite-dimensional real vector space is continuous. Indeed, let $Q\subset U$ be a small closed cube; by convexity the function is bounded above on $Q$ by the maximum $M$ of its values at the vertices. If $c$ is the center of $Q$ and $c\pm v\in Q$, then $f(c+v)\geq2f(c)-f(c-v)\geq2f(c)-M$, so $f$ is also bounded below on $Q$. If $y,z$ lie in the concentric cube of half the size, extend the segment from $z$ to $y$ beyond $y$ to the point $w$ at distance $s$ from $y$, where $s$ is half the side length of the smaller cube, so that $w\in Q$; the slope inequality for convex functions gives $f(y)-f(z)\leq|y-z|(f(w)-f(y))/|w-y|\leq|y-z|(M-\inf_Qf)/s$, and by symmetry $f$ is Lipschitz on the smaller cube. Applied to the open convex cone $\mathcal B$, this proves continuity there.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positivity-loci}
Let $\alpha\in\mathcal E$. Then the non-nef locus $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)$ is a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $X$, and it is empty if and only if $\alpha$ is nef. If $\alpha$ is big, then $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$ is the pole set of the potential of a single K\"ahler current in $\alpha$ with analytic singularities; it is a proper analytic subset of $X$, it contains $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)$, and it is empty if and only if $\alpha$ is K\"ahler. Finally, for every class $\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:non-kahler-non-nef-allowance}
 E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=
       \bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The non-nef locus.} By the monotonicity in $\varepsilon$, $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)$ is the union over positive integers $j,k$ of the sets $\{x:\nu(V_{\theta+\omega/j},x)\geq1/k\}$. Each of them is analytic by \cref{thm:siu-lelong-level-sets}, and proper, because it is contained in the pole set of $V_{\theta+\omega/j}$. If $\alpha$ is nef, then $\alpha+\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ is K\"ahler for every $\varepsilon>0$, so $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}$ is bounded by \cref{cor:strict-minimal-current-kahler} and all these Lelong numbers vanish. Conversely, suppose that $\nu(V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},x)=0$ for all $x\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}, $\mathcal I(mV_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega})$ is the unit ideal at every point, so the approximations $G_m$ of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum} for the background $\theta+\varepsilon\omega$ are smooth and satisfy $\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-(\varepsilon+c/m)\omega$. Hence $\alpha$ is nef.

\emph{Realization of the non-K\"ahler locus.} Let $\alpha$ be big. K\"ahler currents with analytic singularities exist in $\alpha$ by \cref{prop:bounded-potential-nef}, and their pole sets are closed analytic subsets of $X$. Since $X$ has a countable basis, countably many of the open complements of these pole sets already have the same union as all of them: for every element of a countable basis that is contained in one of these complements, choose one such complement. The intersection of the corresponding countably many pole sets $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots$ is therefore $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. Let $\mathcal I_{Z_k}$ be the ideal sheaf of $Z_k$, which is coherent by \cref{thm:analytic-ideal-coherence}. The increasing sequence of coherent ideals $\mathcal I_{Z_1}+\cdots+\mathcal I_{Z_k}$, whose zero sets are $Z_1\cap\cdots\cap Z_k$, is stationary on the compact manifold $X$ by \cref{lem:strong-analytic-noetherian}. Hence $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=Z_1\cap\cdots\cap Z_N$ for some $N$. Let $T_k=\theta+\ddc\varphi_k$, $k\leq N$, be the corresponding K\"ahler currents, and choose $\delta>0$ with $T_k\geq\delta\omega$ for all $k\leq N$. Then $\varphi=\max_k\varphi_k$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta-\delta\omega)$, it has analytic singularities by \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations}, and its pole set is exactly $Z_1\cap\cdots\cap Z_N=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. Thus $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ is a K\"ahler current realizing $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$, which is a proper analytic subset because $\varphi$ is finite almost everywhere. If $\nu(\alpha,x)>0$, then $\nu(\varphi,x)\geq\nu(V_\theta,x)=\nu(\alpha,x)>0$ by minimality of $V_\theta$ and \cref{prop:big-class-minimal-multiplicity}, so $\varphi(x)=-\infty$; hence $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$.

If $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\varnothing$, the potential $\varphi$ just constructed has analytic singularities and no poles, so it is bounded, and \cref{prop:bounded-potential-nef} shows that $\alpha$ is K\"ahler. Conversely, if $\alpha$ contains a K\"ahler form $\theta+\ddc u$, then $u$ is smooth and has analytic singularities without poles, so $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\varnothing$.

\emph{The identity \eqref{eq:non-kahler-non-nef-allowance}.} Suppose first that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. Then $\alpha$ is big, and there is a K\"ahler current $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq\delta\omega$ with analytic singularities and $\varphi(x)>-\infty$; in particular $\varphi$ is bounded near $x$ and $\nu(\varphi,x)=0$. For $0<\varepsilon<\delta$, the current $\theta-\varepsilon\omega+\ddc\varphi$ is positive and lies in $\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\}$, which is therefore pseudo-effective, and the bound $\nu(\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\},x)\leq\nu(\varphi,x)=0$ shows that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\})$.

Conversely, suppose that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\})$ for some $\varepsilon>0$. By the convention in \cref{def:minimal-class-multiplicities}, the class $\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ is pseudo-effective. For $0<\eta<\varepsilon/2$ put $u=V_{\theta-(\varepsilon-\eta)\omega}$; then $\nu(u,x)\leq\nu(\alpha-\varepsilon\{\omega\},x)=0$. By \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}, $\mathcal I(mu)$ is the unit ideal near $x$ for every $m$, and the approximation $G_m$ of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum} for $u$ and the background $\theta-(\varepsilon-\eta)\omega$ is smooth near $x$ and satisfies
\[
 \theta+\ddc G_m\geq(\varepsilon-\eta-c/m)\omega.
\]
For large $m$ this is a K\"ahler current in $\alpha$ with analytic singularities whose potential is finite at $x$. Hence $\alpha$ is big and $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. This proves \eqref{eq:non-kahler-non-nef-allowance}; the argument also shows that $\alpha$ is big as soon as the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:non-kahler-non-nef-allowance} is not all of $X$, so the formula is consistent with the convention $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=X$ for classes that are not big.
\end{proof}

We now turn to modifications. Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism, where $Y$ is also a connected compact K\"ahler manifold, with K\"ahler form $\omega_Y$. Pullback of smooth forms induces a linear map $\pi^*\colon H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)\to H^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb R)$. Pushforward of currents, $(\pi_*S)(\chi)=S(\pi^*\chi)$, maps closed real $(1,1)$-currents on $Y$ to closed real $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, preserves positivity, and satisfies $\pi_*\ddc f=\ddc\pi_*f$ for distributions $f$; hence it induces a linear map $\pi_*\colon H^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb R)\to H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ with $\pi_*\{S\}=\{\pi_*S\}$. If $T=\theta+\ddc\varphi$ is a closed positive current on $X$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the pullbacks $(\varphi+h)\circ\pi$ of its local potentials, where $\ddc h=\theta$, are not identically $-\infty$, because $\varphi\circ\pi\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$ by \cref{prop:smooth-target-psh-modification}. Hence the pullback $\pi^*T$ of \cref{prop:potential-current-pullback} is defined, and\index{current!pullback by a modification}
\[
 \pi^*T=\pi^*\theta+\ddc(\varphi\circ\pi),\qquad\{\pi^*T\}=\pi^*\{T\}.
\]
A prime divisor $E\subset Y$ is \emph{exceptional}\index{exceptional divisor} for $\pi$ if $\pi(E)$ has codimension at least two in $X$. By \cref{prop:smooth-bimeromorphic-divisor-locus}, every other prime divisor of $Y$ is the strict transform of the prime divisor $\pi(E)$ of $X$, and $\pi$ is an isomorphism near its generic points.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positivity-under-modifications}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be as above. Then $\pi^*$ maps nef classes to nef classes, and $\pi_*$ maps modified nef classes to modified nef classes. The pushforward of a non-divisorial closed positive current on $Y$ is non-divisorial.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By compactness, $\pi^*\omega\leq C\omega_Y$ for a constant $C$. If $\theta_\varepsilon\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ are nef representatives of a class on $X$, then $\pi^*\theta_\varepsilon\geq-C\varepsilon\omega_Y$, so the pullback class is nef. Let $S$ be a non-divisorial closed positive current on $Y$ and $D\subset X$ a prime divisor, with strict transform $D'$. Near generic points of $D$ the map $\pi$ is a biholomorphism, so $\pi_*S$ corresponds there to $S$ near the generic points of $D'$, and $\nu(\pi_*S,D)=\nu(S,D')=0$. Hence $\pi_*S$ is non-divisorial.

Let $\beta$ be a modified nef class on $Y$ and $t>0$. The class $\beta+t\{\omega_Y\}$ is big, and it is modified nef because $\mathcal{MN}$ is a convex cone containing the K\"ahler classes. By \cref{prop:big-class-minimal-multiplicity}, a positive current with minimal singularities in this class is non-divisorial. Its pushforward is a non-divisorial positive current in $\pi_*\beta+t\pi_*\{\omega_Y\}$, so this class is modified nef: the definition is satisfied with $T_\varepsilon$ equal to this current for every $\varepsilon$. These classes tend to $\pi_*\beta$ as $t\downarrow0$, and $\mathcal{MN}$ is closed, so $\pi_*\beta$ is modified nef.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nondivisorial-pull-push-exceptional}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be as above, and let $S$ be a non-divisorial closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $Y$. Then
\[
 \pi^*\pi_*S=S+\sum_E c_E[E],\qquad c_E\geq0,
\]
where $E$ ranges over the finitely many exceptional prime divisors of $\pi$. For every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, $\pi_*\pi^*T=T$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, $\pi_*[E]=0$ if $E$ is exceptional, and $\pi_*[E]=[\pi(E)]$ otherwise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A\subset X$ be an analytic subset of codimension at least two outside which $\pi$ is an isomorphism. The positive current $\pi^*\pi_*S$, defined through local potentials as above, agrees with $S$ on $Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(A)$. Their difference is a closed real current with measure coefficients supported on the proper analytic subset $\pi^{-1}(A)$ of $Y$. By \cref{thm:analytic-order-zero-support}, it is a finite real linear combination of the currents of integration along the hypersurface components of $\pi^{-1}(A)$, which are exactly the exceptional prime divisors. Near a generic point of such a divisor $E$, the difference is $c_E[E]$, so the local potentials of $\pi^*\pi_*S$ and of $S$ differ by $c_E\log|s_E|^2$ plus a pluriharmonic function; hence $c_E=\nu(\pi^*\pi_*S,E)-\nu(S,E)=\nu(\pi^*\pi_*S,E)\geq0$, since $S$ is non-divisorial.

Similarly, $\pi_*\pi^*T-T$ is a closed current of order zero supported on $A$, which has codimension at least two, so it vanishes by \cref{thm:analytic-order-zero-support}. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the current $\pi_*[E]$ is positive, closed and supported on the analytic set $\pi(E)$. If $E$ is exceptional, $\pi(E)$ has codimension at least two and $\pi_*[E]=0$ by the same theorem. Otherwise $\pi$ maps $E$ isomorphically onto $\pi(E)$ near generic points, so $\pi_*[E]-[\pi(E)]$ is supported on a subset of $\pi(E)\cap A$, of codimension at least two in $X$, and vanishes as well.
\end{proof}
\index{current!non-divisorial pull-push}\index{divisor!pushforward}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-classes-under-modifications}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be as above. Then $\pi^*$ and $\pi_*$ map big classes to big classes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $C$ with $\pi^*\omega\leq C\omega_Y$. Since $\pi_*$ preserves positivity, \cref{lem:nondivisorial-pull-push-exceptional} gives $\pi_*\omega_Y\geq C^{-1}\pi_*\pi^*\omega=C^{-1}\omega$. Hence a current $S\geq\delta\omega_Y$ on $Y$ has $\pi_*S\geq\delta C^{-1}\omega$, which proves the assertion for $\pi_*$.

Let $\alpha$ be big on $X$ and $T\in\alpha$ with $T\geq\delta\omega$. Choose a smooth closed representative $\eta$ of the class $\{\pi_*\omega_Y\}$, a constant $B>0$ with $\eta\leq B\omega$, and $t>0$ with $tB\leq\delta$, so that $T-t\eta\geq0$. The positive current $T-t\eta+t\pi_*\omega_Y$ lies in $\alpha$. By \cref{lem:nondivisorial-pull-push-exceptional}, applied to the smooth, hence non-divisorial, current $\omega_Y$, its pullback is
\[
 \pi^*(T-t\eta)+t\omega_Y+t\sum_E c_E[E]\geq t\omega_Y.
\]
This is a K\"ahler current in $\pi^*\alpha$, so $\pi^*\alpha$ is big.
\end{proof}
\index{big class!bimeromorphic pullback and pushforward}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nef-boundary-minimal-current}
The zero class $\alpha=0$ is nef, so $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(0)=\varnothing$ by \cref{thm:positivity-loci}. It is not big: a K\"ahler current $T\geq\delta\omega$ would have $\int_XT\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq\delta\int_X\omega^n>0$, whereas this integral vanishes for every closed current in the zero class. Hence $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(0)=X$. More generally, $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=X$ for every class that is not big, while $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)=\varnothing$ for every nef class; on the boundary of the pseudo-effective cone the two loci therefore detect different failures of positivity. This is also why the minimal multiplicities are defined with the allowances $\varepsilon\omega$ rather than through $V_\theta$: for a class that is not big, \cref{prop:big-class-minimal-multiplicity} gives only the inequality $\nu(\alpha,x)\leq\nu(V_\theta,x)$, since the proof of equality uses a K\"ahler current in $\alpha$.
\end{example}

\section{Curvature and metrics defined by sections}
\label{sec:global-line-bundle-metrics}
Let $L\to X$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric $h_0$. Recall from \cref{def:quadratic-line-bundle-metric} that its curvature form, which we denote by $c_1(L,h_0)$, equals $-\ddc\log|e|_{h_0}^2$ for every local holomorphic frame $e$. Any other smooth metric is $h_1=h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$ with $f$ smooth and real, and then $c_1(L,h_1)=c_1(L,h_0)+\ddc f$. Hence the class $c_1(L)=\{c_1(L,h_0)\}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ does not depend on the metric. The curvature of a tensor product of metrics is the sum of the curvatures, and the curvature of a pullback metric is the pullback of the curvature, because the logarithms of the squared norms of local frames add, respectively compose.

A line bundle is \emph{positive}\index{line bundle!positive} if it admits a smooth metric with positive definite curvature form. It is \emph{very ample}\index{line bundle!very ample} if its full space of global holomorphic sections defines a holomorphic embedding into a projective space: this space is finite dimensional (see the proof of \cref{lem:compact-section-hilbert-space}), and for a basis $s_0,\ldots,s_N$ we require that the sections have no common zero and that $x\mapsto[s_0(x):\cdots:s_N(x)]$ is a holomorphic embedding of $X$ into $\mathbb P^N$. A compact complex manifold is \emph{projective}\index{projective manifold} if it admits a holomorphic embedding into a projective space.

\begin{definition}\label{def:psh-quadratic-line-metric}
A \emph{plurisubharmonic metric}\index{metric!plurisubharmonic} on $L$ is a quadratic metric of the form $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ with $\varphi\in\PSH(X,c_1(L,h_0))$. It assigns the value zero to the zero vector, also over the pole set of $\varphi$, and the value $+\infty$ to a nonzero vector over a pole of $\varphi$. Its \emph{Chern current}\index{curvature!Chern current} is
\[
 c_1(L,h)=c_1(L,h_0)+\ddc\varphi.
\]
\end{definition}
This definition uses the pointwise function $\varphi$, that is, the canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative; for an arbitrary measurable weight, the distributional curvature inequality would determine the metric only almost everywhere. If $h_0$ is replaced by $h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$, then $\varphi$ is replaced by $\varphi-f$, so the class of plurisubharmonic metrics and the pointwise metric $h$ do not depend on $h_0$. In particular, $L$ admits a plurisubharmonic metric if and only if $c_1(L)$ is pseudo-effective. The tensor product of two plurisubharmonic metrics is defined by adding their local weights; the norm of a nonzero vector is infinite over a pole of either factor. The pullback of a plurisubharmonic metric by a holomorphic map is a plurisubharmonic metric provided the pulled-back weights are not identically $-\infty$ on any connected component; the restriction to a submanifold is defined under the same condition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:line-bundle-poincare-lelong}
For a nonzero holomorphic section $s$ of $L$ and a smooth metric $h_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-bundle-poincare-lelong}
 c_1(L,h_0)+\ddc\log|s|_{h_0}^2=[Z(s)],
\end{equation}
where $[Z(s)]$ is the current of integration along the zero divisor of $s$, counted with multiplicities. In particular $[Z(s)]$ represents $c_1(L)$.
\end{proposition}
\index{Poincar\'e--Lelong formula!line bundles}
\begin{proof}
In a local frame write $s=fe$. Since $\log|s|_{h_0}^2=\log|f|^2+\log|e|_{h_0}^2$, the left side equals $\ddc\log|f|^2$, which is the current of integration along the zero divisor of $f$ by \cref{thm:poincare-lelong}. These local divisors agree on overlaps, because the transition functions have no zeros.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:projective-fubini-study-curvature}
Let $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^N}(1)$ be the dual of the tautological line bundle on $\mathbb P^N$, and give it the metric $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ dual to the Euclidean norm on the tautological line; this is the \emph{Fubini--Study metric}\index{Fubini--Study form}. On the chart $[1:z_1:\cdots:z_N]$ the tautological line is spanned by $(1,z)$, so the coordinate section $X_0$ has squared norm $|X_0(1,z)|^2/|(1,z)|^2=(1+|z|^2)^{-1}$, and the curvature is
\[
 \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2).
\]
The matrix of $\partial_j\partial_{\bar k}\log(1+|z|^2)$ is $\bigl((1+|z|^2)\delta_{jk}-\bar z_jz_k\bigr)/(1+|z|^2)^2$, which is positive definite by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $|\sum_j\bar z_j\xi_j|^2\leq|z|^2|\xi|^2$. On $\mathbb P^1$, integration in polar coordinates gives $\int_{\mathbb P^1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}(1+|z|^2)^{-2}\,\mathrm dV=1$. The function $\log|X_1|_{\mathrm{FS}}^2=\log\bigl(|z|^2/(1+|z|^2)\bigr)$, which vanishes at the point at infinity, is the weight of a singular plurisubharmonic metric whose Chern current is the Dirac current $[0]$ at the point $[1:0]$, by \eqref{eq:line-bundle-poincare-lelong}.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}\label{def:section-fubini-study-metrics}
A \emph{Fubini--Study metric}\index{metric!Fubini--Study} on $L$ is a metric $h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ with
\[
 \varphi=\frac1m\max_{1\leq i\leq N}
          \bigl(\log|s_i|_{h_0^m}^2+\lambda_i\bigr),
 \qquad m,N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1},\quad \lambda_i\in\mathbb Q,
\]
where $s_1,\ldots,s_N$ are holomorphic sections of $L^m$, not all zero. We denote the set of these metrics by $\FS(L)$. The set of \emph{generalized Fubini--Study metrics}\index{metric!generalized Fubini--Study} is the smallest set of plurisubharmonic metrics that contains $\FS(L)$ and is closed under minima of metrics and under convex combinations of weights with rational coefficients. A convex combination of weights corresponds to the weighted geometric mean of the quadratic metrics, with value zero on the zero vector and the value $+\infty$ on a nonzero vector over a pole of a factor with positive coefficient.
\end{definition}
The classical Fubini--Study metric of \cref{ex:projective-fubini-study-curvature}, whose weight is the logarithm of a sum of squares rather than a maximum, is not itself in $\FS(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^N}(1))$ when $N\geq1$: its curvature form $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is positive definite, whereas the curvature current of a metric in $\FS(L)$ vanishes on a nonempty open set: one of the finitely many closed sets on which a single term attains the maximum has interior points, and there, away from the zeros of the corresponding section $s_i$, the induced metric on $L^m$ gives $s_i$ the constant norm $\mathrm e^{-\lambda_i/2}$, so the curvature vanishes there by \cref{prop:line-bundle-poincare-lelong}. It differs only by a bounded factor from a metric in this set: with $h_0=h_{\mathrm{FS}}$, the coordinate sections $X_0,\ldots,X_N$, $m=1$ and $\lambda_i=0$ give the metric $h_{\mathrm{FS}}\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ with $\varphi=\max_i\log|X_i|_{\mathrm{FS}}^2$, and $1\leq\mathrm e^{-\varphi}\leq N+1$ because $|X_i|_{\mathrm{FS}}^2=|z_i|^2/\sum_j|z_j|^2$ in homogeneous coordinates.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fubini-study-finite-operations}
Fubini--Study metrics do not depend on the choice of $h_0$, they have analytic singularities, and $\FS(L)$ is closed under minima and rational convex combinations of weights. Consequently the generalized Fubini--Study metrics are exactly the metrics in $\FS(L)$. The set $\FS(L)$ is empty if no positive tensor power of $L$ has a nonzero holomorphic section, even when $L$ admits plurisubharmonic metrics.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a local frame $e$ write $s_i=f_ie^m$. The local weight $-\log|e|_h^2=\varphi-\log|e|_{h_0}^2$ of the metric equals $m^{-1}\max_i(\log|f_i|^2+\lambda_i)$, which does not involve $h_0$; it is plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty$, since $X$ is connected and some $s_i\ne0$. It differs by a bounded function from $m^{-1}\log\sum_i\mathrm e^{\lambda_i}|f_i|^2$, so it has analytic singularities with the rational coefficient $1/m$; the pole set is the common zero set of the $s_i$.

Consider two such metrics, given by sections $s_i$ of $L^m$ with constants $\lambda_i$ and sections $r_j$ of $L^l$ with constants $\mu_j$. Replacing $s_i$ by $s_i^{M/m}$ and $\lambda_i$ by $(M/m)\lambda_i$, and similarly for the $r_j$, where $M$ is a common multiple of $m$ and $l$, does not change the potentials, so we may assume that both are given in degree $M$. The maximum of the two potentials, which corresponds to the minimum of the metrics, is then given by the union of the two lists. For a rational $t=a/b\in(0,1)$, the combination $t\varphi+(1-t)\psi$ of the two maxima is the maximum over all pairs $(i,j)$ of the corresponding combinations, that is,
\[
 t\varphi+(1-t)\psi=\frac1{Mb}\max_{i,j}\Bigl(\log\bigl|s_i^ar_j^{b-a}\bigr|_{h_0^{Mb}}^2+a\lambda_i+(b-a)\mu_j\Bigr),
\]
where the sections $s_i^ar_j^{b-a}$ of $L^{Mb}$ are not all zero and the constants are rational. Thus $\FS(L)$ is closed under both operations, and by induction under every finite sequence of them, so the generated set is $\FS(L)$ itself. If no positive tensor power of $L$ has a nonzero section, no presentation as in \cref{def:section-fubini-study-metrics} exists.
\end{proof}

\section{Compact interpolation and global Bergman kernels}
\label{sec:compact-point-bergman}
The local extension theorems of \cref{chap:q04} produce holomorphic functions with prescribed values on pseudoconvex domains. On the compact manifold $X$ we obtain global holomorphic sections in the same way, by cutting off a local solution and correcting it with an $L^2$ estimate for $\bar\partial$ on line bundles; the curvature must dominate the cost of a logarithmic pole at the interpolation point. After the $L^2$ estimate and the interpolation theorem at a point, we describe the weighted spaces of global sections and their Bergman kernels. All norms use the volume $\mathrm dV=\omega^n/n!$. The \emph{canonical bundle}\index{canonical bundle} $K_X=\Lambda^{n,0}T^*X$ is the holomorphic line bundle of $(n,0)$-forms; we give it the metric induced by $\omega$, and its dual $K_X^{-1}$ the dual metric $h_{K^{-1},\omega}$. We write $H^0(X,L)$ for the vector space of global holomorphic sections of $L$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs}
Let $\psi$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ with a proper pole set $A=\{\psi=-\infty\}$, smooth on $X\setminus A$, and such that $\psi(y)\to-\infty$ as $y\to x$ for every $x\in A$. Then there are a K\"ahler form $\sigma\geq\omega/2$ on $X\setminus A$ and functions $\chi_R\in C^\infty_c(X\setminus A)$, $R\geq1$, with $0\leq\chi_R\leq1$, $\chi_R\to1$ pointwise as $R\to\infty$, and $|\mathrm d\chi_R|_\sigma\leq C/R$ for a constant $C$. This applies in particular when $\psi$ is a finite sum of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions with gentle analytic singularities and of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that are smooth outside a point $x_k$ and equal to $c_k\log|z-z(x_k)|^2$ plus a smooth function near $x_k$, in local coordinates, with $c_k>0$; then $A$ is the union of their pole sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $b\geq0$ with $\ddc\psi\geq-b\omega$, and subtract a constant so that $\psi<-2b-2$. Put $\rho=-\log(-\psi)$ on $X\setminus A$. The chain rule gives
\[
 \ddc\rho=-\frac1\psi\ddc\psi+
       \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi\psi^2}\partial\psi\wedge\bar\partial\psi.
\]
Since $0<-1/\psi<1/(2b+2)$, the first term is at least $-\omega/2$, and since $\partial\rho=-\partial\psi/\psi$, the form $\sigma=\omega+\ddc\rho$ satisfies
\[
 \sigma\geq\frac\omega2+\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\rho\wedge\bar\partial\rho.
\]
It is a K\"ahler form on $X\setminus A$. The rank-one form $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\rho\wedge\bar\partial\rho$ has, in a $\sigma$-unitary coframe, the single eigenvalue $|\partial\rho|_\sigma^2/(2\pi)$, so the last inequality gives $|\partial\rho|_\sigma^2\leq2\pi$; hence $|\mathrm d\rho|_\sigma$ is bounded by a numerical constant. The function $-\rho=\log(-\psi)$ tends to $+\infty$ at $A$, and its sublevel sets $\{-\rho\leq t\}=\{\psi\geq-\mathrm e^t\}$ are closed in $X$, because $\psi$ is upper semicontinuous, and disjoint from $A$; so they are compact subsets of $X\setminus A$. Choose a smooth function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$ equal to one on $(-\infty,1]$ and to zero on $[2,\infty)$, with $0\leq\chi\leq1$, and put $\chi_R=\chi(-\rho/R)$. Then $\chi_R$ has compact support in $X\setminus A$, equals one on $\{-\rho\leq R\}$, and $|\mathrm d\chi_R|_\sigma\leq\sup|\chi'|\,|\mathrm d\rho|_\sigma/R$. The same gradient bound shows that every path in $X\setminus A$ approaching $A$ has infinite $\sigma$-length, since $-\rho$ is unbounded along it; thus $\sigma$ is complete.

For the last assertion, note that a finite sum of such functions is quasi-plurisubharmonic and smooth outside the union of the pole sets, because a function with gentle analytic singularities is smooth outside its pole set; and it tends to $-\infty$ at each point of that union, since the other terms are bounded above.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $F\to M$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h$ such that $c_1(F,h)\geq a\omega$ for a constant $a>0$. Suppose that $M$ carries a K\"ahler form $\sigma\geq\omega/2$ and functions $\chi_R$ with the properties stated in \cref{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs}. Let $g$ be an $F$-valued $(n,1)$-form with $\bar\partial g=0$ in the sense of distributions and $\int_M|g|_{\omega,h}^2\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. Then there is an $F$-valued $(n,0)$-form $u$ with $\bar\partial u=g$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-bundle-l2-estimate}
 \int_M|u|_{\omega,h}^2\,\mathrm dV
       \leq\frac1{2\pi a}\int_M|g|_{\omega,h}^2\,\mathrm dV.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The Bochner--Kodaira identity.} In a holomorphic frame of $F$ with squared norm $\mathrm e^{-p}$, the Chern connection on $F$-valued forms has the components $D'=\partial-\partial p\wedge$ and $D''=\bar\partial$, and $D'D''+D''D'=\partial\bar\partial p\wedge$; note that $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial p=2\pi c_1(F,h)$. Write $\Lambda_\sigma$ for the adjoint of the Lefschetz operator of $\sigma$, and let $D'^*,D''^*$ be the formal adjoints for the metrics $\sigma,h$. Fix a point, choose holomorphic coordinates in which $\sigma=\mathrm i\sum_j\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ at the point and the first derivatives of the coefficients of $\sigma$ vanish there, as in the proof of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}, and a frame with $\partial p=0$ at the point. Let $\epsilon_k,\bar\epsilon_k$ denote exterior multiplication by $\mathrm dz_k,\mathrm d\bar z_k$, and $\iota_k,\bar\iota_k$ their pointwise adjoints, the contractions. At the point they satisfy $\iota_j\epsilon_k+\epsilon_k\iota_j=\delta_{jk}$ and $\bar\iota_j\bar\epsilon_k+\bar\epsilon_k\bar\iota_j=\delta_{jk}$, while operators of different types anticommute; moreover $\Lambda_\sigma=-\mathrm i\sum_j\bar\iota_j\iota_j$, as the adjoint of $\mathrm i\sum_j\epsilon_j\bar\epsilon_j$. Since the first derivatives of the metric and of $p$ vanish at the point, there
\[
 D'=\sum_k\epsilon_k\partial_k,\quad D''=\sum_k\bar\epsilon_k\partial_{\bar k},\quad
 D'{}^*=-\sum_k\iota_k\partial_{\bar k},\quad D''{}^*=-\sum_k\bar\iota_k\partial_k,
\]
where $\partial_k,\partial_{\bar k}$ act on the coefficients. The anticommutation rules give $\bar\iota_j\iota_j\epsilon_k-\epsilon_k\bar\iota_j\iota_j=\delta_{jk}\bar\iota_j$ and $\bar\iota_j\iota_j\bar\epsilon_k-\bar\epsilon_k\bar\iota_j\iota_j=-\delta_{jk}\iota_j$, hence $[\Lambda_\sigma,D']=-\mathrm i\sum_k\bar\iota_k\partial_k$ and $[\Lambda_\sigma,D'']=\mathrm i\sum_k\iota_k\partial_{\bar k}$ at the point. These are the K\"ahler identities\index{K\"ahler identities}
\[
 D''{}^*=-\mathrm i[\Lambda_\sigma,D'],\qquad
 D'{}^*=\mathrm i[\Lambda_\sigma,D''],
\]
which therefore hold everywhere, the point being arbitrary. Substituting them into $\Delta''=D''D''{}^*+D''{}^*D''$ and $\Delta'=D'D'{}^*+D'{}^*D'$, the mixed compositions $D''\Lambda_\sigma D'$ and $D'\Lambda_\sigma D''$ cancel in the difference, and the curvature identity gives the Bochner--Kodaira formula
\[
 \Delta''-\Delta'=\mathrm i\bigl[D'D''+D''D',\Lambda_\sigma\bigr]=[2\pi c_1(F,h),\Lambda_\sigma],
\]
where, for a real $(1,1)$-form $\gamma$, $[\gamma,\Lambda_\sigma]u=\gamma\wedge\Lambda_\sigma u-\Lambda_\sigma(\gamma\wedge u)$. Put $B_\sigma=[2\pi c_1(F,h),\Lambda_\sigma]$. For a compactly supported smooth $(n,1)$-form $v$, the form $D'v$ vanishes for degree reasons, and integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kahler-bundle-bochner}
 \|D''{}^*v\|_\sigma^2+\|D''v\|_\sigma^2
 =\|D'{}^*v\|_\sigma^2+
       \int_M\langle B_\sigma v,v\rangle_\sigma\,\mathrm dV_\sigma.
\end{equation}
If the curvature form is diagonalized in a $\sigma$-unitary coframe, $B_\sigma$ multiplies the coefficient of the $j$th $(0,1)$-covector of an $(n,1)$-form by the $j$th eigenvalue of $2\pi c_1(F,h)$ with respect to $\sigma$. In particular $B_\sigma$ is positive definite on $(n,1)$-forms.

\emph{Density.} Let $T=D''\colon L^2_{(n,0)}\to L^2_{(n,1)}$ and $S=D''\colon L^2_{(n,1)}\to L^2_{(n,2)}$ be the operators with maximal distributional domains, for the norms defined by $\sigma$ and $h$. They are closed and densely defined, and $ST=0$. We show that compactly supported smooth forms are dense in $\operatorname{Dom}T^*\cap\operatorname{Dom}S$ for the graph norm $\|v\|+\|T^*v\|+\|Sv\|$. First, multiplication by the cutoffs $\chi_R$ reduces the problem to forms with compact support: the product rules for $T^*(\chi_Rv)$ and $S(\chi_Rv)$ are proved from the definition of the adjoint as in \cref{lem:three-weight-density}, and the error terms have norm at most $C\|v\|/R$ because $|\mathrm d\chi_R|_\sigma\leq C/R$. Second, a compactly supported form is approximated by local convolutions, after a finite partition of unity. For a first-order operator $P=\sum_la_l(x)\partial_l+b(x)$ with smooth matrix coefficients and a mollifier $\rho_t$, the commutator $[P,\rho_t*\,\cdot\,]$ applied to $v$ equals
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_l\int(a_l(x)-a_l(y))\,\partial_l\rho_t(x-y)\,v(y)\,\mathrm dy\\
 &\qquad+\sum_l\int(\partial_l a_l)(y)\,\rho_t(x-y)\,v(y)\,\mathrm dy
   +\bigl(b\,(\rho_t*v)-\rho_t*(bv)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
On a fixed compact set, the mean-value bound $|a_l(x)-a_l(y)|\leq C|x-y|$ and the scaling of $\rho_t$ show that these operators are bounded on $L^2$ uniformly in $t$. For smooth $v$ the commutator tends to zero in $L^2$, and by the uniform bound and the density of smooth functions in $L^2$ it tends to zero for every $v\in L^2$. Applying this to $T^*$ and to $S$ simultaneously proves the density.

\emph{The estimate.} For $(n,1)$-forms $g$ and $v$, the pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the positive operator $B_\sigma$ gives $|\langle g,v\rangle_\sigma|^2\leq\langle B_\sigma^{-1}g,g\rangle_\sigma\langle B_\sigma v,v\rangle_\sigma$; integrating and using \eqref{eq:kahler-bundle-bochner},
\[
 \Bigl|\int_M\langle g,v\rangle_\sigma\,\mathrm dV_\sigma\Bigr|^2
 \leq\int_M\langle B_\sigma^{-1}g,g\rangle_\sigma\,\mathrm dV_\sigma\cdot\bigl(\|T^*v\|_\sigma^2+\|Sv\|_\sigma^2\bigr)
\]
for compactly supported smooth $v$. The first integral does not depend on $\sigma$. To see this, write an $(n,1)$-form locally as a fixed $(n,0)$-covector wedged with a $(0,1)$-form. The squared norm of the $(n,0)$-part times the volume form does not depend on the K\"ahler metric, while the inverse of $B_\sigma$ cancels the inverse metric in the norm of the $(0,1)$-part; the result is the squared norm of the $(0,1)$-part for the dual of the curvature form $2\pi c_1(F,h)$ itself. Since $2\pi c_1(F,h)\geq2\pi a\omega$, the first integral is at most the right side of \eqref{eq:compact-bundle-l2-estimate}. Moreover $g\in L^2_{(n,1)}$ for $\sigma$ and $h$, because the $(n,0)$-density is unchanged and $\sigma\geq\omega/2$ at most doubles the squared norm of a $(0,1)$-covector. By the density just proved, the inequality extends to all $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*\cap\operatorname{Dom}S$: along a graph-convergent sequence the left side converges, and the right side converges. Also $g\in\ker S$. \Cref{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability} therefore gives $u\in\operatorname{Dom}T$ with $Tu=g$ and $\|u\|_\sigma^2$ at most the first integral. Finally, the squared norm of an $(n,0)$-form times the volume form does not depend on the K\"ahler metric, which gives exactly \eqref{eq:compact-bundle-l2-estimate}.
\end{proof}
\index{Bochner--Kodaira identity!K\"ahler line bundle}\index{H\"ormander estimate!K\"ahler line bundle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:l2-holomorphic-analytic-removal}
Let $A$ be a nowhere dense analytic subset of a complex manifold $U$. A holomorphic function on $U\setminus A$ which, extended arbitrarily to $A$, belongs to $L^2_{\loc}(U)$ extends holomorphically to $U$. The same holds for sections of a holomorphic line bundle.
\end{lemma}
\index{removable singularity!square-integrable holomorphic functions}
\begin{proof}
The question is local. Near a point of $A$ choose a holomorphic function $b\not\equiv0$ vanishing on $A$, and coordinates centered at the point in which the lowest-order nonzero homogeneous term of $b$ does not vanish at any of the coordinate unit vectors. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem, on a sufficiently small polydisc every complex line parallel to a coordinate axis meets the zero set of $b$ in finitely many points. Let $f$ be the given function. By Fubini's theorem, $f$ is square integrable on almost every such line. On such a line, near each of the finitely many punctures, the Laurent expansion of $f$ has no terms of negative order: by orthogonality of the powers on circles, a term of order $-j$ with $j\geq1$ would make the integral of $|f|^2$ diverge like $\int_0^rt^{1-2j}\,\mathrm dt$. Hence the restriction of $f$ to almost every line extends holomorphically, and its $\bar\partial$-derivative along the line vanishes as a distribution. Integrating against compactly supported test functions and using Fubini's theorem, we find that every derivative $\partial f/\partial\bar z_k$ vanishes as a distribution on the polydisc. A distribution with $\bar\partial f=0$ satisfies $\Delta f=4\sum_k\partial_{z_k}\partial_{\bar z_k}f=0$, so it is smooth by Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}) and therefore holomorphic. This holomorphic function agrees with $f$ on the complement of $A$, and the local extensions agree on overlaps. For sections of a line bundle, apply the result in local holomorphic frames.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact-point-extension}
There are constants $N,C>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega)$, with the following property. Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h_0$ and a plurisubharmonic metric $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ such that $c_1(L,h)\geq N\omega$. Let $x\in X$ with $\varphi(x)>-\infty$ and $v\in L_x$. Then there is $s\in H^0(X,L)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-point-extension}
 s(x)=v,\qquad
 \int_X|s|_h^2\,\mathrm dV\leq C|v|_h^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v=0$, take $s=0$; assume $v\neq0$. We first fix the geometric data. Choose finitely many coordinate charts and, inside them, balls whose concentric balls of half the radius cover $X$; then there is $r_0>0$ such that for every $x\in X$ some chart contains the coordinate ball $B$ of radius $4r_0$ centered at $x$. Let $\lambda$ be a smooth cutoff on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ equal to one on the ball of radius $2r_0$ and zero outside the ball of radius $3r_0$, and put
\[
 \psi_x=\lambda(z-z(x))\,n\log\frac{|z-z(x)|^2}{16r_0^2}\ \text{ on }B,\qquad\psi_x=0\ \text{ outside }B,
\]
where $z$ are the chart coordinates. Then $\psi_x\leq0$, $\psi_x$ is plurisubharmonic where $\lambda(z-z(x))=1$, and on the annulus where $\lambda(z-z(x))$ is not constant the logarithm and its first two derivatives are bounded independently of $x$. Hence there is $b_0\geq0$, independent of $x$, with
\[
 \ddc\psi_x\geq-b_0\omega,
 \qquad \psi_x=n\log|z-z(x)|^2+O(1)\text{ near }x.
\]
Choose $b_1\geq0$ with $c_1(K_X^{-1},h_{K^{-1},\omega})\geq-b_1\omega$, and put $N=b_0+b_1+2$.

In a holomorphic frame of $L$ over $B$, the local weight of $h$ is plurisubharmonic, because $c_1(L,h)\geq0$. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied on the ball of radius $r_0$ around $x$ (whose diameter is fixed), there is a holomorphic section $f$ of $L$ over this ball with $f(x)=v$ and
\[
 \int_{|z-z(x)|<r_0}|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV\leq C_0|v|_h^2;
\]
here $C_0$ does not depend on $x$, because the volume densities are uniformly comparable on the finite cover. Let $\chi$ be a cutoff equal to one for $|z-z(x)|<r_0/2$ and zero for $|z-z(x)|>3r_0/4$, obtained by translating a fixed one. Put $G=\chi f$, extended by zero, and $g=\bar\partial G=f\,\bar\partial\chi$. The form $g$ is supported in the annulus $r_0/2\leq|z-z(x)|\leq3r_0/4$, on which $\psi_x$ and $\bar\partial\chi$ are bounded independently of $x$.

Apply \cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation} to $\varphi$ with the background $c_1(L,h_0)-N\omega$. It gives functions $\varphi_j\downarrow\varphi$ with gentle analytic singularities, analytic pole sets $A_j$, and $c_1(L,h_0)+\ddc\varphi_j\geq(N-\varepsilon_j)\omega$ with $\varepsilon_j\to0$. Since $\varphi_j(x)\geq\varphi(x)>-\infty$, no $A_j$ contains $x$. Put
\[
 F=L\otimes K_X^{-1},\qquad
 h_{F,j}=h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j-\psi_x}\otimes h_{K^{-1},\omega}.
\]
For $j$ so large that $\varepsilon_j\leq1$, this metric is smooth on $M_j=X\setminus(A_j\cup\{x\})$ and its curvature is at least $(N-\varepsilon_j-b_0-b_1)\omega\geq\omega$. By \cref{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs}, applied to $\varphi_j+\psi_x$, the manifold $M_j$ carries a K\"ahler form $\sigma\geq\omega/2$ and cutoffs as required in \cref{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}. The canonical identification of $L$-valued $(0,q)$-forms with $F$-valued $(n,q)$-forms preserves the pointwise norms for $\omega$, because the $(n,0)$-covector and its dual cancel. Under this identification $g$ is $\bar\partial$-closed, and since $\varphi_j\geq\varphi$,
\[
 \int_{M_j}|g|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j-\psi_x}\,\mathrm dV
 \leq C_1\int_{|z-z(x)|<r_0}|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV
 \leq C_1C_0|v|_h^2,
\]
with $C_1$ depending only on the fixed cutoffs and the bounds for $\psi_x$ on the annulus. \Cref{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}, with $a=1$, gives $u_j$ on $M_j$ with $\bar\partial u_j=g$ and
\[
 \int_{M_j}|u_j|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j-\psi_x}\,\mathrm dV\leq\frac{C_1C_0}{2\pi}|v|_h^2.
\]

The section $s_j=G-u_j$ is holomorphic on $M_j$. The weights $\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j-\psi_x}$ are bounded below by a positive constant, since $\varphi_j\leq\varphi_1$ is bounded above and $\psi_x\leq0$; hence $s_j$ is square integrable for $h_0$ near $A_j\cup\{x\}$, and \cref{lem:l2-holomorphic-analytic-removal} extends it to a global holomorphic section. Near $x$ we have $g=0$ and no point of $A_j$, so $u_j=G-s_j$ is holomorphic near $x$. Since $\mathrm e^{-\psi_x}$ is comparable to $|z-z(x)|^{-2n}$ near $x$, the finiteness of the weighted integral of $u_j$ forces $u_j(x)=0$, exactly as in \cref{prop:logarithmic-point-interpolation}. Hence $s_j(x)=G(x)=v$. Since $\psi_x\leq0$, dropping the factor $\mathrm e^{-\psi_x}$ only decreases the integrals, and with $|G|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j}\leq|f|_h^2$ on the ball we obtain
\[
 \int_X|s_j|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi_j}\,\mathrm dV
 \leq C_2|v|_h^2,
\]
where $C_2$ depends only on the fixed geometric cutoffs and on the constant of the local point estimate.

Since $\varphi_j\leq\varphi_1\leq\sup_X\varphi_1$, the sections $s_j$ are bounded in $L^2$ for $h_0$. The submean inequality for holomorphic functions and the Cauchy estimates on a finite cover by larger charts bound them and their derivatives uniformly, and the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem gives a subsequence converging uniformly to a global holomorphic section $s$ with $s(x)=v$. Since $\varphi_j\downarrow\varphi$, Fatou's lemma gives \eqref{eq:compact-point-extension} with $C=C_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact-section-hilbert-space}
Let $L$ and $T$ be holomorphic line bundles on $X$ with smooth quadratic metrics $h_0$ and $h_T$, and let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume form on $X$. For an integer $k\geq1$ and a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$, the space
\[
 \mathcal H_k=\left\{s\in H^0(X,L^k\otimes T):
       \int_X|s|_{h_0^k\otimes h_T}^2\mathrm e^{-k\varphi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty\right\}
\]
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space for the displayed squared norm $\|s\|_k^2$. If $\mathcal H_k\ne\{0\}$ and $s_1,\ldots,s_{d_k}$ is an orthonormal basis, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-bergman-potential}
 \varphi_k=\frac1k\log\sup_{\|s\|_k\leq1}|s|_{h_0^k\otimes h_T}^2
          =\frac1k\log\sum_{i=1}^{d_k}|s_i|_{h_0^k\otimes h_T}^2.
\end{equation}
Writing $\theta=c_1(L,h_0)$ and $\theta_T=c_1(T,h_T)$, the function $\varphi_k$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta+k^{-1}\theta_T)$ and has gentle analytic singularities with coefficient $1/k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The space $H^0(X,E)$ of holomorphic sections of a line bundle $E$ is finite dimensional. Indeed, fix a smooth metric on $E$ and consider the unit ball of $H^0(X,E)$ for the unweighted $L^2$ norm. On a finite collection of coordinate charts, with smaller charts still covering $X$, the submean inequality and the Cauchy estimates bound these sections and their first derivatives uniformly on the smaller charts. By the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem every sequence in the unit ball has a uniformly convergent subsequence, whose limit is holomorphic and has norm at most one. An infinite orthonormal sequence would contradict this compactness. Hence $H^0(X,L^k\otimes T)$ and its linear subspace $\mathcal H_k$ are finite dimensional. The weighted integral defines an inner product on $\mathcal H_k$: the function $\varphi$ is finite almost everywhere, so the weight is positive almost everywhere, and a section of norm zero vanishes almost everywhere and hence identically. A finite-dimensional inner product space is complete.

At a point $y$, with the coefficients $c_i$ of $s=\sum_ic_is_i$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|s(y)|^2\leq\sum_i|c_i|^2\sum_i|s_i(y)|^2$, with equality when the $c_i$ are proportional to the complex conjugates of the values of the $s_i$ at $y$ in a local frame. This proves the equality in \eqref{eq:compact-bergman-potential}. In local frames with $|e|_{h_0}^2=\mathrm e^{-p_0}$ and $|e_T|_{h_T}^2=\mathrm e^{-p_T}$, write $s_i=f_ie^k\otimes e_T$. Then
\[
 \varphi_k=\frac1k\log\sum_i|f_i|^2-p_0-\frac{p_T}k,
\]
and $\theta+k^{-1}\theta_T+\ddc\varphi_k=k^{-1}\ddc\log\sum_i|f_i|^2\geq0$ by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}. Hence $\varphi_k\in\PSH(X,\theta+k^{-1}\theta_T)$, with analytic singularities of type $(1/k,\mathfrak b)$, where $\mathfrak b$ is the coherent ideal generated locally by the $f_i$. Moreover $\mathrm e^{k\varphi_k}=\sum_i|s_i|^2$ is smooth on $X$. On every log resolution $\pi$ of $\mathfrak b$, with $\pi^*\mathfrak b=\mathcal O(-D)$, the pulled-back $f_i$ are a local equation of $D$ times a tuple without common zeros, whose logarithmic squared norm is smooth. These are the conditions of \cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:projective-line-bergman-kernel}
On $\mathbb P^1$ take $L=\mathcal O(1)$ with its Fubini--Study metric, $T$ trivial, $\varphi=0$ and $\mathrm dV=\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$. A section of $L^k$ is $f(z)X_0^k$ on the chart $[1:z]$ with $f$ entire, and in the chart at infinity, with $\zeta=1/z$, it equals $\zeta^kf(1/\zeta)X_1^k$; holomorphicity at $\zeta=0$ and Cauchy's estimates show that $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$. Integration over circles makes the monomials orthogonal, and with $t=|z|^2$,
\[
 \|z^j\|_k^2=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^j}{(1+t)^{k+2}}\,\mathrm dt
       =\frac{j!(k-j)!}{(k+1)!},\qquad 0\leq j\leq k;
\]
the last equality follows from the substitution $u=t/(1+t)$ and repeated integration by parts. Thus the sections $\sqrt{(k+1)\binom{k}{j}}X_0^{k-j}X_1^j$, $0\leq j\leq k$, form an orthonormal basis, and the sum of their squared norms is
\[
 \frac{k+1}{(1+|z|^2)^k}\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}|z|^{2j}=k+1.
\]
Consequently $\varphi_k=k^{-1}\log(k+1)$, which tends to zero uniformly. The calculation is consistent with the normalizations: integrating the constant $k+1$ against $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, whose total mass is one, gives $\dim\mathcal H_k=k+1$.
\end{example}

\section{Extension from a compact submanifold}
\label{sec:compact-submanifold-extension}
In \cref{thm:compact-point-extension} the error $\bar\partial(\chi f)$ of the cutoff is supported away from the interpolation point. To extend a section from a submanifold $Y$ of positive dimension, we must glue local extensions, and the gluing error then meets $Y$ itself. We control it in two steps. First, local extensions with a logarithmic gain (\cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}) make the gluing error integrable against the critical weight $R_Y^{-q}$, where $q$ is the codimension of $Y$ and $R_Y$ is a smooth function vanishing to second order exactly along $Y$. Second, for weights with gentle analytic singularities, a computation on a resolution identifies the limit of the cutoff error with the norm of the prescribed section on $Y$ (\cref{lem:gentle-weighted-normal-residue}). This yields an estimate whose constant depends only on the geometry, and decreasing approximation then gives the result for arbitrary singular metrics.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$ carrying a K\"ahler form $\sigma\geq\omega/2$ and functions $\chi_R$ with the properties stated in \cref{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs}. Let $F\to M$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h$, and let $a,b$ be smooth functions on $M$ that are bounded above and below by positive constants. Suppose that the real $(1,1)$-form
\[
 H=a\,2\pi c_1(F,h)-\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial a
             -\frac{\mathrm i}{b}\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a
\]
is positive definite, and put $B_\omega=[H,\Lambda_\omega]$ on $(n,1)$-forms. Let $g$ be an $F$-valued $(n,1)$-form with $\bar\partial g=0$ in the sense of distributions, $g\in L^2_{(n,1)}(\omega,h)$, and
\[
 M_g=\int_M\langle B_\omega^{-1}g,g\rangle_{\omega,h}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\]
Then there is an $F$-valued $(n,0)$-form $u$ with $\bar\partial u=g$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-kahler-correction}
 \int_M\frac{|u|_{\omega,h}^2}{a+b}\,\mathrm dV\leq M_g.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Bochner--Kodaira identity \eqref{eq:kahler-bundle-bochner} was derived for an arbitrary smooth metric, before any positivity was used; we apply it to the metric $ah$. Let $\delta_h=D''{}^*$ be the formal adjoint for the metric $h$ and the K\"ahler form $\sigma$, and $C_a=(\bar\partial a\wedge)^*$ the contraction with the dual of $\bar\partial a$. In a frame, the weight of $ah$ is $p-\log a$, so
\[
 \delta_{ah}=\delta_h-a^{-1}C_a,\qquad
 a\,2\pi c_1(F,ah)=a\,2\pi c_1(F,h)
            -\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial a
            +\frac{\mathrm i}{a}\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a.
\]
On $(n,1)$-forms, the commutator with the last term is $[\frac{\mathrm i}{a}\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a,\Lambda_\sigma]=a^{-1}C_a^*C_a$. Writing \eqref{eq:kahler-bundle-bochner} for $ah$ in terms of $h$, expanding $a|\delta_hv-a^{-1}C_av|^2$, cancelling the term $a^{-1}|C_av|^2$ that appears on both sides, and dropping the nonnegative term $\|D'{}^*v\|^2$, we obtain for compactly supported smooth $v$
\[
 \int_M a\bigl(|\delta_hv|^2+|D''v|^2\bigr)
 \geq\int_M\langle[a\,2\pi c_1(F,h)
                      -\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial a,\Lambda_\sigma]v,v\rangle
            +2\Real\int_M\langle\delta_hv,C_av\rangle.
\]
All three integrals are taken with $h$, $\sigma$ and $\mathrm dV_\sigma$. The inequality $2\Real\langle A,B\rangle\geq-b|A|^2-b^{-1}|B|^2$ and the identity $b^{-1}|C_av|^2=\langle[\frac{\mathrm i}{b}\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a,\Lambda_\sigma]v,v\rangle$ consequently give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-kahler-bochner}
 \int_M\langle[H,\Lambda_\sigma]v,v\rangle
       \leq\int_M(a+b)|\delta_hv|^2+a|D''v|^2.
\end{equation}

Consider the Hilbert spaces of $(n,0)$-, $(n,1)$- and $(n,2)$-forms with the respective weights $h/(a+b)$, $h$ and $ah$, and the operators $T=D''$ and $S=D''$ with maximal domains between them. Testing against compactly supported forms shows that $T^*=(a+b)\delta_h$, so $\|T^*v\|^2$ is the first term on the right of \eqref{eq:twisted-kahler-bochner}, and $\|Sv\|^2$ is the second. Compactly supported smooth forms are dense in $\operatorname{Dom}T^*\cap\operatorname{Dom}S$ for the graph norm: the cutoffs $\chi_R$ give compact supports, with error norms of order $C/R$ because $a$ and $b$ are bounded above and below, and the local smoothing argument is the one in the proof of \cref{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}. As there, the pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for $[H,\Lambda_\sigma]$ and \cref{thm:hilbert-complex-solvability} give a solution of $\bar\partial u=g$ whose norm in the first space is bounded by $\int\langle[H,\Lambda_\sigma]^{-1}g,g\rangle\,\mathrm dV_\sigma$. This integral, and the squared norm of $(n,0)$-forms times the volume form, do not depend on the K\"ahler metric, by the computation in the proof of \cref{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}; hence the bound is $M_g$, and we obtain \eqref{eq:twisted-kahler-correction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}
Let $m\geq0$ and $q\geq1$ be integers, and let $D=B'\times B''\Subset\mathbb C^{m}\times\mathbb C^q$ be a product of balls centered at the origin, of radii $r'$ and $r''$, with $r''<\mathrm e^{-2}$; when $m=0$, $B'=\{0\}$. We write points of $D$ as $(z',w)$. Let $p$ be plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline D$, with $p(z',0)$ not identically $-\infty$, and let $f$ be holomorphic near $\overline{B'}$ with $\int_{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{B'}<\infty$. Then there is a holomorphic function $F$ on $D$ with $F(z',0)=f(z')$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-critical-gain-extension}
 \int_D\frac{|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}}{|w|^{2q}(-\log|w|^2)^2}\,\mathrm dV
       \leq C\int_{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{B'},
\end{equation}
where both volumes are Euclidean and $C$ depends only on $m$, $q$, $r'$ and $r''$. When $m=0$, the right side is $C|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(0)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $f=0$, take $F=0$. Put $n=m+q$, let $\beta$ be the Euclidean K\"ahler form on $\mathbb C^n$, and assume first that $p$ is smooth near $\overline D$. Set $R=|w|^2$, $\psi=\log R$ and $\psi_\varepsilon=\log(\varepsilon+R)$ for small $\varepsilon>0$. Replacing $p$ by $P=p+\pi|z|^2$, where $z=(z',w)$, we may assume that $\ddc P\geq\beta$; this changes both sides of \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension} by factors bounded in terms of $r'$ and $r''$ only.

\emph{The twisted form.} On $D\setminus\{w=0\}$ consider the trivial line bundle with the metric $\mathrm e^{-P-q\psi}$, and put
\[
 a_\varepsilon=\mu(\psi_\varepsilon),\quad\mu(t)=\varepsilon-t+\log(1-t),\qquad b_\varepsilon=(2-\psi_\varepsilon)^2.
\]
Since $R<\mathrm e^{-4}$ on $D$, we have $\psi_\varepsilon<\log(\varepsilon+\mathrm e^{-4})<-3$ for small $\varepsilon$, so $a_\varepsilon\geq-\psi_\varepsilon\geq1$; and for fixed $\varepsilon$ both functions are bounded above and below by positive constants, since $\psi_\varepsilon\geq\log\varepsilon$. We have $\mu'(t)=-1-(1-t)^{-1}$ and $\mu''(t)=-(1-t)^{-2}$, hence $b_\varepsilon=-\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)^2/\mu''(\psi_\varepsilon)$ and $1\leq-\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)\leq2$. With these choices the gradient terms cancel in the form $H$ of \cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate}: since $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial a_\varepsilon=\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon+\mu''(\psi_\varepsilon)\mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon$ and $b_\varepsilon^{-1}\mathrm i\partial a_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial a_\varepsilon=-\mu''(\psi_\varepsilon)\mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon$, we get
\[
 H_\varepsilon=a_\varepsilon\bigl(\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial P
                         +q\,\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi\bigr)
                -\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)\,
                         \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon.
\]
Both logarithmic Hessians are positive, and a direct computation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-normal-hessian}
 \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon
   =\frac{R}{\varepsilon+R}\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi
       +\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\,
                    \mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Since $a_\varepsilon\geq1$, $-\mu'\geq1$ and $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial P=2\pi\ddc P\geq2\pi\beta$, it follows that $H_\varepsilon\geq2\pi\beta+(\varepsilon/R)\,\mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon$.

\emph{Complete cutoffs.} The manifold $D\setminus\{w=0\}$ carries a K\"ahler form and cutoffs as required in \cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate}. Indeed, put $\tau_0=-\log(r'^2-|z'|^2)-\log(r''^2-|w|^2)$, omitting the first term if $m=0$, and $\tau_1=-\log(-\psi)$. Both are plurisubharmonic, so $\sigma=\beta+\ddc\tau_0+\ddc\tau_1\geq\beta$ is a K\"ahler form. Each of the two terms $\tau$ of $\tau_0$ is of the form $-\log g$ with $-\ddc g\geq0$, so $\ddc\tau\geq\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\tau\wedge\bar\partial\tau$; and, as in the proof of \cref{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs}, $\ddc\tau_1\geq\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\tau_1\wedge\bar\partial\tau_1$. Hence these functions have bounded gradients for $\sigma$. The function $\tau_0-\tau_1$ tends to $+\infty$ at the boundary of $D$ and along $\{w=0\}$, and its sublevel sets are compact in $D\setminus\{w=0\}$. If $\lambda\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$ equals one on $(-\infty,1]$ and zero on $[2,\infty)$, the functions $\chi_j=\lambda((\tau_0-\tau_1)/j)$ are compactly supported, tend to one, and have gradients $O(1/j)$. The canonical bundle of $\mathbb C^n$ is trivialized by $\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n$, whose norm is constant, so we identify functions with $(n,0)$-forms, as in the proof of \cref{thm:compact-point-extension}; this multiplies all the pointwise norms below by the same constant factor.

\emph{The correction.} Let $G(z',w)=f(z')$. Choose $\rho\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $0\leq\rho\leq1$, equal to one on $(-\infty,1/2]$ and to zero on $[1,\infty)$, and put $G_\varepsilon=\rho(R/\varepsilon)G$. Since $\bar\partial R/\varepsilon=(1+R/\varepsilon)\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon$,
\[
 g_\varepsilon=\bar\partial G_\varepsilon=\rho'(R/\varepsilon)(1+R/\varepsilon)
                         \bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon\,G,
\]
which is supported in $\{\varepsilon/2\leq R\leq\varepsilon\}$. For an $(n,1)$-form $v$, the rank-one part of the lower bound for $H_\varepsilon$ gives
$|\langle\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge G,v\rangle|^2
 \leq(R/\varepsilon)|G|^2\langle[H_\varepsilon,\Lambda]v,v\rangle$. Hence, on the support of $g_\varepsilon$, where $R/\varepsilon\leq1$, the pointwise dual norm of $g_\varepsilon$ is at most $C|G|^2$, and its dual energy is at most
\[
 C\int_{\{\varepsilon/2\leq R\leq\varepsilon\}}
                          |G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
In polar coordinates in $w$, the integral of $R^{-q}$ over the shell $\varepsilon/2\leq|w|^2\leq\varepsilon$ is a fixed multiple of $\int_{\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}}^{\sqrt\varepsilon}\mathrm dt/t=\frac12\log2$. Since $P$ is uniformly continuous on $\overline D$ and $G$ does not depend on $w$, the last integral tends to a fixed multiple of $\int_{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-P(z',0)}\,\mathrm dV_{B'}$ as $\varepsilon\to0$. \Cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate} gives $u_\varepsilon$ on $D\setminus\{w=0\}$ with $\bar\partial u_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon$ and
\[
 \int_{D}\frac{|u_\varepsilon|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}}{a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon}\,\mathrm dV\leq C\int_{\{\varepsilon/2\leq R\leq\varepsilon\}}
                          |G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}\,\mathrm dV.
\]
For fixed $\varepsilon$ the denominator is bounded and $\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}$ is bounded below, so $u_\varepsilon$ is square integrable, and $F_\varepsilon=G_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$, which is holomorphic off $\{w=0\}$, extends holomorphically to $D$ by \cref{lem:l2-holomorphic-analytic-removal}. The difference $u_\varepsilon=G_\varepsilon-F_\varepsilon$ is then smooth near $\{w=0\}$, and it vanishes there: a nonzero value at a point $(z'_0,0)$ would make $\int|u_\varepsilon|^2|w|^{-2q}$ diverge near that point, since $\int|w|^{-2q}\,\mathrm dV(w)$ diverges logarithmically at $w=0$ in $\mathbb C^q$. Hence $F_\varepsilon(z',0)=G_\varepsilon(z',0)=f(z')$.

\emph{The limit $\varepsilon\to0$.} There is a numerical constant with $a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon\leq C(-\log(\varepsilon+R))^2$, so the correction estimate bounds $u_\varepsilon$ in the weaker weight $(\varepsilon+R)^{-q}(-\log(\varepsilon+R))^{-2}\mathrm e^{-P}$. In this weight the squared norm of $G_\varepsilon$ is at most $C_P(-\log\varepsilon)^{-2}$, because its support has normal volume $O(\varepsilon^q)$ and $(\varepsilon+R)^{-q}\leq\varepsilon^{-q}$ there; the constant $C_P$ is finite for the fixed smooth weight $P$. Consequently the squared norms of $F_\varepsilon$ in this weight have $\limsup$ at most a constant, depending only on $m,q,r',r''$, times $\int_{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-P(z',0)}$. Since $t^{-q}(-\log t)^{-2}$ is bounded below on $(0,\mathrm e^{-3}]$, the $F_\varepsilon$ are bounded in $L^2(D)$, and a subsequence converges locally uniformly to a holomorphic $F$ with $F(z',0)=f(z')$. By Fatou's lemma, $F$ satisfies \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension} with $P$ in place of $p$, and removing the bounded factor $\mathrm e^{-\pi|z|^2}$ gives \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension} for smooth $p$.

\emph{General $p$.} Take smooth plurisubharmonic functions $p_l\downarrow p$ on a neighborhood of $\overline D$, obtained by convolution. Their restrictions $p_l(z',0)$ decrease to $p(z',0)$, so the right sides of \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension} for $p_l$ are at most the right side for $p$, and the smooth case gives extensions $F_l$ satisfying \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension} for $p_l$ with the same constant. Since $p_l\leq p_1$, the weights have a common positive lower bound, so the $F_l$ are bounded in $L^2(D)$ and a subsequence converges locally uniformly on $D$, including on $\{w=0\}$. The limit $F$ satisfies $F(z',0)=f(z')$, and Fatou's lemma gives \eqref{eq:local-critical-gain-extension}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:global-normal-pole}
Let $Y\subset X$ be a nonempty connected closed complex submanifold of codimension $q\geq1$. There is a smooth function $R_Y\geq0$ on $X$, vanishing exactly on $Y$, whose Hessian at each point of $Y$ is positive definite in the normal directions, and a constant $b\geq0$ depending only on $X$, $\omega$ and $R_Y$, such that
\[
 \ddc\log R_Y\geq-b\omega,\qquad
 \ddc\log(R_Y+\varepsilon)\geq-b\omega\quad(\varepsilon>0).
\]
The function $\log R_Y$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(1,\mathcal I_Y)$, where $\mathcal I_Y$ is the ideal sheaf of $Y$. Multiplying $R_Y$ by a small positive constant, we may assume that $\max_XR_Y<\mathrm e^{-4}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cover a neighborhood of $Y$ by finitely many coordinate charts with coordinates $(z',w)$, $w\in\mathbb C^q$, in which $Y=\{w=0\}$, and add one open set whose closure does not meet $Y$. Choose smooth functions $\chi_i\geq0$ with compact supports in these sets and $\sum_i\chi_i^2=1$ on $X$; for instance, divide a finite family of bump functions by the square root of the sum of their squares. On a normal chart put $K_i=\sum_{l=1}^q|w_l|^2$, on the additional set put $K_0=1$, and define $R_Y=\sum_i\chi_i^2K_i$, each term being extended by zero outside its chart.

The functions $K_i$ are uniformly comparable on the overlaps where they occur. Near $Y$, two tuples of normal coordinates generate the same ideal $\mathcal I_Y$, and Taylor's formula in the normal variables expresses each tuple as a matrix of holomorphic functions times the other, with coefficients bounded on slightly smaller charts. Away from $Y$, the positive functions $K_i$ are bounded above and below on the compact supports involved, and the additional set meets the normal charts only at a positive distance from $Y$. Hence there is one constant $C\geq1$ with $K_j\leq CK_i$ on $\Supp\chi_i\cap\Supp\chi_j$ for all $i,j$. At every point some $\chi_i^2\geq1/J$, where $J$ is the number of charts, so $K_j/R_Y\leq JC$ wherever $K_j$ contributes and $R_Y>0$, and likewise $(K_j+\varepsilon)/(R_Y+\varepsilon)\leq JC$.

Apply the identity \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian} from the proof of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}, with $K_j+\varepsilon$ in place of the functions denoted there by $\mathrm e^{w_j}$, so that $S=R_Y+\varepsilon$. The term with pairwise differences is semipositive, and so is the term with the Hessians $\ddc\log(K_j+\varepsilon)$, since $\log(\sum_l|w_l|^2+\varepsilon)$ is plurisubharmonic. The remaining term is
\[
 2\sum_j\frac{K_j+\varepsilon}{R_Y+\varepsilon}
       \left(\chi_j\ddc\chi_j-
                   \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\right),
\]
which is bounded below by $-b\omega$ because the cutoffs have bounded first and second derivatives and the ratios are bounded by $JC$. This proves the second inequality, and the first follows as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, since $\log(R_Y+\varepsilon)$ decreases to $\log R_Y$. At a point of $Y$ the second-order Taylor term of $R_Y$ is $\sum_i\chi_i^2$ times the squared norms of the linear parts of the normal coordinate tuples, each of which is positive definite on the normal space; hence it is positive definite, uniformly along the compact manifold $Y$. Finally, on the blowup of $Y$ (or on any log resolution of $\mathcal I_Y$), every normal tuple is a local equation of the exceptional divisor times a tuple without common zeros, because the tuples generate $\mathcal I_Y$; so $\log R_Y$ is the logarithm of the squared modulus of that equation plus a smooth function. Since moreover $\exp(\log R_Y)=R_Y$ is smooth on $X$, and the resolution is K\"ahler by \cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}, $\log R_Y$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(1,\mathcal I_Y)$. Multiplying $R_Y$ by a constant $\lambda>0$ adds $\log\lambda$ to $\log R_Y$ and replaces $\log(R_Y+\varepsilon)$ by $\log\lambda+\log(R_Y+\varepsilon/\lambda)$, so the inequalities persist.
\end{proof}

In the next lemma we use log discrepancies (\cref{def:divisorial-order-discrepancy}). Let $Z$ be a complex manifold and $E\subset Z$ a smooth divisor. We consider finite sequences of blowups $Z_k\to Z_{k-1}\to\cdots\to Z_0=Z$ with smooth centers in which the \emph{boundary} of $Z_0$ is $E$, the boundary of $Z_i$ is the union of the inverse image of the boundary of $Z_{i-1}$ and the exceptional divisor of $Z_i\to Z_{i-1}$, and each center has normal crossings with the boundary of the model on which it lies. For a prime divisor $D$ on the final model, put $b_D=\operatorname{ord}_D(E)$, the vanishing order along $D$ of the pullback of a local equation of $E$, and $A_D=A_Z(D)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-divisor-discrepancy-margin}
In this situation, $A_D-b_D=0$ if $D$ is the strict transform of $E$, and $A_D-b_D>0$ for every other prime divisor $D$ in the boundary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $Z$ itself, $A_E=b_E=1$, and every other prime divisor $D$ of $Z$ has $A_D=1$ and $b_D=0$. Suppose the statement holds on a model, and blow up a center $C$ of codimension $c\geq2$ that has normal crossings with the boundary and is contained in exactly $r$ boundary components, near its generic point. Since these $r$ components and $C$ have normal crossings, $r\leq c$. In the coordinates of \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}, the Jacobian determinant of the blowup vanishes to order $c-1$ along the new exceptional divisor $E_{\mathrm{new}}$, and the pullback of a local equation of each component containing $C$ vanishes to order one along $E_{\mathrm{new}}$. Hence $A_{\mathrm{new}}=c+\sum_{D\supset C}(A_D-1)$ and $b_{\mathrm{new}}=\sum_{D\supset C}b_D$, the sums running over the boundary components containing $C$ (other divisors through $C$ have $A_D=1$ and $b_D=0$ and contribute nothing). Therefore
\[
 A_{\mathrm{new}}-b_{\mathrm{new}}
       =c-r+\sum_{D\supset C}(A_D-b_D).
\]
All terms are nonnegative by the induction hypothesis. If one of the components containing $C$ has a positive margin, the new margin is positive. Otherwise only the strict transform of $E$ can contain $C$, so $r\leq1$ and $c-r\geq1$. Strict transforms keep their log discrepancies and orders, hence their margins. Blowing up a center of codimension one is an isomorphism and creates no new divisor. Induction on the number of blowups proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:critical-gain-initial-gluing}
Let $L\to X$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h_0$ and a plurisubharmonic metric $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi}$. Let $Y$ be a nonempty connected closed complex submanifold of codimension $q\geq1$ such that the restriction of $h$ to $Y$ is defined, and let $R_Y$ be as in \cref{lem:global-normal-pole}, with $\max_XR_Y<\mathrm e^{-4}$. If $f$ is a holomorphic section of $L|_Y$ with $\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y<\infty$, there are $\kappa>0$, finitely many holomorphic extensions $F_i$ of $f$ on open sets near $Y$, and smooth cutoffs $\eta_i$ such that $G=\sum_i\eta_iF_i$ is a smooth global section of $L$ with $G|_Y=f$, $\bar\partial G|_Y=0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strengthened-critical-initial-norm}
 \int_X\frac{(|G|_{h_0}^2+|\bar\partial G|_{\omega,h_0}^2)
                         \mathrm e^{-(1+\kappa)\Phi}}
                    {R_Y^q(-\log R_Y)^2}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-gluing-error-decay}
 \int_{\{R_Y<\varepsilon\}}|\bar\partial G|_{\omega,h}^2R_Y^{-q}\,\mathrm dV
                  \longrightarrow0\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm dV_Y=\omega|_Y^{n-q}/(n-q)!$; if $Y$ is a point, $\mathrm dV_Y$ is the unit mass at that point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a holomorphic frame of $L$ the local weight $p$ of $h$ is plurisubharmonic, and its restriction to $Y$ is plurisubharmonic, because the restriction is defined and $Y$ is connected. If $Y$ is a point, the value of $p$ there is finite and we take any $\kappa>0$. Otherwise, by the strong openness theorem (\cref{cor:strong-openness}), every point of $Y$ has a neighborhood in $Y$ on which $f$ is square integrable with the weight $\mathrm e^{-(1+\kappa)p|_Y}$ for some $\kappa>0$; since $Y$ is compact, finitely many coordinate products $B'_i\times B''_i$ of the form of \cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}, with tangential balls slightly larger than needed, cover $Y$, and one $\kappa>0$ works for all of them. \Cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}, applied in a frame with the plurisubharmonic weight $(1+\kappa)p$, gives holomorphic extensions $F_i$ of $f$ on these products whose critical integrals with the logarithmic gain are finite. On smaller products, the smooth factors coming from the frames and the comparison $R_Y\asymp|w|^2$ preserve this finiteness. Choose cutoffs $\eta_i$ supported in the smaller products whose sum is one on a neighborhood of $Y$, and put $G=\sum_i\eta_iF_i$, each term extended by zero. Then $G|_Y=f$. Since all $F_i$ are holomorphic with the same restriction $f$, we have $\bar\partial G=\sum_i(\bar\partial\eta_i)F_i$, and on $Y$ this equals $f\,\bar\partial\sum_i\eta_i=0$. The bounds on the cutoffs and their derivatives give \eqref{eq:strengthened-critical-initial-norm}.

Put $\gamma=|\bar\partial G|_{\omega,h_0}^2$. Since $\bar\partial G$ is smooth and vanishes on $Y$, we have $\gamma\leq CR_Y$ near $Y$. Let $A=\{R_Y<\varepsilon\}$; all integrals below are taken with $\mathrm dV$. H\"older's inequality with the exponents $1+\kappa$ and $(1+\kappa)/\kappa$ gives
\[
 \int_A\gamma\,\mathrm e^{-\Phi}R_Y^{-q}
 \leq\left(\int_A\frac{\gamma\mathrm e^{-(1+\kappa)\Phi}}
                             {R_Y^q(-\log R_Y)^2}\right)^{1/(1+\kappa)}
      \left(\int_A\gamma R_Y^{-q}(-\log R_Y)^{2/\kappa}\right)^{\kappa/(1+\kappa)}.
\]
The first factor is bounded by \eqref{eq:strengthened-critical-initial-norm}. In normal polar coordinates, using $R_Y\asymp|w|^2$ and $\gamma=O(R_Y)$, the integrability of $\gamma R_Y^{-q}(-\log R_Y)^{2/\kappa}$ near $Y$ reduces to $\int_0^r(-\log t)^{2/\kappa}\,\mathrm dt<\infty$. Hence the second factor tends to zero as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, by the absolute continuity of the integral, which proves \eqref{eq:critical-gluing-error-decay}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gentle-weighted-normal-residue}
Let $L\to X$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h_0$ and a plurisubharmonic metric $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi}$, where $\Phi$ has gentle analytic singularities. Let $Y$ be a nonempty connected closed complex submanifold of codimension $q\geq1$ to which $h$ restricts, let $f$ be a holomorphic section of $L|_Y$ with $\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y<\infty$, and let $G$ be constructed from $f$ as in \cref{lem:critical-gain-initial-gluing}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gentle-weighted-normal-residue}
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \int_{\{\varepsilon/2\leq R_Y\leq\varepsilon\}}
                       |G|_h^2R_Y^{-q}\,\mathrm dV
       \leq C_Y\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y,
 \qquad \mathrm dV_Y=\omega|_Y^{n-q}/(n-q)!,
\end{equation}
where $C_Y$ depends only on $Y$, $R_Y$ and $\omega$, and not on $\Phi$ or $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{A resolution adapted to $\Phi$ and $Y$.} Let $\operatorname{Bl}_YX\to X$ be the blowup of $Y$, with exceptional divisor $E$; for $q=1$ this is the identity and $E=Y$. By the coordinate description of the blowup, its relative canonical divisor is $(q-1)E$. Let $\mathfrak a$ be the ideal in the type of $\Phi$. We apply \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input} on the compact manifold $\operatorname{Bl}_YX$ to the pullback of $\mathfrak a$, which is nonzero, and to the divisor $E$. This gives a finite sequence of blowups with smooth centers, each having normal crossings with the accumulated divisor of \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, which starts with $E$; this accumulated divisor is the boundary in the sense of \cref{lem:smooth-divisor-discrepancy-margin}. On the final model the boundary has simple normal crossings, and the pullback of $\mathfrak a$ is the ideal of an effective divisor supported in the boundary. Denote the composite map to $X$ by $\pi$. It is a composite of blowups along closed submanifolds that principalizes $\mathfrak a$, that is, a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ in the sense of \cref{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}. Since \cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities} requires a smooth remainder on every log resolution, $\Phi\circ\pi$ has a smooth remainder.

\emph{Divisor orders.} In a normal-crossing chart on the final model write
\[
 \Phi\circ\pi=\sum_D a_D\log|s_D|^2+v,\qquad
 R_Y\circ\pi=\prod_D|s_D|^{2b_D}r,
\]
with $v$ smooth, $r$ smooth and strictly positive, and $a_D,b_D\geq0$. The Jacobian factor of the pulled-back volume is $\prod_D|s_D|^{2(A_D-1)}$ times a smooth positive function, where $A_D=A_X(D)$ is the log discrepancy. Let $F_i$ be one of the holomorphic extensions used in $G$, and suppose that the chart lies over the product on which $\eta_i$ is supported. Then $F_i\circ\pi$ vanishes to some integral order $e_{i,D}\geq0$ along each coordinate divisor $D$ of the chart. By the proof of \cref{lem:critical-gain-initial-gluing}, the integral of $|F_i|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-(1+\kappa)\Phi}R_Y^{-q}(-\log R_Y)^{-2}$ over that product is finite, and this forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-gain-divisor-orders}
 e_{i,D}+A_D\geq(1+\kappa)a_D+qb_D\quad(b_D>0),
 \qquad e_{i,D}+A_D>(1+\kappa)a_D\quad(b_D=0).
\end{equation}
Indeed, fix the other coordinates away from their divisors and integrate radially in $s_D$: the integrand behaves like $|s_D|^{2(e_{i,D}+A_D-(1+\kappa)a_D-qb_D)-2}(-\log|s_D|^2)^{-2}$ when $b_D>0$, and the squared logarithm in the denominator makes the exponent $-1$ of the radial variable integrable but cannot compensate a smaller exponent. If $b_D=0$, the logarithmic factor is bounded above and below near generic points of $D$, and the inequality must be strict. This is the computation of \cref{thm:analytic-log-resolution-integrability} with one additional logarithmic factor.

Shrinking the chart, we may assume that for each $i$ either the chart lies over the product on which $\eta_i$ is supported or $\eta_i\circ\pi$ vanishes on it, because $\Supp\eta_i$ is a compact subset of that open product. Then, on a smaller chart, the pullback of $G=\sum_i\eta_iF_i$ is the monomial $\prod_Ds_D^{e_D}$, with $e_D=\min_ie_{i,D}$ over the indices $i$ of the first kind, times a smooth bounded function. The density of $|G|_h^2R_Y^{-q}\,\mathrm dV$ is therefore bounded by a constant times $\prod_D|s_D|^{2(\gamma_D-1)}$, where
\[
 \gamma_D=e_D-a_D-qb_D+A_D.
\]
If $a_D>0$, \eqref{eq:critical-gain-divisor-orders} gives $\gamma_D\geq\kappa a_D>0$, and if $a_D=b_D=0$, it gives $\gamma_D>0$. If $a_D=0$ and $b_D>0$, the formula for the relative canonical divisor of the first blowup gives $A_D=A_{\operatorname{Bl}_YX}(D)+(q-1)\operatorname{ord}_D(E)$, and $b_D=\operatorname{ord}_D(E)$ because $\mathcal I_Y$ pulls back to the ideal of $E$; hence
\[
 A_D-qb_D=A_{\operatorname{Bl}_YX}(D)-\operatorname{ord}_D(E).
\]
By \cref{lem:smooth-divisor-discrepancy-margin}, applied with $Z=\operatorname{Bl}_YX$, this is positive unless $D$ is the strict transform of $E$. For that divisor $b_E=1$ and $A_E=q$, and $a_E=0$: since the restriction of $h$ to $Y$ is defined, generic points of $Y$ lie outside the pole set of $\Phi$. Hence $\gamma_E=e_E\geq0$, and the strict transform of $E$ is the only divisor along which $\gamma_D$ can vanish.

\emph{The annular limit.} By a finite partition of unity on the compact inverse image of $Y$, it suffices to consider the integral over a small chart. If no coordinate divisor of the chart is the strict transform of $E$ with $\gamma_E=0$, the density is integrable, the shells $\{\varepsilon/2\leq R_Y\leq\varepsilon\}$ shrink to the null set $\pi^{-1}(Y)$, and dominated convergence shows that the integral over the shell tends to zero. Otherwise let $t$ be the coordinate defining the strict transform of $E$. The density is $|t|^{-2}$ times a smooth bounded coefficient and the factors $|s_D|^{2(\gamma_D-1)}$, $D\ne E$, which are integrable since $\gamma_D>0$. For fixed values of the remaining coordinates $s$, the shell is $\varepsilon/2\leq|t|^2Q(s)r(t,s)\leq\varepsilon$, with $Q(s)=\prod_{D\ne E}|s_D|^{2b_D}$. Since $r$ is bounded above and below, the shell lies in a range of $\log|t|$ of bounded length, so the integral of $|t|^{-2}$ over it is bounded independently of $\varepsilon$ and $s$; this gives an integrable dominating function. As $\varepsilon\to0$, the shell shrinks to $t=0$, the smooth coefficient tends to its value at $t=0$, and the integral of $|t|^{-2}$ over the shell tends to $\pi\log2$. By dominated convergence, only the restriction of the density to the strict transform of $E$ contributes to the limit, and this contribution vanishes if $e_E>0$.

\emph{Identification of the limit.} The argument just given, applied after multiplying the density by a continuous function with compact support, shows that the integrals over the shells converge to $\pi\log2$ times the total mass of a measure $\mu$ on the strict transform of $E$; in a chart as above, $\mu$ has the density obtained by restricting to $t=0$ the smooth coefficient of $|t|^{-2}$, multiplied by the factors $|s_D|^{2(\gamma_D-1)}$, $D\ne E$. Since $\mu$ is determined by the limits of integrals over the shells $\{\varepsilon/2\leq R_Y\leq\varepsilon\}$, it does not depend on the coordinates in which it is computed, and since its density in every chart is integrable, it gives no mass to proper analytic subsets. We may therefore compute $\mu$ on the dense open subset of the strict transform of $E$ lying over the complement of the pole set of $\Phi$ in $Y$ and over which the further blowups are isomorphisms, using coordinates of the first blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_YX$.

Let $(z',w)$ be coordinates on $X$ in which $Y=\{w=0\}$, and write $\mathrm dV=J(z',w)\,\mathrm dV(z')\,\mathrm dV(w)$ with $J$ smooth and positive. By the construction in the proof of \cref{lem:global-normal-pole}, $R_Y=Q_{z'}(w)+O(|w|^3)$, where $Q_{z'}(w)=\sum_i\chi_i(z',0)^2|\ell_i(z')w|^2$ is built from the linear parts $\ell_i(z')$ in $w$ of the normal coordinate tuples used there; it is a positive definite Hermitian form on $\mathbb C^q$. Over this chart, $\operatorname{Bl}_YX$ is covered by charts with coordinates $(z',t,\xi)$, $\xi\in\mathbb C^{q-1}$, in which, after renumbering the coordinates $w$, we have $w=t(1,\xi)$ and $E=\{t=0\}$ (for $q=1$ there is no $\xi$, and $t=w$). There $\mathrm dV(w)=|t|^{2(q-1)}\,\mathrm dV(t)\,\mathrm dV(\xi)$ and $R_Y=|t|^2\bigl(Q_{z'}(1,\xi)+O(|t|)\bigr)$, so away from the poles of $\Phi$ the density of $|G|_h^2R_Y^{-q}\,\mathrm dV$ is $|t|^{-2}$ times the smooth function
\[
 |G|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi}\bigl(Q_{z'}(1,\xi)+O(|t|)\bigr)^{-q}J,
\]
whose restriction to $t=0$ equals $|f(z')|_h^2Q_{z'}(1,\xi)^{-q}J(z',0)$, because $G|_Y=f$. The points of the projective normal fibers not covered by such a chart form a hyperplane in each fiber and are negligible. Integrating over $\xi$, we find that the image of $\mu$ under the projection to $Y$ has the density $c(z')|f(z')|_h^2$ with respect to $\mathrm dV(z')$, where
\[
 c(z')=J(z',0)\int_{\mathbb C^{q-1}}Q_{z'}(1,\xi)^{-q}\,\mathrm dV(\xi).
\]
Since $Q_{z'}(1,\xi)\geq c_0(1+|\xi|^2)$ with $c_0>0$ locally uniformly in $z'$, and $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-q}$ is integrable on $\mathbb C^{q-1}$, the function $c$ is locally bounded. It depends only on $\omega$, $R_Y$ and the chart. Covering the compact manifold $Y$ by finitely many such charts and comparing $\mathrm dV(z')$ with $\mathrm dV_Y$, we obtain \eqref{eq:gentle-weighted-normal-residue}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact-submanifold-extension}
Let $Y\subset X$ be a nonempty closed connected complex submanifold, and put $\mathrm dV_Y=\omega|_Y^{\dim Y}/(\dim Y)!$, the unit mass if $Y$ is a point. There are constants $N,C>0$, depending only on $X$, $\omega$ and $Y$, with the following property. Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h_0$ and a plurisubharmonic metric $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ such that $c_1(L,h)\geq N\omega$, and suppose that the restriction of $h$ to $Y$ is defined, that is, the local weights of $h$ are not identically $-\infty$ on $Y$. Then every $f\in H^0(Y,L|_Y)$ with $\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y<\infty$ has an extension $s\in H^0(X,L)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-submanifold-extension}
 s|_Y=f,\qquad
 \int_X|s|_h^2\,\mathrm dV\leq C\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $Y=X$, take $s=f$, any $N$ and $C=1$. Suppose that $Y$ has codimension $q\geq1$; if $f=0$, take $s=0$. Fix $R=R_Y$ and the constant $b$ of \cref{lem:global-normal-pole}, with $\max_XR<\mathrm e^{-4}$. Give $K_X^{-1}$ the metric $h_{K^{-1}}$ induced by $\omega$, and choose $b_K\geq0$ with $c_1(K_X^{-1},h_{K^{-1}})\geq-b_K\omega$. We first prove the theorem for a weight $\Phi$ with gentle analytic singularities, as for the approximations of \cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}, under the curvature bound
\[
 c_1(L,h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi})\geq N_0\omega,
 \qquad N_0=b_K+(q+2)b+2.
\]

\emph{Step 1: the twisted estimate.} For the given $f$ choose $G$ and $\kappa$ by \cref{lem:critical-gain-initial-gluing}. During this step write $h=h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi}$, and let $A$ be the pole set of $\Phi$. On $M=X\setminus(A\cup Y)$ consider $F=L\otimes K_X^{-1}$ with the metric $h\otimes h_{K^{-1}}\mathrm e^{-q\psi}$, where $\psi=\log R$; the canonical identification of $L$-valued functions with $F$-valued $(n,0)$-forms preserves the squared norms times the volume. The manifold $M$ carries a K\"ahler form and cutoffs as required in \cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate}, by \cref{lem:analytic-complement-cutoffs} applied to $\Phi+\psi$, which is quasi-plurisubharmonic, smooth on $M$, and tends to $-\infty$ exactly at the points of $A\cup Y$. The resulting K\"ahler form depends on $\Phi$, but the estimate of \cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate} does not involve it.

Put $\psi_\varepsilon=\log(\varepsilon+R)$, and let $a_\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon$ and $\mu$ be as in the proof of \cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}. Since $R<\mathrm e^{-4}$, for each small fixed $\varepsilon$ these functions are bounded above and below by positive constants, $a_\varepsilon\geq1$ and $1\leq-\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)\leq2$. The same cancellation of gradient terms gives
\[
 H_\varepsilon=a_\varepsilon\bigl(2\pi c_1(L,h)
              +2\pi c_1(K_X^{-1},h_{K^{-1}})
              +q\,\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi\bigr)
        -\mu'(\psi_\varepsilon)\,
                         \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon.
\]
The identity \eqref{eq:logarithmic-normal-hessian} holds for the smooth positive function $R$ on $M$. Since $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\psi=2\pi\ddc\psi\geq-2\pi b\omega$, $0\leq R/(\varepsilon+R)\leq1$ and $-\mu'\leq2$, it gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-normal-twisted-curvature}
 H_\varepsilon\geq
  2\pi\bigl(a_\varepsilon(N_0-b_K-qb)-2b\bigr)\omega
       +\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\,
                  \mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon
 \geq 2\pi\omega+\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\,
                  \mathrm i\partial\psi_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where in the last step we used $a_\varepsilon\geq1$ and $N_0-b_K-qb=2b+2$. All constants in this display depend only on the geometry.

\emph{Step 2: the correction.} With the cutoff $\rho$ of the proof of \cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}, put $G_\varepsilon=\rho(R/\varepsilon)G$. Its $\bar\partial$-closed derivative splits as
\[
 g_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon^{(1)}+g_\varepsilon^{(2)},\qquad
 g_\varepsilon^{(1)}=\rho'(R/\varepsilon)(1+R/\varepsilon)
                         \bar\partial\psi_\varepsilon\,G,
 \qquad g_\varepsilon^{(2)}=\rho(R/\varepsilon)\bar\partial G,
\]
regarded as $F$-valued $(n,1)$-forms. As in the local case, the rank-one part of \eqref{eq:global-normal-twisted-curvature} bounds the dual curvature energy of $g_\varepsilon^{(1)}$ by $C_1\int_{\{\varepsilon/2\leq R\leq\varepsilon\}}|G|_h^2R^{-q}\,\mathrm dV$, whose $\limsup$ is at most $C_2\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y$ by \cref{lem:gentle-weighted-normal-residue}. The term $2\pi\omega$ bounds the dual energy of $g_\varepsilon^{(2)}$ by $C_3\int_{\{R<\varepsilon\}}|\bar\partial G|_{\omega,h}^2R^{-q}\,\mathrm dV$, which tends to zero by \eqref{eq:critical-gluing-error-decay}. Both forms are square integrable for the metric of $F$: for $g_\varepsilon^{(1)}$, the derivative of the cutoff is bounded for fixed $\varepsilon$, and \eqref{eq:strengthened-critical-initial-norm} bounds the integral of $|G|_h^2R^{-q}$ over the shell, where the logarithm is bounded and $\mathrm e^{-\Phi}\leq\mathrm e^{\kappa\sup_X\Phi}\mathrm e^{-(1+\kappa)\Phi}$; for $g_\varepsilon^{(2)}$ use \eqref{eq:critical-gluing-error-decay}. Since the dual energy of a sum is at most twice the sum of the dual energies, \cref{lem:twisted-kahler-line-estimate} gives $u_\varepsilon$ on $M$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-normal-correction-bound}
 \bar\partial u_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon,\qquad
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \int_M\frac{|u_\varepsilon|_h^2R^{-q}}
                 {a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon}\,\mathrm dV
       \leq C_4\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y.
\end{equation}
The constants $C_1,\ldots,C_4$ depend only on the geometry and the fixed cutoff $\rho$; neither the exponent $\kappa$ nor the derivatives of the local extensions $F_i$ enter them.

\emph{Step 3: holomorphic sections with the trace $f$.} For fixed $\varepsilon$ the denominator $a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon$ is bounded, and $h\geq\mathrm e^{-\sup_X\Phi}h_0$ because $\Phi$ is bounded above on the compact manifold $X$. Hence $s_\varepsilon=G_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$, which is holomorphic on $M$, is square integrable for $h_0$, and it extends to a global holomorphic section by \cref{lem:l2-holomorphic-analytic-removal}. Then $u_\varepsilon=G_\varepsilon-s_\varepsilon$ is smooth on $X$. Near a point of $Y\setminus A$, the weight $\Phi$ is smooth, so the finiteness of $\int|u_\varepsilon|^2R^{-q}$ forces $u_\varepsilon=0$ on $Y$ by the radial argument of the local case. Hence $s_\varepsilon=G=f$ on $Y\setminus A$. Since the restriction of $h$ to $Y$ is defined, $A\cap Y$ is a proper analytic subset of the connected manifold $Y$, and the identity theorem gives $s_\varepsilon|_Y=f$.

\emph{Step 4: the norm bound.} For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$,
\[
 (a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon)R^q
     \leq C_5 R^q(-\log R)^2\leq C_6,
\]
because $\psi\leq\psi_\varepsilon<0$, so that $a_\varepsilon+b_\varepsilon\leq C(-\psi_\varepsilon)^2\leq C(-\psi)^2$, and because $R^q(-\log R)^2$ is bounded on $X$. Consequently \eqref{eq:global-normal-correction-bound} bounds $\int_X|u_\varepsilon|_h^2\,\mathrm dV$ by $C_6$ times the integral there. The section $G$ has finite $h$-norm, since \eqref{eq:strengthened-critical-initial-norm} controls it, $\Phi$ being bounded above and $R^q(-\log R)^2$ bounded; therefore $\|G_\varepsilon\|_h\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, by absolute continuity of the integral on the shrinking supports. The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\|s_\varepsilon\|_h^2
            \leq C_7\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y,
\]
with $C_7=C_6C_4$. The sections $s_\varepsilon$ are bounded in $L^2$ for $h_0$, so by the submean inequality and the Cauchy estimates on a finite cover a sequence $\varepsilon_k\to0$ gives sections converging uniformly in local frames to a holomorphic section $s$. Its restriction to $Y$ is $f$, and Fatou's lemma gives $\|s\|_h^2\leq C_7\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y$. This proves the theorem for gentle weights with the constant $C_7$, which depends only on the geometry.

\emph{Step 5: arbitrary weights.} Put $N=N_0+1$. By \cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}, applied to $\varphi$ with the background $c_1(L,h_0)-N\omega$, there are $\Phi_j\downarrow\varphi$ with gentle analytic singularities of the kind required in the gentle case and $c_1(L,h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi_j})\geq(N-\varepsilon_j)\omega$, where $\varepsilon_j\to0$; for large $j$ the curvature is at least $N_0\omega$. Since $\Phi_j\geq\varphi$, the restrictions of the metrics $h_0\mathrm e^{-\Phi_j}$ to $Y$ are defined, and the norms of $f$ for them are at most its norm for $h$. The gentle case gives $s_j\in H^0(X,L)$ with $s_j|_Y=f$ and $\int_X|s_j|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi_j}\,\mathrm dV\leq C_7\int_Y|f|_h^2\,\mathrm dV_Y$. Since $\Phi_j\leq\Phi_1$, the $s_j$ are bounded in $L^2$ for $h_0$, and a subsequence converges uniformly to a holomorphic section $s$ with $s|_Y=f$. The weights $\mathrm e^{-\Phi_j}$ converge to $\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ wherever $\varphi$ is finite, hence almost everywhere, and Fatou's lemma gives \eqref{eq:compact-submanifold-extension} with $C=C_7$.
\end{proof}
\index{Ohsawa--Takegoshi theorem!compact submanifold extension}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:compact-positive-metric-extension}
Let $L$ and $T$ be holomorphic line bundles on $X$ with smooth quadratic metrics $h_0$ and $h_T$, put $\theta=c_1(L,h_0)$, and choose $b_T\geq0$ with $c_1(T,h_T)\geq-b_T\omega$. Let $\varphi$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic with $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$, and let $Y\subset X$ be a nonempty closed connected complex submanifold such that $\varphi|_Y$ is not identically $-\infty$. Let $N$ and $C$ be the constants of \cref{thm:compact-submanifold-extension}. Then for every integer $k\geq1$ with $k\delta-b_T\geq N$, every holomorphic section $f$ of $(T\otimes L^k)|_Y$ with finite norm for $h_T\otimes h_0^k\mathrm e^{-k\varphi|_Y}$ extends to a holomorphic section $s$ of $T\otimes L^k$ on $X$ with
\[
 \int_X|s|_{h_T\otimes h_0^k}^2\mathrm e^{-k\varphi}\,\mathrm dV
       \leq C\int_Y|f|_{h_T\otimes h_0^k}^2
                         \mathrm e^{-k\varphi|_Y}\,\mathrm dV_Y.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The metric $h_T\otimes h_0^k\mathrm e^{-k\varphi}$ on $T\otimes L^k$ is plurisubharmonic, its curvature current is $c_1(T,h_T)+k(\theta+\ddc\varphi)\geq(k\delta-b_T)\omega\geq N\omega$, and its restriction to $Y$ is defined. Apply \cref{thm:compact-submanifold-extension}. (The finiteness of the norm on $Y$ means that $f$ lies locally in the multiplier ideal of $k\varphi|_Y$. If the volumes $\omega^n$ and $\omega|_Y^{\dim Y}$ are used instead of $\mathrm dV$ and $\mathrm dV_Y$, the constant $C$ is multiplied by $n!/(\dim Y)!$.)
\end{proof}
