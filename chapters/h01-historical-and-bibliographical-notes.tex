\chapter{Historical and bibliographical notes}
\label{chap:h01}

These notes give the sources of the results in this book. For each chapter we indicate where the main results were first proved or where they can be found in the literature, compare our statements, proofs and normalizations with those of the sources, and point to alternative presentations. The sections follow the order of the chapters: each section concerns one chapter, except that the notes on \cref{chap:q11} occupy three sections, and the last section concerns \cref{chap:a01}. A few ideas recur throughout the book: a function is recovered from its distributional derivatives, exceptional sets are controlled by capacities, nonlinear measures are constructed by approximation with bounded potentials, and singular potentials are compared through their envelopes. These ideas have different origins, and their formulation has changed as the theory moved from domains in Euclidean space to compact complex manifolds.

When comparing the book with another source one has to take its conventions into account as well as its hypotheses. We use $\ddc=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/(2\pi)$; Lelong numbers are normalized so that $\log|z|^2$ has Lelong number $1$ at the origin, and multiplier ideals are defined with the weight $\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$. Several of the sources cited below use the operator $\mathrm{dd^c}=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/\pi$, measure Lelong numbers against $\log|z|$ and use the weight $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$. If the operator is multiplied by a constant $c>0$, then at fixed potentials a product of $p$ factors of the form $\ddc u$ is multiplied by $c^p$. A source that measures Lelong numbers against $\log|z|$ and uses the weight $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$ assigns to $\varphi=u/2$ the Lelong numbers and multiplier ideals that we assign to $u$. We record these conversions where they matter. Theorem and page numbers always refer to the version of a work that is cited; when this is a preprint and not the published article, which may have different numbering or scope, we say so. Two existence theorems are used without proof, namely the resolution and principalization theorems \cref{thm:analytic-resolution-input,thm:marked-order-one-input}; their source is given in \cref{sec:history-analytic-spaces}.

\section{Measures and distributions}
\label{sec:history-measures}
The representation of positive linear functionals by measures, \cref{thm:positive-functional-measure}, is the Riesz representation theorem. For its formulation on locally compact, $\sigma$-compact spaces see \cite[Theorem 24]{Tao09Radon}; on open subsets of Euclidean space our proof constructs the measure from a metric outer measure and identifies the functional by slicing functions into level sets. The regularity and density statements in \cref{prop:borel-regularity,prop:continuous-density} correspond to \cite[Theorem 21, Exercise 22 and Proposition 10]{Tao09Radon}. For the measurability part of the outer-measure construction compare \cite[Theorem 5]{Tao10Products}. The assertions on product measures and the interchange of integrals in \cref{prop:tonelli-fubini} constitute the Tonelli--Fubini theorem for $\sigma$-finite measures; compare \cite[Theorem 36 and its proof]{Tao10Products}.

Distribution theory defines derivatives by duality with test functions. For the local order bound, regularization by mollifiers and the fact that positive distributions are measures (\cref{def:distribution,prop:distribution-smoothing,thm:positive-distribution-measure}) compare \cite[Section 1, Exercises 4--5; Section 2, Definition 1, Exercise 15 and Lemma 2]{Tao09Distributions}. That source writes the test function first and gives the space of distributions the conjugate complex structure. Our pairing $\langle u,\chi\rangle=\int u\chi\,\mathrm d x$ is complex linear in both variables, which is the convention needed for currents later in the book; after this change the derivative of a distribution is defined with the same sign.

The selection theorem for sequences of measures, \cref{thm:local-measure-selection}, is proved by a diagonal argument over a countable family of test functions. The compactness theorem \cref{thm:local-measure-compactness}, which concerns nets, rests instead on the compactness of a product of intervals (Tychonoff's theorem). We prove the latter with ultrafilters in \cref{lem:compact-product}; this is where the axiom of choice enters. Since subnets are always Willard subnets in this book, \cref{prop:compact-net-characterization} characterizes compactness by subnets in this sense.

The Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem, \cref{prop:arzela-ascoli}, is proved in the usual way, by showing that an equicontinuous and uniformly bounded family is totally bounded for the uniform metric.

For the differentiation of integrals compare \cite[Section 2, Theorems 32 and 36, Exercise 38 and Lemma 39]{Tao10Differentiation}. In \cref{thm:lebesgue-differentiation} we use cubes instead of balls. The covering argument is the same: a finite disjoint subfamily of the cubes covers the original cubes after enlargement by the factor three, and the constant in the maximal inequality becomes $3^d$. The Poincar\'e-type inequality \cref{lem:weak-poincare-cube} follows by integrating the fundamental theorem of calculus along segments parallel to the coordinate axes.

For the Radon--Nikodym theorem compare \cite[Corollary 1, following Theorem 2]{Tao09RN}. We only need the case of a Radon measure that is dominated, at least locally, by a constant multiple of another, \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density}, which we prove by an $L^2$ argument on dyadic cubes; half-open cubes are used, so that no assumption on the measure of cube boundaries is needed. This lemma provides the densities of the coefficient measures in \cref{lem:bounded-psh-current-chain-rule}.

\section{Harmonic functions and boundary values}
\label{sec:history-harmonic}
Fundamental solutions, the regularity of distributional solutions of the Laplace equation, the Poisson formula and Harnack's inequality are treated in \cite[Theorems 3.1.2--3.1.7, pp.~117--123]{Hor07}. As there, the fundamental solution $E$ of \cref{prop:laplacian-fundamental-solution} satisfies $\Delta E=\delta_0$, whereas our Green functions are positive and satisfy $-\Delta G=\delta_x$. Our proof of Weyl's lemma, \cref{thm:weyl}, uses radial averaging and convolution; Theorem 3.1.3 of that book also contains an analytic continuation statement, which we do not need. The Poisson formula and Harnack's inequality (\cref{thm:ball-dirichlet,prop:harnack-ball}) are stated with the explicit Poisson kernel and its two-sided bounds, and they include the case of dimension one.

Perron's method, the solution of the Dirichlet problem for continuous boundary data and barriers are developed in \cite[Section 3.3, pp.~171--174, especially Theorem 3.3.3, Definition 3.3.4 and Theorem 3.3.5]{Hor07}. In \cref{thm:perron-harmonic,thm:perron-resolutivity} we work with continuous subsolutions. For the characterization of regular boundary points by barriers compare also \cite[Theorem 3.4.17, pp.~219--220]{Hor07}. Our local barrier criterion, \cref{prop:local-barrier}, uses a continuous function that is positive on the nearby boundary except at the given point; the theorem in H\"ormander's book allows more general exceptional sets. The Green function of \cref{thm:dirichlet-green} is constructed only on bounded regular domains; within this class the theorem also shows that Green functions increase with the domain and converge along increasing sequences of domains whose union is again bounded and regular.

\section{Subharmonic functions and polar sets}
\label{sec:history-subharmonic}
The characterizations of subharmonic functions by comparison with harmonic functions and by mean values, their local integrability, the Riesz decomposition and the distributional characterization are developed in \cite[Theorem 3.2.3, Corollary 3.2.8 and Theorems 3.2.9--3.2.11, pp.~143--148]{Hor07}; see \cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison,prop:subharmonic-local-integrability,thm:riesz-decomposition,thm:subharmonic-distribution}. Since a subharmonic function is the limit of its means at every point, the subharmonic representative of a distribution with positive Laplacian is unique at every point, not only almost everywhere. Our Riesz measure is $\Delta u$, without a factor $1/(2\pi)$; thus a logarithmic pole in the plane has Riesz mass $2\pi$.

For the compactness theorem and Hartogs' lemma (\cref{thm:subharmonic-compactness,thm:hartogs-subharmonic}) compare \cite[Theorems 3.2.12--3.2.13, pp.~149--151]{Hor07}. Theorem 3.2.13 there also contains convergence statements in Sobolev spaces and against measures, which we do not use. Choquet's lemma and the regularized upper envelope (\cref{lem:choquet-selection,thm:subharmonic-envelope}) correspond to \cite[Proposition 3.4.4, pp.~205--206]{Hor07}; for the pointwise supremum of an arbitrary family, \cref{thm:subharmonic-envelope} asserts measurability with respect to the completed Lebesgue measure. The local definition of polar sets and the removable singularity theorem (\cref{def:polar-set,thm:polar-removable}) can be compared with \cite[Definition 3.4.1 and Theorem 3.4.3, pp.~203--205]{Hor07}. Our proof works directly with the local subharmonic functions of the definition, and the connectedness of the complement of a closed polar set in a domain follows from the maximum principle.

\section{Energy, capacity and exceptional sets}
\label{sec:history-capacity}
The continuity principle and the restriction of a measure to compact sets on which its potential is continuous (\cref{thm:potential-continuity-principle}) follow the argument of \cite[Theorems 3.4.6--3.4.7, pp.~207--208]{Hor07}; the approximation lemma of Lusin type used there is \cref{lem:lusin-finite-measure}. The criterion for polarity of compact sets is \cite[Theorem 3.4.5, pp.~206--208]{Hor07}. Our relative capacity of a compact set is the total mass of the Riesz measure $\Delta u$ of its regularized relative extremal function $u$, and our Green kernel is positive with $-\Delta G=\delta$; the numerical values of the capacity depend on these normalizations and differ from those of capacities normalized in other ways.

Strong subadditivity, outer regularity and capacitability are developed in \cite[Section 3.4, pp.~209--216, especially Theorems 3.4.9 and 3.4.12 and Proposition 3.4.13]{Hor07}. Choquet's capacitability theorem, \cref{thm:choquet-capacitability}, which underlies the capacitability assertion of that section, is proved here by approximating continuous images of countable intersections of countable unions of compact sets from inside by compact sets. The convergence of integrals against measures with continuous potentials in \cref{lem:continuous-potential-testing} is part of \cite[Theorem 3.2.13, pp.~150--151]{Hor07}, and the fact that the exceptional sets of the limit theorems are polar, \cref{thm:polar-limit-discrepancy}, corresponds to \cite[Theorem 3.4.14, pp.~216--217]{Hor07}. It strengthens the equality almost everywhere of an upper envelope and its regularization in \cref{thm:subharmonic-envelope}.

For the identity between Green energy and Dirichlet energy and for quasi-continuity compare \cite[Propositions 3.4.19 and 3.4.21, pp.~221--223]{Hor07}. In \cref{prop:bounded-green-energy} we work with the explicit, symmetric Green kernel of a ball. The positivity of the Newtonian energy and of the logarithmic energy (\cref{prop:kernel-energy-positivity}) is proved by expressing the kernels as integrals of Gaussians. For the logarithmic kernel we prove positivity and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality only for signed measures of total mass zero, and they are used only in that form. The equilibrium measure is obtained by minimizing the energy, and \cref{prop:equilibrium-frostman} describes its potential (Frostman's theorem) with our normalization of the kernels.

The example \cite[Example~6.1.3]{XiaSampleBook} uses an atom-free measure on a polar Cantor set and refers to \cite[p.~31]{Car83} for the Cantor set. Such a measure $\mu$ is constructed explicitly in \cref{prop:diffuse-polar-cantor-measure}: the Cantor set $K$ is built from a rapidly decreasing sequence of lengths, so that the subharmonic function $p(z)=\int_K\log|z-a|^2\,\mathrm d\mu(a)$ equals $-\infty$ at every point of $K$. The remaining ingredients of that example, namely the potential on the projective line, its integrability and the computation of its mass in degree one, are \cref{prop:projective-logarithmic-measure-potential,cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal,ex:diffuse-polar-zero-nonpluripolar-mass}.

\section{Fine topology and balayage}
\label{sec:history-fine}
Fuglede attributes the classical fine topology to Cartan and the quasi-Lindel\"of principle to Doob in \cite[Introduction, p.~227]{Fug71Fine}. His preliminaries \cite[Section 1, pp.~228--230]{Fug71Fine} are formulated in an axiomatic harmonic space satisfying the domination axiom and admitting a positive potential, and they assume the general duality theory of sweeping before fine connectedness is studied. In the Euclidean setting of this book the quasi-Lindel\"of principle, \cref{thm:fine-quasi-lindelof}, follows directly from Choquet's lemma and the polarity of the exceptional set of an upper envelope. By \cref{ex:uncountable-polar-cantor}, the fine topology is not Lindel\"of, so a countable subfamily can only be expected to cover the union of a family of finely open sets up to a polar set.

Reduction and balayage (\cref{thm:compact-reduction,thm:compact-balayage,thm:finite-energy-balayage}) are treated only on a ball, where the Green kernel is explicit and symmetric and Green energy is a Dirichlet integral by \cref{prop:finite-green-energy}. In this setting the swept measure is determined by an orthogonality relation for the energy, which also gives additivity; the general duality of sweeping recalled by Fuglede is not used. \Cref{ex:balayage-harmonic-measure} expresses the swept potential of a Dirac measure through harmonic measure, for a regular domain whose complement in the ball is also regular. \Cref{prop:classical-plurifine-distinction}, which shows that for $n\geq2$ the classical fine topology of $\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ differs from the topology generated by the upper semicontinuous functions that are subharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on complex lines, is proved directly from the definitions.

\section{Fourier analysis and complex-line incidence}
\label{sec:history-fourier-incidence}
For Fourier inversion on the Schwartz space and Plancherel's theorem (\cref{lem:fourier-gaussian,thm:plancherel}) compare \cite[Exercises 38--42, formula (11), Theorem 43 and Exercise 44]{Tao09Fourier}. Our Fourier transform uses the character $\mathrm e^{-2\pi\mathrm i x\cdot\xi}$. Gaussian integration gives the exact constant in the Fourier expression of the Riesz energy, \cref{thm:riesz-fourier-energy}; the kernel there is $|x-y|^{-s}$, without the normalization of a fundamental solution.

For the dyadic decomposition compare \cite[Proposition 4.3 and proof, pp.~23--24]{Tao06Fourier3}; for the weak type $(1,1)$ estimate and its consequences by interpolation and duality compare \cite[Lemma 2.7, Corollaries 2.9--2.10 and proofs, pp.~7--9]{Tao06Fourier4}. We treat only the Hessian multipliers $\xi_a\xi_b/|\xi|^2$ (\cref{lem:hessian-multiplier-kernel}), whose kernels are obtained by differentiating the heat kernel. Their weak type $(1,1)$ bound and their $L^p$ bounds are \cref{lem:hessian-multiplier-weak-one,thm:hessian-multiplier-lp}; the exponents $p>2$ are obtained by duality. The interpolation inequality \cref{lem:gradient-lp-interpolation} and Morrey's inequality \cref{lem:morrey-sobolev-representative} are used for metric Laplacians in \cref{chap:q10}.

The star-shaped annular sets, the projections onto complex hyperplanes and the incidence argument follow \cite[Lemmas 1.3--1.5, pp.~2--4]{PSHD}; this manuscript carries no author's name and no publication data. The required Borel selection is provided by \cref{lem:positive-circle-selection}. The sets of high values on circles are closed and, by the submean inequality, have positive length, so a median with respect to arc length gives a Borel choice, and no general measurable selection theorem for compact-valued maps is required. The unitarily invariant measures are constructed from Gaussian matrices, and volumes in projective space are controlled by an explicit estimate for projective balls instead of volume charts. In \cref{cor:borel-line-subharmonic-bound} the function is assumed to be Borel; in the proof of \cref{thm:automatic-psh-usc} this measurability is established before the corollary is applied.

\section{Differential forms, currents and holomorphic divisors}
\label{sec:history-complex-currents}
For positive forms and currents, the characterization of weak positivity by restriction to complex subspaces and the fact that positive currents have order zero, compare \cite[Chapter III, Section 1.A--B, pp.~129--134, especially (1.4), (1.6) and (1.14)]{DemBook}. In addition, \cref{prop:positivity-cones} shows that $\beta^p$ lies in the interior of the cone of strongly positive forms, which gives the trace bounds for positive currents; the proof averages over sign changes and permutations of the coordinates. Our currents are indexed by bidegree, whereas Demailly's subscripts there refer to bidimension. Pushforward and pullback of currents (\cref{prop:proper-current-pushforward,prop:submersion-current-pullback}) correspond to \cite[Chapter III, (1.17), p.~134]{DemBook}; for the pushforward the map need only be proper on the support of the current.

The mixed Hodge--Riemann theorem of linear algebra, due to Timorin, is recorded in \cite[Proposition 2.1, p.~4]{DN2005mixedv2}, the second arXiv version of the article. \Cref{lem:mixed-discriminant-signature} is its case of real $(1,1)$-forms, which we prove by induction on principal minors and deformation to identity matrices. The bilinear form in that lemma is the pairing given by the wedge product, and its sign is determined directly by the trace formula.

For local potentials of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents compare \cite[Chapter III, Section 1.18 and (1.19), p.~135]{DemBook}. Our proof of \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential} is different: we subtract the complex Hessian of the potential of the trace measure, and the remainder, which has trace zero and therefore harmonic coefficients, is $\ddc$-exact by the smooth local result \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}. In \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition} the composition with a holomorphic map is taken pointwise, for the canonical representative; it is proved by radial regularization.

Our proof of the Weierstrass preparation theorem, \cref{thm:local-weierstrass-preparation}, is the proof by contour integrals in \cite[Chapter II, Section 2.A, pp.~79--80, Theorem (2.1)]{DemBook}, where it is attributed to Siegel. The Poincar\'e--Lelong formula, \cref{thm:poincare-lelong}, is \cite[Chapter III, (2.15), pp.~143--144]{DemBook}. The expression $(\mathrm{i}/\pi)\partial\bar\partial\log|f|$ appearing there equals our $\ddc\log|f|^2$, while the operator $\mathrm{dd^c}$ used subsequently in that book is twice ours. In our proof, slicing by coordinate lines and \cref{lem:divisor-exceptional-projections} show that $\ddc\log|f|^2$ puts no mass on the singular locus of the divisor, so that it equals the current of integration over the regular part, weighted by multiplicities. \Cref{ex:poincare-lelong-products} gives the corresponding identity in $L^1_{\loc}$ for quotients of holomorphic functions.

\section{Plurisubharmonic functions and invariant minima}
\label{sec:history-plurisubharmonic}
The theorem that a function which is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on complex lines is automatically upper semicontinuous is proved in the unsigned manuscript \cite[Theorem 1.1 and its proof, pp.~1--6]{PSHD}, and our proof of \cref{thm:automatic-psh-usc} completes the argument given there. In that manuscript, Lemma 1.2, pp.~1--2, proves measurability by induction on the dimension, using means over bounded fibres; Proposition 2.1, pp.~4--5, identifies a finite function that is subharmonic on lines with the canonical representative; and the final proof, p.~6, uses truncation. The incidence results needed for this argument are proved in \cref{chap:p06}, and \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition} describes the restrictions of the canonical representative to complex lines without using the theorem. We keep the usual definition of plurisubharmonic functions (\cref{def:plurisubharmonic}), which requires upper semicontinuity; \cref{thm:automatic-psh-usc}, proved immediately afterwards, shows that this condition is redundant.

The distributional characterization, upper envelopes and compactness of plurisubharmonic functions (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative,thm:psh-upper-envelopes,thm:psh-compactness-hartogs}) are deduced from the corresponding results for subharmonic functions in \cref{chap:p03,chap:p04}. At this stage the set where an upper envelope differs from its regularization is only known to be polar, as a subset of $\mathbb R^{2n}$, in local charts. The extension theorem \cref{thm:psh-small-set-extension} gives both local boundedness and extension across a closed set whose $(2n-3)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure vanishes; to apply it to analytic sets of codimension at least two (\cref{cor:analytic-codimension-two-psh-removal}) one needs the local estimate for the dimension of analytic sets.

For $L^p$ integrability and convergence in $L^p_{\loc}$ compare \cite[Theorem 1.48, parts 1--2 and proof Steps 1--2, pp.~30--32]{GZ17}. As there, we pass from discs to polydiscs (\cref{lem:negative-subharmonic-disc-lp,prop:negative-psh-polydisc-lp,thm:psh-local-lp-integrability}), but we use the Riesz decomposition with a compactly supported measure instead of the Poisson--Jensen formula on the boundary. Since our Riesz measure is $\Delta v$, its logarithmic potential carries the factor $1/(2\pi)$. The bound \eqref{eq:negative-psh-lp-lone-bound} gives uniform estimates on smaller polydiscs, and convergence in $L^p_{\loc}$ then follows from H\"older's inequality. The identity with the factor two in the truncation display of \cite[p.~32]{GZ17} is incorrect, as is also noted in an unofficial list of errata for that book.

The local exponential integrability estimates (\cref{lem:negative-subharmonic-disc-exponential,prop:negative-psh-ball-exponential}) are proved with the same compactly supported Riesz decomposition: Jensen's inequality controls an inverse power of the kernel on the disc, and integration over the complex lines through a point gives the estimate in several variables. The exponent depends only on a local $L^1$ bound. A finite covering by charts then gives the uniform estimate for normalized quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on a compact manifold, \cref{cor:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound}.

The partial Legendre transform is due to Kiselman \cite{Kis78}. For his minimum principle and the regularization argument in its proof compare \cite[Chapter I, Section 7.B, Theorem (7.5) and its proof, p.~57]{DemBook}. In \cref{thm:kiselman-invariant-exhaustion} the existence of an invariant exhaustion is a hypothesis, and the smooth case is proved with a Schur complement. Such an exhaustion is constructed for product tubes over open convex sets in \cref{cor:kiselman-product-tube}; the case of general pseudoconvex tubes, \cref{cor:kiselman-pseudoconvex-tube}, uses in addition the plurisubharmonicity of $-\log$ of the boundary distance.

\section{Bounded products and nonlinear capacity}
\label{sec:history-bounded-products}
The recursive definition of products of currents $\ddc u$ with $u$ locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimates and continuity along decreasing sequences are developed in \cite[Chapter III, Section 3, (3.1)--(3.11), pp.~144--150]{DemBook}; see \cref{thm:cln-bounded,thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}. In our proof of decreasing continuity, the integration by parts formula \cref{lem:bt-common-collar} for functions that coincide near the boundary of a ball is combined with an induction on the number of factors, which also gives the symmetry of the product at each stage. The weighted estimate \cref{thm:cln-weighted} is proved only for the measures $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}$, not for products with an arbitrary closed positive current.

For continuity along increasing sequences, the behavior of products under maxima and the comparison principle (\cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity,prop:bt-maximum-locality,thm:bt-comparison}) compare \cite[Theorem 3.23, pp.~89--91, and Theorems 3.27 and 3.29, pp.~93--94]{GZ17}. The Monge--Amp\`ere capacity, quasi-continuity and convergence in capacity (\cref{def:monge-ampere-capacity,thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity,thm:bt-capacity-continuity}) appear in \cite[Proposition 4.19, Theorem 4.20 and Lemma 4.21, pp.~110--112, and Theorem 4.26, pp.~114--115]{GZ17}. Our capacity is defined with competitors taking values between zero and one, whereas the competitors in \cite[Definition 4.16, p.~108]{GZ17} take values between $-1$ and $0$; since adding a constant does not change Monge--Amp\`ere measures, the two normalizations give the same capacity. Both Demailly's book and \cite{GZ17} use the operator $\mathrm{dd^c}=(\mathrm{i}/\pi)\partial\bar\partial$, twice ours, so their Monge--Amp\`ere measures and capacities are $2^n$ times ours. The domination principle \cref{cor:bt-local-domination} is \cite[Proposition~1.2.10]{XiaSampleBook}; we prove it by a strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic perturbation and the comparison principle on a relatively compact strict sublevel set.

The estimate \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} is attributed to B\l{}ocki in \cite[Proposition 3.21, pp.~88--89]{GZ17}. The power $M^{n+1}$ of the oscillation printed there should be $M^n$: the proof uses $n$ integrations by parts, and the left-hand side is homogeneous of degree $n$ in $v$. We prove the estimate with $M^n$, under the assumption that $u=h$ near the boundary of the ball, first for smooth functions and then, by regularization and \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights}, for bounded ones. \Cref{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity} shows that the set where an upper envelope differs from its regularization has capacity zero; this is used in \cref{chap:q03} to show that such sets are pluripolar.

The solution of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem on the ball, \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}, goes back to \cite{BT76}. Averaging over automorphisms that move the center exactly to a prescribed point is carried out for polydiscs in \cite[Section 2.3, Theorem 2.3.2 and Proposition 2.3.3, pp.~50--52]{Blo02Pluripotential}; \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms} provides the corresponding automorphisms of the ball. Compare also \cite[Theorems 5.13--5.14, pp.~143--149]{GZ17}. The determinant is identified by means of the Taylor expansion almost everywhere of functions with bounded real Hessian, \cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor}, which is proved with the differentiation of integrals and the Poincar\'e inequality on cubes. The function $\widehat u$ of \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} is the largest plurisubharmonic function on the ball whose upper limits at the boundary are at most the values of $u$. Together with the comparison principle it shows that a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function is maximal if and only if its Monge--Amp\`ere measure vanishes (\cref{thm:bounded-psh-maximality}); in particular, maximality is a local property for locally bounded functions.

The characterization of the inequality $(\ddc u)^n\geq f\eta^n$, where $\eta=\ddc|z|^2$, by upper test functions and the linearization of the determinant (\cref{prop:bounded-monge-ampere-linear-density}) are proved in \cite[Proposition 1.5 and Lemma 1.7, pp.~5--7]{EGZ10ViscosityV1}, the first arXiv version of the later article by Eyssidieux, Guedj and Zeriahi. The passage from upper test functions to distributional inequalities, which the proof in that preprint quotes from H\"ormander, is \cref{lem:constant-elliptic-upper-tests}. The trace matrices used there have determinant $n^{-n}$; multiplying them by $n$ gives our matrices of determinant one. The inequality for mixed products of smooth functions and its extension to bounded functions whose Monge--Amp\`ere measures have densities with respect to Lebesgue measure are stated in \cite[Theorems 1.1--1.2, pp.~1--2]{Din07MixedV1}, the first arXiv version of Dinew's article; the extension is attributed there to Ko\l{}odziej and B\l{}ocki. We prove the case of continuous densities, \cref{cor:bounded-mixed-continuous-density}, by linearization and decreasing convolution, and the case of a common measure in \cref{cor:bounded-common-measure-mixed}. At fixed potentials, the smooth measure displayed by Dinew is $(4\pi)^n$ times ours.

The Dirichlet problem on a ball with continuous density, \cref{thm:continuous-density-ball-dirichlet}, follows the argument of \cite[Theorems 5.13--5.14 and Corollary 5.15, pp.~143--149]{GZ17}. Since the automorphisms of \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms} move the center exactly to a prescribed point, the preliminary Lipschitz comparison with translations used there is not needed. Under such an automorphism $S$ the $n$-th root of the density transforms into $(f^{1/n}\circ S)|\det_{\mathbb C}DS|^{2/n}$; both the absolute value and the derivative are needed. The symmetric error is quadratic, and the half-sum is taken only once; this corrects the formulas printed on pp.~145--146. The stability estimate \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability} contains a term for the boundary data, which is omitted on p.~149, and its proof does not require monotone approximation of the boundary data and of the density. Densities are taken with respect to $\eta^n=(\ddc|z|^2)^n$; at fixed potentials, the operator of \cite{GZ17} gives $2^n$ times our measures.

The extension to bounded densities (\cref{lem:local-comparison-capacity-sublevels,thm:bounded-density-ball-dirichlet}) corresponds to \cite[Theorem 5.17 and Lemma 5.18, pp.~150--151]{GZ17}. In \cref{lem:local-comparison-capacity-sublevels} the comparison is made on strict sublevel sets, and only the shifted sublevel set is required to be relatively compact. In the proof of \cref{thm:bounded-density-ball-dirichlet} the approximating densities have an explicit common bound. Convergence in capacity of the approximating solutions is proved without assuming that the solution for a bounded measurable density is continuous inside the ball: Hartogs' lemma is applied on compact sets outside the exceptional set of quasi-continuity, and the opposite inequality is controlled by the common bound for the densities and convergence in $L^1$. Approximating the $n$-th roots of the densities by convolution extends the stability estimate \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability} to bounded densities.

Cegrell's class of bounded plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values is recalled in \cite[Section 2, p.~3]{Din07MixedV1}. The moment condition used there to approximate a measure by its averages over cubes is stated in Theorem~2.2, pp.~4--5, whose conclusions on approximation and stability are quoted from other sources. \Cref{thm:bounded-subsolution-local-energy-moments} establishes the moment condition for measures dominated by the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded plurisubharmonic function; it follows from B\l{}ocki's estimate \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} by integration over level sets, after truncating the function with zero boundary values and passing to a smaller ball. The constants depend explicitly on the mass of the measure and on the oscillation of the subsolution, and the estimate also holds in dimension one. By \cref{prop:canonical-grid-energy-control}, which combines it with the local $L^p$ bound \cref{prop:negative-psh-polydisc-lp}, the cube averages satisfy the moment condition uniformly. Applied to the solutions with zero boundary values, this gives uniform bounds for their local energy moments, and applied to the measure $\eta^n$, a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure, it gives compactness in $L^1$ along subsequences. At fixed potentials Dinew's operator multiplies measures and local energy moments by $(4\pi)^n$; because of the power of the mass on its right-hand side, both sides of \eqref{eq:local-energy-measure-moment} are multiplied by the same factor.

The convergence of the energy integrals of the approximating solutions is asserted in \cite[Theorem 2.2(3), p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}, where it is quoted from Cegrell. \Cref{thm:canonical-grid-self-integral-convergence} proves it for compactly supported measures dominated by the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded plurisubharmonic function, along every subsequence of solutions that converges in $L^1$. One ingredient is the continuity of integrals against a fixed measure that does not charge sets of capacity zero, \cref{lem:bounded-psh-fixed-measure-continuity}, which is proved with convex combinations of minimal norm and the fact that the exceptional sets of upper envelopes have capacity zero.

Convergence in capacity, as a consequence of the convergence of the energy integrals, is quoted in \cite[Theorem 2.3, p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}. In \cref{thm:canonical-grid-capacity-convergence} we derive it from the comparison of capacities of strict sublevel sets (\cref{lem:local-comparison-capacity-sublevels}) and the bound on second moments, using that the approximating solutions vanish on the boundary and that the limit is nonpositive. By \cref{lem:zero-boundary-truncation-mass}, truncation preserves the total Monge--Amp\`ere mass of a bounded plurisubharmonic function with zero boundary values; the lemma also bounds the change of the measure caused by truncation in terms of the energy, and shows that Monge--Amp\`ere measures with bounded energy are uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to capacity.

The definition of the solution as an envelope of subsolutions is recalled in \cite[Definition 2.4 and the following paragraph, p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}. We solve the Dirichlet problem with zero boundary values, and identify the solution with this envelope, for compactly supported measures on a ball that are dominated by the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded plurisubharmonic function (\cref{thm:compact-bounded-subsolution-ball-dirichlet}).

The mixed energy inequalities used in the approximation argument are recorded in \cite[Section 2, p.~4]{Din07MixedV1} and attributed there to Cegrell. For their proof by integration by parts and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality compare \cite[Lemma 5.4 and Theorem 5.5, pp.~168--170]{Ceg04}; Theorem 5.5 there has a fixed weight and exponents $1/n$. \Cref{thm:bounded-mixed-local-energy} is the inequality with $n+1$ functions that we need, with an explicit but weaker constant. If $\ddc$ is multiplied by a constant $c>0$, both sides of \eqref{eq:bounded-mixed-local-energy} are multiplied by $c^n$. \Cref{cor:mixed-products-energy-capacity-continuity} uses this bound to discard the sets where the functions are very negative before continuity in capacity is applied; the limit functions there are bounded.

Dinew's inequality for bounded functions and a common measure is \cite[Theorem 3.3, p.~9]{Din07MixedV1}. Its proof by approximation, Proposition~3.1 and Lemma~3.2, pp.~7--9, relies on results on the local Dirichlet problem, energies and continuity in capacity that are quoted from other sources. \Cref{thm:bounded-multifactor-common-measure} allows $n$ different functions; its proof uses the same approximation by cube averages, with these inputs proved in the preceding sections. The inequality for mixed discriminants, \cref{lem:multifactor-mixed-discriminant}, follows from the reversed Cauchy--Schwarz inequality given by \cref{lem:mixed-discriminant-signature}, and linearization of the determinant then gives the case of continuous densities, \cref{cor:bounded-multifactor-continuous-density}. In this bounded setting the common measure may charge pluripolar sets. The extension to unbounded functions in Cegrell's classes, \cite[Theorem~3.4]{Din07MixedV1}, is not treated in this chapter.

The computation on contact sets in \cite[Lemma 2.4.7, Step 1]{XiaSampleBook} uses Stampacchia's theorem that weak derivatives vanish almost everywhere on level sets, but it also asserts that a function in $W^{2,1}$ has a classical real Hessian. A complete argument is given in \cite[Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2, pp.~4--8]{DNT2019v1}, the first arXiv version. There $C^{1,\overline1}$ means that the Laplacian with respect to a metric is bounded, not that the full real Hessian is bounded, and elliptic $W^{2,p}$ regularity is used. In this book the vanishing of weak derivatives on level sets is \cref{lem:weak-derivative-zero-set}, proved with a smooth truncated chain rule, and functions with bounded Euclidean Laplacian have real $W^{2,2}$ derivatives by \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}. With these lemmas we prove \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact,prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density} for regular potentials and weakly differentiable barriers; the density on the contact set does not require the determinant of the barrier to be integrable on the whole domain. The operator used in \cite{DNT2019v1} is $\mathrm{dd^c}=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$, which is $2\pi$ times ours.

\section{Lelong numbers and multiplier ideals}
\label{sec:history-lelong-multiplier}
For the Jensen--Lelong formula compare \cite[Chapter III, (6.1)--(6.11), pp.~162--166]{DemBook}. We only need its form on Euclidean balls, \cref{thm:euclidean-jensen-lelong}, which follows from Stokes' formula and spherical means. Demailly's operator there is twice ours, and he measures growth against $\log|z|$; the density in \eqref{eq:lelong-ball-density} is written with our $\ddc$ and $\log|z|^2$, so that $\nu(\log|z|^2,0)=1$.

The coherence of multiplier ideals, \cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence}, and its proof by generation from a weighted Hilbert space of holomorphic functions are presented in \cite[Proposition 5.7, pp.~36--37]{DemAnalyticOnline}; page numbers refer to the manuscript on Demailly's web page. The weight there is $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$, which becomes our $\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ after doubling the potential. For the strong Noetherian property and the Artin--Rees lemma used in the proof see \cite[Chapter II, (3.22), p.~90, and Chapter IX, (1.1)--(1.3), pp.~403--404]{DemBook}. We prove the strong Noetherian property in \cref{lem:strong-analytic-noetherian}, and the uniform generation by elements of the Hilbert space, with bounded coefficients, in \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation}, using the completeness of the Hilbert space.

Siu's theorem on the analyticity of the upper level sets of Lelong numbers, \cref{thm:siu-lelong-level-sets}, originated in \cite{Siu74}. The proof through the orders of vanishing of multiplier ideals is Demailly's method; compare \cite[Corollary 14.3, p.~138]{DemAnalyticOnline} and \cite[Theorem 10.2 and Lemma 10.3, p.~42]{Bou23Notes}, the version of Boucksom's notes dated 11 September 2023. The proofs of the restriction formula and of subadditivity by products and the diagonal (\cref{thm:multiplier-restriction-subadditivity}), and the proof of the pullback formula by the graph (\cref{cor:multiplier-holomorphic-pullback}), are taken from \cite[(15.1)--(15.3), pp.~158--159]{DemAnalyticOnline}. In \eqref{eq:multiplier-restriction}, the restriction of $\mathcal I(\varphi)$ to $Z$ means its image ideal in $\mathcal O_Z$.

Strong openness, \cref{cor:strong-openness}, is due to Guan and Zhou \cite{GZ15Strong}. Our proof of \cref{thm:monotone-multiplier-stability,cor:strong-openness} follows their argument by analytic curves and extension in Sections 3.1--3.2, pp.~609--611, of the published article, and its monotone version in Section 3.3, pp.~611--612. The curve is constructed in \cref{lem:bounded-domination-curve} by blowing up an ideal, and the lower bound for the norms of extensions is \cref{lem:curve-extension-norm-lower-bound}. In the induction, the slices are taken in a fixed polydisc; by compactness of its closure, finitely many neighborhoods on which the weights are integrable give a common index before the extension theorem is applied, and the constant in the extension does not depend on that index or on the weight.

That a weight with zero Lelong number at a point has trivial multiplier ideals there, \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}, is a special case of Skoda's integrability theorem; compare \cite[Lemma 5.6(a), pp.~35--36]{DemAnalyticOnline}. Here it is deduced from the bounds for the orders of multiplier ideals in \cref{lem:multiplier-order-lelong-bounds}, which rely on the extension with prescribed point value of \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}. To compare with that source, note that its potential $\varphi=u/2$ has the same Lelong number, measured against $\log|z|$, as our $u$ measured against $\log|z|^2$, and that its weight $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$ equals $\mathrm e^{-u}$.

For discrepancies, the change of variables formula and the valuative criterion for multiplier ideals compare \cite[Proposition 10.12, Theorem 10.13 and Corollary 10.18, pp.~44--46]{Bou23Notes}; its conventions $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$ and $\log|z|$ are converted as above. We state the criterion, \cref{thm:general-valuative-multiplier-criterion}, for a holomorphic germ, with one positive parameter $\varepsilon$ that works for all divisors over a neighborhood of the point. For analytic singularities, the strict threshold on a log resolution in \cref{thm:analytic-log-resolution-integrability} involves every component of the resolved divisor that meets the fibre over the point, including the strict transforms of hypersurfaces in $X$, not only the exceptional ones.

\section{Global potentials and positivity loci}
\label{sec:history-global-potentials}
For elliptic operators on compact manifolds and the Laplacian on forms compare \cite[Chapter VI, Section 2, pp.~289--290, and Sections 3.1--3.2, pp.~291--294]{DemBook}. The results on Sobolev spaces, compactness and regularity that we need are proved directly in \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, by Fourier truncation, difference quotients and duality. The proof of the $\ddc$-lemma, \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}, uses the scalar trace equation and the identity for the Hodge star of primitive $(1,1)$-forms; the factor $-1/(4\pi)$ in the trace identity \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star} comes from our normalization of $\ddc$ and from the sign convention that makes the Riemannian Laplacian nonnegative.

The method of primitive representatives for the mixed Hodge--Riemann relations is developed in \cite[Propositions 2.3--2.4 and proof of Theorem A, pp.~5--8]{DN2005mixedv2}. For real $(1,1)$-classes, \cref{lem:mixed-scalar-poisson,thm:mixed-hodge-index} make a representative primitive at every point by solving a scalar equation in divergence form. The operator $\mathrm{dd^c}=\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial/\pi$ of that source is twice ours; doubling its correcting function gives the same representative. \Cref{cor:nef-mixed-intersection-geometric-mean} derives the lower bound of mixed intersection numbers of nef classes by the geometric mean of their volumes from the concavity of the logarithms of mixed intersection numbers, first for K\"ahler classes and then, by perturbation, for nef classes.

For positive cones, potentials with minimal singularities and the divisorial Zariski decomposition compare \cite[Sections 2.1--2.3, pp.~47--49, Sections 2.7--2.8, pp.~51--53, and Section 3.1, pp.~54--55]{Bou04}. These locators refer to the published article, whose title and numbering differ from those of the arXiv version of 2002. Siu's decomposition, \cref{thm:siu-divisorial-decomposition}, is proved by subtracting divisors with the help of the logarithmic growth established earlier; the convergence of the series of divisors and the uniqueness of the decomposition are then proved. The proof of \cref{prop:big-class-minimal-multiplicity} is the argument of Proposition 3.6 of the published article, by convex combination with a K\"ahler current. As in that article, minimal multiplicities are defined by means of currents $T\geq-\varepsilon\omega$; for big classes they coincide with the Lelong numbers of a potential with minimal singularities, and in general they are bounded above by these Lelong numbers.

That the non-K\"ahler locus of a big class is the pole set of a single K\"ahler current with analytic singularities is \cite[Definition 3.16 and Theorem 3.17, pp.~59--60]{Bou04}. Our proof of \cref{thm:positivity-loci} follows Boucksom's argument, based on the stationarity of increasing sequences of coherent ideals on a compact manifold and on taking maxima, with the approximation results of \cref{chap:q06}. It also proves the identity \eqref{eq:non-kahler-non-nef-allowance}, with conventions that include classes that are not big or not pseudo-effective. The last part of Boucksom's Theorem 3.17, a criterion in terms of restrictions to arbitrary analytic subsets, is not used in this book.

The conventions for psh metrics, written as quadratic forms, and for the Fubini--Study metrics defined by sections are those of \cite[Chapter on plurisubharmonic functions, Definitions 1.8.3, 1.8.6 and 1.8.7]{XiaSampleBook}. In \cref{prop:fubini-study-finite-operations} the convex combinations have rational coefficients, and closure under the operations is proved by clearing denominators of the degrees and multiplying sections; in this way the analytic singularities keep rational coefficients. The local weight of a metric in \cite[Section 2.4, pp.~49--50]{Bou04} is $\mathrm e^{-2\varphi}$; doubling the potential gives our weight $\mathrm e^{-\varphi}$ for quadratic metrics, and the curvature current is the same.

For the $L^2$ estimate for line bundles on K\"ahler manifolds, \cref{lem:kahler-bundle-l2-estimate}, compare \cite[Chapter VI, Section 6.1, pp.~304--306, and Chapter VII, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3, pp.~329--330]{DemBook}. We prove the commutator identity, the density of smooth forms for the graph norm and the solvability in Hilbert space, and we show that for forms of top degree the curvature term in the estimate does not depend on the auxiliary complete metric. Complete metrics and weights with logarithmic poles are also used in \cite[Theorem 5.1 and its proof, pp.~18--22]{Hisamoto2012v2}; in this preprint version, Theorem 5.1 assumes a smooth metric and Theorem 1.4 assumes projectivity. \Cref{thm:compact-point-extension} concerns extension from a point of a compact K\"ahler manifold; it is proved by applying the gentle approximations of \cref{chap:q06} to the error introduced by a fixed cutoff function, which is integrable. The weighted spaces of sections in \cref{lem:compact-section-hilbert-space} provide the finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and the curvature of the background used in \cite[Proposition~1.8.2]{XiaSampleBook}.

For the twisted $L^2$ estimate and the treatment of the error from the initial gluing compare \cite[Section 2, pp.~5--9, and Appendix, pp.~14--15]{Cao2017v2}; page numbers refer to the second arXiv version, posted on 3 October 2017 and dated 4 October in the manuscript. Theorem 1.3 there treats a submanifold defined by a global section of a vector bundle, under assumptions on curvature and approximation. \Cref{thm:compact-submanifold-extension} applies to an arbitrary closed connected submanifold $Y$; the pole along $Y$ is given by the function $R_Y$ of \cref{lem:global-normal-pole}, a sum of local squared normal distances weighted by squares of cutoff functions, and the estimate is first proved for weights with gentle analytic singularities. The local gain from the logarithmic factor is \cref{lem:local-critical-gain-extension}, the error of the initial gluing is controlled by strong openness and H\"older's inequality in \cref{lem:critical-gain-initial-gluing}, and \cref{lem:gentle-weighted-normal-residue} computes the limit over thin annular neighborhoods of $Y$ for gentle weights. Approximation by decreasing gentle weights then gives the estimate for the original metric, with the same constant. \Cref{cor:compact-positive-metric-extension} recovers \cite[Theorem~1.8.1]{XiaSampleBook}; the threshold for the tensor power and the conversion between the volume forms, which involves factorials, are stated explicitly.

The extension of potentials from a submanifold, \cref{thm:holder-positive-potential-extension}, follows \cite[Theorem 6.1 and its proof, Lemma 6.2, pp.~19--22]{DRWNXZ2026v3}; page numbers refer to the third arXiv version of the article published as \cite{DRWNXZ}. That source uses the same operator $\ddc=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/(2\pi)$ (p.~5) and the same notion of rational analytic singularities with bounded remainder (p.~6). The lemma on poles along the submanifold quoted there is replaced by \cref{lem:global-normal-pole}. Strict positivity near the submanifold follows from an estimate for block matrices, and the local extensions are formed as logarithms of sums of exponentials, using \cref{lem:background-logarithmic-sum}. The exponent of the normal pole is taken small enough to absorb the error coming from the H\"older continuity of the projection. Since the inequalities between extended-valued functions involved hold also at common poles, the indices that only matter near the boundaries of the charts can be discarded, so that the glued maximum involves a fixed set of indices near each point.

Josefson's theorem on compact complex manifolds, \cref{thm:compact-complex-global-josefson}, is due to Vu \cite[Theorem 1.1 and its proof, pp.~8--9]{Vu2018v1}; page numbers refer to the first arXiv version of the article \cite{Vu19}. The operator $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/\pi$ used there is twice ours. We follow Vu's argument, with a weighted sum of global poles and two extremal limits, but replace his comparison of capacities in exponential form by the estimate \eqref{eq:hermitian-extremal-capacity-bound}. Positivity of the mass is needed only for the one bounded limit function, not as a uniform lower bound over all bounded potentials.

For products with a background form that is not closed and the modified comparison principle compare \cite[Section 1, pp.~3--4, and Theorem 2.3 and its proof, pp.~5--6]{KN2015v2}, the second arXiv version of the article by Ko\l{}odziej and Nguyen, whose operator is again twice ours. The one-factor inequality needed there is \cref{lem:hermitian-one-factor-comparison}; we prove it for smooth approximations with a convex cutoff and a partition of unity, and pass to the limit by the continuity of Bedford--Taylor products with polynomial weights (\cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights}) and uniform approximation of the weights by polynomials. Of the modified comparison principle we need only the version with the fixed margin $\omega_v\geq\frac12\omega$, \cref{prop:hermitian-small-gap-comparison}. In \cref{prop:hermitian-extremal-mass-support} the background form is frozen on small balls, so that the balayage of ordinary plurisubharmonic functions applies; the error of this replacement is $O(r^3)$, and together with a local trace estimate it shows that the extremal function has no Monge--Amp\`ere mass outside the compact set. In this way the proof avoids solving the homogeneous Dirichlet problem or smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equations on Hermitian manifolds.

\section{Non-pluripolar products}\label{sec:history-nonpluripolar}
The definition of non-pluripolar products by truncation at finite levels, locally and on compact K\"ahler manifolds, is developed in \cite[Section 1.2, Proposition 1.4, Lemma 1.5 and Proposition 1.6, pp.~203--207]{BEGZ10}. Multilinearity is proved in \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit,prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus} by truncating all factors at a common level. In \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} the convergence of the restricted products is stated for Borel sets $E_k$ such that $\mathbf1_{E_k}\to1$ almost everywhere for the trace measure of the product; this holds if the $E_k$ increase and their union has pluripolar complement, whereas the condition on the union alone does not suffice. In \cref{ex:nonpluripolar-infinite-local-mass} the computation of the Hessian shows that the function is plurisubharmonic with the sign opposite to the one displayed in \cite[Example 1.3, pp.~204--205]{BEGZ10}.

That non-pluripolar products are closed, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}, is \cite[Theorem 1.8 and Lemma 1.9, pp.~207--210]{BEGZ10}. The gradient $\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$ of the product of a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a closed positive current $\Theta$, and the corresponding chain rule, are established in \cref{lem:bounded-psh-current-chain-rule} by decreasing approximation; the proof of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed} then estimates the error made by the cutoff functions. The gradient energy $\mathrm dv\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v\wedge\Theta$, with $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ as in \eqref{eq:dc-operator}, is used only as a positive measure, and no closedness property of it is needed. On a compact K\"ahler manifold, Stokes' formula for bounded potentials (\cref{prop:bounded-global-cohomological-mass}) and the mass bound with shifted backgrounds (\cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}) are proved before global truncations are used.

The lower bound for mixed products in terms of a common measure is \cite[Proposition 1.11 and its proof, p.~211]{BEGZ10}; it applies Dinew's inequality for bounded functions before passing to the restrictions at finite levels. \Cref{prop:nonpluripolar-multifactor-common-measure} is the statement for $n$ currents, deduced from \cref{thm:bounded-multifactor-common-measure}; the case of two currents, \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-common-measure-mixed}, follows by repeating factors. Since non-pluripolar products put no mass on pluripolar sets, the domination hypotheses force the densities to vanish almost everywhere for the common measure on the union of the local pole sets; this allows us to remove the restrictions to finite levels for an arbitrary measure. The bound for convex combinations, \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-common-measure-convex}, follows from multilinearity and positivity.

The compatibility of non-pluripolar products with Bedford--Taylor products of bounded factors, \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-bounded-factor-compatibility}, is \cite[Proposition~2.2.1(iii)]{XiaSampleBook}. Its proof uses the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate relative to a current, \cref{lem:relative-weighted-cln}, and the fact that Bedford--Taylor products with a current that does not charge pluripolar sets again do not charge them, \cref{lem:bounded-product-preserves-nonpolar-current}; the products are then identified with the help of bounded plurisubharmonic cutoff functions. For the identity in degree one, \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}, compare \cite[Proposition 3.3, pp.~3--4]{ElKadiri2023v3}, in its third arXiv version. That proposition concerns the domain of definition of the Monge--Amp\`ere operator and refers to an earlier theorem for the equality. We prove the identity first in one variable, by approximating the Riesz measure from below by measures with continuous potentials, and then in several variables by integration over the fibres of coordinate projections. Strong positivity (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-strong-positivity}) passes from smooth approximations, whose products are strongly positive by \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, to Bedford--Taylor products, and then to their restrictions to Borel sets, because for currents with measure coefficients it is a pointwise property, by the duality between the weakly and strongly positive cones.

The fibre integration formula and its application to mixed products on compact manifolds follow \cite[Chapter on non-pluripolar products, Lemma 2.1.1 and Corollary 2.2.1]{XiaSampleBook}. In \cref{lem:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} we prove the measurability of the fibre measures that is needed to integrate indicator functions of level sets, and \cref{cor:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini} passes to the limit along the increasing restricted products. The behavior under modifications, \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-modification-pushforward}, follows from invariance under changes of coordinates and from the fact that non-pluripolar products do not charge pluripolar sets.

The comparison of masses in \cref{lem:small-locus-mixed-mass-comparison}, for potentials that are locally bounded outside the closed pole set of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function, follows the argument with compact supports in \cite[Theorem 1.16 and its proof, pp.~217--218]{BEGZ10}. The auxiliary projective construction is due to Witt Nystr\"om; we cite the preprint \cite[Sections 3--4, pp.~4--9]{WittNystrom2017v1}, whose final version is \cite{WN19}. The projection onto the simplex and the integration over angles are carried out in \cref{lem:projective-simplex-fibre-formula}. With the exterior powers used in this book, which are not divided by factorials, the formula there contains the factor $\binom{n+N}{n}$, and the Hessian contains $2\sum_i\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i$. Both factors follow by direct differentiation and expansion, and they are missing from the calculation displayed in the preprint; the common positive binomial factor cancels in the proof of monotonicity. \Cref{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity} handles classes that need not be big by adding $\varepsilon\omega$, so it does not depend on the criterion for bigness by positive mass.

The invariance of mixed masses under bounded perturbations, the lower bound for limits in capacity and the monotonicity of mixed masses are proved in \cite[Proposition 2.1, Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 2.4, pp.~2054--2057]{DDNL18mono}. Our \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} uses the local continuity of weighted products under convergence in capacity and truncations at fixed levels; a bound with shifted smooth backgrounds gives compactness even when the original smooth forms are not positive. \Cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass} first obtains invariance from \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity} by polarization, and then monotonicity by decreasing maxima. Positive mixed intersection numbers and full mass are introduced in \cite[Definition 1.17, Proposition 1.20 and Definition 1.21, pp.~218--222]{BEGZ10}; \cref{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound} gives the bound for masses that we use. The comparison and domination principles, \cref{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison,cor:nonpluripolar-domination-transfer}, follow \cite[Corollaries 4.1--4.2, pp.~8--9]{WittNystrom2017v1}; they keep the comparison hypotheses on singularities, and pointwise domination requires a potential with minimal singularities, as stated there.

The version with varying weights, \cref{cor:weighted-nonpluripolar-capacity-limits}, is \cite[Theorem 2.6 and its proof, pp.~10--12]{DDNL2023survv2}; page numbers refer to the second arXiv version of the survey published as \cite{DDNLsurv}. This is the reference given in \cite[Theorem~2.4.2]{XiaSampleBook}. We keep the argument with a cutoff at a finite level and complementary weights, but work locally in every mixed degree before gluing on the compact manifold; the limit is taken with the theorem for bounded weights proved earlier. \Cref{prop:psh-capacity-topologies} establishes the statement of \cite[Proposition~2.3.1]{XiaSampleBook} for arbitrary monotone nets: the two notions of convergence in capacity are compared through quasi-continuity, with error sets measured by the distance $|\arctan u-\arctan v|$; decreasing nets are handled by compactness and increasing nets by Choquet's lemma. The equivalence is asserted for plurisubharmonic functions, not for arbitrary Borel functions with values in $[-\infty,\infty]$. The survey uses $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\mathrm i(\bar\partial-\partial)/(2\pi)$, so its operator $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ is twice ours. At fixed potentials and with doubled backgrounds, each product of $p$ factors is $2^p$ times ours, and this common factor cancels in the inequalities for lower limits and for masses.

That a class containing a current of positive non-pluripolar mass is big, used in \cite[Proposition~2.4.1]{XiaSampleBook}, is \cite[Proposition 1.22, p.~222]{BEGZ10}. There the criterion is attributed to Boucksom; in the published article \cite[Section 4.3, Theorem 4.7, pp.~1057--1061]{Bou02b} the numbering differs from that citation, and the volume is defined through the determinant of the absolutely continuous part. Our proof of \cref{thm:positive-nonpluripolar-mass-bigness} uses instead the Monge--Amp\`ere equations, solved earlier in \cref{chap:q11}, in the big classes obtained by adding $\varepsilon\{\omega\}$ to the given class, and the mixed trace estimate \cref{lem:prescribed-density-mixed-trace}. It also shows that a pseudo-effective class is big if and only if its volume is positive, without using an identity between the determinant volume and the non-pluripolar volume.

Lamari's duality criterion, \cref{lem:kahler-lamari-criterion}, is \cite[Lemma 3.3 and its proof, pp.~275--276]{Lamari1999}; the cohomological statement on annihilators is on p.~267. We prove the K\"ahler case that we need by extending a positive linear functional with the Hahn--Banach argument, together with the scalar Poisson equation and the identity for the trace and the Hodge star proved earlier. The pointwise trace estimate for smooth forms and the integrated Cauchy--Schwarz argument are \cite[Lemmas 3.1--3.2 and their proofs, pp.~4--6]{Popovici2014v1}, in the first arXiv version. \Cref{lem:prescribed-density-mixed-trace} states the estimate for an arbitrary strictly positive form $\tau$ of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, and extends it, by local decreasing convolution and linearization of the determinant, to currents with potentials bounded outside an analytic set; the first factor on its left-hand side is the full mass of $S\wedge\tau$, including any mass on the poles. The preprint writes $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$ for potentials, whereas our proof uses the normalization of this book throughout.

\section{Envelopes and model potentials}\label{sec:history-envelopes}
Envelopes with obstacles, rooftop envelopes and envelopes of singularity types are used throughout \cite{XiaSampleBook}. The argument for lower semicontinuous obstacles and the two reductions for quasi-continuous obstacles are in \cite[Lemma~2.4.3 and Theorem~2.4.4]{XiaSampleBook}. We follow them in \cref{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact,thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}, using the local homogeneous Dirichlet problem and the capacity theory of \cref{chap:q08}. Outside the pole set of a reference potential with analytic singularities, potentials with minimal singularities are locally bounded, so the argument can be carried out in charts where they are bounded, and bounded weights then pass through the local truncations. The concentration of the Monge--Amp\`ere measure on the contact set is proved without any differentiability of the envelope.

The concentration theorem of that source also allows pseudo-effective classes and quasi-continuous obstacles that are not bounded above. We obtain this generality in \cref{cor:pseudoeffective-obstacle-contact}, after the criterion for bigness: if the class is not big, every non-pluripolar measure of top degree vanishes, while in a big class, capping the obstacle strictly above the envelope changes neither the envelope nor its contact set.

For functions whose Laplacian with respect to a metric is bounded, and for the reduction to regular contact, compare \cite[p.~4 and Lemma 3.1, pp.~4--5]{DNT2019v1}. Such functions are locally in $W^{2,2}$ by \cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}, which is derived from the elliptic theory of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} with two cutoffs, the first at the level of $H^{-1}$ and the second at the level of $L^2$. For a regular plurisubharmonic potential, positivity and the trace identity then bound the complex second derivatives. \Cref{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density} applies the vanishing of weak derivatives on level sets to continuous barriers with bounded metric Laplacian; the computation on the contact set needs only this local regularity. The stronger elliptic $W^{2,p}$ estimate quoted in the source is proved separately in \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp}, by freezing the principal coefficients, absorbing the derivatives of lower order, and bounding the inverse of the shifted Laplacian in $L^p$ by testing the equation against a regularization of $u|u|^{p-2}$. \Cref{cor:metric-laplacian-barrier-determinants} then shows that the signed determinant of such barriers is integrable on all of $X$.

Approximation of envelopes by Monge--Amp\`ere equations with an exponential penalty, and the limit as the parameter tends to infinity, are due to Berman; the equations and estimates are presented in \cite[equations (2.1)--(2.2), pp.~282--283]{Tos18}, where smooth solvability is referred to Aubin and Yau. We prove solvability for positive parameters in \cref{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation} by the continuity method, with a contraction argument in Sobolev spaces and a priori estimates along the path. The computation with the difference of connections follows the tensor calculation in \cite[Proposition 2.8 and Theorem 2.9, pp.~24--26]{SongWeinkove2012v1}, the first arXiv version of Song and Weinkove's lecture notes, where the explicit formula in the parabolic case is attributed to Phong, \v Se\v sum and Sturm. Our calculation is elliptic: the penalized equation bounds the Ricci tensor and its covariant derivative in terms of the difference of connections. The constants in the uniform trace bound and in the convergence to the envelope (\cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace,prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}) depend only on the fixed geometry, on bounds for the obstacle and on an upper bound for its Laplacian. The source writes forms with $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$; in the notation of \cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}, the fixed matrix $h$ used for measuring represents $2\pi\omega$ and the background matrix $k$ represents $2\pi\kappa$, so that $g=k+\partial\bar\partial a$ corresponds to the equation written with our $\ddc$.

That the envelope of the minimum of finitely many functions with bounded metric Laplacian again has bounded metric Laplacian is \cite[Theorem 2.5(ii), p.~5]{DarvasRubinstein2014v1}, in the first arXiv version. We prove it in \cref{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop} by smoothing the finite minima and applying the uniform trace estimate above; the negative term given by the variance of the gradients preserves the upper bound for the Laplacian. Convolution in charts and the elliptic estimates for all finite $p$ (\cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp}) control the commutator with the Laplacian, whose coefficients vary; the metric used for measuring is kept fixed even when the K\"ahler background changes. In particular, the barriers are never assumed to have bounded real or complex Hessian.

The estimate for the full real Hessian (\cref{lem:penalized-real-hessian,thm:kahler-real-c11-envelope}) follows Tosatti's argument with penalized equations, \cite[pp.~283--287]{Tos18}. For the absorption of the negative term with third derivatives, Tosatti refers to the calculation of Chu, Tosatti and Weinkove, which is given in full in the preprint \cite[Section 2, Lemmas 2.1--2.2, pp.~4--10]{ChuTosattiWeinkove2017v2}; we cite this version separately from the published article \cite{CTW17}. Here the formulas for derivatives of eigenvalues and every absorption step are proved directly. The perturbation by a projection subtracts one from the eigenvalues other than the largest one; keeping track of this shift gives the harmless bound $2\lambda+3$ in the comparison of positive forms. The real metric associated with $2\pi\kappa$ is $2\Real(k_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\otimes\mathrm d\bar z_j)$, so that its Hessian trace is $2D_ka$. Since the estimate uses only an upper bound for the Hessian of the obstacle, the same proof applies to minima of finitely many smooth functions, and then to rooftop envelopes of finitely many $C^{1,1}$ functions.

The regularity of envelopes in big classes on the ample locus is \cite[Theorem 1.1, pp.~1347--1349]{DNT23}; this locator refers to the statement in the published article. The complete proof and the extension to finitely many obstacles are given in the preprint \cite[Lemmas 3.1, 3.3--3.5, Section 4 and Theorem 4.2, pp.~3--19]{DNT2023v3}. The smoothing by the flow of a Chern connection goes back to \cite[Proposition 2.9, pp.~5--6, Proposition 3.8, pp.~8--12, and Theorem 4.1, pp.~12--16]{DemFlowOnline}, a twenty-page manuscript cited with its own page numbers. In \cref{lem:chern-joint-smoothing} we carry out the computation with jets of a fixed finite order instead of an extension to infinite order, and compute the integrals of the weights directly. \Cref{thm:big-metric-laplacian-rooftop} follows the two successive smoothing estimates of Di Nezza and Trapani and their use of high powers of the equations of the divisors on a resolution. Local holomorphic changes of the radius give the required uniform estimates without introducing the total space of the line bundle used by them, and \cref{lem:varying-log-radius-minimum} handles the minimum over varying radii at the upper end point. We keep a fixed quadratic correction in the smoothing, keep the smooth background separate from the nonsmooth obstacle, and recover the varying metric Laplacian from the quadratic error by an expansion of the adjoint kernel. The final local estimate in real Sobolev spaces follows from the Euclidean Hessian multipliers of \cref{chap:p06}. Demailly's normalization $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/\pi$ is twice our $\ddc$, while the auxiliary operator in our kernel computation is $\mathcal D=2\pi\ddc$.

The formula for the Monge--Amp\`ere measure on the contact set for arbitrary singular potentials is \cite[Theorem 3.2 and its proof, pp.~5--8]{DNT2019v1}, where the method is credited to earlier work of Witt Nystr\"om. In \cref{thm:irregular-mixed-contact-density} we use the smooth decreasing approximations of \cref{lem:smooth-decreasing-usc-approximation}, the regularity of rooftop envelopes, the continuity of products with bounded weights and the vanishing of weak derivatives on level sets. We simplify the passage to the limit in the weights at levels by restricting the limiting inequality to the contact set and cancelling the positive weight there, so that a single truncation level and a single positive parameter suffice. For a general barrier, whose metric Laplacian need not control its full Hessian, we keep the regular intermediate envelope. A comparison of polynomials handles a positive shift of the background and polarization. \Cref{cor:big-regular-obstacle-density,cor:pseudoeffective-regular-obstacle-density} give the formula of the source for the density on the contact set of an ordinary obstacle: the first for big classes; the second, proved after the criterion for bigness, also covers pseudo-effective classes that are not big, in which all non-pluripolar measures of top degree vanish. All measures are taken with our normalization; with the operator $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$ of the source, products of $n$ factors, pure or mixed, are $(2\pi)^n$ times ours.

The argument showing that envelopes of singularity types preserve mixed masses is in \cite[Section 3B, p.~2060]{DDNL18mono}; the continuity along increasing sequences that it requires is \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity,prop:p-envelope-calculus}. The bounds for the Monge--Amp\`ere measures of rooftop envelopes and of envelopes of singularity types are \cite[Lemma 3.7 and Theorem 3.8, pp.~2060--2061]{DDNL18mono}. In \cref{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure,cor:rooftop-contact-inequality,prop:p-envelope-contact-concentration} they are derived from convergence within a bounded singularity type and from the theorem on contact sets for obstacles. Here a measure is concentrated on the contact set if the complement of this Borel set has measure zero; this says nothing about the closed support of the measure.

The extrapolation argument and the domination principle (\cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation,thm:relative-full-mass-domination}) follow \cite[Lemma~2.4.4 and Theorem~2.4.5]{XiaSampleBook}. In \cref{lem:mass-preserving-model-majorant}, a fixed point that preserves the mass is obtained as the regularized limit of an increasing sequence that almost maximizes an integral height at every step; this replaces the transfinite iteration in the proof in the source. Compare \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization,prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization} with \cite[Theorems 3.12 and 3.14, pp.~2063--2064]{DDNL18mono}. There the domination principle is derived from the construction with prescribed densities in Corollary 3.9, p.~2062, whereas here, in a big class, it follows from extrapolation and the concentration on contact sets. The example \cref{ex:loglog-full-mass-envelope} with a $\log\log$ singularity follows \cite[Example~3.1.1]{XiaSampleBook}; its mass is computed with the identity in degree one, \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}.

The criterion for the existence of rooftop envelopes, the substitution of potentials of full mass and the mass inequality (\cref{prop:positive-mass-rooftop-existence,prop:rooftop-full-mass-substitution,thm:rooftop-mass-diamond}) follow \cite[Proposition~3.1.5, Lemma~3.1.2 and Theorem~3.1.3]{XiaSampleBook}. The bound for upper limits needed there is isolated in \cref{lem:decreasing-zero-contact-mass}. Its proof uses bounded weights at levels and domination by the contact measure of the potential with minimal singularities; it does not claim weak convergence of the measures along a decreasing sequence, which may lose mass.

Envelopes with an obstacle and a constraint on the singularity type (\cref{def:singularity-constrained-obstacle,prop:singularity-obstacle-calculus,prop:singularity-obstacle-contact}) extend \cite[Definition~3.1.4]{XiaSampleBook}. The bound on the contact set for bounded quasi-continuous obstacles follows from the theorems on relative envelopes and on concentration for obstacles; it does not identify a smooth density. \Cref{prop:relative-mixed-comparison} is \cite[Proposition~3.1.15]{XiaSampleBook}, with an explicit defect term for potentials that do not have full mass. The relative capacity of \cref{def:relative-monge-ampere-capacity}, defined with competitors between $\phi-1$ and $\phi$, reduces to \cite[Definition~2.3.1]{XiaSampleBook} when $\phi$ has minimal singularities. Its basic properties and the estimate for sublevel sets (\cref{prop:relative-capacity-basic,prop:relative-capacity-sublevel-estimate}) follow directly from multilinearity, positivity and the relative comparison principle. \Cref{thm:model-order-rooftop-extrapolation} gives a complete proof of \cite[Theorem~6.1.1]{XiaSampleBook}; in big classes it provides the margin for rooftop envelopes used in \cite[Theorem 3.1.1]{VuXiaNakamaye}.

Our definition of model potentials keeps the condition of positive mass from \cite[Definition~3.1.3]{XiaSampleBook}. For the constraints by multiplier ideals used in the chapter on envelopes of that book, \cref{prop:multiplier-singularity-order} combines the uniform valuative criterion \cref{thm:general-valuative-multiplier-criterion} with the formula $\nu(v,E)=\inf_j\nu(v_j,E)$ for the Lelong numbers along divisors of an upper envelope $v=(\sup_jv_j)^*$, \cref{lem:divisorial-envelope-closure}; in the proof of the latter, a local pullback argument shows that these Lelong numbers are preserved under the countable selection of Choquet's lemma. This provides the closure property used in \cite[Propositions~3.2.6 and~3.2.9]{XiaSampleBook}; the construction itself and its algebraic consequences are not treated here.

The comparison of capacities in \cref{sec:global-relative-capacity-comparison} follows Lu \cite[Lemmas 3.1--3.4 and Theorem 3.5, pp.~8--13]{Lu2020v1}; page numbers refer to this 17-page arXiv version of the article \cite{Lu21}. Integration by parts for bounded potentials on a compact manifold, \cref{lem:bounded-global-integration-by-parts}, is derived from the calculus of gradients in \cref{sec:bt-current-gradients} and from weighted products; approximation by smooth bounded potentials and \cref{lem:global-singular-weighted-cln} then give the weighted estimate. The envelopes with increasing penalties of \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope} provide the concentration on compact sets and the two growth bounds, which replace the properties of relative extremal functions quoted in the preprint. The comparison then proceeds in three steps: powers of model potentials in K\"ahler classes (\cref{prop:kahler-model-capacity-powers}), comparison downwards for potentials of full mass (\cref{lem:full-mass-capacity-downward-comparison}), and comparison in both directions (\cref{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison}). The upper and lower comparisons use the different model references $P_\kappa[\psi]$ and $P_\theta[\psi]$, and the amplification parameter is the chosen $b>1$ throughout. The theorem assumes that the class is big; positive mass is not used to deduce bigness. Finally, \cref{prop:global-local-capacity-equivalence} compares, by a local extension construction, these capacities with the local capacities of \cref{chap:q08}; in particular, local convergence in capacity implies convergence in each relative capacity.

\section{Relative energy and integration by parts}\label{sec:history-energy}
The identity for differences of bounded type, \cref{lem:singular-monge-ampere-difference-identity}, follows \cite[Lemma 4.1, pp.~13--15]{Lu2020v1}. Its proof reduces to bounded potentials by truncation; for a strictly positive background, an average of the potentials provides a reference of positive mass that controls capacities uniformly, and an expansion in polynomials then removes this restriction on the background. The general integration by parts formula, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-bounded-difference-integration-by-parts}, follows the argument by differentiation and polarization in \cite[Proof of Theorem 1.2, pp.~15--16]{Lu2020v1}; as above, these page numbers refer to the arXiv version. In his introduction, pp.~1--3, Lu credits the earlier integration by parts formula to Mingchen Xia. The general formula is stated in the introduction of \cite[Theorem 1.1 and discussion, pp.~1--2]{Xia2019v1}, the first arXiv version, which also describes a proof by a projective construction; the proof followed here is Lu's later argument with capacities, based on the capacity theory and on Stokes' formula for bounded potentials established in the preceding chapters. For a difference $b$ of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, the product $\ddc b\wedge\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{n-1}\rangle$ is defined through differences of positive non-pluripolar products, and its independence of the chosen decomposition is proved before it is used.

Relative energy classes and the relative energy are defined in \cite[Definitions~3.1.5--3.1.6]{XiaSampleBook}, and the cocycle formula for potentials of bounded type, the bounds by tangents, concavity and continuity along decreasing sequences are \cite[Lemmas~3.1.3--3.1.5 and Proposition~3.1.16]{XiaSampleBook}. In \cref{prop:bounded-type-energy-variation-and-cocycle} the first variation of the energy along a segment is computed directly from the integral formula for the energy and the integration by parts theorem, and the cocycle formula follows by integrating it. In \cref{prop:relative-energy-canonical-truncations,prop:relative-energy-decreasing-continuity} the energy is extended by means of the canonical truncations $\max\{u,\phi-k\}$, and continuity is proved for decreasing nets, not only for sequences, the limit being the pointwise limit.

For \cref{thm:relative-finite-energy-characterization,lem:relative-cross-energy-estimate,prop:relative-finite-energy-cocycle} compare \cite[Proposition~3.1.17, Lemma~3.1.6, Corollary~3.1.6 and Proposition~3.1.18]{XiaSampleBook}. In our proof, a uniform bound at finite levels for the integral of a potential against its own Monge--Amp\`ere measure bounds the mass discarded by truncation by a constant divided by the level, so that finite energy by itself implies full mass. The estimate for mixed tails, \cref{lem:relative-cross-energy-estimate}, then allows each term of the cocycle formula to pass to the limit, using only the comparison principle, plurifine locality and the integrability of each potential against its own measure.

Continuity of the energy along increasing nets, \cref{prop:relative-energy-increasing-continuity}, follows \cite[Proposition~3.1.20]{XiaSampleBook}; the uniform bound for the tails is \eqref{eq:relative-energy-uniform-truncation-error}. The proof of \cite[Proposition~3.1.21]{XiaSampleBook} uses an exponential Monge--Amp\`ere equation; in \cref{prop:relative-finite-energy-rooftop-closure} we instead integrate the bound for the measures of rooftop envelopes on contact sets, \cref{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality}. This closure property guarantees that the slices of Perron envelopes have finite energy. The closure under upper envelopes of families and the envelopes of mutual singularities follow \cite[Propositions~3.1.23--3.1.24]{XiaSampleBook}.

The derivative of the energy along affine segments, \cref{prop:finite-energy-affine-first-variation}, also appears in \cite[Lemma~3.1.11]{XiaSampleBook}. Since potentials of finite energy are integrable with respect to the mixed measures of one another, the coefficients are finite, and the derivative follows from the cocycle formula, which is a polynomial in the parameter. The family of smooth potentials on $\mathbb P^1$ in \cref{ex:lone-convergence-energy-drop} shows that convergence in $L^1$ does not imply convergence of the energy.

The characterization of solutions as maximizers of a functional and the derivative of the energy of projections (\cref{thm:relative-variational-characterization,prop:projected-energy-first-variation}) follow the variational approach of \cite[Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.2, pp.~207--209]{BBGZ13}, whose normalization of the operator agrees with ours. We prove the derivative of the energy of projections directly, with a model potential as reference, from the theorem on contact sets and the bounds of the energy by tangents.

For Monge--Amp\`ere equations relative to a model potential, with prescribed measure and with an exponential factor, compare \cite[Theorem 4.3, p.~20; Theorem 5.3 and Lemma 5.4, pp.~24--25]{DDNL2020equationsv3}; page numbers refer to the third arXiv version. That source uses $\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial$; its potential corresponding to our $u$ is $u/(2\pi)$, and its exponential parameter is $2\pi\lambda$. We solve the equations with bounded densities (\cref{thm:bounded-density-relative-prescribed-measure,thm:bounded-density-relative-exponential-equation}) by compactness in the variational approach. The relative comparison and domination principles give \cref{prop:relative-exponential-equation-domination} for every finite non-pluripolar measure, when the reference has positive mass.

For the continuity of integrals against a fixed non-pluripolar measure compare \cite[Theorem 3.10, pp.~203--205]{BBGZ13}. \Cref{lem:bounded-psh-nonpluripolar-measure-continuity} proves the case of uniformly bounded functions, with weak compactness in Hilbert space, projection onto the closed convex hulls of tails and the pluripolarity of the exceptional sets of upper envelopes. Applied to exponentials, it allows the same variational construction for arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measures. The lower bound for limits in \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-measure-lower-bound-lone-limit} follows \cite[Lemma 2.8, pp.~12--13]{DDNL2020equationsv3}; a fixed lower measure is restricted before weak limits are taken. The existence of solutions with prescribed measure, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-relative-prescribed-measure-existence}, is obtained instead by letting the exponential parameter tend to zero; for the existence statement compare Theorem 4.7, p.~23, of that preprint.

Uniqueness for the relative equations, \cite[Theorem 4.29 and its proof, pp.~2079--2080]{DDNL18mono}, adapts the argument of Ko\l{}odziej and Dinew based on the concentration of mass; in that section the model reference has positive mass and small unbounded locus. Our \cref{thm:relative-prescribed-measure-uniqueness} allows an arbitrary model reference of positive mass. It follows the same strategy, using the existence theorem, the comparison and domination principles for full mass and the lower bound for products with respect to a common measure (\cref{cor:nonpluripolar-common-measure-convex}): an auxiliary measure concentrated on a strict sublevel set is compared with a convex combination of the two solutions, and the domination principle is needed to turn inequalities almost everywhere for the common measure into pointwise inequalities.

The iteration with capacities in \cref{thm:relative-capacity-dominated-boundedness,cor:relative-lp-capacity-boundedness} follows \cite[Theorem 3.3 and its proof, pp.~14--15]{DDNL2020equationsv3}. Here the measure is assumed to be dominated by the power $1+\delta$ of the capacity, and an iteration with geometrically decreasing steps shows directly that the capacities of the relevant sublevel sets vanish beyond a finite level. The uniform bound for moments is \cref{cor:normalized-global-psh-lp-bound}.

The estimates of volume and of measures with $L^p$ densities by capacity (\cref{prop:relative-volume-capacity-exponential,cor:relative-lp-capacity-domination}) follow \cite[Proposition 4.30, pp.~2081--2082]{DDNL18mono}, which assumes a small unbounded locus and uses uniform Skoda integrability; the formulation for arbitrary model potentials is \cite[Lemma 3.9 and Proposition 3.10, p.~18]{DDNL2020equationsv3}. Here the prerequisites are the uniform exponential bound \cref{cor:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound} and the penalized envelopes of \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope}. At fixed potentials, the background $2\pi\theta$ of the source gives measures and capacities that are $(2\pi)^n$ times ours, while their ratios to the mass of the reference are unchanged.

The inequality between mixed masses and the geometric mean of the masses, with its consequences for the concavity of roots and logarithms of masses, is \cite[Theorem 6.1 and its proof, p.~26]{DDNL2020equationsv3}; this is the third arXiv version of the article of Darvas, Di Nezza and Lu published as \cite{DDNL19log}, and it is the result referred to in \cite[Theorem~2.4.1]{XiaSampleBook}. \Cref{thm:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean} follows the proof in the source by relative Monge--Amp\`ere equations. It uses the estimate for a common measure, the characterization of model potentials, the preservation of mixed masses by envelopes and the criterion for bigness, and for this reason it is placed after that criterion. The manifold is assumed to be connected, and vanishing masses and the conventions for logarithms at the end points are treated separately. At fixed potentials, the operator $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$ of the source multiplies all measures of top degree by $(2\pi)^n$; by homogeneity this factor cancels in the inequality.

The identities for envelopes of arbitrary mass in \cref{thm:arbitrary-mass-ceiling-envelope} are \cite[Theorem 1.2, p.~4]{PSWZ26}, an arXiv preprint. Their proof in Section~5, Theorem~5.1, pp.~37--42, uses a uniform bound for normalized tails and the recovery of volume for perturbations of positive mass; \cref{prop:positive-mass-singularity-envelope-limit} follows this approach. Monotonicity gives the limit directly, and the solutions with constant density that are needed are provided by \cref{thm:bounded-density-relative-prescribed-measure}. Applying the limit to a competitor for the ceiling proves the identity $C_\theta(u)=P_\theta[u]$, without a separate formula for the regularization of the ceiling and without a second family of perturbations. The source uses the operator $\mathrm{dd^c}=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/\pi$ (p.~7). At fixed potentials, its backgrounds $2\theta$ and $2\omega$ give measures of top degree that are $2^n$ times ours, while the normalized tail ratios and the constant densities of the auxiliary equations are unchanged.

\section{Weak geodesics}\label{sec:history-weak-geodesics}
The definition of subgeodesics on a strip and their convexity and boundary behavior (\cref{def:relative-subgeodesic,prop:subgeodesic-convexity-and-continuity}) follow \cite[Definition~4.1.1 and Proposition~4.1.2]{XiaSampleBook}, and \cref{lem:subgeodesic-family-upper-envelope,lem:subgeodesic-operations-and-pasting,thm:relative-full-mass-perron-geodesic} follow the arguments of \cite[Proposition~4.1.3, Lemma~4.1.1 and Proposition~4.2.1]{XiaSampleBook}. Kiselman's minimum principle and the identity for envelopes of mutual singularities show that the Perron envelope is not identically $-\infty$ and has the prescribed end points. The formula of \cref{prop:perron-rooftop-legendre-formula}, which expresses the Perron envelope through rooftop envelopes and a Legendre transform, follows by taking supporting affine functions of the convex function of time on each fibre where it is finite; the regularization in the space variable is retained.

That the relative energy is affine along weak geodesics, \cref{thm:relative-geodesic-energy-affine}, follows the argument of \cite[Proposition~4.3.3]{XiaSampleBook} on a compact extension of the strip. It uses the fibre integration formula \cref{cor:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini} and integration by parts for bounded differences, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-bounded-difference-integration-by-parts}. The vanishing of the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a maximal plurisubharmonic function, bounded or not, is \cref{lem:unbounded-maximal-nonpluripolar-zero}: the homogeneous balayages of the bounded truncations decrease to the maximal function, and continuity of weighted products in capacity removes the boundaries of the truncations. The maximality of the complexified Perron envelope, \cref{lem:perron-complexification-maximal}, is proved with its envelope under rotations of the annulus. Approximating the end points by their decreasing truncations then extends the identity from potentials of bounded type to potentials of finite energy.

\section{Extremal slopes and singularity cutoffs}\label{sec:history-geodesic-rays}
The identity for the supremum of the difference along a weak geodesic is \cite[Proposition 4.2.2]{XiaSampleBook}, in the chapter on rays; for the infimum, \cite[Proposition~4.2.3]{XiaSampleBook} refers to an analogous argument. \Cref{prop:geodesic-extremal-difference-slopes} gives this argument by reversing the segment and using canonical representatives; it also identifies the essential extrema with the extrema over the set where the potentials are finite.

The assertion on bounded shifts that follows \cite[Example~4.3.1]{XiaSampleBook} is justified there by comparing long segments with truncated end points. \Cref{prop:long-perron-cutoff-type-stability} makes this comparison quantitative: a bound $C$ at the terminal end point contributes at most $Cs/T$ at time $s$ on a segment of length $T$. \section{Convex duality and real Monge--Amp\`ere measures}
\label{sec:history-convex-measures}
For the separation of convex sets and for the local regularity and subgradients of convex functions (\cref{lem:finite-dimensional-convex-separation,prop:convex-local-regularity-and-subgradients}) compare \cite[Theorem 2.1.10 and Corollaries 2.1.11--2.1.13, pp.~44--45; Theorem 2.1.22, pp.~55--56]{Hor07}. Closed convex functions and Legendre conjugation are treated in \cite[Propositions 2.2.1--2.2.2 and Theorem 2.2.4, pp.~66--68; Theorem 2.2.5, pp.~68--69; Theorem 2.2.8, p.~70]{Hor07}. The duality statements that we use are \cref{thm:finite-dimensional-fenchel-duality,prop:convex-envelope-algebra}; in the formula for a sum in which the infimum is attained, both functions are assumed to be finite on the whole space.

The classes of convex functions, the formulas involving slope bodies and the real Monge--Amp\`ere measures follow the appendix on convex analysis of \cite{XiaSampleBook}; as there, the factor $n!$ is kept, with Lebesgue measure normalized by the lattice. In \cref{thm:alexandrov-measure-construction}, measurability with respect to the completed Lebesgue measure and countable additivity are proved using the fact that the inverse of the subgradient map is single-valued almost everywhere. \Cref{thm:real-monge-ampere-vague-continuity} concerns convergence against compactly supported continuous functions, and \cref{ex:real-mass-escaping-to-infinity} shows that convergence against bounded continuous functions requires an additional tightness condition. The mixed measures of \cref{prop:smooth-and-mixed-real-monge-ampere} are obtained from the positivity of matrices, convolution and polarization. We keep the convention of the source for full mass when a slope body has empty interior, and \cref{ex:convex-degenerate-full-mass-sum} shows that the hypotheses on slope bodies in the formula for sums cannot be omitted.

The lemma on the finiteness of rotation-invariant plurisubharmonic functions, \cite[Lemma 1.2.1]{XiaSampleBook}, is proved there by the logarithmic convexity of circle means, and it provides the finiteness step in \cite[Lemma~5.2.1]{XiaSampleBook}. \Cref{prop:invariant-psh-convexity,cor:toric-psh-finiteness} give a direct proof: smoothing preserves invariance under imaginary translations, the complex Hessian of an invariant function is one quarter of the real Hessian, and local integrability excludes the value $-\infty$; the converse follows from supporting affine functions and the submean inequality on circles. This result does not depend on Kiselman's minimum principle.

For the conjugate of the maximum of finitely many convex functions, the convex appendix of \cite{XiaSampleBook} uses \cite[Theorem 16.5]{Roc70}. \Cref{lem:compact-domain-finite-convex-envelope} proves the case of functions whose domains lie in a compact set, which is the case of the finite suprema in the chapter on ample toric varieties, \cite[Proposition 5.2.4]{XiaSampleBook}; compactness takes care of points whose weights tend to zero. The computation of the energy in \cite[Proposition 5.2.9, Step 1]{XiaSampleBook} integrates a bounded function on a noncompact space against real Monge--Amp\`ere measures; \cref{lem:full-mass-real-measure-bounded-testing} justifies this passage to the limit by a cutoff and the equality of total masses.

The asymptotic count of lattice points used in \cite[Section~5.2]{XiaSampleBook} follows from the case of saturated cones in \cite[Theorem 1.15, p.~939]{KK12}, where Kaveh and Khovanskii reduce the assertion to Riemann sums; their introduction states the more general theorem on the growth of semigroups on p.~929. \Cref{thm:convex-body-lattice-riemann-sums} gives a complete proof for convex bodies by means of fundamental cells, allowing arbitrary translations, bodies of smaller dimension and sublinear errors at the boundary. The continuity of volume needed there is \cref{prop:convex-body-volume-continuity}, and \cref{cor:bounded-boundary-error-lattice-count} isolates the two-sided bound used in the toric computation of dimensions.

The chapter on ample toric varieties in \cite{XiaSampleBook} relies on the theory of toric varieties developed in \cite{CLS11}. Here the smooth projective toric variety of a Delzant lattice polytope is constructed directly from its characters in \cref{thm:delzant-projective-monomial-charts}; its fan and its boundary divisors are identified in \cref{prop:normal-fan-and-uniform-ray-coefficients,prop:toric-orbits-and-prime-divisors}, and the basis of global sections given by characters, \cref{thm:toric-character-section-basis}, is obtained from Laurent expansions in the charts at the vertices. No general classification of toric varieties is needed.

The potential of \cref{thm:guillemin-reference-metric}, built from the function $G_0=\sum_\rho\ell_\rho\log\ell_\rho$, is Guillemin's reference potential. For the Legendre computation compare \cite[Proposition 2, pp.~7--8; Theorem 1, pp.~9--10]{CDG03v1}, in the first arXiv version. That source uses $G=\frac12\sum_\rho\ell_\rho\log\ell_\rho$ and holomorphic logarithmic coordinates $y+\mathrm i\theta$; with $x=2y=\log|w|^2$ we have $G_0=2G$ and $F_0(x)=2F(x/2)$, up to an additive constant. Its convention $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f=-\mathrm df\circ J$ gives $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=2\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$; with the curvature convention of this book the form is divided by $2\pi$. Smooth extension and strict positivity in the monomial charts are proved by the inverse function theorem at every face, including the vertices.

\section{Analytic spaces and modifications}\label{sec:history-analytic-spaces}
The Weierstrass division theorem and the Noetherian property of the ring of germs of holomorphic functions (\cref{prop:weierstrass-division,prop:analytic-noetherian}) follow \cite[Chapter II, (2.3)--(2.7), pp.~80--81]{DemBook}. For coherent sheaves and Oka's coherence theorem (\cref{lem:coherent-sheaf-algebra,thm:oka-coherence}) compare \cite[Chapter II, (3.8)--(3.22), pp.~87--90]{DemBook}. Local irreducible components, local parametrization, the Nullstellensatz, dimension and Cartan's theorem on the coherence of the ideal sheaf of an analytic set are treated in \cite[Chapter II, (4.1)--(4.31), pp.~91--100]{DemBook}; the algebraic facts on finite field extensions and discriminants used in these constructions are included in \cref{lem:finite-integral-algebra,thm:analytic-local-parametrization}. In our proof of Cartan's theorem, \cref{thm:analytic-ideal-coherence}, a single denominator works at all nearby stalks, which is more than finite generation of each stalk.

The Nullstellensatz and the strict decrease of dimension for proper analytic subgerms of an irreducible germ (\cref{cor:analytic-nullstellensatz,prop:analytic-dimension-drop}) are deduced from the same local parametrization: division by the discriminant identifies each prime ideal with the ideal of its zero set, and the connectedness of the generic sheets gives the decrease of dimension. The proof of the Nullstellensatz includes the description of the radical as an intersection of prime ideals.

The support theorem for closed currents of order zero, \cref{thm:analytic-order-zero-support}, is the case of bidegree $(1,1)$ of \cite[Chapter III, (2.10)--(2.14), pp.~141--143]{DemBook}. Our proof uses smooth normal coordinates, convolution in tangential directions and induction over the singular loci of the analytic set; the theorem is applied in \cref{lem:nondivisorial-pull-push-exceptional}. In the proof of \cref{prop:big-classes-under-modifications}, the pullback is corrected with the pushforward of a K\"ahler form, which gives the required strict positivity directly.

For universal denominators of weakly holomorphic functions and for normalization (\cref{prop:weak-holomorphic-denominator,thm:analytic-normalization}) compare \cite[Chapter II, (7.1)--(7.15), pp.~110--116]{DemBook}. The cusp and the pair of crossing lines in \cref{ex:cusp-node-normalization} illustrate the difference between being normal and being unibranch. The Remmert--Stein extension theorem and Remmert's proper mapping theorem are proved together in \cite[Chapter II, (8.7)--(8.8), pp.~118--121]{DemBook}. Our proof of \cref{thm:remmert-stein-extension,thm:proper-analytic-image} also treats them together, but it uses the resolution of singularities, \cref{thm:analytic-resolution-input}, to reduce to proper maps defined on manifolds, and it proves the statements on finite maps and on the dimension of hypersurfaces separately.

The resolution and principalization theorems, \cref{thm:analytic-resolution-input,thm:marked-order-one-input}, are the analytic versions of \cite[Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, pp.~611 and 614; Definitions 2.1--2.2, pp.~618--619]{BM08}; that the results apply to complex analytic spaces is stated on p.~611 there. These two existence theorems are used without proof in this book. The relative principalization theorem, \cref{thm:relative-principalization}, follows the lemma on relative principalization in the unpublished manuscript \cite[Lemma \texttt{relative-principalization}]{VuXiaNakamaye}. At each step, its centers in the ambient manifold divide the ideal by exactly one exceptional factor, as required for the controlled transform of the restricted ideal. In our statement the submanifold is closed and the ambient manifold is connected, so that the global blowups and the identities between ideals hold as stated.

The proof that proper bimeromorphic maps onto unibranch spaces have connected fibres, \cref{thm:unibranch-connected-fibers}, follows the argument with complements of branches in \cite[Appendix on unibranch spaces]{XiaSampleBook}; properness is used to place the inverse image of a small neighborhood inside neighborhoods of the pieces of the fibre. The local upper bound near analytic subsets of codimension at least two in a reduced space, \cref{prop:singular-codimension-two-upper-bound}, is proved by slicing with curves over a finite projection and the maximum principle for subharmonic functions. It complements the extension theorem on manifolds, \cref{cor:analytic-codimension-two-psh-removal}, which follows from the criterion in terms of Hausdorff measure.

The corollary on the realization of divisors, in the appendix of \cite{XiaSampleBook} on unibranch spaces, underlies the comparison of multiplier ideals with Lelong numbers along divisors in Part I of that book. \Cref{thm:divisor-smooth-blowup-realization} proves this corollary by principalizing the successive centers and decreasing the order of the Jacobian along a fixed divisor. The definition of plurisubharmonic functions on reduced spaces by local ambient extension, \cref{def:psh-reduced-space}, suffices for pullback by holomorphic maps, for the maximum principle on compact fibres and for the upper bound obtained by slicing with curves. On the manifolds used in Part I of that book, the descent of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions under modifications, \cref{prop:smooth-target-psh-modification}, is proved directly by extension across an analytic set of codimension at least two.
