\section{Measures and distributions}
\label{sec:history-measures}
The representation of positive functionals by measures is the Riesz representation theorem. The locally compact, $\sigma$-compact formulation is discussed in \cite[Theorem 24]{Tao09Radon}; \cref{thm:positive-functional-measure} gives a proof on Euclidean open sets by a metric outer measure and level-set approximation. The regularity and density statements in \cref{prop:borel-regularity,prop:continuous-density} correspond to \cite[Theorem 21, Exercise 22 and Proposition 10]{Tao09Radon}. The metric outer-measure argument includes the measurability construction; compare \cite[Theorem 5]{Tao10Products}. The product-measure and integral-interchange assertions in \cref{prop:tonelli-fubini} are the $\sigma$-finite Tonelli--Fubini theorem; compare \cite[Theorem 36 and its proof]{Tao10Products}.

Distribution theory interprets differentiation by duality against smooth tests. The local finite-order bound, mollification, and positivity-to-measure argument in \cref{def:distribution,prop:distribution-smoothing,thm:positive-distribution-measure} can be compared with \cite[Section 1, Exercises 4--5; Section 2, Definition 1, Exercise 15 and Lemma 2]{Tao09Distributions}. That source places the test function first and uses a conjugate complex structure on distributions. Our pairing is $\langle T,\chi\rangle=\int u\chi\,\mathrm d x$, complex linear in both variables, as required for the later current conventions. The derivative sign is the same after this conversion. The proof of \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing} includes the passage of Riemann sums through a distribution, rather than assuming that interchange.

The measure selection and compactness statements in \cref{thm:local-measure-selection,thm:local-measure-compactness} distinguish the countable test-family argument from compactness of a product of scalar intervals. The latter uses the axiom of choice, through the ultrafilter proof in \cref{lem:compact-product}. \Cref{prop:compact-net-characterization} gives the subnet construction for the precise Willard convention used throughout this book.

\section{Harmonic functions and boundary values}
\label{sec:history-harmonic}
The fundamental solutions, distributional regularity, Poisson formula and Harnack inequality appear in \cite[Theorems 3.1.2--3.1.7, pp.~117--123]{Hor07}. That source's fundamental solution has $\Delta E=\delta_0$, as in \cref{prop:laplacian-fundamental-solution}; our positive Green kernel instead solves $-\Delta G=\delta_x$. \Cref{thm:weyl} proves the homogeneous distributional regularity statement directly by radial averaging and convolution, without the analytic continuation assertion of that source's Theorem 3.1.3. Dimension one is included separately in the fundamental solution, Poisson formula and Harnack bounds.

The Perron replacement argument, resolutivity for continuous boundary data, and barriers are developed in \cite[Section 3.3, pp.~171--174, especially Theorem 3.3.3, Definition 3.3.4 and Theorem 3.3.5]{Hor07}. \Cref{thm:perron-harmonic,thm:perron-resolutivity} use continuous subsolutions and prove the required maximum and replacement properties before forming the envelopes. The equivalence between regularity and barriers can also be compared with \cite[Theorem 3.4.17, pp.~219--220]{Hor07}. Our \cref{prop:local-barrier} assumes a continuous extension positive on the nearby boundary except at the distinguished point; it makes no claim about the more general exceptional sets considered there. The Green construction in \cref{thm:dirichlet-green} has the boundedness and boundary regularity assumptions at its point of use and proves the exhaustion limit within that class.

\section{Subharmonic functions and polar sets}
\label{sec:history-subharmonic}
The comparison and mean characterizations, local integrability, potentials and distributional characterization are developed in \cite[Theorem 3.2.3, Corollary 3.2.8 and Theorems 3.2.9--3.2.11, pp.~143--148]{Hor07}. \Cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution} constructs the representative using a compactly supported Riesz potential and Weyl's lemma; the sphere means then prove uniqueness pointwise. Our Riesz measure is $\Delta u$, with no factor $1/(2\pi)$, so a plane logarithmic pole has mass $2\pi$.

For compactness and Hartogs' lemma compare \cite[Theorems 3.2.12--3.2.13, pp.~149--151]{Hor07}. \Cref{thm:subharmonic-compactness} states the distributional-to-$L^1$ implication with a common local upper bound and proves the integral bounds and convolution limit it uses. It does not assert the stronger Sobolev or measure-weighted convergence results of Theorem 3.2.13. The countable selection and regularization argument in \cref{lem:choquet-selection,thm:subharmonic-envelope} corresponds to \cite[Proposition 3.4.4, pp.~205--206]{Hor07}; completed Lebesgue measurability is sufficient for the unrestricted pointwise supremum. The local definition of polarity and the removal argument in \cref{def:polar-set,thm:polar-removable} can be compared with \cite[Definition 3.4.1 and Theorem 3.4.3, pp.~203--205]{Hor07}. The proof here uses local polar functions directly and supplies the connectedness conclusion through the maximum principle.

\section{Energy, capacity and exceptional sets}
\label{sec:history-capacity}
The continuity principle and the construction of continuous-potential restrictions in \cref{thm:potential-continuity-principle} follow the argument of \cite[Theorems 3.4.6--3.4.7, pp.~207--208]{Hor07}; \cref{lem:lusin-finite-measure} supplies the measure-theoretic approximation used there. The compact polarity criterion is \cite[Theorem 3.4.5, pp.~206--208]{Hor07}. Our relative capacity is the total Riesz mass of the regularized extremal function, with $\Delta u$ as measure and the positive kernel solving $-\Delta G=\delta$. It should therefore not be identified numerically with a differently normalized capacity.

Strong subadditivity, outer regularity and capacitability are developed in \cite[Section 3.4, pp.~209--216, especially Theorems 3.4.9 and 3.4.12 and Proposition 3.4.13]{Hor07}. The continuous-potential testing argument in \cref{lem:continuous-potential-testing} is the measure-testing part of \cite[Theorem 3.2.13, pp.~150--151]{Hor07}; the polar discrepancy conclusion in \cref{thm:polar-limit-discrepancy} corresponds to \cite[Theorem 3.4.14, pp.~216--217]{Hor07}. This continuation strengthens the almost-everywhere envelope equality proved in \cref{thm:subharmonic-envelope}.

For the Dirichlet identity and quasi-continuity compare \cite[Propositions 3.4.19 and 3.4.21, pp.~221--223]{Hor07}. \Cref{prop:bounded-green-energy} proves the $L^2$ gradient limit by a Cauchy argument and uses the explicitly symmetric ball Green kernel. Newtonian positivity and conditional logarithmic positivity in \cref{prop:kernel-energy-positivity} are proved separately by Gaussian integrals; the zero-mass restriction explains why an unrestricted logarithmic Cauchy--Schwarz inequality is not used. The minimization argument and \cref{prop:equilibrium-frostman} give the equilibrium characterization within these precise kernel conventions.

The atom-free polar-Cantor measure used in \cite[Example~6.1.3, labelled \texttt{ex:BBJ}]{XiaSampleBook} is supplied explicitly by \cref{prop:diffuse-polar-cantor-measure}. The source cites Carleson's book, p.~31, for its Cantor-set input. Our rapidly decreasing binary construction makes the measure's own logarithmic potential equal to $-\infty$ on its entire support. \Cref{prop:projective-logarithmic-measure-potential,cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal,ex:diffuse-polar-zero-nonpluripolar-mass} give the spherical potential, integrability and first-degree mass calculation underlying that example.

\section{Fine topology and balayage}
\label{sec:history-fine}
Fuglede attributes the classical fine topology to Cartan and the quasi-Lindel\"of principle to Doob in \cite[Introduction, p.~227]{Fug71Fine}. His preliminaries \cite[Section 1, pp.~228--230]{Fug71Fine} work in an axiomatic harmonic space with a domination axiom and a positive potential; they assume general sweeping duality before studying fine connectedness. Our \cref{thm:fine-quasi-lindelof} instead follows from the Euclidean polar envelope discrepancy and Choquet selection already proved. \Cref{ex:uncountable-polar-cantor} supplies the countability obstruction explicitly.

The compact reduction and sweeping statements in \cref{thm:compact-reduction,thm:compact-balayage,thm:finite-energy-balayage} use the symmetric Green kernel of a ball and the finite-energy identity proved in \cref{prop:finite-green-energy}. Orthogonality determines the swept measure and proves additivity; the manuscript does not use the unproved general axiomatic duality recalled by that source. The harmonic-measure formula in \cref{ex:balayage-harmonic-measure} retains the stated boundary regularity. The topological example in \cref{prop:classical-plurifine-distinction} distinguishes the real subharmonic generating class from the complex-line class directly, without transferring fine connectedness results to the latter.

\section{Fourier analysis and complex-line incidence}

For the Euclidean Schwartz-space inversion and Plancherel argument compare \cite[Exercises 38--42, formula (11), Theorem 43 and Exercise 44]{Tao09Fourier}. \Cref{lem:fourier-gaussian,thm:plancherel} use a real differential equation for the Gaussian transform, Gaussian damping for inversion, and simultaneous $L^1$ and $L^2$ approximation for compatibility of the two transforms. The convention is $\mathrm e^{-2\pi\mathrm i x\cdot\xi}$. Gaussian integration gives the exact Riesz-energy constant in \cref{thm:riesz-fourier-energy}; the kernel there is $|x-y|^{-s}$ without the fundamental-solution normalization.

For the dyadic decomposition compare \cite[Proposition 4.3 and proof, pp.~23--24]{Tao06Fourier3}; for the weak $(1,1)$ estimate and its interpolation and adjoint consequences compare \cite[Lemma 2.7, Corollaries 2.9--2.10 and proofs, pp.~7--9]{Tao06Fourier4}. \Cref{lem:hessian-multiplier-kernel} constructs only the Hessian multipliers needed here, from differentiated heat kernels. \Cref{lem:hessian-multiplier-weak-one,thm:hessian-multiplier-lp} give the complete cancellation and distribution-function argument, including the adjoint passage above $p=2$. \Cref{lem:gradient-lp-interpolation,lem:morrey-sobolev-representative} supply the interpolation and continuous-representative estimates used in the metric-Laplacian application.

The annular-star, complex-hyperplane projection and incidence arguments reconstruct Lemmas 1.3--1.5, pp.~2--4, of the supplied manuscript \emph{Subharmonicity on All Affine Complex Lines Forces Upper Semicontinuity}. That manuscript has no printed author or publication information. \Cref{lem:positive-circle-selection} supplies an explicit Borel selector for the actual closed high-value circle sections: the submean inequality gives them positive arc length, so an arc-length median suffices. No general compact-section uniformization theorem is needed for this argument. The invariant measures are constructed from Gaussian matrices, and the projective ball estimate replaces an appeal to manifold volume charts. \Cref{cor:borel-line-subharmonic-bound} retains Borel measurability as a hypothesis; the subsequent complex-line characterization must supply it before applying that result.

\section{Plurisubharmonic functions and invariant minima}
\label{sec:history-plurisubharmonic}
The automatic upper semicontinuity theorem \cref{thm:automatic-psh-usc} completes the argument of the supplied unsigned manuscript \emph{Subharmonicity on All Affine Complex Lines Forces Upper Semicontinuity}. Its Lemma 1.2, pp.~1--2, proves measurability by induction using bounded fiber means, and Proposition 2.1, pp.~4--5, identifies the given finite line-subharmonic function with a canonical representative. Its final proof, p.~6, uses truncation. The incidence prerequisite was reconstructed in \cref{chap:p06}; \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition} supplies the canonical representative's line restrictions independently of the upper semicontinuity theorem. Thus the usual definition remains in \cref{def:plurisubharmonic}, with its redundant condition proved immediately afterwards.

The distributional, envelope and compactness assertions in \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative,thm:psh-upper-envelopes,thm:psh-compactness-hartogs} use the real subharmonic foundations of \cref{chap:p03,chap:p04}. The discrepancy conclusion at this stage is real polarity in charts. The extension criterion \cref{thm:psh-small-set-extension} proves both local boundedness and removal from a Hausdorff-null hypothesis; translating analytic codimension into that hypothesis requires the local dimension estimate for analytic sets.

For finite-$p$ integrability and convergence, compare \cite[Theorem 1.48, parts 1--2 and proof Steps 1--2, pp.~30--32]{GZ17}. \Cref{lem:negative-subharmonic-disc-lp,prop:negative-psh-polydisc-lp,thm:psh-local-lp-integrability} use the same one-variable-to-polydisc mechanism, with compactly supported Riesz decomposition in place of a boundary Poisson--Jensen formula. Our Riesz measure is $\Delta v$, so its logarithmic potential has coefficient $1/(2\pi)$. The norm bound \eqref{eq:negative-psh-lp-lone-bound} gives uniformity directly on smaller polydiscs. H\"older interpolation proves convergence; the equality with factor two in the printed truncation display on p.~32 is false, as also observed in the supplied unofficial reader errata.

The partial Legendre transformation is due to Kiselman \cite{Kis78}. The invariant minimum principle and its regularization argument are developed in \cite[Chapter I, Section 7.B, Theorem (7.5) and its proof, p.~57]{DemBook}. \Cref{thm:kiselman-invariant-exhaustion} makes the invariant exhaustion an explicit hypothesis and proves the smooth case by a Schur complement. \Cref{cor:kiselman-product-tube} constructs such an exhaustion for every open convex product tube. The general pseudoconvex-tube statement additionally needs the plurisubharmonic boundary-distance criterion.


\section{Bounded products and nonlinear capacity}
\label{sec:history-bounded-products}
The recursive construction, bounded CLN estimates and decreasing continuity are developed in \cite[Chapter III, Section 3, (3.1)--(3.11), pp.~144--150]{DemBook}. \Cref{lem:bt-common-collar,thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} supply the compact-difference integration-by-parts identity and the weighted induction, obtaining symmetry at each stage before passing its weighted limit. The weighted $L^1$ estimate \cref{thm:cln-weighted} has base current $1$ and keeps the collar term; it does not extend that estimate to an arbitrary singular current.

For increasing continuity, maximum locality and comparison compare \cite[Theorem 3.23, pp.~89--91, and Theorems 3.27 and 3.29, pp.~93--94]{GZ17}. Capacity, quasi-continuity and capacity convergence appear in \cite[Proposition 4.19, Theorem 4.20 and Lemma 4.21, pp.~110--112, and Theorem 4.26, pp.~114--115]{GZ17}. The capacity normalization uses competitors between zero and one. Both cited sources use $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}=(\mathrm{i}/\pi)\partial\bar\partial$, twice our operator, so their $n$-fold measure and capacity are $2^n$ times ours.

The difference estimate is attributed to B\l{}ocki in \cite[Proposition 3.21, pp.~88--89]{GZ17}. Its printed oscillation power $M^{n+1}$ must be $M^n$: there are $n$ integration-by-parts substitutions, and the left side has degree $n$ in the test potential. \Cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} proves that power under an explicit common-collar hypothesis and passes to bounded potentials by polynomial-weight continuity. \Cref{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity} gives the capacity-zero discrepancy needed to identify exceptional envelopes with pluripolar sets.


The homogeneous Dirichlet construction goes back to \cite{BT76}. The exact-center automorphism averaging is developed for polydiscs in \cite[Section 2.3, Theorem 2.3.2 and Proposition 2.3.3, pp.~50--52]{Blo02Pluripotential}; \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms} gives its ball version. Compare also \cite[Theorems 5.13--5.14, pp.~143--149]{GZ17}. \Cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor} proves the almost-everywhere Taylor expansion needed to identify the determinant, using differentiation of integrals and the cube Poincar\'e estimate. The canonical replacement in \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} is the largest boundary-envelope solution for a locally bounded potential. \Cref{thm:bounded-psh-maximality} uses that replacement and bounded comparison; its local conclusion concerns locally bounded functions.

\Cref{lem:local-comparison-capacity-sublevels,thm:bounded-density-ball-dirichlet} give the bounded-density continuation in \cite[Theorem 5.17 and Lemma 5.18, pp.~150--151]{GZ17}. Hartogs\textquoteright{} lemma is applied on compact subsets outside the quasi-continuity exception; the opposite difference is controlled by that common bound and $L^1$ convergence. This proves local capacity convergence without assuming interior continuity of the measurable-density solution. Root convolution also extends \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability} to this regime.

Cegrell's bounded zero-boundary class is recalled in \cite[Section 2, p.~3]{Din07MixedV1}. The moment condition used for canonical approximation is stated there in Theorem~2.2, pp.~4--5; its approximation and stability conclusions are quoted from other sources. \Cref{thm:bounded-subsolution-local-energy-moments} proves the moment condition for measures dominated by a bounded local subsolution, directly from our earlier B\l{}ocki estimate and layer integration. Its common collar is obtained on a smaller ball after truncating the zero-boundary test potential. The constants retain the measure mass and subsolution oscillation, and the estimate also holds in dimension one. \Cref{prop:canonical-grid-energy-control} combines the earlier local $L^p$ bound with half-open cube averages to preserve that criterion. Testing it on each zero-boundary solution gives uniform local energy moments, and the quadratic reference measure gives $L^1$ subsequential compactness. At fixed potentials Dinew's operator scales measures and local energy moments by $(4\pi)^n$; the measure-mass factor in \eqref{eq:local-energy-measure-moment} gives the same overall scaling.

The self-integral convergence is asserted in \cite[Theorem 2.2(3), p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}, where it is quoted from Cegrell. \Cref{thm:canonical-grid-self-integral-convergence} proves the compactly supported bounded-subsolution case for any volume $L^1$-convergent subsequence. The finite-center local $L^p$ estimate then controls cell averages. Hartogs' lemma bounds their positive error, while the uniform second moment controls the negative tails and turns convergence in measure into convergence of the energy integrals.

Capacity stability from the self-integral error is quoted in \cite[Theorem 2.3, p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}. \Cref{thm:canonical-grid-capacity-convergence} derives it here from the earlier strict sublevel comparison and second-moment tails. The finite mass and total variation estimate for the limit's bounded truncations follow by bounded-product continuity and continuous approximation of Borel tests.

The subsolution envelope is recalled in \cite[Definition 2.4 and the following paragraph, p.~5]{Din07MixedV1}. \Cref{thm:compact-bounded-subsolution-ball-dirichlet} proves the compact-support, zero-boundary case on a ball. Capacity-controlled weights and fixed-measure energy continuity pass comparison to the canonical limit at null levels of both finite measures. A strict quadratic perturbation then bounds the limit and supplies its boundary trace; finite-mass comparison proves the envelope and uniqueness.

The mixed energy inequalities used in the approximation argument are recorded in \cite[Section 2, p.~4]{Din07MixedV1} and attributed there to Cegrell. Both sides of the resulting inequality scale by the same $n$-th power under a positive rescaling of $\ddc$. \Cref{cor:mixed-products-energy-capacity-continuity} discards deep sets using this energy bound before applying bounded capacity continuity; no product of unbounded limit potentials is defined.

The bounded common-measure inequality is proved in \cite[Theorem 3.3, p.~9]{Din07MixedV1}. Its approximation proof, Proposition~3.1 and Lemma~3.2, pp.~7--9, relies on quoted local Dirichlet, energy and capacity-continuity results. \Cref{thm:bounded-common-measure-mixed} uses the canonical-grid mechanism with those inputs proved in the preceding sections. The unbounded Cegrell-class extension in Theorem~3.4 is outside the scope of this local chapter.

The contact-set calculation in the supplied non-pluripolar chapter \cite[Lemma \texttt{DNTroof}, Step 1]{XiaSampleBook} invokes Stampacchia's zero-set locality, but also asserts a classical real Hessian from $W^{2,1}$ membership.
