\chapter{Non-pluripolar products}
\label{chap:q09}

In \cref{chap:q08} we multiplied the currents $\ddc u_1,\ldots,\ddc u_p$ of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions by the Bedford--Taylor recursion $\ddc u_1\wedge T=\ddc(u_1T)$. Many potentials that arise in global geometry are unbounded. On a compact K\"ahler manifold every closed positive $(1,1)$-current can be written as $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ with $\theta$ a smooth form and $\varphi$ quasi-plurisubharmonic (\cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}), and $\varphi$ takes the value $-\infty$ wherever the current has a positive Lelong number, for instance along a hypersurface carrying a current of integration. For such potentials the recursion breaks down, because it multiplies the measure coefficients of a current by a function that is no longer bounded. In this chapter we construct a product for arbitrary plurisubharmonic potentials which keeps positivity, closedness, multilinearity and locality, at the price of ignoring everything that happens on pluripolar sets.

The construction truncates the potentials and discards their poles. We replace each $u_j$ by $\max\{u_j,-k\}$, take the Bedford--Taylor product, and keep only its restriction to the set where all $u_j>-k$. On that set the truncated potentials coincide with the original ones. Since bounded products are local in the plurifine topology (\cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality}), the restrictions obtained for different $k$ are therefore compatible and increase with $k$. Their limit is the \emph{non-pluripolar product} $\langle\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle$; by construction it gives no mass to pluripolar sets. Two phenomena without counterpart in the bounded theory appear. The limit may have infinite mass on compact sets, as an example in a bidisc shows (\cref{ex:nonpluripolar-infinite-local-mass}). And the mass carried by the poles is lost: in degree one, $\langle\ddc u\rangle$ is the restriction of $\ddc u$ to $\{u>-\infty\}$ (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}), so the current of integration at a point of $\mathbb P^1$ has zero non-pluripolar product, and even a potential with zero Lelong numbers and trivial multiplier ideals can have zero non-pluripolar mass (\cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-nonpluripolar-mass}). When the local masses are finite, the product is a closed positive current (\cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}). Since the indicators of the level sets cannot be differentiated, the proof replaces them by bounded plurisubharmonic cutoff functions whose gradients are controlled by the masses.

On a compact K\"ahler manifold $X$ the local masses are always finite: every tuple of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents has a non-pluripolar product, and its total mass is bounded by a cohomological quantity (\cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}). The total mass is, however, no longer determined by the cohomology classes of the factors; it depends on the singularities of the potentials. The central result of the second half of the chapter is that it is monotone in the singularities: if $\theta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form, $\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\psi\leq\varphi+C$ for a constant $C$, then the less singular potential $\varphi$ has the larger mass (\cref{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity}). When all potentials are locally bounded outside the closed pole set of a fixed quasi-plurisubharmonic function, this follows from Stokes' formula applied to differences of potentials that have compact support away from that set. Arbitrary singularities are reduced to this case by an auxiliary potential on $X\times\mathbb P^N$, whose total mass is an average of the masses along a segment of potentials, with weights $Nt^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt$ concentrating at the endpoint $t=1$ as $N\to\infty$. Along the way we prove that non-pluripolar products are lower semicontinuous under convergence in capacity, and continuous when no mass is lost in the limit (\cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}).

Monotonicity has several consequences that are used in the rest of the book. Mixed masses do not change under bounded perturbations of the potentials. In a big class, potentials with minimal singularities have the largest masses; these maximal masses define the positive intersection numbers and the volume of pseudo-effective classes. The full-mass classes, the comparison principle and the domination principle at the end of the chapter are basic tools for the envelopes of \cref{chap:q10}, and the fibre formula of \cref{sec:nonpluripolar-fibres} is used for relative energy in \cref{chap:q11}. Big classes have positive volume; the converse, that a pseudo-effective class of positive volume is big, requires solving Monge--Amp\`ere equations and is proved in \cref{sec:positive-mass-bigness}.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-nonpluripolar}.

\section{Truncation and plurifine locality}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-construction}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $U\subset X$ be a coordinate domain, and fix an integer $1\leq p\leq n$. Let $u_1,\ldots,u_p$ be plurisubharmonic functions on $U$, and recall that $\{u_j=-\infty\}$ is the pole set of $u_j$. For each integer $k\geq1$ put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u_{j,k}=\max\{u_j,-k\},\qquad
 O_k=\bigcap_{j=1}^p\{u_j>-k\},\\
 S_k=\mathbf1_{O_k}\ddc u_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{p,k}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The functions $u_{j,k}$ are locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, so the product in $S_k$ is the Bedford--Taylor product of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product}, and multiplication by $\mathbf1_{O_k}$ restricts its coefficient measures to $O_k$. The set $O_k$ is Borel, because plurisubharmonic functions are upper semicontinuous, and it is plurifine open, because they are plurifine continuous; it need not be open in the ordinary topology. On $O_k$ every $u_{j,k}$ coincides with $u_j$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}
For $l\geq k$ the current $S_l-S_k$ is positive, and $\mathbf1_{O_k}S_l=S_k$. The currents $S_k$ converge weakly to a positive current $S$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition}
 \sup_k\int_K S_k\wedge\beta^{n-p}<\infty
 \quad\text{for every compact set }K\subset U,
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is the Euclidean K\"ahler form \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form} of the coordinates. In that case $\mathbf1_{O_k}S=S_k$ for every $k$, the coefficient measures of $S_k$ converge to those of $S$ in total variation on compact subsets of $U$, and $S$ gives zero mass to every pluripolar set. Moreover, if $E_k\subset O_k$ are Borel sets such that $\mathbf1_{E_k}\to1$ almost everywhere for the trace measure of $S$, then the coefficient measures of $\mathbf1_{E_k}S_k$ converge to those of $S$ when integrated against any bounded Borel function with compact support in $U$. This is the case, in particular, if the sets $E_k$ increase and $U\setminus\bigcup_kE_k$ is pluripolar.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $l\geq k$. On $O_k$ we have $u_{j,l}=u_j=u_{j,k}$ for every $j$, so by \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality} the level-$l$ and level-$k$ products have the same restriction to $O_k$. Since $O_k\subset O_l$, this is the identity $\mathbf1_{O_k}S_l=S_k$. Consequently $S_l-S_k=\mathbf1_{O_l\setminus O_k}\ddc u_{1,l}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{p,l}$, and this current is positive. Indeed, if $R$ is a positive current with measure coefficients and $\varphi$ is a smooth compactly supported strongly positive test form of complementary bidegree, then $R\wedge\varphi$ is a positive measure, since $R(\chi\varphi)\geq0$ for every smooth $\chi\geq0$; hence $\int_ER\wedge\varphi\geq0$ for every Borel set $E$.

Suppose that \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} holds. The trace measures $S_k\wedge\beta^{n-p}$ increase with $k$ and, by \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition}, converge to a locally finite measure. Hence the trace mass of the positive current $S_l-S_k$ on a compact set $K$ tends to zero as $k,l\to\infty$. By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, the total variation on $K$ of each coefficient measure of $S_l-S_k$ is at most a fixed multiple of this trace mass. Therefore each coefficient measure of $S_k$ is a Cauchy sequence in total variation on compact sets, and the limits are the coefficients of a current $S$, which is positive as a weak limit of positive currents. Conversely, suppose that $S_k\to S$ weakly for a positive current $S$. For every smooth compactly supported $\chi\geq0$, the numbers $\int\chi S_k\wedge\beta^{n-p}$ increase with $k$ and converge to $\int\chi S\wedge\beta^{n-p}$, which therefore bounds them; choosing $\chi=1$ on $K$ gives \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition}.

Assume now that \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} holds. Letting $l\to\infty$ in $\mathbf1_{O_k}S_l=S_k$ gives $\mathbf1_{O_k}S=S_k$. Every bounded product gives zero mass to pluripolar sets (\cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity,prop:bt-capacity-basic,cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}), hence so does every $S_k$, and by convergence in total variation so does $S$. In particular $S$ vanishes on $U\setminus\bigcup_kO_k$, the union of the pole sets of the $u_j$. Finally, let $E_k$ be as in the statement. Since $E_k\subset O_k$, we have $\mathbf1_{E_k}S_k=\mathbf1_{E_k}S$. By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, the total variation of each coefficient measure of $S$ is locally finite and absolutely continuous with respect to the trace measure. Hence $\mathbf1_{E_k}\to1$ almost everywhere for each of these variations, and dominated convergence proves the assertion. If the $E_k$ increase and $U\setminus\bigcup_kE_k$ is pluripolar, then $\mathbf1_{E_k}\to1$ outside this pluripolar Borel set, which $S$ does not charge.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:nonpluripolar-product}
If \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} holds, we say that the non-pluripolar product of $u_1,\ldots,u_p$ is \emph{locally finite}, and we call the limit $S$ of \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} the \emph{non-pluripolar product}
\[
 \langle\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle=S.
\]
The angle brackets distinguish this construction from a product that retains mass on pluripolar sets. The empty product ($p=0$) is $1$.
\end{definition}
\index{non-pluripolar product!truncation construction}
If all $u_j$ are locally bounded, then every relatively compact open subset of $U$ is contained in $O_k$ for large $k$. In this case the non-pluripolar product is locally finite and coincides with the Bedford--Taylor product, and we omit the brackets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonpluripolar-strong-positivity}
Every locally finite non-pluripolar product $S$ is strongly positive: $S(\varphi)\geq0$ for every compactly supported smooth weakly positive form $\varphi$ of bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$. This is the analogue for currents of the strongly positive cone of \cref{prop:positivity-cones}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First let all potentials be locally bounded. Locally they are limits of decreasing sequences of smooth plurisubharmonic functions. The complex Hessians of these functions are semipositive $(1,1)$-forms, and an exterior product of such forms is strongly positive, so their products pair nonnegatively with every weakly positive form. By \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, the Bedford--Taylor product is the weak limit of these smooth products, and therefore it pairs nonnegatively with every smooth compactly supported weakly positive test form.

Next let $R$ be a strongly positive current with measure coefficients. We show that strong positivity is then a pointwise property of $R$, and therefore passes to the restrictions $\mathbf1_ER$ to Borel sets $E$. Since $R$ is in particular positive, it has a trace measure $\mu$. By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero,lem:dominated-measure-density}, we can write $R=H\mu$, where $H$ is a $\mu$-measurable function with values in the real $(p,p)$-forms. For a constant weakly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form $c$ and a smooth compactly supported function $\chi\geq0$, the form $\chi c$ is a weakly positive test form, so $\int\chi\,(H\wedge c)\,\mathrm d\mu\geq0$; hence $H\wedge c\geq0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. Letting $c$ run through a countable dense subset of the weakly positive cone, we obtain a $\mu$-null set $N$ such that $H(x)\wedge c\geq0$ for every $x\notin N$ and, by density, for every weakly positive $c$. By definition the weakly positive cone is dual to the strongly positive cone, and the latter is closed (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}); by finite-dimensional cone duality, $H(x)$ is strongly positive for $x\notin N$. Conversely, this pointwise property gives $\int_ER\wedge\varphi=\int_EH\wedge\varphi\,\mathrm d\mu\geq0$ for every Borel set $E$ and every smooth weakly positive test form $\varphi$. Thus $\mathbf1_ER$ is strongly positive.

Applied to the bounded products in the definition of $S_k$, this shows that every $S_k$ is strongly positive. Since $S_k\to S$ weakly by \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}, so is $S$.
\end{proof}
\index{positive current!strong positivity}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}
Let $u_1,\ldots,u_p$ and $v_1,\ldots,v_p$ be plurisubharmonic on $U$. If $\ddc u_j=\ddc v_j$ for every $j$, then the non-pluripolar product of the $u_j$ is locally finite if and only if that of the $v_j$ is, and then the two products are equal; thus local finiteness and the product depend only on the currents $\ddc u_j$. The product is symmetric in its factors and local in the plurifine topology: if $u_j=v_j$ on a plurifine open set $O$ for every $j$ and both products are locally finite, then they have the same restriction to $O$. Finally, the product is multilinear. Namely, let $u_1'$ be plurisubharmonic on $U$. The product of $u_1+u_1',u_2,\ldots,u_p$ is locally finite if and only if the products of $u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_p$ and of $u_1',u_2,\ldots,u_p$ are, and in that case
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle\ddc(u_1+u_1')\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle
 &=\langle\ddc u_1\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle\\
 &\quad+\langle\ddc u_1'\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Similarly, for real $c>0$ the product of $cu_1,u_2,\ldots,u_p$ is locally finite if and only if that of $u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_p$ is, and then it is $c$ times the latter.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In this proof we write $P^{(m)}(w_1,\ldots,w_p)=\ddc\max\{w_1,-m\}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc\max\{w_p,-m\}$ for plurisubharmonic $w_1,\ldots,w_p$ and an integer $m\geq1$.

\emph{Symmetry and locality.} Each truncated product $S_k$ is symmetric in its factors, hence so is the limit. Suppose that $u_j=v_j$ on the plurifine open set $O$ for every $j$, and that both products are locally finite. The plurifine open sets $W_k=O\cap\bigcap_j\{u_j>-k\}=O\cap\bigcap_j\{v_j>-k\}$ increase with $k$. On $W_k$ the truncations $\max\{u_j,-k\}$ and $\max\{v_j,-k\}$ coincide, so the two level-$k$ products agree on $W_k$ by \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality}, and by \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} so do the two non-pluripolar products. The union of the $W_k$ is $O$ minus the union of the pole sets of the $u_j$, and the removed set is pluripolar, so it carries no mass for either product. The quasi-Lindel\"of argument in the proof of \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality} (see \cref{thm:plurifine-quasi-lindelof}) shows that $O$ and the $W_k$ are measurable for the completed coefficient measures, so the restrictions to $O$ are well defined, and they agree.

\emph{Dependence on the currents $\ddc u_j$.} Suppose that $\ddc u_j=\ddc v_j$ for every $j$. The distribution $v_j-u_j$ then has zero Laplacian, so by Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}) it is a smooth function $w_j$, and its complex Hessian vanishes: $w_j$ is pluriharmonic. The plurisubharmonic functions $v_j$ and $u_j+w_j$ agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere. Fix a relatively compact open set $V\subset U$ and an integer $C$ such that $|w_j|\leq C$ on $V$ for all $j$. Write $O_k(u),S_k(u)$ and $O_k(v),S_k(v)$ for the level sets and truncated products of the two tuples. On $V\cap O_k(v)$ we have $u_j=v_j-w_j>-k-C$, hence
\[
 \max\{v_j,-k\}=v_j=\max\{u_j,-k-C\}+w_j .
\]
The functions $\max\{v_j,-k\}$ and $\max\{u_j,-k-C\}+w_j$ are bounded and plurisubharmonic on $V$ and coincide on $V\cap O_k(v)$, and adding the pluriharmonic function $w_j$ does not change Bedford--Taylor products. Since moreover $V\cap O_k(v)\subset O_{k+C}(u)$, plurifine locality gives on $V$
\[
 S_k(v)=\mathbf1_{O_k(v)}P^{(k+C)}(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\mathbf1_{O_k(v)}S_{k+C}(u)\leq S_{k+C}(u).
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} for the $u_j$ implies \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} for the $v_j$ on compact subsets of $V$, and exchanging the two tuples (that is, replacing $w_j$ by $-w_j$) gives the converse. If both products are locally finite, the display and \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} give $\mathbf1_{O_k(v)}\langle\ddc v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc v_p\rangle=\mathbf1_{O_k(v)}\langle\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle$ on $V$ for every $k$. The sets $O_k(v)$ exhaust the complement of the union of the pole sets, which are the same for the two tuples and carry no mass for either product. Hence the two products coincide.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\emph{Additivity.} Let $u_1'$ be plurisubharmonic and put
\[
 E_k=\{u_1>-k\}\cap\{u_1'>-k\}
                    \cap\bigcap_{j=2}^p\{u_j>-k\}.
\]
These plurifine open sets increase with $k$ and exhaust the complement of the union of the pole sets of $u_1,u_1',u_2,\ldots,u_p$. On $E_k$ we have $\max\{u_1+u_1',-2k\}=u_1+u_1'=\max\{u_1,-k\}+\max\{u_1',-k\}$ and $\max\{u_j,-2k\}=\max\{u_j,-k\}$ for $j\geq2$. Plurifine locality and the additivity of bounded products (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}) therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonpluripolar-additivity-on-levels}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf1_{E_k}P^{(2k)}(u_1+u_1',u_2,\ldots,u_p)
 &=\mathbf1_{E_k}P^{(k)}(u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_p)\\
 &\quad+\mathbf1_{E_k}P^{(k)}(u_1',u_2,\ldots,u_p).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Before local finiteness is known we argue with trace measures. By \cref{prop:finite-locus-truncation}, for each of the three tuples the trace measures of the truncated products increase to a Borel measure, possibly infinite on compact sets, which gives zero mass to every pluripolar set; for a locally finite product it is the trace measure of the product. The set $E_k$ is contained in the level-$2k$ set of the first tuple and in the level-$k$ sets of the other two, so by the identity $\mathbf1_{O_k}S_l=S_k$ of \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}, the restriction of each limit measure to $E_k$ is the restriction of the corresponding trace measure in \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-additivity-on-levels}. Hence, on $E_k$, the limit measure of the first tuple is the sum of those of the other two. Letting $k\to\infty$, monotone convergence gives this equality on every Borel subset of $\bigcup_kE_k$, and hence everywhere, because the complement of $\bigcup_kE_k$ is pluripolar. Since all three measures are positive, the first is locally finite if and only if the other two are; this is the asserted equivalence of local finiteness. When the products are locally finite, \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-additivity-on-levels} and \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} give the additivity identity on each $E_k$, and letting $k\to\infty$ proves it. For $c>0$ the same argument applies to the sets $\{u_1>-k\}\cap\{cu_1>-k\}\cap\bigcap_{j\geq2}\{u_j>-k\}$, on which $\max\{cu_1,-k\}=c\max\{u_1,-k\}$, and proves the homogeneity assertion.
\end{proof}

Let now $T_1,\ldots,T_p$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on the complex manifold $X$. By \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential}, every point of $X$ has a coordinate neighborhood on which $T_j=\ddc u_j$ with $u_j$ plurisubharmonic. By \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}, local finiteness and the local non-pluripolar product do not depend on the choice of these potentials, and the construction is compatible with restriction to smaller open sets. We say that the non-pluripolar product of $T_1,\ldots,T_p$ is \emph{locally finite} if this holds near every point; the local products then glue to a positive current $\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle$ on $X$, which is closed by \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed} below. When $X$ is compact we also say simply that the product is \emph{finite}. Repeated factors are written as powers, as in $\langle T^j\wedge S^{r-j}\rangle$, and for $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j$ we also write $\langle(\theta_1+\ddc\varphi_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\theta_p+\ddc\varphi_p)\rangle$. The assertions of \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus} carry over to these products, with multilinearity in the factors $T_j$.
\index{non-pluripolar product!of closed positive currents}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nonpluripolar-infinite-local-mass}
Let $U=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb C^2:|x|^2<1/2,\ |y|<\mathrm e^{-1}\}$, put $L=-\log|y|$, and define
\[
 u(x,y)=(|x|^2-1)\sqrt L\quad(y\ne0),\qquad u(x,0)=-\infty.
\]
On $U\setminus\{y=0\}$ we have $L>1$, the function $u$ is smooth, and its complex second derivatives are
\[
 u_{x\bar x}=\sqrt L,\qquad
 u_{y\bar y}=\frac{1-|x|^2}{16|y|^2L^{3/2}},\qquad
 u_{x\bar y}=-\frac{\bar x}{4\bar y\sqrt L},\qquad
 \det(u_{j\bar k})=\frac{1-2|x|^2}{16|y|^2L}.
\]
Since $u_{x\bar x}>0$ and $\det(u_{j\bar k})>0$, the complex Hessian is positive definite, so $u$ is plurisubharmonic off the axis $\{y=0\}$. Moreover $u<-\sqrt L/2$ is bounded above and tends to $-\infty$ at the axis. By \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension}, the plurisubharmonic extension across the axis takes the value $\limsup u=-\infty$ there, which is the displayed value; hence $u$ is plurisubharmonic on $U$.

Off the axis, $(\ddc u)^2$ is the smooth measure with density a fixed positive multiple of $\det(u_{j\bar k})$, by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}. The sets $O_k=\{u>-k\}$ are open, since $u$ is continuous as an extended-real function, and they increase to $U\setminus\{y=0\}$; on $O_k$ the truncation $\max\{u,-k\}$ equals $u$, so $S_k=\mathbf1_{O_k}(\ddc u)^2$. Take $K=\{|x|^2\leq1/4,\ |y|\leq r\}$ with $0<r<\mathrm e^{-1}$. On $K$ we have $1-2|x|^2\geq1/2$, and polar coordinates in $y$ give
\[
 \int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{\mathrm dA(y)}{|y|^2L}=2\pi\int_0^r\frac{\mathrm dt}{t(-\log t)}=\infty .
\]
Hence $\sup_k\int_KS_k=\int_{K\setminus\{y=0\}}(\ddc u)^2=\infty$, and \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-local-mass-condition} fails. Thus the non-pluripolar product of an arbitrary plurisubharmonic function need not be locally finite.
\end{example}

\section{Closedness and cutoff gradients}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-closedness}
The indicators $\mathbf1_{O_k}$ cannot be differentiated, so closedness of the non-pluripolar product does not follow directly from closedness of the truncated products. We replace the indicators by bounded plurisubharmonic cutoff functions and control the resulting error with the gradient estimates of \cref{sec:bt-current-gradients}. Here $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ is the real operator of \eqref{eq:dc-operator}, so that $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\ddc$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}
Every locally finite non-pluripolar product is closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Closedness is a local property, so we may shrink $U$ and subtract constants to assume that $u_j\leq0$ for every $j$. If $p=n$ the product has top degree and is closed for degree reasons, so let $p<n$. Fix a constant strongly positive $(n-p-1,n-p-1)$-form $\gamma$ and put
\[
 \Theta_k=\gamma\wedge\bigwedge_j\ddc u_{j,k},\qquad
 S_\gamma=\gamma\wedge\Bigl\langle\bigwedge_j\ddc u_j\Bigr\rangle.
\]
The $\Theta_k$ are closed positive currents of bidimension $(1,1)$. By \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}, $\mathbf1_{O_k}\Theta_k=\mathbf1_{O_k}S_\gamma$ increases to $S_\gamma$, and in particular these restrictions have uniformly bounded mass on compact sets. We shall prove that $\mathrm dS_\gamma=0$.

\emph{Cutoff functions.} Put $u=\sum_ju_j\leq0$. Choose a smooth convex increasing function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$ with $\chi=0$ on $(-\infty,1/2]$ and $\chi(1)=1$, and put $w_k=\chi(\mathrm e^{u/k})$. Since $\mathrm e^{u/k}$ is plurisubharmonic with values in $[0,1]$, $w_k$ is plurisubharmonic and $0\leq w_k\leq1$. As $k\to\infty$, $w_k$ increases to $1$ at every point where $u>-\infty$, while $w_k=\chi(0)=0$ on the pole set of $u$. Moreover $w_k=0$ outside $O_k$: if $u_j\leq-k$ for some $j$, then $u\leq-k$ and $\mathrm e^{u/k}\leq\mathrm e^{-1}<1/2$. Choose a smooth function $\zeta$ on $\mathbb R$ with $0\leq\zeta\leq1$, $\zeta=0$ on $(-\infty,1/2]$ and $\zeta=1$ near $1$. Then $\zeta(w_k)$ and $\zeta'(w_k)$ vanish outside $O_k$, so that $\zeta(w_k)\Theta_k=\zeta(w_k)S_\gamma$. At every point where $u>-\infty$ we have $\zeta(w_k)\to1$ and $\zeta'(w_k)\to0$, and $S_\gamma$ gives the pole set of $u$ zero mass. Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
 \zeta(w_k)\Theta_k\longrightarrow S_\gamma,
 \qquad \zeta'(w_k)\longrightarrow0
                         \quad S_\gamma\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]

\emph{Gradient bound.} Let $\eta$ be a compactly supported smooth real one-form, and let $\tau\geq0$ be a smooth compactly supported function equal to one near $\Supp\eta$. By \eqref{eq:bounded-current-gradient-measure}, the gradient measure of $w_k$ with respect to $\Theta_k$ is $E_{\Theta_k}(w_k)=\frac12\ddc(w_k^2\Theta_k)-w_k\ddc w_k\wedge\Theta_k$, and the last term is a positive measure because $w_k\geq0$. Hence
\[
 \int\tau E_{\Theta_k}(w_k)
 \leq\tfrac12\int w_k^2\ddc\tau\wedge\Theta_k
 \leq C\int_{\Supp\tau}
                           \mathbf1_{O_k}\Theta_k\wedge\beta\leq C'.
\]
The second inequality uses $w_k=0$ outside $O_k$, $w_k\leq1$, and the bound of the coefficients of $\ddc\tau\wedge\Theta_k$ by a constant times the trace measure (\cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}). The constant $C'$ is independent of $k$ because $\mathbf1_{O_k}\Theta_k\leq S_\gamma$ and $S_\gamma$ is locally finite.

\emph{Conclusion.} By the chain rule \eqref{eq:bounded-current-chain-rule}, $\mathrm d(\zeta(w_k)\Theta_k)=\zeta'(w_k)\,\mathrm dw_k\wedge\Theta_k$. We apply \eqref{eq:current-gradient-cauchy-schwarz} with $a=\zeta'(w_k)$. The constant $C_\eta$ there depends only on $\eta$, so it is the same for every $k$. The first factor on the right is bounded by the gradient bound, and the second tends to zero, since
\[
 |\zeta'(w_k)|^2\Theta_k\wedge\beta
       =|\zeta'(w_k)|^2\mathbf1_{O_k}\Theta_k\wedge\beta
       \leq |\zeta'(w_k)|^2 S_\gamma\wedge\beta
\]
and the right side tends to zero on $\Supp\eta$ by dominated convergence. Hence the pairing of $\mathrm d(\zeta(w_k)\Theta_k)$ with $\eta$ tends to zero. Since $\zeta(w_k)\Theta_k\to S_\gamma$, the pairing of $\mathrm dS_\gamma$ with $\eta$ vanishes, so $\mathrm dS_\gamma=0$.

Finally, $\gamma\wedge\mathrm d\langle\bigwedge_j\ddc u_j\rangle=\mathrm dS_\gamma=0$ for every constant strongly positive $\gamma$. These forms span the constant real $(n-p-1,n-p-1)$-forms (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}), and their products with smooth functions and the coordinate one-forms $\mathrm dz_i,\mathrm d\bar z_i$ span the test forms of the two bidegrees against which $\mathrm d\langle\bigwedge_j\ddc u_j\rangle$ is evaluated. Therefore the non-pluripolar product is closed.
\end{proof}

\section{Bounded factors and the first-degree product}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-bounded-factors}
Let $v$ be locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, and let $S=\langle\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle$ be locally finite. Since $S$ is closed and positive, the recursion of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product} defines $\ddc v\wedge S$, and we expect this to be the non-pluripolar product of $v,u_2,\ldots,u_p$. To prove it we must know that $\ddc v\wedge S$, like $S$, gives no mass to the pole sets of $u_2,\ldots,u_p$. This follows from a weighted form of the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality. We then identify the non-pluripolar product in degree one, which requires a measurable form of Fubini's theorem for Bedford--Taylor products.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-weighted-cln}
Let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(r,r)$, $r\geq1$, on a coordinate domain $U\subset\mathbb C^n$, and let $K,L$ be compact sets with $K\Subset\Int L\subset L\Subset U$. Let $V\leq0$ be plurisubharmonic on $U$ and integrable on $L$ with respect to the trace measure $T\wedge\beta^r$, and let $v$ be locally bounded and plurisubharmonic on $U$. Then
\[
 \int_K(-V)\,\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}
       \leq C_{K,L}\|v\|_{L^\infty(L)}
                             \int_L(-V)T\wedge\beta^r,
\]
where the constant $C_{K,L}$ depends only on $K$, $L$ and the dimension, and not on $T$, $V$ or $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: $r\geq2$ and $V$ smooth.} If $\|v\|_{L^\infty(L)}=0$, then $v=0$ almost everywhere on $L$, hence everywhere on $\Int L$, and the left side vanishes. Otherwise both sides are homogeneous of degree one in $v$, and the left side does not change when a constant is added to $v$; replacing $v$ by $v/(2\|v\|_{L^\infty(L)})-3/2$, it therefore suffices to prove that $\int_K(-V)\,\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}\leq C\int_L(-V)T\wedge\beta^r$ for plurisubharmonic functions $v$ satisfying $-2\leq v\leq-1$ on $\Int L$. Cover $K$ by finitely many balls $B(a,r')$ such that $\overline{B(a,R)}\subset\Int L$ for some $R>r'$; it suffices to bound the integral over one ball $B(a,r')$.

Put $B=B(a,R)$, $\psi=|z-a|^2-R^2$ and $b=\ddc\psi=\beta/\pi$. Fix $r'<r''<R$, put $A=2/(R^2-r''^2)$ and $\widetilde v=\max\{v,A\psi\}$. On $B(a,r'')$ we have $A\psi\leq-2\leq v$, so $\widetilde v=v$; on the collar $\{R^2-1/A<|z-a|^2<R^2\}$ we have $A\psi>-1\geq v$, so $\widetilde v=A\psi$. The function $\widetilde v$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$, and its bounds depend only on the radii. Choose $0<\delta<1/A$ and put $B_\delta=\{\psi<-\delta\}$, so that $\widetilde v=A\psi$ on $B\setminus B_\delta$ and $B(a,r')\subset B_\delta$. Choose a smooth function $\rho$ with $0\leq\rho\leq R^2$, with compact support in $B$ and equal to $-\psi$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_\delta}$; this is possible because $0<-\psi\leq R^2$ on $B$. Put $S=\ddc\widetilde v\wedge T$ and $Q=\ddc V\wedge T$; since $V$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic, $Q$ is a closed positive current of bidimension $(r-1,r-1)$. Since $\ddc\rho=-b$ near $\overline{B_\delta}$ and $S=Ab\wedge T$ on $B\setminus B_\delta$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{B_\delta}(-V)S\wedge b^{r-1}
 &=\int_B VS\wedge b^{r-2}\wedge\ddc\rho
       -A\int_{B\setminus B_\delta}
                          VT\wedge b^{r-1}\wedge\ddc\rho\\
 &=\int_B\rho\,\ddc\widetilde v\wedge Q\wedge b^{r-2}
       -A\int_{B\setminus B_\delta}
                          VT\wedge b^{r-1}\wedge\ddc\rho.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the second line we integrate by parts against the smooth compactly supported function $\rho$: since $V$ is smooth and $\ddc\widetilde v\wedge T\wedge b^{r-2}$ is closed, $\ddc(V\ddc\widetilde v\wedge T\wedge b^{r-2})=\ddc V\wedge\ddc\widetilde v\wedge T\wedge b^{r-2}$, and the Bedford--Taylor product is symmetric with respect to the smooth factor.

We bound the two terms on the right. Choose a compact set $L'$ with $\overline B\subset\Int L'$ and $L'\subset\Int L$. Since $0\leq\rho\leq R^2$, the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality (\cref{thm:cln-bounded}) applied to $Q$ bounds the first term by a constant times the trace mass of $Q$ on $\overline B$. To bound this mass, test $Q$ against a smooth cutoff $0\leq\chi\leq1$ equal to one on $\overline B$ and supported in $L'$:
\[
 \int\chi\,\ddc V\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}=\int V\,T\wedge\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}\leq C\int_L(-V)T\wedge\beta^r,
\]
since $V\leq0$ and the coefficients of $T\wedge\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ are bounded by a fixed multiple of the trace measure. The collar term has the same bound, because $\ddc\rho$ is a fixed smooth form. Finally, $S=\ddc v\wedge T$ on $B(a,r')$ and $-V\geq0$, so the left side of the display bounds $\pi^{1-r}\int_{B(a,r')}(-V)\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}$. Summing over the finitely many balls proves the estimate, with a constant depending only on the chosen radii.

\emph{Step 2: $r\geq2$ and $V$ arbitrary.} Choose a compact set $L_1$ with $K\subset\Int L_1$ and $L_1\subset\Int L$, depending only on $K$ and $L$. The smooth radial regularizations $V_l$ of $V$ (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}) are defined and plurisubharmonic near $L_1$ for large $l$, and decrease to $V$. They satisfy $0\leq-V_l\leq-V$, because $V\leq0$ and $V$ is bounded by its ball averages. Step 1 on $(K,L_1)$ gives
\[
 \int_K(-V_l)\,\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}\leq C_{K,L_1}\|v\|_{L^\infty(L)}\int_L(-V)T\wedge\beta^r .
\]
As $l\to\infty$, $-V_l$ increases to $-V$, and monotone convergence against the positive measure $\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ proves the estimate.

\emph{Step 3: $r=1$.} Let $D'\Subset D''\Subset D$ be discs in $\mathbb C$, and let $P\colon U\times D\to U$ be the projection. By \cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback}, $P^*T$ is a closed positive current of bidimension $(2,2)$, and $V\circ P$ and $v\circ P$ are plurisubharmonic. Apply the case $r=2$ to these data and the compact sets $K\times\overline{D'}\subset\Int(L\times\overline{D''})$. Since $P^*T$ and $\ddc(v\circ P)\wedge P^*T$ contain no differential of the disc variable, only the terms containing its area form survive in the trace integrals, and integration over the discs produces positive area factors on both sides. Dividing by the factor on the left proves the case $r=1$, with a constant depending only on $K$, $L$ and the fixed discs.
\end{proof}

\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality!relative weighted}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-product-preserves-nonpolar-current}
Let $T$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(r,r)$, $r\geq1$, on a coordinate domain, whose trace measure gives zero mass to every pluripolar set. Then for every locally bounded plurisubharmonic $v$, the trace measure of $\ddc v\wedge T$ also gives zero mass to every pluripolar set. Consequently the same holds for every Bedford--Taylor product $\ddc v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc v_q\wedge T$, $1\leq q\leq r$, of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions with base $T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since pluripolar sets are locally contained in pole sets, it suffices to work near a compact set $K$ and to consider a set contained in $\{V=-\infty\}$, where $V\leq0$ is plurisubharmonic. Let $L$ be a compact neighborhood of $K$ and put $\mu=T\wedge\beta^r$ on $L$. This is a finite measure that does not charge $\{V=-\infty\}$, so $t=-V$ is finite $\mu$-almost everywhere. By dominated convergence, $\int_L\min\{t/R,1\}\,\mathrm d\mu\to0$ as $R\to\infty$; we may therefore choose an increasing sequence $R_j\geq2^j$ such that
\[
 \int_L\min\{t/R_j,1\}\,\mathrm d\mu\leq2^{-j}.
\]
The function $g(t)=\sum_{j\geq1}\min\{t/R_j,1\}$ is finite, increasing and concave on $[0,\infty)$, continuous because the series converges uniformly on bounded intervals, and it tends to infinity with $t$. Hence $\widetilde V=-g(-V)$ is a convex increasing function of $V$ and therefore plurisubharmonic; it is nonpositive, equals $-\infty$ on $\{V=-\infty\}$, and satisfies $\int_L(-\widetilde V)\,\mathrm d\mu\leq1$. By \cref{lem:relative-weighted-cln}, $\widetilde V$ is integrable on $K$ for the trace measure of $\ddc v\wedge T$, which therefore gives the pole set of $\widetilde V$ zero mass. A countable cover proves the assertion for every pluripolar set, and induction on $q$ gives the assertion for iterated products.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonpluripolar-bounded-factor-compatibility}
Let $u_1,\ldots,u_p$ be plurisubharmonic on a coordinate domain, $1\leq p\leq n$, and suppose that $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are locally bounded for some $0\leq q\leq p$. If $S=\langle\ddc u_{q+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle$ is locally finite, then the non-pluripolar product of $u_1,\ldots,u_p$ is locally finite and
\[
 \langle\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle
              =\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge S,
\]
where the right side is the Bedford--Taylor product of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product} with base $S$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $q=0$ there is nothing to prove. Inserting the bounded factors $u_q,u_{q-1},\ldots,u_1$ one at a time, we reduce to the case $q=1$. So let $v=u_1$ be locally bounded and $S=\langle\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_p\rangle$ locally finite. By \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}, $S$ is a closed positive current, so $R=\ddc v\wedge S$ is defined; it is closed and positive, and by \cref{lem:bounded-product-preserves-nonpolar-current} it gives zero mass to pluripolar sets, because $S$ does. If $p=1$, then $S=1$ and the assertion is that of bounded Bedford--Taylor theory. Let $p\geq2$. The assertion is local, so we may assume $u_j\leq0$ for $j\geq2$ and put
\[
 O_k=\bigcap_{j\geq2}\{u_j>-k\},\qquad
 \Theta_k=\bigwedge_{j\geq2}\ddc\max\{u_j,-k\},\qquad
 w_k=\chi\bigl(\mathrm e^{\sum_{j\geq2}u_j/k}\bigr),
\]
with $\chi$ as in the proof of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}. As shown there, the $w_k$ are bounded plurisubharmonic functions that vanish outside $O_k$, and the sets $\{w_k>0\}$ increase to the set where all $u_j$, $j\geq2$, are finite.

Take smooth plurisubharmonic functions $v_l$ decreasing to $v$ near a given compact set. By \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}, $S$ and $\Theta_k$ have the same restriction to $O_k$; since $w_k$ vanishes outside $O_k$ and $\ddc v_l$ is smooth,
\[
 w_k\ddc v_l\wedge S=w_k\ddc v_l\wedge\Theta_k.
\]
The weighted form of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, applied with the fixed weight $w_k$ and the fixed base currents $S$ on the left and $\Theta_k$ on the right, gives $w_kR=w_k\ddc v\wedge\Theta_k$ in the limit $l\to\infty$. Hence $R$ and $\ddc v\wedge\Theta_k$ have the same restriction to $\{w_k>0\}$. Now fix $j$. For $k\geq j$, the bounded functions $\max\{u_i,-k\}$ and $\max\{u_i,-j\}$ coincide on $O_j$, so $\ddc v\wedge\Theta_k=\ddc v\wedge\Theta_j$ on $O_j$ by \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality}. Since the sets $\{w_k>0\}\cap O_j$, $k\geq j$, exhaust $O_j$, we conclude that $\mathbf1_{O_j}R=\mathbf1_{O_j}\ddc v\wedge\Theta_j$.

Consider finally the full tuple $v,u_2,\ldots,u_p$ on a relatively compact open set on which $|v|<j_0$. For $j\geq j_0$, its level-$j$ set is $O_j$ and $v$ needs no truncation, so its truncated product at level $j$ is $\mathbf1_{O_j}\ddc v\wedge\Theta_j=\mathbf1_{O_j}R$. These restrictions are bounded by $R$, hence have locally bounded mass, and they increase to $R$ because $R$ gives the pole sets of $u_2,\ldots,u_p$ zero mass. Therefore the non-pluripolar product of $v,u_2,\ldots,u_p$ is locally finite and equals $R=\ddc v\wedge S$.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!bounded-factor compatibility}

To identify the first-degree product we shall integrate planar measures over almost every fibre of a coordinate projection. We first prove the fibre formula for bounded potentials, with the Borel weights needed for truncation.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-product-fibre-fubini}
Let $r,m\geq0$ be integers, let $Q\subset\mathbb C^r$ and $D\subset\mathbb C^m$ be domains with coordinates $x$ and $z$, and let $p_D\colon Q\times D\to D$ be the projection. Let $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $Q\times D$, and put
\[
 M=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_r,\qquad
 M_z=\bigwedge_{j=1}^r\ddc_x u_j(\,\cdot\,,z),
\]
the Bedford--Taylor products on $Q\times D$ and on the fibres $Q\times\{z\}$. For every smooth real $(m,m)$-form $\eta$ on $D$ and every bounded Borel function $h$ on $Q\times D$ with compact support, the fibre integral $z\mapsto\int_Q h(x,z)\,M_z(x)$ is Borel measurable and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-product-fibre-fubini}
 \int_{Q\times D}h\,p_D^*\eta\wedge M
       =\int_D\left(\int_Q h(x,z)\,M_z(x)\right)\eta(z).
\end{equation}
If $\eta\geq0$, the identity holds for every nonnegative Borel function $h$, with possibly infinite values.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $r=0$ or $m=0$, one factor of $Q\times D$ is a point, carrying counting measure, and the corresponding product is the empty product $1$; then \eqref{eq:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} holds trivially. Let $r,m\geq1$.

\emph{Continuous weights.} First let $\eta\geq0$ and let $h$ be continuous with compact support. On a product neighborhood of $\Supp h$ there are smooth plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to the $u_j$. In the smooth exterior product, wedging with $p_D^*\eta$ kills every term containing a differential of the base variable $z$, so the ordinary Fubini theorem gives \eqref{eq:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} for the smooth approximations. By \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, the approximating products converge weakly on the total space and on every fibre. Since the approximations are uniformly bounded, \cref{thm:cln-bounded} bounds the fibre masses uniformly for $z$ in the compact projection of $\Supp h$, and dominated convergence in $z$ proves the formula. A finite partition of unity treats supports that are not contained in one such neighborhood.

\emph{Measurability.} Fix $U\Subset Q$ and $V\Subset D$. For $a\in C_c(U)$, the function $z\mapsto\int a\,M_z$ is Borel, being the pointwise limit of the corresponding continuous functions defined by the smooth approximations. Every relatively open set $A\subset U$ admits continuous functions $0\leq a_l\leq1$ with compact support in $U$ increasing to $\mathbf1_A$; they can be built from the distance to the complement and a compact exhaustion. By monotone convergence, $z\mapsto M_z(A)$ is Borel, also for $A=U$, and these measures are finite on $U$, uniformly for $z\in V$. The class of Borel sets $A\subset U$ for which $z\mapsto M_z(A)$ is Borel is therefore closed under complements in $U$ and countable disjoint unions. It contains the open sets, which are closed under finite intersections and generate the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, so the monotone class argument in the proof of \cref{prop:tonelli-fubini} shows that it contains every Borel set. The same argument applies to sets $E\subset U\times V$: the class of Borel sets $E$ for which $z\mapsto M_z(E_z)$ is Borel contains the Borel rectangles and is closed under complements and countable disjoint unions, so it contains all Borel sets. Increasing exhaustions of $Q$ and $D$ extend this to the whole product.

\emph{Borel weights.} Let $\eta\geq0$. By the previous step,
\[
 \lambda(E)=\int_D M_z(E_z)\,\eta(z)
\]
is defined for Borel sets $E\subset Q\times D$, and it is a measure: countable additivity follows from monotone convergence on each fibre and then on $D$. The uniform local bound for the fibre masses makes $\lambda$ locally finite, so it is a Radon measure by \cref{prop:borel-regularity}. By the first step, $\lambda$ and $p_D^*\eta\wedge M$ agree on continuous compactly supported functions, so they are equal. Approximation by simple functions proves the formula for nonnegative Borel $h$, and then for bounded real Borel $h$ with compact support. On a compact subset of $D$, every smooth real top-degree form is the difference of two smooth nonnegative ones, which proves the formula for signed $\eta$.
\end{proof}
\index{fibre integration!Bedford--Taylor products}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}
Let $u$ be plurisubharmonic on a coordinate domain. Then its non-pluripolar product of degree one is locally finite and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}
 \langle\ddc u\rangle=\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u,
\end{equation}
where $\ddc u$ on the right is the distributional complex Hessian. In particular the current $\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u$ is closed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Upper bound.} The assertion is local, so we may assume $u\leq0$. The truncations $u_l=\max\{u,-l\}$ decrease to $u$ and converge to it in $L^1_{\loc}$, so $\ddc u_l\to\ddc u$ weakly. For fixed $k$ and every $l\geq k$, plurifine locality gives $\mathbf1_{\{u>-k\}}\ddc u_k=\mathbf1_{\{u>-k\}}\ddc u_l\leq\ddc u_l$, and letting $l\to\infty$ yields $\mathbf1_{\{u>-k\}}\ddc u_k\leq\ddc u$. Hence the truncated products have locally bounded mass, the non-pluripolar product $S=\langle\ddc u\rangle$ is locally finite, and $0\leq S\leq\ddc u$. Since $S$ gives no mass to the pole set of $u$, it follows that $S\leq\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u$. It remains to prove the reverse inequality.

\emph{One variable.} Let $n=1$ and put $\mu=\ddc u$, a positive measure. Let $V$ be a relatively compact open subset of the domain, and choose a smooth compactly supported function $\eta\geq0$ equal to one on $V$. With $\nu=\eta\mu$ and the fundamental solution $E_2$ of \eqref{eq:laplacian-fundamental-solution}, \cref{thm:riesz-decomposition} gives
\[
 u=4\pi E_2*\nu+h\quad\text{on }V,
\]
where $h$ is harmonic; the factor $4\pi$ appears because $\ddc u=(\Delta u)/(4\pi)$ as planar measures, while the Riesz measure is $\Delta u$. Put $p=E_2*\nu$ and $\nu_f=\mathbf1_{\{p>-\infty\}}\nu$; on $V$ we have $\{p>-\infty\}=\{u>-\infty\}$. The kernel $E_2$ is bounded above on the compact set of differences of points of $\Supp\nu$, so removing positive mass from $\nu$ lowers its potential on $\Supp\nu$ by at most a constant: $E_2*\nu_f\geq p-C$ there. Hence $E_2*\nu_f$ is finite $\nu_f$-almost everywhere, and \cref{thm:potential-continuity-principle} writes $\nu_f=\sum_i\nu_i$ with compactly supported measures $\nu_i$ whose potentials are finite and continuous. For each $N$, the measure $\nu^{(N)}=\sum_{i\leq N}\nu_i$ has the continuous subharmonic potential $v_N=4\pi E_2*\nu^{(N)}$, with $\ddc v_N=\nu^{(N)}$. On $V$ we have $\mu=\nu\geq\nu_f\geq\nu^{(N)}$, so the distribution $u-v_N$ has positive Laplacian on $V$. As a pointwise function, $u-v_N$ is upper semicontinuous and its ball averages converge to its values, because those of $u$ do and $v_N$ is continuous; hence it is the canonical subharmonic representative, and it is subharmonic on $V$. Since $v_N$ is locally bounded, multilinearity (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}) gives
\[
 S=\langle\ddc v_N\rangle+\langle\ddc(u-v_N)\rangle
                                  \geq\ddc v_N=\nu^{(N)}\quad\text{on }V.
\]
Letting $N\to\infty$ yields $S\geq\nu_f=\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\mu$ on $V$, which proves the proposition for $n=1$.

\emph{Several variables.} Let $n\geq2$ and work on a polydisc. Fix a coordinate direction with variable $x\in\mathbb C$, write $z\in\mathbb C^{n-1}$ for the remaining coordinates, and let $\mathrm dV_z$ denote the Euclidean volume form of the $z$-coordinates, viewed as an $(n-1,n-1)$-form on the polydisc. For almost every $z$, the fibre function $u_z=u(\,\cdot\,,z)$ is not identically $-\infty$ and is locally integrable, by Fubini's theorem; let $\mu_z=\ddc_xu_z$ be its planar Hessian measure. The measurability argument in the proof of \cref{lem:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} applies to these measures: smooth decreasing approximations of $u$ converge on every such fibre, which makes $z\mapsto\int a\,\mathrm d\mu_z$ Borel for continuous $a$ with compact support, and the monotone class argument extends this to all Borel sets. Testing $\mu_z$ against a planar cutoff shows that $\mu_z(D')\leq C\int_{D''}(-u_z)\,\mathrm dA$ for fixed discs $D'\Subset D''$, and this bound is integrable in $z$. Applying Fubini's theorem to $\int u\,\ddc_x\phi\wedge\mathrm dV_z$ for smooth test functions $\phi$, we therefore obtain the identity of Radon measures
\[
 \ddc u\wedge\mathrm dV_z=\mu_z\,\mathrm dV_z,
\]
meaning that $\int g\,\ddc u\wedge\mathrm dV_z=\int(\int g\,\mathrm d\mu_z)\,\mathrm dV_z$ for every bounded Borel function $g$ with compact support; the discarded fibres form a null set of $z$.

Now let $g\geq0$ be bounded Borel with compact support, and apply \cref{lem:bounded-product-fibre-fubini}, with $r=1$, to the bounded function $u_k=\max\{u,-k\}$ and the weight $g\mathbf1_{\{u>-k\}}$:
\[
 \int g\mathbf1_{\{u>-k\}}\ddc u_k\wedge\mathrm dV_z
 =\int\Bigl(\int g\mathbf1_{\{u_z>-k\}}\ddc_x\max\{u_z,-k\}\Bigr)\mathrm dV_z.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, the left side increases to $\int gS\wedge\mathrm dV_z$ by \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}. On every fibre with $u_z\not\equiv-\infty$, the inner integrals increase to $\int g\,\mathrm d\langle\ddc_xu_z\rangle=\int g\mathbf1_{\{u_z>-\infty\}}\,\mathrm d\mu_z$, by the one-variable case. Monotone convergence in $z$ and the preceding identity of measures give $S\wedge\mathrm dV_z=\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u\wedge\mathrm dV_z$. Summing over the $n$ coordinate directions, we find that the trace measures of $S$ and of $\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u$ coincide, up to the same positive normalizing factor. Thus $\mathbf1_{\{u>-\infty\}}\ddc u-S$ is a positive current with zero trace measure, and it vanishes by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}. This proves \eqref{eq:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}; closedness follows from \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!first-degree polar restriction}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:projective-logarithmic-measure-potential}
Let $\mu$ be a probability measure supported on a compact set $K\subset\mathbb C\subset\mathbb P^1$, and let $\omega$ be the Fubini--Study form of \cref{ex:projective-fubini-study-curvature}, which has total mass one on $\mathbb P^1$. Then
\[
 q(z)=\int_K\log\frac{|z-a|^2}{(1+|z|^2)(1+|a|^2)}\,\mathrm d\mu(a)
\]
defines a nonpositive $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^1$ with $\omega+\ddc q=\mu$. Its Lelong number at every point $x\in\mathbb P^1$ is $\mu(\{x\})$, where $\mu$ is regarded as a measure on $\mathbb P^1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the affine chart put $\rho(z)=\log(1+|z|^2)$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \omega=\ddc\rho=\frac{\mathrm d x\wedge\mathrm d y}{\pi(1+|z|^2)^2},
 \qquad\int_{\mathbb P^1}\omega=1.
\]
The integrand is nonpositive, since $|z-a|^2\leq(1+|z|^2)(1+|a|^2)$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, and its logarithmic singularities have local $L^1$ norms bounded uniformly in $a\in K$. On the affine chart, $q+\rho$ is the function $\int_K\log|z-a|^2\,\mathrm d\mu(a)$ minus the constant $\int_K\log(1+|a|^2)\,\mathrm d\mu(a)$, and by Fubini's theorem and the upper semicontinuity and submean argument used in \cref{prop:diffuse-polar-cantor-measure}, this function is subharmonic and is its own canonical representative. Integrating the identity $\ddc\log|z-a|^2=\delta_a$ of \cref{thm:poincare-lelong} against $\mu$ gives $\omega+\ddc q=\mu$ on the affine chart.

Near infinity use the coordinate $w=1/z$, in which the integrand becomes
\[
 \log|1-aw|^2-\log(1+|w|^2)-\log(1+|a|^2).
\]
Since $K$ is bounded, this expression and its derivatives are bounded uniformly in $a\in K$ for $w$ close to zero. Hence $q$ extends smoothly across infinity, and $q+\log(1+|w|^2)$ is harmonic there. Thus $q$ is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb P^1$ and $\omega+\ddc q=\mu$ globally. At infinity both the Lelong number and the point mass vanish. At an affine point $x$, \eqref{eq:lelong-density-limit} applied to the local subharmonic function $q+\rho$ gives $\nu(q,x)=\lim_{r\downarrow0}\mu(B(x,r))=\mu(\{x\})$.
\end{proof}
\index{Fubini--Study form!logarithmic measure potential}\index{Lelong number!atoms on a curve}

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-nonpluripolar-mass}
Let $\omega$ be as in \cref{prop:projective-logarithmic-measure-potential}. There is a nonpositive $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function $q$ on $\mathbb P^1$ such that
\[
 \nu(q,x)=0\quad\text{for every }x,\qquad
 \mathcal I(tq)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\quad(t>0),\qquad
 \langle\omega+\ddc q\rangle=0.
\]
Let $\mu$ be the atom-free probability measure on the compact polar set $K$ of \cref{prop:diffuse-polar-cantor-measure}, and let $q$ be its potential from \cref{prop:projective-logarithmic-measure-potential}. Since $\mu$ has no atoms, all Lelong numbers of $q$ vanish, and \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal} gives the assertion on multiplier ideals. On the affine chart, $q$ differs from the function $p(z)=\int_K\log|z-a|^2\,\mathrm d\mu(a)$ of \cref{prop:diffuse-polar-cantor-measure} by the smooth function $-\log(1+|z|^2)$ and a constant, and $p=-\infty$ on $K$; hence $q=-\infty$ on $K$. Applying \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction} to local potentials of $\omega+\ddc q$, we obtain
\[
 \langle\omega+\ddc q\rangle
       =\mathbf1_{\{q>-\infty\}}\mu=0,
\]
because $\mu$ is carried by $K$. By contrast, the potential $0$ has the same multiplier ideals and non-pluripolar mass one. Thus exponential integrability at every scale does not determine the non-pluripolar mass.
\end{example}
\index{non-pluripolar mass!diffuse polar measure}\index{multiplier ideal!non-pluripolar mass}

\section{Global finiteness and bounded cohomological mass}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-global-mass}
From now on, $(X,\omega)$ is a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and in this and the following sections $\theta$ and $\theta_j$ always denote smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. (Dimension zero is excluded because it carries no products of positive degree; the only product there is the empty one, the counting measure.) If $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is locally bounded, then near each point $\theta+\ddc\varphi=\ddc(h+\varphi)$, where $h$ is a smooth local potential of $\theta$ and $h+\varphi$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic. By \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}, the Bedford--Taylor products of such local potentials do not depend on the choice of $h$; hence they glue to closed positive currents $\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j)$ on $X$, which can be multiplied by smooth forms. We first prove Stokes' formula for these products and then show that non-pluripolar products on $X$ are always finite.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-global-cohomological-mass}
Let $1\leq p\leq n$, let $\varphi_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ be locally bounded for $1\leq j\leq p$, and let $\eta$ be a smooth closed $(n-p,n-p)$-form on $X$. Then
\[
 \int_X\bigwedge_{j=1}^p(\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j)\wedge\eta
       =\int_X\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_p\wedge\eta.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $j$, let $R$ be the closed positive current $\bigwedge_{i>j}(\theta_i+\ddc\varphi_i)$, and let $\eta'$ be a smooth closed form of complementary degree. Locally $\theta_j=\ddc h_j$ with $h_j$ smooth, and since $R$ is closed, the recursion gives
\[
 (\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j)\wedge R=\ddc((h_j+\varphi_j)R)=\theta_j\wedge R+\ddc(\varphi_jR).
\]
Both sides are globally defined, because $\varphi_j$ is a global bounded function. Moreover $\int_X\ddc(\varphi_jR)\wedge\eta'=\int_X\varphi_jR\wedge\ddc\eta'=0$, since $\eta'$ is closed. Hence $\int_X(\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j)\wedge R\wedge\eta'=\int_X\theta_j\wedge R\wedge\eta'$. Apply this successively for $j=1,2,\ldots,p$, with $\eta'=\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_{j-1}\wedge\eta$ at the $j$th step, which is smooth and closed. After $p$ steps the integral becomes $\int_X\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_p\wedge\eta$.
\end{proof}
\index{Stokes' theorem!bounded potentials}

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-global-integration-by-parts}
Let $R$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on $X$, and let $a,b$ be bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$, or differences of such functions. Then
\[
 \int_X a\,\ddc b\wedge R
   =\int_X b\,\ddc a\wedge R
   =-\int_X\mathrm da\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R.
\]
Here the Hessian products are defined locally by the Bedford--Taylor recursion, after adding smooth local functions that make $a$ and $b$ plurisubharmonic, and the mixed gradient $\mathrm da\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R$ is the finite signed measure obtained by polarizing the energy seminorm of \cref{lem:bounded-psh-current-chain-rule}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a relatively compact coordinate chart, add smooth functions to make $a$ and $b$ bounded plurisubharmonic. By \cref{lem:bounded-psh-current-chain-rule} they have gradients with respect to $R$, and the gradients of smooth decreasing approximations $a_l\downarrow a$, $b_l\downarrow b$ converge in the energy seminorm on compact sets. Subtracting the added smooth functions, the same holds for the original $a$ and $b$. Polarization of the energy seminorm and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality show that the mixed gradient is a locally finite signed measure, and a finite cover of $X$ shows that its total variation is finite. It is symmetric in $a$ and $b$, because the energy pairing is symmetric on real functions; equivalently, only the $(1,1)$-components of $\mathrm da\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b$ survive after wedging with $R$.

For the smooth approximations the product rule reads
\[
 \mathrm d(a_l\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b_l\wedge R)
       =\mathrm da_l\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b_l\wedge R
                                  +a_l\,\ddc b_l\wedge R.
\]
As $l\to\infty$, the coefficients of $a_l\,\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b_l\wedge R$ converge locally in total variation: the gradients converge in the energy seminorm, the weights $a_l$ are uniformly bounded and converge pointwise, and dominated convergence applies to the energy of the limit. The mixed gradient measures converge in total variation by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, and $a_l\ddc b_l\wedge R\to a\ddc b\wedge R$ by weighted decreasing continuity of bounded products, after expanding the smooth corrections (\cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights}). Hence the product rule also holds for $a$ and $b$ in the sense of distributions. Since $R$ is closed of pure bidegree, $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(bR)$ is a global current that does not depend on the local corrections, so the local identities glue. Pairing the global identity with the constant function $1$ on the compact manifold $X$ kills the left side and gives $\int_Xa\,\ddc b\wedge R=-\int_X\mathrm da\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R$. Interchanging $a$ and $b$ and using the symmetry of the mixed gradient gives the other equality, and linearity extends both to differences.
\end{proof}
\index{integration by parts!bounded global potentials}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}
Let $T_1,\ldots,T_p$, $1\leq p\leq n$, be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on the compact K\"ahler manifold $X$. Then their non-pluripolar product is finite. More precisely, write $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j$ with $\varphi_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$, and choose $C\geq0$ such that the forms $\tau_j=\theta_j+C\omega$ are positive definite. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound}
 \int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}
       \leq\int_X\tau_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_p\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\end{equation}
If the forms $\theta_j$ are semipositive, the same bound holds with $C=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The decomposition $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j$ exists by \cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}. Since $\tau_j\geq\theta_j$ and $\tau_j$ is positive, $\varphi_j$ is $\tau_j$-plurisubharmonic, and so is every constant; hence $\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}$ is a bounded $\tau_j$-plurisubharmonic function, and
\[
 R_{j,k}=\tau_j+\ddc\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}
\]
is a closed positive current. By \cref{prop:bounded-global-cohomological-mass}, the total mass $\int_XR_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge R_{p,k}\wedge\omega^{n-p}$ equals the right side of \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound} for every $k$.

\emph{Local finiteness.} Let $W$ be a relatively compact coordinate chart, and choose smooth functions $h_j$ and $h$ near $\overline W$ with $\ddc h_j=\theta_j$ and $\ddc h=\omega$. Then $u_j=\varphi_j+h_j$ is a plurisubharmonic potential of $T_j$ on $W$. Let $B$ be an integer with $h_j\leq B$ on $W$ for all $j$, fix an integer $l\geq1$, and put $O_l(u)=\bigcap_j\{u_j>-l\}$. On $W\cap O_l(u)$ we have $\varphi_j>-l-B$, so for every integer $k\geq l+B$ the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\max\{u_j,-l\}+Ch$ and $\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}+h_j+Ch$ both equal $u_j+Ch$ there; the second is a local potential of $R_{j,k}$. By plurifine locality,
\[
 \mathbf1_{O_l(u)}\bigwedge_j(\ddc\max\{u_j,-l\}+C\omega)=\mathbf1_{O_l(u)}\bigwedge_jR_{j,k}\quad\text{on }W.
\]
Expanding the left side into positive terms gives
\[
 \mathbf1_{O_l(u)}\bigwedge_j\ddc\max\{u_j,-l\}
       \leq\mathbf1_{O_l(u)}\bigwedge_j R_{j,k}\leq\bigwedge_jR_{j,k}.
\]
On a compact set $K\subset W$ we have $\beta^{n-p}\leq C_K\omega^{n-p}$, so the trace masses on $K$ of the truncated local products are bounded by $C_K$ times the right side of \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound}, uniformly in $l$. Hence the non-pluripolar product is locally finite everywhere, and $\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle$ is a closed positive current by \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}.

\emph{Mass bound.} Apply the same argument to the currents $T_j+C\omega=\tau_j+\ddc\varphi_j$, whose local potentials are $u_j+Ch$. The sets $G_k=\bigcap_j\{\varphi_j>-k\}$ increase and exhaust the complement of the union of the pole sets of the $\varphi_j$. On $G_k$ the global truncations $\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}$ coincide with $\varphi_j$. If $B'$ is an integer with $h_j+Ch\geq-B'$ on $W$ for all $j$, then on $G_k\cap W$ all local potentials satisfy $u_j+Ch>-k-B'$, so they coincide there with their truncations at level $k+B'$, and also with the local potentials $\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}+h_j+Ch$ of $R_{j,k}$. By plurifine locality and \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}, $\mathbf1_{G_k}\bigwedge_jR_{j,k}=\mathbf1_{G_k}\langle\bigwedge_j(T_j+C\omega)\rangle$, and these restrictions increase to $\langle\bigwedge_j(T_j+C\omega)\rangle$. Their total masses against $\omega^{n-p}$ are at most the right side of \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound}, hence so is that of the limit. Finally, multilinearity and positivity give $\langle\bigwedge_j T_j\rangle\leq\langle\bigwedge_j(T_j+C\omega)\rangle$, which proves \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound}. If the $\theta_j$ are semipositive, constants are $\theta_j$-plurisubharmonic, and the same argument works with $C=0$.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!global finiteness}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nonpluripolar-divisor-zero-mass}
On $\mathbb P^1$, the current of integration $[0]$ at the point $0$ satisfies $\langle[0]\rangle=0$. Indeed, near $0$ it has the potential $\log|z|^2$, whose truncation $\max\{\log|z|^2,-k\}$ is harmonic off the circle $\{\log|z|^2=-k\}$. The Hessian of the truncation is therefore carried by this circle, which is disjoint from $\{\log|z|^2>-k\}$; away from $0$ the current vanishes. Thus $[0]$ has mass one while $\langle[0]\rangle$ has mass zero: discarding the poles can change the cohomology class, even in degree one.
\end{example}

\section{Fibre integration and modifications}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-fibres}
We now pass the fibre formula of \cref{lem:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} through the truncations that define non-pluripolar products on a product of compact K\"ahler manifolds. The resulting identity, weighted by a bounded difference of potentials, is the form needed for relative energy in \cref{chap:q11}. We also show that non-pluripolar products commute with modifications.

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini}
Let $X$ and $Z$ be connected compact K\"ahler manifolds of dimensions $r$ and $m$, with projections $p_X,p_Z$ from $X\times Z$. Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $\gamma\geq0$ be a smooth closed $(1,1)$-form on $Z$. Put
\[
 \Theta=p_X^*\theta+p_Z^*\gamma,\qquad
 \Phi=p_X^*\varphi,\qquad
 T=\Theta+\ddc W,\quad S=\Theta+\ddc\Phi,
\]
where $W\in\PSH(X\times Z,\Theta)$ satisfies $\Phi-C\leq W\leq\Phi+C$ for a constant $C\geq0$. Then for every $z\in Z$ the function $W_z=W(\,\cdot\,,z)$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, and for every integer $0\leq j\leq r$ and every smooth real $(m,m)$-form $\eta$ on $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\int_{X\times Z}(W-\Phi)\,p_Z^*\eta\wedge
                              \langle T^j\wedge S^{r-j}\rangle\\
 &\qquad=\int_Z\left(\int_X(W_z-\varphi)
            \langle(\theta+\ddc W_z)^j\wedge
                         (\theta+\ddc\varphi)^{r-j}\rangle\right)\eta(z).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The same identity holds with both difference weights replaced by $1$. The weights are undefined only on the common pole set of the two potentials involved, where they may be given any bounded values.
\end{corollary}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
In product charts choose smooth local potentials $a(x)$ of $\theta$ and $b(z)$ of $\gamma$. Then $W+a+b$ is plurisubharmonic, so its restriction $W_z+a+b(z)$ to a fibre is plurisubharmonic in $x$; moreover $W_z\geq\varphi-C$ is not identically $-\infty$. Hence $W_z\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. All the products in \eqref{eq:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini} are finite by \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}, and \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound} bounds the fibre masses uniformly in $z$, because the shifted background can be chosen from the fixed form $\theta$, independently of $W_z$.

Put $w=W+a+b$, $v=\Phi+a+b$ and $O_k=\{w>-k\}\cap\{v>-k\}$ in a product chart. Let $\rho\geq0$ be a smooth function with compact support in the chart, let $G\geq0$ be bounded and Borel, and let $\eta\geq0$. Apply \eqref{eq:bounded-product-fibre-fubini} to the bounded functions $\max\{w,-k\}$ and $\max\{v,-k\}$, with multiplicities $j$ and $r-j$, and to the weight $\rho G\mathbf1_{O_k}$. As $k\to\infty$, the restricted mixed measures increase to the non-pluripolar products, on the total space and on every fibre, by \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}. (If $j=0$ or $j=r$, the condition defining $O_k$ involves a potential that does not occur in the product; the sets $O_k$ still increase and miss only a pluripolar set, which carries no mass, so the last assertion of that lemma applies.) Monotone convergence therefore gives the fibre formula for the non-pluripolar products with weight $\rho G$.

Taking $G=1$ gives the unweighted identity. Since $\Phi-C\leq W\leq\Phi+C$, the potentials $W$ and $\Phi$ have the same pole set, and so do $W_z$ and $\varphi$; take $G=W-\Phi+C\in[0,2C]$ off this set and $G=C$ on it, and subtract $C$ times the unweighted identity. Summing over a finite partition of unity by such functions $\rho$, and writing a signed $\eta$ as a difference of nonnegative forms, gives \eqref{eq:compact-nonpluripolar-fibre-fubini}. All integrals are finite by the uniform mass bound and $|W-\Phi|\leq C$, and the values assigned on the common pole set do not matter, because this set is pluripolar and carries no mass for any of the products.
\end{proof}
\index{fibre integration!non-pluripolar products}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonpluripolar-modification-pushforward}
Let $\pi\colon\widetilde X\to X$ be a modification between compact K\"ahler manifolds, and let $1\leq p\leq\dim X$. For closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots,T_p$ on $X$,
\[
 \pi_*\langle\pi^*T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^*T_p\rangle
                  =\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle,
\]
and for closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $R_1,\ldots,R_p$ on $\widetilde X$,
\[
 \pi_*\langle R_1\wedge\cdots\wedge R_p\rangle
                   =\langle\pi_*R_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_*R_p\rangle.
\]
In particular, in top degree the corresponding total masses agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pullback $\pi^*T_j$ is defined by pulling back local plurisubharmonic potentials; their pluriharmonic differences pull back to pluriharmonic functions, so the result is well defined. A pulled-back potential is not identically $-\infty$ on any component, because $\pi$ is biholomorphic over a dense open set. Pushforward of a closed positive current is closed and positive, since pullback of smooth forms commutes with differentiation and preserves strong positivity. All products in the statement are therefore defined, and they are finite by \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}.

Choose a proper analytic subset $A\subset X$ such that $\pi$ is a biholomorphism over $X\setminus A$. Over $X\setminus A$ the two sides of the first identity agree, because non-pluripolar products do not depend on the choice of potentials and are invariant under biholomorphic changes of coordinates. The right side gives the pluripolar set $A$ zero mass. The product upstairs gives the proper analytic set $\pi^{-1}(A)$ zero mass, so its pushforward gives $A$ zero mass as well, for the total variation of every coefficient. Hence the equality off $A$ extends to all coefficient measures. The same argument on the biholomorphism locus proves the second identity. Testing against the constant function $1$ in top degree proves the assertion on masses.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!modification pushforward}

\section{Capacity convergence and loss of mass}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-capacity-limits}
Bedford--Taylor products of locally uniformly bounded potentials are continuous under convergence in capacity (\cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}). For unbounded potentials this fails, because mass can escape into the poles in the limit (\cref{ex:nonpluripolar-mass-loss}). What survives is a lower semicontinuity statement, which becomes continuity when the total masses do not drop in the limit. We first extend convergence in capacity to functions with poles.

\begin{definition}\label{def:extended-psh-capacity-convergence}
For $s,t\in[-\infty,\infty)$ put $d_*(s,t)=|\arctan s-\arctan t|$, where $\arctan(-\infty)=-\pi/2$. A net $(u_a)$ of plurisubharmonic functions on an open set $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ converges in \emph{extended capacity} to a plurisubharmonic function $u$ if, for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\delta>0$, the outer capacity of $K\cap\{d_*(u_a,u)>\delta\}$ tends to zero, the capacity being taken as in \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence}. A net of $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions on a compact manifold converges in extended capacity if the local plurisubharmonic functions obtained by adding smooth local potentials of $\theta$ do so on the charts of a finite coordinate cover. This does not depend on the choice of the local potentials or of the cover; see the proof of \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations} below.
\end{definition}
\index{convergence!capacity for unbounded potentials}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}
Let $(u_k)$ be a sequence of plurisubharmonic functions on an open set $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ that is locally uniformly bounded above, and let $u$ be plurisubharmonic on $D$. Then $u_k\to u$ in extended capacity if and only if $\max\{u_k,-C\}\to\max\{u,-C\}$ locally in capacity for every $C>0$. A decreasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions with plurisubharmonic limit converges to it in extended capacity. If a sequence $(u_k)$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ converges in extended capacity to an element of $\PSH(X,\theta)$, then the suprema $\sup_Xu_k$ are bounded above.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set $K$ and let $M$ be an upper bound for all $u_k$ and $u$ on $K$. On $[-\infty,M]$, the function $\max\{s,-C\}$ is a Lipschitz function of $\arctan s$: it is constant for $\arctan s\leq\arctan(-C)$, and the derivative of the tangent is bounded on the compact interval $[\arctan(-C),\arctan M]$. Hence extended capacity convergence implies capacity convergence of the truncations. Conversely, $|\arctan s-\arctan\max\{s,-C\}|\leq\pi/2-\arctan C$ for all $s$, so that
\[
 d_*(u_k,u)\leq|\max\{u_k,-C\}-\max\{u,-C\}|+2(\pi/2-\arctan C),
\]
where we also used $|\arctan s-\arctan t|\leq|s-t|$ for finite $s,t$. Choosing $C$ with $2(\pi/2-\arctan C)<\delta/2$, the set $\{d_*(u_k,u)>\delta\}$ is contained in $\{|\max\{u_k,-C\}-\max\{u,-C\}|>\delta/2\}$, which proves the equivalence.

Adding the same bounded real function $h$ to $s$ and $t$ changes $d_*$ by at most a bounded factor: since $1+\sigma^2\leq2(1+h^2)(1+(\sigma+h)^2)$, the formula $\arctan t-\arctan s=\int_s^t\mathrm d\sigma/(1+\sigma^2)$ gives $d_*(s+h,t+h)\leq2(1+h^2)d_*(s,t)$. Hence extended capacity convergence of local potentials does not depend on the choice of smooth local potentials, and the local comparison of capacities in \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic} handles changes of charts.

If $u_k$ decreases to $u$, then for every $C$ the truncations decrease to the bounded plurisubharmonic function $\max\{u,-C\}$, so they converge in capacity by \cref{prop:psh-decreasing-capacity}; the equivalence just proved gives extended capacity convergence.

For the global assertion, put $v_k=u_k-\sup_Xu_k$. By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}, $\int_X|v_k|\,\omega^n$ is bounded. Suppose that $s_k=\sup_Xu_k\to+\infty$ along a subsequence. By Markov's inequality, the volume of $\{u_k<s_k/2\}=\{v_k<-s_k/2\}$ tends to zero, so $u_k\to+\infty$ outside sets of small volume. On the other hand, let $K$ be a compact subset of a chart with positive volume. The local potential of $u$ is bounded above on $K$, so for large $k$ the set $\{u_k\geq s_k/2\}\cap K$ is contained in a set where the arctangent distance of the local potentials exceeds a fixed $\delta_0>0$. The capacity of the latter set tends to zero, and hence so does its volume, because capacity dominates volume (\cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}). This contradicts the fact that the volume of $\{u_k\geq s_k/2\}\cap K$ tends to that of $K$. Hence the suprema are bounded above. (If $X$ is disconnected, the argument applies to each component.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}
Let $T_{j,k}=\theta_j+\ddc u_{j,k}$ and $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc u_j$ be closed positive currents on $X$, for $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$ and $k\geq1$, and suppose that $u_{j,k}\to u_j$ in extended capacity as $k\to\infty$, for every $j$. Put $R_k=\langle T_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p,k}\rangle$ and $R=\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle$. Then for every smooth strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta$ on $X$,
\[
 \liminf_k\int_X R_k\wedge\eta\geq\int_X R\wedge\eta.
\]
If moreover $\limsup_k\int_X R_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}\leq\int_X R\wedge\omega^{n-p}$, then $R_k\to R$ weakly. The lower inequality also holds locally, on any complex manifold and for compactly supported $\eta$, whenever the local products are finite and the potentials converge in extended capacity and are locally uniformly bounded above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower inequality.} Work on a relatively compact coordinate ball, add fixed smooth local potentials of the $\theta_j$, and denote the resulting plurisubharmonic functions by $v_{j,k}$ and $v_j$. They are uniformly bounded above, because the suprema of the $u_{j,k}$ are bounded above by \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}. For $C,\varepsilon>0$ put
\[
 v_{j,k}^C=\max\{v_{j,k},-C\},\qquad
 a_{j,k}=\frac{v_{j,k}^C+C}{v_{j,k}^C+C+\varepsilon},\qquad
 a_k=\prod_j a_{j,k},
\]
and define $v_j^C$, $a_j$ and $a$ from the limits in the same way. These weights take values in $[0,1]$, and $a_j$ and $a$ are quasi-continuous. By \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}, $v_{j,k}^C\to v_j^C$ in capacity, and since $s\mapsto(s+C)/(s+C+\varepsilon)$ is Lipschitz on $[-C,\infty)$, also $a_k\to a$ in capacity. The weight $a_k$ vanishes wherever some $v_{j,k}\leq-C$, and on the plurifine open set where all $v_{j,k}>-C$ the product $R_k$ coincides with the bounded product of the $v_{j,k}^C$, by \cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit} and \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality}. Hence
\[
 a_k R_k=a_k\bigwedge_j\ddc v_{j,k}^C,
\]
and likewise $aR=a\bigwedge_j\ddc v_j^C$. Let $\chi\geq0$ be a smooth function with compact support in the ball. By the weighted capacity continuity of \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity},
\[
 \int\chi\,aR\wedge\eta=\lim_k\int\chi\,a_kR_k\wedge\eta\leq\liminf_k\int\chi R_k\wedge\eta,
\]
where the inequality holds because $0\leq a_k\leq1$. Now let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and then $C\to\infty$. The weights $a$ increase to $1$ outside the union of the pole sets of the $v_j$, which $R$ does not charge, so monotone convergence gives $\int\chi R\wedge\eta\leq\liminf_k\int\chi R_k\wedge\eta$. This is the local lower inequality; it used only the finiteness of the local products and the local upper bounds. A finite partition of unity by such functions $\chi$ gives the global inequality.

\emph{Convergence.} By \eqref{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound}, the masses $\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}$ are bounded, since the shifted backgrounds depend only on the fixed forms $\theta_j$. By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero,thm:local-measure-selection}, every subsequence of $(R_k)$ has a weakly convergent subsequence. Its limit $R'$ is positive, and $R'\geq R$ by the lower inequality applied to all strongly positive test forms. The additional hypothesis gives $\int_X(R'-R)\wedge\omega^{n-p}\leq0$, so the positive current $R'-R$ has zero trace mass and vanishes. Thus every subsequence of $(R_k)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly to $R$, which proves $R_k\to R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nonpluripolar-mass-loss}
On $\mathbb P^1$ let $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini--Study form, normalized by $\int\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}=1$, and put $u=\log|z|^2-\log(1+|z|^2)$ and $u_k=\max\{u,-k\}$. \section{Mass comparison and projective fibres}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-mass-monotonicity}
We now prove that the total mass of a non-pluripolar product decreases when the potentials become more singular. If all potentials are locally bounded outside a closed set $A$ that is the pole set of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function, this follows from Stokes' formula, because the relevant difference of potentials can be arranged to have compact support in $X\setminus A$. The general case is reduced to this one by a construction on $X\times\mathbb P^N$: the new potential is bounded outside a fixed pole set, and its total mass is an average of masses along a segment of potentials, with weights that concentrate at the endpoint as $N\to\infty$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:small-locus-mixed-mass-comparison}
Let $\varphi_j,\psi_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ for $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and put $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc\varphi_j$ and $T'_j=\theta_j+\ddc\psi_j$. Suppose that $\varphi_j\geq\psi_j-O(1)$ for every $j$. Assume moreover that there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\tau$ on $X$ such that $A=\{\tau=-\infty\}$ is closed and $\tau$ and all the $\varphi_j,\psi_j$ are locally bounded on $X\setminus A$. Then
\[
 \int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}
 \geq\int_X\langle T'_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T'_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\]
If every difference $\varphi_j-\psi_j$ is bounded, equality holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We replace the factors $T_j$ by $T'_j$ one at a time; it suffices to treat one step, in which a single factor changes. Write it as $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ before and $\theta+\ddc\psi$ after the change, and let $\Theta$ be the non-pluripolar product of the remaining factors (some primed, some not) wedged with $\omega^{n-p}$. By \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness,thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}, $\Theta$ is a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$, and it gives zero mass to the pluripolar set $A$. On $X\setminus A$ all potentials are locally bounded, so there all the non-pluripolar products involved are Bedford--Taylor products, and the products of the two tuples, wedged with $\omega^{n-p}$, are $(\theta+\ddc\varphi)\wedge\Theta$ and $(\theta+\ddc\psi)\wedge\Theta$. Since none of these products charges $A$, it suffices to prove
\[
 \int_{X\setminus A}(\theta+\ddc\psi)\wedge\Theta\leq\int_{X\setminus A}(\theta+\ddc\varphi)\wedge\Theta .
\]
After adding constants we may assume $\varphi\geq\psi$ and $\tau\leq0$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and put $f_k=\max\{\psi+\varepsilon\tau,\varphi-k\}$. Choose a K\"ahler form $\Omega$, a large multiple of $\omega$, such that $\theta+\Omega+\ddc(\psi+\varepsilon\tau)\geq0$; then $\theta+\Omega+\ddc f_k\geq0$ for every $k$. Since $\psi+\varepsilon\tau-\varphi\leq\varepsilon\tau$, we have $f_k=\varphi-k$ on the open neighborhood $\{\tau<-k/\varepsilon\}$ of $A$. Hence the function $f_k-\varphi+k=\max\{\psi+\varepsilon\tau-\varphi+k,0\}$ takes values in $[0,k]$ and has compact support in $X\setminus A$. By the recursion with base $\Theta$ and Stokes' formula on $X\setminus A$,
\[
 \int_{X\setminus A}\ddc f_k\wedge\Theta
          =\int_{X\setminus A}\ddc\varphi\wedge\Theta.
\]
All the signed measures here have finite mass: adding the smooth form $\theta+\Omega$ makes them positive, and their total masses are then bounded by \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}. On $X\setminus A$ the functions $f_k$ decrease to the locally bounded function $\psi+\varepsilon\tau$ as $k\to\infty$. By decreasing continuity with the fixed base $\Theta$ (\cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}), the positive measures $(\theta+\Omega+\ddc f_k)\wedge\Theta$ converge weakly on $X\setminus A$ to $(\theta+\Omega+\ddc(\psi+\varepsilon\tau))\wedge\Theta$, and the mass of the limit on the open set $X\setminus A$ is at most the lower limit of the masses. Therefore
\[
 \int_{X\setminus A}(\theta+\Omega+\ddc\psi+\varepsilon\ddc\tau)\wedge\Theta
 \leq\int_{X\setminus A}(\theta+\Omega+\ddc\varphi)\wedge\Theta.
\]
The terms containing $\theta+\Omega$ cancel. The measure $\ddc\tau\wedge\Theta$ on $X\setminus A$ also has finite total variation, as one sees by adding a smooth positive form, so letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the desired inequality. If the differences are bounded, the same argument with the roles of $\varphi$ and $\psi$ exchanged gives equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-simplex-fibre-formula}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open, let $u$ be smooth and plurisubharmonic and $v$ locally bounded and plurisubharmonic on $U$, and let $N\geq1$ be an integer. Put $\Sigma_N=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:x_i\geq0,\ t(x)=\sum_i x_i\leq1\}$, and define on $U\times\mathbb C^N$
\[
 F=\sup_{x\in\Sigma_N}
       \left((1-t(x))u+t(x)v+
                    \sum_i x_i\log|z_i|^2-\sum_i x_i^2\right),
\]
with the convention that a zero coefficient times $-\infty$ is zero. The canonical extension of this function is locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, and with $p\colon U\times\mathbb C^N\to U$ the projection,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projective-simplex-fibre-formula}
 p_*(\ddc F)^{n+N}
       =\binom{n+N}{n}N\int_0^1
                (\ddc((1-t)u+tv))^n t^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt.
\end{equation}
The pushforward is locally finite: if $v-u\geq-C$ on an open set $E\subset U$, then over $E$ the measure $(\ddc F)^{n+N}$ is carried by $\{|z_i|\leq\mathrm e^{1+C/2}\text{ for all }i\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: structure of $F$ for smooth $v$.} Suppose first that $v$ is smooth, and work where every $z_i\ne0$. Put $a=v-u$, $y_i=\log|z_i|^2$ and $b_i=(y_i+a)/2$. Since
\[
 (1-t(x))u+t(x)v+\sum_ix_iy_i-\sum_ix_i^2=u+|b|^2-|x-b|^2,
\]
the supremum is attained exactly at the Euclidean closest point $x=P(b)$ of $\Sigma_N$ to $b=(b_1,\ldots,b_N)$; it exists by compactness and is unique by strict concavity. Differentiating the squared distance along the segment from $P(b)$ to a point $s\in\Sigma_N$ gives the variational inequality $(b-P(b))\cdot(s-P(b))\leq0$, and applying it twice gives $|P(b)-P(b')|\leq|b-b'|$. Comparing the two suprema at their respective maximizing points shows that the derivative of $F$ in $y_i$ is $x_i$, and the same argument gives its derivatives in the base variables. Consequently $F$ is of class $C^1$ with locally Lipschitz gradient.

The projection is explicit: $x_i=\max\{b_i-\lambda,0\}$, where $\lambda=0$ if $\sum_i\max\{b_i,0\}\leq1$, and otherwise $\lambda>0$ is the unique solution of $\sum_i\max\{b_i-\lambda,0\}=1$ (the left side is strictly decreasing on the range where it is positive). These coordinates satisfy the variational inequality and hence give the projection. On the region where the active set $I=\{i:x_i>0\}$ is fixed and $\lambda>0$, we have $\lambda=(\sum_{i\in I}b_i-1)/|I|$. Thus the projection is affine on finitely many polyhedral regions, with rank $|I|$ when $\lambda=0$ and $|I|-1$ when $\lambda>0$. On the \emph{interior region}, where all $x_i>0$ and $t(x)<1$, we have $x_i=(y_i+a)/2$ and $F=u+\sum_ix_i^2$. On every other region the positive semidefinite complex Hessian of $F$ has a vertical null vector, and hence zero determinant. Indeed, if $x_j=0$ for some $j$ on the region, then $F$ does not depend on $z_j$ there. If instead all $x_i>0$ and $t(x)=1$, then $\lambda=\bar b-1/N$ with $\bar b=N^{-1}\sum_ib_i$, and a short computation gives $F=v+N^{-1}\sum_iy_i-1/N+\sum_i(b_i-\bar b)^2$; here $\sum_iy_i$ is pluriharmonic, $v$ depends only on the base variables, and $b_i-\bar b$ depends only on the differences of the $y_l$, which are constant along the holomorphic curves $s\mapsto(\mathrm e^sz_1,\ldots,\mathrm e^sz_N)$ in a fibre. Hence the Hessian of $F$ vanishes on the vertical vector $\sum_iz_i\,\partial/\partial z_i$ tangent to these curves, and since it is semidefinite, this vector is a null vector. The region boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero: for a fixed base point each is given by a nonconstant linear equation in $y$, and Fubini's theorem applies.

The continuous, locally Lipschitz gradient of $F$ has locally bounded distributional derivatives, as one sees by testing its bounded difference quotients and using \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density}. Convolution, Lebesgue differentiation and dominated convergence of the determinants, together with \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity} and \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}, identify the Bedford--Taylor measure $(\ddc F)^{n+N}$ with the measure given by the almost everywhere defined Hessian determinant. Hence the region boundaries carry no mass, and neither do the lower-rank regions.

\emph{Step 2: the interior region.} There direct differentiation gives
\[
 \ddc F=(1-t(x))\ddc u+t(x)\ddc v
                       +2\sum_i\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i.
\]
The first two terms involve only the base variables, so their sum has vanishing $(n+1)$st power; each $\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i$ has vanishing square. Expanding the $(n+N)$th power, only the terms with $n$ base factors and the $N$ distinct factors $2\,\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i$ survive, with coefficient $\binom{n+N}{n}$. In such a term the base factors already have full base degree, so only the fibre parts of $\mathrm dx_i$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i$ contribute. On a fibre write $z_i=r_i\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\alpha_i}$. With our normalization, $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}y_i=\mathrm d\alpha_i/(2\pi)$, so $2\,\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x_i=\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d\alpha_i/(2\pi)$ there. Integrating over the angular variables pushes the $N$th power of $\sum_i\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d\alpha_i/(2\pi)$ forward to $N!\,\mathrm dx$ on $\Sigma_N$. Finally, $|\{x\in\Sigma_N:t(x)\leq t\}|=t^N/N!$, by scaling the simplex and an elementary iterated integration, so integrating first over the levels of $t$ turns $N!\,\mathrm dx$ into $Nt^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt$. This proves \eqref{eq:projective-simplex-fibre-formula} for smooth $v$.

\emph{Step 3: extension and support.} Near the coordinate hyperplanes, $F$ is bounded below by the branch $x=0$, which equals $u$, and the displayed supremum bounds it locally from above. By \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension}, $F$ has a unique plurisubharmonic extension across the hyperplanes; this is the canonical extension, and the hyperplanes, being pluripolar, carry no mass for its Monge--Amp\`ere measure. Together with Step 1, this shows that the measure is carried by the interior region. If $a\geq-C$ at a base point, then at the points of the interior region over it we have $y_i=2x_i-a\leq2+C$, hence $|z_i|\leq\mathrm e^{1+C/2}$.

\emph{Step 4: general $v$.} For locally bounded $v$, take smooth plurisubharmonic $v_l\downarrow v$ on a smaller base neighborhood, and let $F_l$ be the corresponding functions. They decrease pointwise to $F$, since the two suprema differ by at most $v_l-v$, because $0\leq t(x)\leq1$. They are locally uniformly bounded. If $v-u\geq-C$ on an open set $E$, then also $v_l-u\geq-C$ there, so by Step 3 the measures $(\ddc F_l)^{n+N}$ give no mass to the open set of points over $E$ at which some $|z_i|>\mathrm e^{1+C/2}$. By \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, these measures converge weakly to $(\ddc F)^{n+N}$, so the limit gives no mass to this open set either, which proves the support assertion. Since $v$ is locally bounded and $u$ is smooth, $v-u$ is bounded below on relatively compact open subsets of the base, over which all these measures are therefore carried by a fixed compact set; hence the pushforwards converge weakly as well. The right side of \eqref{eq:projective-simplex-fibre-formula} converges by the same theorem and dominated convergence in $t$, using the uniform Chern--Levine--Nirenberg bound for $t\in[0,1]$. Exhausting $U$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:auxiliary-projective-mass}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, and let $\varphi_0\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be smooth outside its pole set, which is a proper analytic subset, with $\mathrm e^{m\varphi_0}$ smooth for some $m>0$. Fix an integer $N\geq1$, let $p_X,p_{\mathbb P^N}$ be the projections of $X\times\mathbb P^N$, and let $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini--Study form of \cref{ex:projective-fubini-study-curvature}. For $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots:Z_N]$ put
\[
 s_i=\log\frac{|Z_i|^2}{\sum_{l=0}^N|Z_l|^2},\qquad
 \widetilde\theta=p_X^*\theta+p_{\mathbb P^N}^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}},
\]
and let $\Phi$ be the upper semicontinuous regularization of
\[
 \sup_{x\in\Sigma_N}
       \left((1-t(x))(\varphi_0+s_0)+t(x)\varphi
                              +\sum_{i=1}^N x_i s_i-\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^2\right).
\]
Then $\Phi$ is $\widetilde\theta$-plurisubharmonic and locally bounded outside $A=(\{\varphi_0=-\infty\}\times\mathbb P^N)\cup(X\times\{Z_0=0\})$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auxiliary-projective-mass}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\int_{X\times\mathbb P^N}\langle(\widetilde\theta+\ddc\Phi)^{n+N}\rangle\\
 &\quad=\binom{n+N}{n}N\int_0^1
       \left(\int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc((1-t)\varphi_0+t\varphi))^n\rangle\right)
                                      t^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A reference potential $\varphi_0$ with these properties exists in every big class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The potential $\Phi$.} Each function in the supremum is $\widetilde\theta$-plurisubharmonic: its coefficients of $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ add up to one, and so do its coefficients of the $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic functions $s_i$. The family is locally uniformly bounded above, so its regularized supremum $\Phi$ is $\widetilde\theta$-plurisubharmonic. The branch $x=0$ is $\tau=\varphi_0+s_0$, which is finite off $A$; hence $\Phi\geq\tau$ is locally bounded outside $A$. The function $\tau$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, its pole set is exactly $A$, and it is locally bounded outside $A$.

\emph{Existence of $\varphi_0$.} Since the class is big, it contains a K\"ahler current $\theta+\ddc\psi\geq\delta\omega$, $\delta>0$. Apply \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum} to $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta-\delta\omega)$: for every $m$ it gives a function $G_m$ with $\mathrm e^{mG_m}$ smooth and $\theta-\delta\omega+\ddc G_m\geq-(c'/m)\omega$. For $m>c'/\delta$, $\varphi_0=G_m$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic; its pole set is the zero set of the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(m\psi)$, a proper analytic subset, and $\varphi_0$ is smooth outside it.

\emph{The mass formula.} Off the pole set of $\varphi_0$ choose $h$ with $\ddc h=\theta$, and work in the chart $Z_0\ne0$ with coordinates $z_i=Z_i/Z_0$. There $s_0=-\log(1+|z|^2)$ and $s_i=\log|z_i|^2-\log(1+|z|^2)$, so adding $h+\log(1+|z|^2)$ to $\Phi$ gives the function $F$ of \cref{lem:projective-simplex-fibre-formula} with $u=\varphi_0+h$ and $v=\varphi+h$. The latter need not be locally bounded, so we replace it by $v_C=\max\{v,u-C\}$, which is locally bounded. Over the plurifine open base set $\{v>u-C\}$ the two constructions coincide, so by plurifine locality their Monge--Amp\`ere measures agree there, and \cref{lem:projective-simplex-fibre-formula} applies to $v_C$. Let $C\to\infty$ in these restricted identities. The base sets exhaust the points where $v$ is finite, and both sides give the remaining pluripolar set zero mass. The product upstairs also gives zero mass to the pluripolar set $A$. The measure-valued integrand on the right is Borel in $t$: by multilinearity it is a polynomial in $t$ whose coefficients are finite mixed non-pluripolar products. Increasing Borel restrictions, monotone convergence and a finite partition of unity therefore give the pushforward formula over $X$, and integrating it gives \eqref{eq:auxiliary-projective-mass}. Finally, the local suprema coincide with their canonical extensions, so no regularization changes the restricted products: as $C\to\infty$, the bounded functions obtained from $v_C$ decrease pointwise to the supremum, by compactness of $\Sigma_N$, and at points where $v=-\infty$ the limit is the branch $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity}
Let $\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\varphi\geq\psi-O(1)$. Then
\[
 \int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc\varphi)^n\rangle
       \geq\int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc\psi)^n\rangle.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\{\theta\}$ is big. Choose a reference potential $\varphi_0$ as in \cref{prop:auxiliary-projective-mass}, and construct the corresponding potentials $\Phi_N$ from $\varphi$ and $\Psi_N$ from $\psi$. Since $0\leq t(x)\leq1$, the inequality $\varphi\geq\psi-O(1)$ gives $\Phi_N\geq\Psi_N-O(1)$ with a constant independent of $N$, and both are locally bounded outside the same set $A$, which is the pole set of the quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi_0+s_0$. Put
\[
 M_\varphi(t)=\int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc((1-t)\varphi_0+t\varphi))^n\rangle,
\]
and define $M_\psi$ in the same way. Applying \cref{lem:small-locus-mixed-mass-comparison} on $X\times\mathbb P^N$ and cancelling the common binomial coefficient in \eqref{eq:auxiliary-projective-mass}, we obtain
\[
 \int_0^1M_\varphi(t)\,Nt^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt\geq\int_0^1M_\psi(t)\,Nt^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt.
\]
Writing $\theta+\ddc((1-t)\varphi_0+t\varphi)=(1-t)T_0+tT_\varphi$ with $T_0=\theta+\ddc\varphi_0$ and $T_\varphi=\theta+\ddc\varphi$, multilinearity shows that $M_\varphi$ is a polynomial in $t$, in particular continuous on $[0,1]$, and $M_\varphi(1)$ is the mass of $\varphi$. The probability measures $Nt^{N-1}\,\mathrm dt$ concentrate at $1$ as $N\to\infty$, since their mass on $[0,1-\delta]$ is $(1-\delta)^N$. By uniform continuity the two integrals tend to $M_\varphi(1)$ and $M_\psi(1)$, which proves the theorem for big classes.

If $\{\theta\}$ is merely pseudo-effective, then for $\varepsilon>0$ the class of $\theta+\varepsilon\omega$ is big, and $\varphi,\psi$ are $(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$-plurisubharmonic. By the big case, the mass of $\varphi$ with respect to $\theta+\varepsilon\omega$ is at least that of $\psi$. By multilinearity and \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}, both masses are polynomials in $\varepsilon$ with finite coefficients, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass}
Let $u_j,v_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ for $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and put $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc u_j$ and $T'_j=\theta_j+\ddc v_j$. If every difference $u_j-v_j$ is bounded, then
\[
 \int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}
 =\int_X\langle T'_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T'_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\]
If only $u_j\geq v_j-O(1)$ for every $j$, then the left side is at least the right side.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Bounded differences.} For real numbers $a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_p>0$, apply \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity} in both directions to the potentials $\sum_ja_ju_j$ and $\sum_ja_jv_j$, whose difference is bounded, with respect to the background $a_0\omega+\sum_ja_j\theta_j$. The two resulting masses are equal. By multilinearity, both are homogeneous polynomials of degree $n$ in $(a_0,\ldots,a_p)$, and they agree on an open set; fixing all but one variable and using the uniqueness of polynomials in one variable successively, we see that their coefficients agree. By \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-bounded-factor-compatibility}, the coefficient of $a_0^{n-p}a_1\cdots a_p$ is $n!/(n-p)!$ times the mixed mass in the statement, which proves equality.

\emph{The ordered case.} Put $v_{j,k}=\max\{u_j-k,v_j\}$. Since $v_j\leq u_j+C_j$ for constants $C_j$, we have $u_j-k\leq v_{j,k}\leq u_j+C_j$, so each tuple $(v_{j,k})_j$ has the same mixed mass as $(u_j)_j$, by the first part. As $k\to\infty$, $v_{j,k}$ decreases to $v_j$ and therefore converges to it in extended capacity (\cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}). The lower inequality of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}, with test form $\omega^{n-p}$, bounds the mixed mass of the $v_j$ by these equal masses.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!bounded-perturbation invariance}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}
Suppose that $u_{j,k}\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ increase almost everywhere to $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ as $k\to\infty$, for $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$. Then the non-pluripolar products $\langle\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\ddc u_{j,k})\rangle$ converge weakly to $\langle\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)\rangle$, and their mixed masses against $\omega^{n-p}$ converge.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The inequality $u_{j,k}\leq u_j$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere, since $\max\{u_{j,k},u_j\}$ and $u_j$ are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere. On coordinate charts add smooth local potentials and truncate below at a fixed level $-C$. The resulting locally uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic sequences increase almost everywhere to the truncations of the limits, so they converge in capacity by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}; by \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}, the original sequences converge in extended capacity. Since $u_{j,k}\leq u_j$, \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass} bounds every mixed mass of the sequence by that of the limit, and \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} gives weak convergence and convergence of the masses. This applies in particular to the regularized supremum of an increasing sequence, which differs from the pointwise supremum only on a pluripolar set, by \cref{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar product!increasing continuity}

\section{Maximal mass and comparison}
\label{sec:nonpluripolar-full-mass}
By the monotonicity just proved, potentials with minimal singularities have the largest non-pluripolar masses in a big class, and these maximal masses are invariants of the classes. We extend them to pseudo-effective classes by perturbing with a K\"ahler class, and then derive the comparison and domination principles for non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measures. Recall that $V_\theta$ denotes the extremal potential of \cref{def:global-minimal-potential}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:positive-mixed-intersection-number}
For big classes $\alpha_j=\{\theta_j\}$, $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, the \emph{positive mixed intersection number} relative to the K\"ahler class $\{\omega\}$ is
\[
 I_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p)
 =\int_X\left\langle\bigwedge_{j=1}^p
           (\theta_j+\ddc V_{\theta_j})\right\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\]
For pseudo-effective classes $\alpha_j$ we define $I_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p)$ as the decreasing limit of $I_\omega(\alpha_1+\varepsilon\{\omega\},\ldots,\alpha_p+\varepsilon\{\omega\})$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; for big classes the two definitions agree, by \cref{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound}. In top degree we put $\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)=I_\omega(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)$, the \emph{volume} of $\alpha$; the factor $\omega^{n-p}$ is then absent.
\end{definition}
\index{positive intersection!mixed number}\index{volume!positive current class}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound}
The numbers of \cref{def:positive-mixed-intersection-number} do not depend on the chosen smooth representatives or minimal potentials, nor on the K\"ahler form within its class. The limit defining them for pseudo-effective classes exists, does not depend on the K\"ahler class used for the perturbation, and agrees with the first definition when all $\alpha_j$ are big. For closed positive currents $T_j\in\alpha_j$,
\[
 \int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}
                       \leq I_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p).
\]
The numbers are positively homogeneous in each class and increase when a pseudo-effective class is added to any big argument. For nef classes they equal the cohomological intersection numbers $\int_X\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_p\wedge\omega^{n-p}$. Big classes have positive volume.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Big classes.} Any two potentials with minimal singularities in a fixed big class differ by a bounded function, and a change of smooth representative changes each potential by a smooth function. Hence \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass} shows that $I_\omega$ does not depend on these choices. The same corollary and minimality give the upper bound for closed positive currents in big classes. Changing $\omega$ within its class changes $\omega^{n-p}$ by a smooth exact form, whose integral against the closed current $\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_p\rangle$ vanishes by Stokes' formula.

Let $\alpha$ be big and $\gamma$ pseudo-effective, represented by a closed positive current $S$. Adding $S$ to a current with minimal singularities in $\alpha$ gives a current in the big class $\alpha+\gamma$, which by the upper bound has mixed mass at most the number with $\alpha$ replaced by $\alpha+\gamma$. By multilinearity and positivity, its mixed mass is at least the number for $\alpha$. Hence replacing a big argument $\alpha$ by $\alpha+\gamma$ increases the number. Homogeneity follows by scaling a minimal potential together with its background.

\emph{Pseudo-effective classes.} By the monotonicity just proved, the perturbed numbers decrease as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; they are nonnegative, so the limit exists. Two K\"ahler forms satisfy $c^{-1}\omega\leq\omega'\leq c\omega$ for some $c\geq1$, so monotonicity places the perturbation by $\varepsilon\omega'$ between the perturbations by $c^{-1}\varepsilon\omega$ and $c\varepsilon\omega$, with the same fixed test form $\omega^{n-p}$; this proves independence of the perturbing class. The same comparison shows that perturbing the arguments by different fixed multiples of $\varepsilon\omega$ gives the same limit, which extends homogeneity to pseudo-effective classes. If all $\alpha_j$ are big, choose $\delta>0$ such that each $\alpha_j$ contains a K\"ahler current $T_j\geq\delta\omega$. Then $(1+\varepsilon/\delta)\alpha_j-(\alpha_j+\varepsilon\{\omega\})=(\varepsilon/\delta)\{T_j-\delta\omega\}$ is pseudo-effective, so monotonicity and homogeneity give
\[
 I_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p)\leq I_\omega(\alpha_1+\varepsilon\{\omega\},\ldots,\alpha_p+\varepsilon\{\omega\})\leq(1+\varepsilon/\delta)^pI_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p),
\]
and the limit is $I_\omega(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p)$. For arbitrary closed positive currents $T_j\in\alpha_j$, the upper bound in the big classes $\alpha_j+\varepsilon\{\omega\}$, applied to $T_j+\varepsilon\omega$, and multilinearity give $\int_X\langle\bigwedge_jT_j\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-p}\leq I_\omega(\alpha_1+\varepsilon\{\omega\},\ldots)$, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the upper bound.

\emph{Nef and big classes.} If the $\alpha_j$ are nef, their perturbations are K\"ahler classes, whose minimal potentials are bounded. By \cref{prop:bounded-global-cohomological-mass}, the perturbed numbers are the integrals $\int_X\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\varepsilon\omega)\wedge\omega^{n-p}$, and their limits are the cohomological intersection numbers. Finally, let $\alpha$ be big and $T_0\in\alpha$ a K\"ahler current with $T_0\geq\delta\omega$. Expanding $T_0=\delta\omega+(T_0-\delta\omega)$ by multilinearity gives $\langle T_0^n\rangle\geq\delta^n\omega^n$, so $\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)\geq\delta^n\int_X\omega^n>0$ by the upper bound.
\end{proof}

The converse, that a pseudo-effective class of positive volume is big, requires the construction of a K\"ahler current in the class. We prove it in \cref{sec:positive-mass-bigness}, after solving Monge--Amp\`ere equations with prescribed measures; the arguments of this chapter only use that big classes have positive volume.

\begin{definition}\label{def:full-nonpluripolar-mass}
A closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T\in\alpha$ has \emph{full Monge--Amp\`ere mass} if $\int_X\langle T^n\rangle=\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)$. We write $\mathcal E(X,\theta)$ for the set of $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that $\theta+\ddc u$ has full Monge--Amp\`ere mass. For $\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the class $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ consists of the potentials $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\leq\phi+O(1)$ and
\[
 \int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc u)^n\rangle
     =\int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc\phi)^n\rangle;
\]
these potentials have \emph{full mass relative to} $\phi$. In a big class, $\mathcal E(X,\theta)=\mathcal E(X,\theta,V_\theta)$. (The arguments distinguish these classes from the pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$ of \cref{chap:global-potentials}.)
\end{definition}
\index{full Monge--Amp\`ere mass}\index{full Monge--Amp\`ere mass!relative to a potential}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:relative-full-mass-capacity-convergence}
Let $u_k\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $u_k\leq\phi+C_k$ for constants $C_k$, let $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, and suppose that $u_k\to u$ in extended capacity. Then
\[
 \langle(\theta+\ddc u_k)^n\rangle
                   \longrightarrow\langle(\theta+\ddc u)^n\rangle
\]
weakly, and the masses converge. In particular this holds for decreasing sequences whose limit has full mass in a big class.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-self-mass-monotonicity}, the mass of each $u_k$ is at most that of $\phi$, which equals the mass of $u$. Hence \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} applies. In the absolute case $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta)$, the bound of every mass by the volume in \cref{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound} plays the same role, and no constants $C_k$ are needed; decreasing sequences converge in extended capacity by \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ write $\mu_u=\langle(\theta+\ddc u)^n\rangle$. Then for all $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 \int_{\{u<v\}}\mu_v
 \leq\int_{\{u<v\}}\mu_u+\operatorname{vol}(\{\theta\})-\mu_u(X).
\]
The last two terms may be omitted if $u$ has full mass or if $u\geq v-O(1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. The function $w=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. The sets $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ and $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$ are disjoint and plurifine open. Indeed, near each point we have $u-v=(u+h)-(v+h)$ for a smooth local potential $h$ of $\theta$, and $u+h$, $v+h$ are plurisubharmonic, so by \cref{prop:plurifine-difference-continuity} the function $u-v$ is plurifinely continuous off the common pole set of $u$ and $v$, which contains neither set. Moreover $w$ equals $v-\varepsilon$ on the first set and $u$ on the second. By plurifine locality,
\[
 \mu_w(X)\geq
 \mu_v(\{u<v-\varepsilon\})+\mu_u(\{u>v-\varepsilon\}).
\]
The left side is at most $\operatorname{vol}(\{\theta\})$ by \cref{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound}. The complement of $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$ consists of $\{u\leq v-\varepsilon\}$, which is contained in $\{u<v\}$ up to the common pole set of $u$ and $v$, and that pluripolar set carries no mass. Hence
\[
 \mu_v(\{u<v-\varepsilon\})\leq\operatorname{vol}(\{\theta\})-\mu_u(X)+\mu_u(\{u<v\}).
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the sets $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ increase to $\{u<v\}$, which proves the inequality. If $u$ has full mass, the last two terms cancel. If $u\geq v-O(1)$, then $w-u$ is bounded, so $\mu_w(X)=\mu_u(X)$ by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass}, and the same computation with $\mu_u(X)$ in place of the volume gives the inequality without these terms.
\end{proof}
\index{comparison principle!non-pluripolar global measures}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonpluripolar-domination-transfer}
Let $u,v,\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\geq v-O(1)$ and $u\geq\rho-O(1)$. If $u\geq v$ almost everywhere for $\mu_u$, then $u\geq v$ almost everywhere for $\mu_\rho$. If, in addition, the class $\{\theta\}$ is big and $u$ has minimal singularities, then $u\geq v$ everywhere on $X$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
After adding a constant to $\rho$ we may assume $\rho\leq u$. For $0<t<1$ put $q_t=(1-t)v+t\rho$. Then $u\geq q_t-O(1)$, and $\{u<q_t\}\subset\{u<v\}$, because $u<(1-t)v+t\rho\leq(1-t)v+tu$ forces $u<v$. Multilinearity and \cref{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison} therefore give
\[
 t^n\mu_\rho(\{u<q_t\})
   \leq\mu_{q_t}(\{u<q_t\})
   \leq\mu_u(\{u<q_t\})\leq\mu_u(\{u<v\})=0.
\]
As $t\downarrow0$, every point where $u<v$ and $\rho>-\infty$ eventually lies in $\{u<q_t\}$, so Fatou's lemma, applied along a sequence $t\downarrow0$, gives $\mu_\rho(\{u<v\}\cap\{\rho>-\infty\})=0$. Since $\mu_\rho$ gives no mass to the pole set of $\rho$, this proves the first assertion.

Now let the class be big and $u$ have minimal singularities. The class contains a K\"ahler current $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\delta\omega$ with $\delta>0$. Then $u\geq\rho-O(1)$ by minimality, and $\mu_\rho\geq\delta^n\omega^n$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:positive-mixed-mass-bound}. The first assertion gives $u\geq v$ almost everywhere with respect to $\omega^n$, that is, almost everywhere for Lebesgue measure in charts. Since $u$ and $v$ become plurisubharmonic after adding a common smooth local potential, and plurisubharmonic functions are limits of their ball averages, it follows that $u\geq v$ everywhere.
\end{proof}
\index{domination principle!non-pluripolar measures}
