\chapter{Envelopes and model potentials}
\label{chap:q10}

Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold and $\theta$ a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form, as in the two preceding chapters. Many potentials are defined as envelopes: given an obstacle $f$, one takes the largest $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function that lies below $f$ outside a pluripolar set. The extremal potential $V_\theta$ of \cref{def:global-minimal-potential} is the envelope of the obstacle $0$. In this chapter we develop the basic theory of such envelopes. Two features of the global setting require care. The obstacle is often only a Borel function, so we must keep track of which inequalities hold everywhere and which only quasi-everywhere. And the family of competitors may be empty: two $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions need not have a common $\theta$-plurisubharmonic minorant (\cref{ex:empty-rooftop-projective-line}).

The basic property of an envelope is that its non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure is carried by the contact set, where the envelope touches the obstacle. For quasi-continuous obstacles in a big class this is \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}. Away from the contact set, the envelope of a lower semicontinuous obstacle can be replaced locally by a maximal plurisubharmonic function, so its measure vanishes there. Quasi-continuity then lets us approximate a general obstacle by lower semicontinuous ones, and bounded weights take care of limits in which the measures themselves need not converge. For regular obstacles one can say more. Using an exponentially penalized Monge--Amp\`ere equation, we show that in a K\"ahler class the envelope of the minimum of finitely many obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian, that is, with bounded Laplacian for the Riemannian metric of $\omega$ (\cref{sec:metric-laplacian-obstacles}), again has bounded metric Laplacian (\cref{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}), and that it is $C^{1,1}$ when the obstacles are $C^{1,1}$ (\cref{thm:kahler-real-c11-envelope}); in a big class its metric Laplacian is bounded on compact subsets of the ample locus (\cref{thm:big-metric-laplacian-rooftop}). On the contact set of an arbitrary potential with such an obstacle, the non-pluripolar measure of the potential coincides with the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the obstacle (\cref{thm:irregular-mixed-contact-density}).

The second half of the chapter studies envelopes that prescribe singularities rather than values. The singularity envelope $P_\theta[u]$ is the upper envelope of all nonpositive potentials $v$ with $v\leq u+C$ for some constant $C$, which may depend on $v$. It has the same non-pluripolar masses as $u$ but may be less singular, and its fixed points of positive mass are called model potentials. The main tool is extrapolation (\cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}): if $u$ is more singular than $v$ and has almost the same mass, then for some $a>1$ the function $v+a(u-v)$, which continues the segment from $v$ to $u$ beyond $u$, still has a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic minorant of positive mass. From this we derive the domination principle for potentials of full mass relative to a reference potential (\cref{thm:relative-full-mass-domination}), the characterization of $P_\theta[u]$ as the largest nonpositive potential that is less singular than $u$ and has the same mass (\cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}), and a mass inequality for rooftop envelopes (\cref{thm:rooftop-mass-diamond}). We also show that comparisons of singularities through multiplier ideals pass to upper envelopes (\cref{lem:divisorial-envelope-closure}).

Finally we introduce the Monge--Amp\`ere capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$ relative to a reference potential $\phi$, prove that it is comparable with the capacity of a K\"ahler form when $\phi$ has positive mass (\cref{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison}), and use it to measure the deep sublevel sets $\{u<\phi-s\}$ of potentials of full mass relative to $\phi$. These estimates are what the next chapter needs in order to integrate by parts with singular potentials. Singularity envelopes of potentials of zero mass, and contact concentration in arbitrary pseudo-effective classes, depend on the Monge--Amp\`ere equations solved there (\cref{sec:arbitrary-mass-singularity-envelopes,sec:positive-mass-bigness}). Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-envelopes}.

\section{Obstacles and rooftops}
\label{sec:global-obstacle-envelopes}
Throughout this chapter $X$ is a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, $\theta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and $\omega$ is a fixed K\"ahler form on $X$. We use $\omega$ only for measuring: volumes, norms and the Laplacians of obstacles are taken with respect to $\omega$, also when an envelope is formed with respect to a different background. The elements of $\PSH(X,\theta)$ (\cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}) are called \emph{potentials}; they are pointwise defined functions, not identically $-\infty$. As in \cref{sec:analytic-approximation-comparison}, we write $u\preceq v$, and say that $u$ is \emph{more singular} than $v$, if $u\leq v+C$ for some constant $C$. We write $u\sim v$, and say that $u$ and $v$ have the same \emph{singularity type}, if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$.
\index{singularity type}

Two facts are used constantly. First, pluripolar sets have Lebesgue measure zero. Second, if two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions satisfy $u\leq v$ almost everywhere, then $u\leq v$ everywhere, because $\max\{u,v\}$ and $v$ are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}). Hence an inequality between quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that holds \emph{quasi-everywhere}, that is, outside a pluripolar set, holds everywhere. In particular, if $u\sim v$, then $u$ and $v$ have the same pole set and $u-v$ is bounded outside it.

Quasi-continuity on $X$ is defined through the local Monge--Amp\`ere capacities of \cref{def:monge-ampere-capacity}. A function $f\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ is \emph{quasi-continuous} if on every coordinate chart, identified with a bounded domain $D\subset\mathbb C^n$, it is quasi-continuous for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ in the sense introduced before \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}: for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ such that the restriction of $f$ to $K\setminus G$ is continuous. Here $[-\infty,+\infty]$ carries its order topology, in which it is compact. By the comparison of capacities in \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}, it suffices to check this on the charts of one finite atlas. Every potential is quasi-continuous, by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity} applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta$, and finite maxima and minima of quasi-continuous functions are quasi-continuous. Modifying a function on a pluripolar set does not affect quasi-continuity, since pluripolar sets have zero outer capacity (\cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}).
\index{quasi-continuity!on a compact manifold}

\begin{definition}\label{def:global-obstacle-envelope}
Let $f\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ be a Borel function, called an \emph{obstacle}, and put
\[
 \mathcal A_\theta(f)=\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq f
                                     \text{ quasi-everywhere}\}.
\]
The members of $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ are called \emph{competitors} for $f$. If $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, the \emph{obstacle envelope} of $f$ is
\[
 P_\theta(f)=\bigl(\sup\mathcal A_\theta(f)\bigr)^*,
\]
where ${}^*$ denotes the upper semicontinuous regularization. If $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ is empty, we put $P_\theta(f)=-\infty$. If $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ is nonempty but not uniformly bounded above, $P_\theta(f)$ is not defined; whenever we use $P_\theta(f)$ for an obstacle that is not bounded above, we first verify the upper bound. If $f\leq M$ quasi-everywhere for a constant $M$, the upper bound is automatic: every competitor satisfies $v\leq M$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere.
\end{definition}
\index{envelope!obstacle}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}
Let $f$ be a Borel obstacle.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, then $P_\theta(f)\in\mathcal A_\theta(f)$; thus $P_\theta(f)$ is the largest competitor for $f$.
 \item If $f$ is upper semicontinuous, then every competitor $v$ for $f$ satisfies $v\leq f$ everywhere.
 \item The envelope does not change when $f$ is modified on a pluripolar set, and $P_\theta(f+c)=P_\theta(f)+c$ for $c\in\mathbb R$. If $g$ is a Borel obstacle with $f\leq g$ quasi-everywhere and $\mathcal A_\theta(g)$ is uniformly bounded above, then $P_\theta(f)\leq P_\theta(g)$.
 \item Suppose that $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, and let $g$ be a Borel obstacle with $f-c\leq g\leq f+c$ quasi-everywhere for a constant $c\geq0$. Then $\mathcal A_\theta(g)$ is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, and
 \[
  P_\theta(f)-c\leq P_\theta(g)\leq P_\theta(f)+c.
 \]
 In particular the two envelopes have the same pole set and differ by at most $c$ outside it.
 \item Let Borel obstacles $f_j$ decrease pointwise to $f$, and suppose that $\mathcal A_\theta(f_1)$ is uniformly bounded above. Then $P_\theta(f_j)$ decreases pointwise to $P_\theta(f)$. Here the families may be empty; in particular, $P_\theta(f_j)$ decreases to the constant $-\infty$ if and only if $\mathcal A_\theta(f)=\varnothing$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) Cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate balls on which $\theta$ has smooth local potentials $h$. Applying \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} on each ball to the plurisubharmonic functions $v+h$, $v\in\mathcal A_\theta(f)$, and taking the union of the resulting countable subfamilies, we find competitors $v_1,v_2,\ldots$ with $(\sup_kv_k)^*=P_\theta(f)$. The finite maxima $w_k=\max\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}$ are again competitors, and they increase with $k$. Each $w_k$ satisfies $w_k\leq f$ outside a pluripolar set $N_k$, and $N=\bigcup_kN_k$ is pluripolar by \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}. By \cref{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}, $\sup_kw_k=P_\theta(f)$ outside a pluripolar set $N'$. Hence $P_\theta(f)\leq f$ outside $N\cup N'$. Since the family is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, $P_\theta(f)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}. Thus $P_\theta(f)$ is a competitor, and by construction it dominates every competitor.

(2) Let $v$ be a competitor, let $x\in X$, and choose a coordinate ball $B(x,r)$ and a smooth function $h$ with $\ddc h=\theta$ near its closure. Since $v+h$ is plurisubharmonic and $v\leq f$ outside a Lebesgue null set, the sub-mean value inequality gives
\[
 v(x)+h(x)\leq\frac1{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}(v+h)\,\mathrm d\lambda
          \leq\sup_{B(x,r)}(f+h).
\]
As $r\to0$, the right side tends to at most $f(x)+h(x)$, because $f+h$ is upper semicontinuous. Hence $v(x)\leq f(x)$.

(3) A modification of $f$ on a pluripolar set does not change $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$, since the union of two pluripolar sets is pluripolar. Moreover $\mathcal A_\theta(f+c)=\mathcal A_\theta(f)+c$, and $\mathcal A_\theta(f)\subset\mathcal A_\theta(g)$ if $f\leq g$ quasi-everywhere. These relations between the families of competitors give the three assertions.

(4) By (3), $\mathcal A_\theta(f)-c\subset\mathcal A_\theta(g)\subset\mathcal A_\theta(f)+c$. Hence $\mathcal A_\theta(g)$ is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, and the two inequalities follow from (3).

(5) By (3) the envelopes $P_\theta(f_j)$ decrease; if some family $\mathcal A_\theta(f_j)$ is empty, then so are all later ones and $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$. Let $v$ be the pointwise limit of $P_\theta(f_j)$. Suppose first that $v(x_0)>-\infty$ for some $x_0$. Then $\sup_XP_\theta(f_j)\geq v(x_0)$ for every $j$, so $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ by \cref{cor:global-decreasing-nets}. By (1), $v\leq P_\theta(f_j)\leq f_j$ outside a pluripolar set $N_j$, so $v\leq\inf_jf_j=f$ outside the pluripolar set $\bigcup_jN_j$. Thus $v$ is a competitor for $f$ and $v\leq P_\theta(f)$; the reverse inequality follows from $P_\theta(f)\leq P_\theta(f_j)$. Suppose next that $v\equiv-\infty$. A competitor $w$ for $f$ would be a competitor for every $f_j$, so $w\leq P_\theta(f_j)$ for all $j$ and $w\leq v$, which is impossible. Hence $\mathcal A_\theta(f)=\varnothing$ and $P_\theta(f)=-\infty=v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:rooftop-envelope}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the \emph{rooftop envelope} of $u$ and $v$ is
\[
 P_\theta(u,v)=P_\theta(\min\{u,v\}).
\]
We also put $P_\theta(u,-\infty)=P_\theta(-\infty,u)=-\infty$ for $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cup\{-\infty\}$.
\end{definition}
\index{rooftop envelope}
The obstacle $\min\{u,v\}$ is upper semicontinuous and bounded above. By \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, a potential is therefore a competitor for it exactly when it lies below both $u$ and $v$ everywhere. Thus $P_\theta(u,v)$ is the largest common minorant of $u$ and $v$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ if they have one, and $P_\theta(u,v)=-\infty$ if they have none.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rooftop-algebra}
The rooftop envelope is commutative, associative, increasing in both arguments and idempotent, that is, $P_\theta(u,u)=u$. For a constant $c$, $P_\theta(u+c,v+c)=P_\theta(u,v)+c$. If $u_j,v_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ decrease pointwise to $u$ and $v$, where the limits may be identically $-\infty$, then $P_\theta(u_j,v_j)$ decreases pointwise to $P_\theta(u,v)$. Finally, for $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $C\geq0$, the rooftop envelope $P_\theta(u+C,V_\theta)$ is a potential with $P_\theta(u+C,V_\theta)\sim u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Commutativity, monotonicity and idempotence follow directly from the description of $P_\theta(u,v)$ as the largest common minorant. For associativity, let also $w\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cup\{-\infty\}$. A potential lies below $P_\theta(u,v)$ and $w$ if and only if it lies below $u$, $v$ and $w$: when $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$ this is the maximality of $P_\theta(u,v)$, and otherwise neither condition can be satisfied. Hence $P_\theta(P_\theta(u,v),w)$ and $P_\theta(u,P_\theta(v,w))$ are both the largest common minorant of $u$, $v$ and $w$, or both equal $-\infty$ if there is none. The behaviour under constant shifts and decreasing limits follows from \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, applied to the obstacles $\min\{u_j,v_j\}$, which are bounded above by $\sup_Xu_1$ and decrease to $\min\{u,v\}$. If $u$ or $v$ is identically $-\infty$, this limit obstacle has no competitor, in accordance with our convention.

For the last assertion put $B=\max\{0,\sup_Xu\}$. Then $u-B\leq0$, so $u-B\leq V_\theta$, and also $u-B\leq u+C$. Hence $u-B$ is a common minorant of $u+C$ and $V_\theta$, and
\[
 u-B\leq P_\theta(u+C,V_\theta)\leq u+C.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:empty-rooftop-projective-line}
On $\mathbb P^1$ let $\theta=\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini--Study form, normalized by $\int_{\mathbb P^1}\theta=1$, and choose $u_0,u_\infty\in\PSH(\mathbb P^1,\theta)$ with $\theta+\ddc u_0=[0]$ and $\theta+\ddc u_\infty=[\infty]$, the currents of integration at the two points. Suppose that $w\in\PSH(\mathbb P^1,\theta)$ is a common minorant of $u_0$ and $u_\infty$. Near $0$ the function $u_0$ is $\log|z|^2$ plus a smooth function, so $\nu(u_0,0)=1$, and $w\leq u_0$ gives $\nu(w,0)\geq1$ by the description of Lelong numbers through logarithmic growth in \cref{prop:lelong-logarithmic-growth}; likewise $\nu(w,\infty)\geq1$. In dimension one, the Lelong number of a subharmonic function $s$ at a point is the mass that the measure $\ddc s$ gives to that point (\cref{thm:euclidean-jensen-lelong}). Hence the positive measure $\theta+\ddc w$ has atoms of mass at least one at $0$ and at $\infty$, whereas its total mass is $\int_{\mathbb P^1}\theta=1$ by Stokes' formula. This contradiction shows that $P_\theta(u_0,u_\infty)=-\infty$, although $u_0$ and $u_\infty$ are both $\theta$-plurisubharmonic.
\end{example}

\section{Measures on contact sets}
\label{sec:envelope-contact-measures}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ we write $\theta_u=\theta+\ddc u$, we denote by $\mu_u=\langle\theta_u^n\rangle$ its non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure, as in \cref{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison}, and by $M(u)=\mu_u(X)$ its total mass. When a different background is used, we say so or indicate it by a subscript. A \emph{contact set} is a set $\{u=f\}$ on which two functions agree; it is a Borel set, but it need not be closed.

Three properties of these measures are used throughout. They give no mass to pluripolar sets, in particular to pole sets, so points where a potential equals $-\infty$ can be ignored in measure-theoretic statements. They are local in the plurifine topology (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}): if two potentials agree on a plurifine open set $O$, their measures agree on $O$; and for potentials $u,v$ and $c\in\mathbb R$ the set $\{u>v+c\}$ is plurifine open by \cref{prop:plurifine-difference-continuity}. Finally, multilinearity and positivity give
\[
 \mu_{(1-t)u+tv}\geq t^n\mu_v
 \qquad(u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ 0\leq t\leq1),
\]
and the mass is monotone: $M(u)\leq M(v)$ if $u\preceq v$, and $M(u)=M(v)$ if $u\sim v$, by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass}.

\subsection{Concentration on the contact set}
\label{sec:envelope-contact-concentration}
We first show that the measure of an envelope is carried by its contact set with the obstacle, beginning with a comparison of the measures of two ordered potentials on the set where they agree.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\leq v$. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mu_u\leq\mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mu_v.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\varepsilon>0$ put $w_\varepsilon=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $w_\varepsilon$ increases to $\max\{u,v\}=v$, so $\mu_{w_\varepsilon}\to\mu_v$ weakly along every sequence $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}. On the plurifine open set $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$ we have $w_\varepsilon=u$, hence $\mathbf1_{\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}\mu_{w_\varepsilon}=\mathbf1_{\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}\mu_u$. This set contains every point of $\{u=v\}$ at which $u$ is finite, and $\mu_u$ does not charge the pole set of $u$. Therefore
\[
 \mu_{w_\varepsilon}\geq\mathbf1_{\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}\mu_u
                    \geq\mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mu_u.
\]
The right side is a fixed finite measure. Integrating against nonnegative continuous functions and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, we obtain $\mu_v\geq\mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mu_u$, and restricting both sides to $\{u=v\}$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big and that $f$ is bounded and lower semicontinuous. Then $u=P_\theta(f)$ has minimal singularities, and $\mu_u$ is carried by $\{u=f\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding a constant to $f$, we may assume that $-C\leq f\leq0$. Then $V_\theta-C\leq f$, so $V_\theta-C$ is a competitor, while every competitor is nonpositive and hence at most $V_\theta$. Thus $V_\theta-C\leq u\leq V_\theta$, and $u$ has minimal singularities.

By \cref{prop:bounded-potential-nef}, the big class contains a K\"ahler current $\theta+\ddc\psi$ with rational analytic singularities. Let $A$ be the pole set of $\psi$, a proper analytic subset of $X$. Since $\psi$ has analytic singularities, it is locally bounded on $X\setminus A$, and since $V_\theta$ has minimal singularities, $V_\theta\geq\psi-C'$ for a constant $C'$. Hence $V_\theta$, and with it $u$, is locally bounded on $X\setminus A$. Since $A$ is pluripolar, $\mu_u(A)=0$.

Let $x\in X\setminus A$ with $u(x)<f(x)$, and choose $\delta>0$ with $u(x)<f(x)-3\delta$. Since $u$ is upper semicontinuous and $f$ is lower semicontinuous, there is a coordinate ball $B\ni x$ with $\overline B\subset X\setminus A$ such that $u<u(x)+\delta$ and $f>f(x)-\delta$ on $\overline B$; thus $\sup_{\overline B}u\leq\inf_{\overline B}f-\delta$. Shrinking $B$, we may assume that $B\Subset B'$ for a coordinate ball $B'\subset X\setminus A$ and that $\theta=\ddc h$ near $\overline{B'}$ for a smooth function $h$ with $\operatorname{osc}_{\overline B}h<\delta$. Since $u$ is bounded near $\overline{B'}$, \cref{cor:global-psh-balayage} gives $\widetilde u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\widetilde u\geq u$, $\widetilde u=u$ on $X\setminus B$ and $(\theta+\ddc\widetilde u)^n=0$ on $B$. By the maximum principle for the plurisubharmonic function $\widetilde u+h$ on $B$,
\[
 \widetilde u\leq\sup_{\overline B}(u+h)-h
            \leq\sup_{\overline B}u+\operatorname{osc}_{\overline B}h
            <\inf_{\overline B}f
 \qquad\text{on }B.
\]
Hence $\widetilde u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere on $X$, so $\widetilde u$ is a competitor and $\widetilde u\leq u$. Therefore $\widetilde u=u$, and $\mu_u=(\theta+\ddc\widetilde u)^n$ vanishes on $B$.

The set $\{u<f\}$ is open, because $f-u$ is lower semicontinuous. Covering $\{u<f\}\setminus A$ by countably many balls as above, we obtain $\mu_u(\{u<f\}\setminus A)=0$. Moreover $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere by \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, so the set $\{u>f\}$ is pluripolar. Since $\mu_u$ gives no mass to $A$ or to $\{u>f\}$, it is carried by $\{u=f\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big and that the Borel obstacle $f$ is quasi-continuous and bounded above quasi-everywhere. If $P_\theta(f)\ne-\infty$, then $\mu_{P_\theta(f)}$ is carried by the contact set $\{P_\theta(f)=f\}$, that is,
\[
 \mu_{P_\theta(f)}(\{P_\theta(f)\ne f\})=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $u=P_\theta(f)$, and let $A$ be the proper analytic set constructed in the proof of \cref{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact}: every potential with minimal singularities is locally bounded on $X\setminus A$, and $A$ is pluripolar.

\emph{Step 1: bounded obstacles.} Suppose first that $f$ is bounded quasi-everywhere. Modifying $f$ on a pluripolar set changes neither $P_\theta(f)$ nor the quasi-continuity of $f$, and it changes the contact set only on a pluripolar set, which $\mu_u$ does not charge. After such a modification and the addition of a constant, we may therefore assume that $-C\leq f\leq0$ everywhere. Using the quasi-continuity of $f$ on the charts of a finite atlas, together with the comparison of capacities in \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}, we find open sets $G_1\supset G_2\supset\cdots$ such that $f$ is continuous on each closed set $X\setminus G_j$ and the local capacities of the sets $G_j$ tend to zero on compact subsets of the charts. (Replacing the open sets by finite intersections keeps $f$ continuous on the complements, which are finite unions of closed sets on each of which $f$ is continuous.) By the Tietze extension theorem, the restriction of $f$ to $X\setminus G_j$ extends to a continuous function $f_j\colon X\to[-C,0]$. Put
\[
 h_j=\sup_{k\geq j}f_k.
\]
These functions are lower semicontinuous, take values in $[-C,0]$, and decrease with $j$. Since $G_k\subset G_j$ for $k\geq j$, we have $h_j=f$ on $X\setminus G_j$. The intersection $\bigcap_jG_j$ has zero outer capacity in each chart and is therefore pluripolar by \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}; outside it, $h_j=f$ for all large $j$. Hence the limit $h$ of the $h_j$ equals $f$ quasi-everywhere.

Let $u_j=P_\theta(h_j)$. By \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, $u_j$ decreases to $P_\theta(h)=u$, and as in the proof of \cref{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact} we have $V_\theta-C\leq u_j,u\leq V_\theta$. Fix a coordinate ball $D\Subset X\setminus A$ with a smooth potential $\rho$ of $\theta$ near $\overline D$, and a function $0\leq\chi\in C_c^\infty(D)$. On $D$ the functions $u_j$ and $u$ are bounded uniformly in $j$. By \cref{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_{u_j}$ is carried by $\{u_j=h_j\}$, so
\[
 \int_X\chi\bigl(1-\mathrm e^{u_j-h_j}\bigr)\,\mu_{u_j}=0.
\]
The integrands with $h_j$ and with $f$ are bounded uniformly in $j$ on the support of $\chi$, and they coincide outside $G_j$. By \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}, applied to the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\rho+u_j$, the measures $\mu_{u_j}$ of $G_j\cap\Supp\chi$ are at most a fixed constant times the local capacity of $G_j\cap\Supp\chi$, which tends to zero. Consequently
\[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\int_X\chi\bigl(1-\mathrm e^{u_j-f}\bigr)\,\mu_{u_j}=0.
\]
The weights $1-\mathrm e^{u_j-f}$ are bounded and quasi-continuous on $D$, and they converge in capacity to $1-\mathrm e^{u-f}$, because the uniformly bounded potentials $u_j$ decrease to $u$ and therefore converge in capacity (\cref{prop:psh-decreasing-capacity}), while $|\mathrm e^{u_j-f}-\mathrm e^{u-f}|\leq\mathrm e^{C}|u_j-u|$. The weighted form of \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity} therefore gives
\[
 \int_X\chi\bigl(1-\mathrm e^{u-f}\bigr)\,\mu_u=0.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative quasi-everywhere, and positive exactly at the points of $D$ where $u<f$. Since $\chi$ is arbitrary, $\mu_u(\{u<f\}\cap D)=0$. Covering $X\setminus A$ by such balls, and recalling that $\mu_u$ does not charge the pluripolar sets $A$ and $\{u>f\}$, we conclude that $\mu_u$ is carried by $\{u=f\}$.

\emph{Step 2: general obstacles.} Adding a constant, we may assume that $f\leq0$ quasi-everywhere. Put $f_j=\max\{f,-j\}$ and $u_j=P_\theta(f_j)$. The obstacles $f_j$ are quasi-continuous, satisfy $-j\leq f_j\leq0$ quasi-everywhere, and decrease to $f$; their families of competitors contain $V_\theta-j$ and are bounded above by $0$. By \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, $u_j$ decreases to $u$, which is a potential by hypothesis, and by Step~1, $\mu_{u_j}$ is carried by $\{u_j=f_j\}$. Moreover $u_j\leq V_\theta$.

Fix a coordinate ball $D\Subset X\setminus A$ as in Step~1 and constants $C,\varepsilon>0$, and put
\[
 v_j^C=\max\{u_j,V_\theta-C\},\qquad
 a_j=\frac{v_j^C-V_\theta+C}{v_j^C-V_\theta+C+\varepsilon},
\]
and define $v^C$ and $a$ in the same way with $u$ in place of $u_j$. On $D$ these truncated potentials are bounded uniformly in $j$ and decrease to $v^C$, so they converge in capacity. The weights satisfy $0\leq a_j\leq1$, they are quasi-continuous, they converge in capacity to $a$, since $s\mapsto(s+C)/(s+C+\varepsilon)$ is Lipschitz on $[-C,0]$, and $a_j=0$ on $\{u_j\leq V_\theta-C\}$.

Next let $b_j=\mathrm e^{u_j-f_j}$ and $b=\mathrm e^{u-f}$ at the points where both exponents are defined, the functions involved are finite and the exponents are nonpositive, and put $b_j=1$ and $b=1$ elsewhere. Since $u\leq f$ and $u_j\leq f_j$ quasi-everywhere and $f\leq0$ quasi-everywhere, the exceptional set is pluripolar: it is contained in the union of the pole set of $u$ and of countably many pluripolar sets. We claim that $b_j\to b$ in capacity on compact subsets of $D$ and that $b$ is quasi-continuous. Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $\varepsilon'>0$. Cover the exceptional pluripolar set by an open set of capacity less than $\varepsilon'$, and enlarge it, using the quasi-continuity of $u$ and $f$, to an open set $G$ of capacity less than $2\varepsilon'$ such that $u$ and $f$ are continuous and finite on the compact set $F=K\setminus G$. Then $u,f\geq-N$ on $F$ for some $N$, so $f_j=f$ on $F$ for $j\geq N$. Since the exponential is $1$-Lipschitz on $(-\infty,0]$, we get $|b_j-b|\leq|u_j-u|$ on $F$ for $j\geq N$. As $u\leq u_j$ and $u\geq-N$ on $F$, we have $u_j-u=\max\{u_j,-N\}-\max\{u,-N\}$ there, and these truncations converge in capacity because they decrease. Hence the capacity of $K\cap\{|b_j-b|>\delta\}$ is at most $2\varepsilon'$ plus a quantity that tends to zero, and $b$ is continuous on $F$. This proves the claim.

Since $a_j$ vanishes outside the plurifine open set $\{u_j>V_\theta-C\}$, on which $v_j^C=u_j$, and since $\mu_{u_j}$ is carried by $\{u_j=f_j\}$, where $b_j=1$ up to a pluripolar set, we have
\[
 a_j(1-b_j)\mu_{v_j^C}=a_j(1-b_j)\mu_{u_j}=0.
\]
The weights $a_j(1-b_j)$ are bounded and converge in capacity to the quasi-continuous function $a(1-b)$. The weighted form of \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}, applied to the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\rho+v_j^C$, gives $a(1-b)\mu_{v^C}=0$ on $D$, and plurifine locality turns this into $a(1-b)\mu_u=0$ on $D$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and then $C\to\infty$. Since $V_\theta$ is finite on $D$, the weights $a$ tend to $1$ at every point of $D$ where $u$ is finite. They are bounded by $1$, and $\mu_u$ does not charge the pole set of $u$, so dominated convergence gives $(1-b)\mu_u=0$ on $D$. Thus $u=f$ holds $\mu_u$-almost everywhere on $D$. Covering $X\setminus A$ by such balls and using $\mu_u(A)=0$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\index{envelope!contact-set concentration}
In \cref{cor:pseudoeffective-obstacle-contact} of the next chapter, the bigness of $\{\theta\}$ and the upper bound on $f$ are removed: it suffices that $\mathcal A_\theta(f)$ be nonempty and uniformly bounded above.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and suppose that $w=P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$. Then
\[
 \mu_w\leq\mathbf1_{\{w=u\}}\mu_u
                       +\mathbf1_{\{w=v\}}\mu_v.
\]
More generally, for finitely many potentials $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ such that $w=P_\theta(\min_ju_j)\ne-\infty$, the measure $\mu_w$ is carried by $\bigcup_j\{w=u_j\}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Potentials are quasi-continuous, hence so is the obstacle $\min\{u,v\}$, which is also bounded above. By \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_w$ is carried by $\{w=\min\{u,v\}\}\subset\{w=u\}\cup\{w=v\}$. Since $w\leq u$ and $w\leq v$ everywhere, \cref{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure} gives $\mathbf1_{\{w=u\}}\mu_w\leq\mathbf1_{\{w=u\}}\mu_u$ and $\mathbf1_{\{w=v\}}\mu_w\leq\mathbf1_{\{w=v\}}\mu_v$. Adding these inequalities proves the first assertion; the intersection of the two contact sets is counted twice, which is harmless for an upper bound. The same argument, applied to the obstacle $\min_ju_j$, proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Singularity envelopes}
\label{sec:singularity-envelopes}
We now replace a pointwise obstacle by a constraint on singularities: we take the upper envelope of all nonpositive potentials that are more singular than a given potential $u$. Since the constant $C$ in $v\leq u+C$ may depend on the competitor $v$, the envelope can be less singular than $u$; this happens in \cref{ex:loglog-full-mass-envelope} below.

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-p-envelope}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the \emph{singularity envelope} of $u$ is
\[
 P_\theta[u]=\bigl(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq0,
                                                   \ v\preceq u\}\bigr)^*.
\]
A \emph{model potential} is a potential $\phi$ with $P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$ and positive mass $M(\phi)>0$. Since $u-\sup_Xu$ is a competitor and all competitors are nonpositive, the singularity envelope is always defined, belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, and satisfies $u-\sup_Xu\leq P_\theta[u]\leq V_\theta$ and $\sup_XP_\theta[u]=0$.
\end{definition}
\index{envelope!singularity $P$-envelope}\index{model potential}\index{singularity envelope}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:p-envelope-calculus}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $P_\theta[u]$ depends only on the singularity type of $u$, and $u\preceq u'$ implies $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[u']$.
 \item If $u\leq0$, then the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u+k,V_\theta)$ increase with $k$, and
 \[
  P_\theta[u]=\left(\lim_{k\to\infty}
                         P_\theta(u+k,V_\theta)\right)^*.
 \]
 \item The singularity envelope preserves all mixed non-pluripolar masses: if $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_p$, $1\leq p\leq n$, are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms and $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$, then
 \[
  \int_X\left\langle\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\ddc P_{\theta_j}[u_j])\right\rangle
                                       \wedge\omega^{n-p}
  =\int_X\left\langle\bigwedge_j(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)\right\rangle
                                       \wedge\omega^{n-p}.
 \]
 In particular $M(P_\theta[u])=M(u)$.
 \item If $u$ has minimal singularities, then $P_\theta[u]=V_\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) The family of competitors depends only on the singularity type of $u$, and it increases when $u$ is replaced by a less singular potential.

(2) The rooftop envelopes increase with $k$ because the rooftop envelope is monotone. Every competitor $v$ for $P_\theta[u]$ satisfies $v\leq u+k$ for some integer $k$ and $v\leq V_\theta$, hence $v\leq P_\theta(u+k,V_\theta)$. Conversely, each $P_\theta(u+k,V_\theta)$ is nonpositive and, by \cref{prop:rooftop-algebra}, has the singularity type of $u$; so it is a competitor. Hence the two families have the same supremum, which proves the formula.

(3) By (1) we may assume $u_j\leq0$. By \cref{prop:rooftop-algebra}, the tuple of rooftop envelopes $P_{\theta_j}(u_j+k,V_{\theta_j})$ has the singularity types of the $u_j$, so it has the same mixed mass as the tuple $(u_j)$ by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass}. By (2), these rooftop envelopes increase almost everywhere to $P_{\theta_j}[u_j]$, and \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity} shows that their mixed masses converge to the mixed mass of the envelopes.

(4) If $u$ has minimal singularities, then $V_\theta\preceq u$, so $V_\theta$ is itself a competitor; it dominates all other competitors, since they are nonpositive.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:p-envelope-contact-concentration}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then
\[
 \mu_{P_\theta[u]}
       \leq\mathbf1_{\{P_\theta[u]=V_\theta\}}\mu_{V_\theta},
\]
and $\mu_{P_\theta[u]}$ is carried by $\{P_\theta[u]=0\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus} we may assume $u\leq0$. Put $w_k=P_\theta(u+k,V_\theta)$. By \cref{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality},
\[
 \mu_{w_k}\leq\mathbf1_{\{w_k=u+k\}}\mu_u+\mathbf1_{\{w_k=V_\theta\}}\mu_{V_\theta}
        \leq\mathbf1_{\{u+k\leq V_\theta\}}\mu_u
        +\mathbf1_{\{P_\theta[u]=V_\theta\}}\mu_{V_\theta};
\]
for the second inequality we used $w_k\leq V_\theta$ and $w_k\leq P_\theta[u]\leq V_\theta$. Since $u$ and $V_\theta$ are finite $\mu_u$-almost everywhere, the sets $\{u+k\leq V_\theta\}$ shrink to a $\mu_u$-null set, and the first term tends to zero in total variation by dominated convergence. By \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}, $w_k$ increases almost everywhere to $P_\theta[u]$, so $\mu_{w_k}\to\mu_{P_\theta[u]}$ weakly by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}. Testing against nonnegative continuous functions gives the asserted inequality. Finally, $V_\theta=P_\theta(0)$, so \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact} shows that $\mu_{V_\theta}$ is carried by $\{V_\theta=0\}$. Hence $\mu_{P_\theta[u]}$ is carried by $\{P_\theta[u]=V_\theta=0\}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Extrapolation and model singularities}
\label{sec:extrapolation-model-singularities}
Throughout this section $\{\theta\}$ is big, and all potentials belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$. Mass is monotone with respect to $\preceq$, but two potentials with the same mass need not have bounded difference. The key step is to reverse a convex combination: if $u\preceq v$ and the mass lost from $v$ to $u$ is small, then for some $a>1$ the function $v+a(u-v)$ still has a minorant of positive mass. Equivalently, $u$ lies above a convex combination $a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})v$. Contact concentration prevents the envelopes in the construction from collapsing. This extrapolation yields the domination principle and identifies the singularity envelopes of potentials of positive mass as model potentials.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mass-preserving-model-majorant}
Let $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\psi\leq0$ and $M(\psi)>0$. Then there is a model potential $\phi\geq\psi$ with $M(\phi)=M(\psi)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a smooth probability volume form $\mathrm dV$ on $X$, put $m=M(\psi)$, and consider
\[
 \mathcal S=\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):\psi\leq v\leq0,
                                             \ M(v)=m\},
 \qquad H(v)=\int_Xv\,\mathrm dV.
\]
The set $\mathcal S$ contains $\psi$, and $H$ takes values in $[H(\psi),0]$ on it. We construct an increasing sequence in $\mathcal S$ that almost maximizes $H$ at every stage. Put $v_0=\psi$. Given $v_k\in\mathcal S$, let
\[
 s_k=\sup\{H(w):w\in\mathcal S,\ w\geq v_k\},
\]
and choose $v_{k+1}\in\mathcal S$ with $v_{k+1}\geq v_k$ and $H(v_{k+1})\geq s_k-2^{-k}$. The regularized limit $\phi=(\sup_kv_k)^*$ is a potential with $\psi\leq\phi\leq0$, and $M(\phi)=m$ by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}; thus $\phi\in\mathcal S$. Since $v_k$ increases to $\phi$ almost everywhere, between the integrable bounds $\psi$ and $0$, dominated convergence gives $H(v_k)\to H(\phi)$.

Let $w\in\mathcal S$ with $w\geq\phi$. Then $w\geq v_k$ for every $k$, so
\[
 H(w)\leq s_k\leq H(v_{k+1})+2^{-k},
\]
and letting $k\to\infty$ gives $H(w)\leq H(\phi)$. Since $w\geq\phi$, it follows that $w=\phi$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. Now $P_\theta[\phi]$ lies between $\phi$ and $0$, because $\phi\leq0$ is a competitor for it, and it has mass $m$ by \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}. Thus $P_\theta[\phi]\in\mathcal S$ and $P_\theta[\phi]\geq\phi$, so $P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$. Since $m>0$, $\phi$ is a model potential.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:model-normalized-supremum}
Let $\phi,\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$ and $\tau\preceq\phi$. Then
\[
 \sup_{\{\phi> -\infty\}}(\tau-\phi)=\sup_X\tau.
\]
In particular, if $\tau\leq0$, then $\tau\leq\phi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The potential $\tau-\sup_X\tau$ is nonpositive and more singular than $\phi$, so it is a competitor for $P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$. Hence $\tau-\phi\leq\sup_X\tau$ wherever $\phi$ is finite. Conversely, $\phi\leq0$, so $\tau-\phi\geq\tau$ wherever $\phi$ is finite. The pole set of $\phi$ is pluripolar, and removing a pluripolar set does not change the supremum of the quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\tau$: an upper bound for $\tau$ off a Lebesgue null set holds everywhere. This proves the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\preceq v$ and $M(u)>0$, and let $a>1$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrapolation-mass-margin}
 a^n\bigl(M(v)-M(u)\bigr)<M(v).
\end{equation}
Then there exists $\eta\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $M(\eta)>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality}
 a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})v\leq u.
\end{equation}
The largest such $\eta$ is the obstacle envelope $E_a(u,v)=P_\theta(au+(1-a)v)$; here the obstacle $au+(1-a)v$ is defined to be $-\infty$ on the pole set of $u$, which contains that of $v$. If $M(u)=M(v)$, then \eqref{eq:extrapolation-mass-margin} holds for every $a>1$, and $M(E_a(u,v))=M(u)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Reduction.} Since $u\preceq v$, there are constants $A,B$ with $u-A\leq v-B\leq0$. If $\eta'$ satisfies \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality} for the pair $u-A$, $v-B$, then $\eta'+aA+(1-a)B$ satisfies it for $u$, $v$; the envelopes of the two pairs differ by the same constant, and masses do not change. We may therefore assume that $u\leq v\leq0$. Then $m=M(v)\geq M(u)>0$, and \cref{lem:mass-preserving-model-majorant} provides a model potential $\phi\geq v$ with $M(\phi)=m$. Since $1-a<0$, we have $au+(1-a)\phi\leq au+(1-a)v$, so it suffices first to construct a potential of positive mass below $au+(1-a)\phi$.

\emph{A decreasing sequence of envelopes.} For $j\geq1$ put $u_j=\max\{u,\phi-j\}$, so that $\phi-j\leq u_j\leq\phi$, and
\[
 f_j=au_j+(1-a)\phi,\qquad \eta_j=P_\theta(f_j),
\]
where $f_j=-\infty$ on the pole set of $\phi$. Outside this pole set, $f_j=\phi+a(u_j-\phi)$ lies between $\phi-aj$ and $\phi$. The obstacle $f_j$ is quasi-continuous: on a compact set on which $u$ and $\phi$ are continuous with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$, the function $f_j$ is continuous at the points where $\phi$ is finite, and at a pole of $\phi$ it is continuous with value $-\infty$ because $f_j\leq\phi$. The potential $\phi-aj$ is a competitor, and every competitor is at most $\phi$. Hence $\eta_j$ is a potential with
\[
 \phi-aj\leq\eta_j\leq\phi,\qquad M(\eta_j)=M(u_j)=m,
\]
where the masses agree because $\eta_j\sim\phi\sim u_j$. Since $u_j$ decreases with $j$, so do $f_j$ and $\eta_j$.

\emph{A contact bound.} Put $\gamma_j=a^{-1}\eta_j+(1-a^{-1})\phi$. Off the pole set of $\phi$, the inequality $\eta_j\leq f_j$, which holds quasi-everywhere, is equivalent to $\gamma_j\leq u_j$; hence $\gamma_j\leq u_j$ everywhere. Let $D_j=\{\gamma_j=u_j>-\infty\}$. By \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_{\eta_j}$ is carried by $\{\eta_j=f_j\}$, whose points off the pole set of $\phi$ lie in $D_j$. Since $\mu_{\gamma_j}\geq a^{-n}\mu_{\eta_j}$, and $\mathbf1_{D_j}\mu_{\gamma_j}\leq\mathbf1_{D_j}\mu_{u_j}$ by \cref{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrapolation-contact-bound}
 \mu_{\eta_j}\leq a^n\mathbf1_{D_j}\mu_{u_j}.
\end{equation}

\emph{A tail estimate.} Fix $k>0$ and let $j>k$. On $D_j$ we have $\eta_j=\phi+a(u_j-\phi)$, so there $\eta_j\leq\phi-ak$ if and only if $u_j\leq\phi-k$, that is, if and only if $u\leq\phi-k$. The plurifine open set $\{u>\phi-k\}$ is contained in $\{u>\phi-j\}$, on which $u_j=u$; hence $\mu_{u_j}=\mu_u$ on $\{u>\phi-k\}$, and $\mu_{u_j}(\{u\leq\phi-k\})=m-\mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})$, pole sets having measure zero. Together with \eqref{eq:extrapolation-contact-bound} this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrapolation-tail-bound}
 \mu_{\eta_j}(\{\eta_j\leq\phi-ak\})
 \leq a^n\left(m-\mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})\right).
\end{equation}

\emph{The envelopes do not collapse.} Suppose that $\eta_j$ decreases to $-\infty$ identically. Then $\sup_X\eta_j\to-\infty$ by \cref{cor:global-decreasing-nets}. Since $\eta_j\preceq\phi$ and $\phi$ is a model potential, \cref{lem:model-normalized-supremum} gives $\eta_j-\phi\leq\sup_X\eta_j$ off the pole set of $\phi$. Hence, for each $k$, $\eta_j\leq\phi-ak$ everywhere once $j$ is large, and the left side of \eqref{eq:extrapolation-tail-bound} equals $M(\eta_j)=m$. Since $\phi$ is finite wherever $u$ is, $\mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})\to M(u)$ as $k\to\infty$, and \eqref{eq:extrapolation-tail-bound} yields $m\leq a^n(m-M(u))$, which contradicts \eqref{eq:extrapolation-mass-margin}. Consequently $\eta_j$ decreases to a potential $\eta$. Letting $j\to\infty$ in $\gamma_j\leq u_j$ gives $a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})\phi\leq u$, and since $v\leq\phi$, also \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality}.

\emph{The largest solution.} The obstacle $f=au+(1-a)v$ satisfies $f=u+(a-1)(u-v)\leq u\leq0$. It is quasi-continuous: on a compact set on which $u$ and $v$ are continuous, $f$ is continuous at the points where $u$ is finite, and at a pole of $u$ it is continuous with value $-\infty$ because $f\leq u$. A potential $\eta$ satisfies \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality} if and only if $\eta\leq f$ quasi-everywhere: the two inequalities are equivalent off the pole set of $u$, and \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality} is an inequality between quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, which holds everywhere once it holds quasi-everywhere. Thus the potentials satisfying \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality} are exactly the competitors for $f$. The potential constructed above is one of them, and all of them are nonpositive. Hence $E_a=E_a(u,v)=P_\theta(f)$ is defined, and by \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member} it is the largest potential satisfying \eqref{eq:extrapolation-convex-inequality}. Moreover $E_a\leq u$, since $f\leq u$.

\emph{Positive mass.} Choose $b>a$ that still satisfies \eqref{eq:extrapolation-mass-margin}. The preceding steps, applied to $b$, give $E_b=E_b(u,v)$. The potential
\[
 q=\frac abE_b+\left(1-\frac ab\right)u
\]
is a competitor for the obstacle $f$ of $E_a$: quasi-everywhere, $E_b\leq bu+(1-b)v$, and therefore
\[
 q\leq au+(1-a)v+\left(1-\frac ab\right)(u-v)\leq au+(1-a)v.
\]
Hence $q\leq E_a$, and the monotonicity of mass together with $\mu_q\geq(1-a/b)^n\mu_u$ yields
\[
 M(E_a)\geq M(q)\geq\left(1-\frac ab\right)^nM(u)>0.
\]
If $M(u)=M(v)$, then \eqref{eq:extrapolation-mass-margin} holds for every $a>1$, so $b$ can be taken arbitrarily large, and we obtain $M(E_a)\geq M(u)$. The reverse inequality follows from $E_a\leq u$.
\end{proof}
\index{envelope!extrapolation}\index{Monge--Ampere mass!extrapolation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-mass-strict-minorant}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $M(u)>0$. Then there are $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\leq u$ and $\delta>0$ such that $\theta_v\geq\delta\omega$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $m=M(V_\theta)$. Since $u\preceq V_\theta$ and $M(u)>0$, we can choose $a>1$ so close to one that $a^n(m-M(u))<m$. \Cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation} gives $\eta\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
$a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})V_\theta\leq u$. Since the class is big, there is $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\psi\leq0$ and $\theta_\psi\geq\delta_0\omega$ for some $\delta_0>0$, and $\psi\leq V_\theta$ because $\psi$ is a competitor for $V_\theta$. Therefore
\[
 v=a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})\psi\leq u,\qquad
 \theta_v=a^{-1}\theta_\eta+(1-a^{-1})\theta_\psi\geq(1-a^{-1})\delta_0\omega.
\]
\end{proof}
\index{positive current!strict minorant}

Recall from \cref{def:full-nonpluripolar-mass} that, for $\chi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the class $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\chi)$ of potentials of full mass relative to $\chi$ consists of the potentials $u\preceq\chi$ with $M(u)=M(\chi)$.

\begin{theorem}[Domination principle]\label{thm:relative-full-mass-domination}
Let $\chi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $M(\chi)>0$, and let $u,v\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\chi)$. If $\mu_u(\{u<v\})=0$, then $u\geq v$ everywhere on $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The potential $\max\{u,v\}$ is more singular than $\chi$ and less singular than $u$, so $M(\chi)\geq M(\max\{u,v\})\geq M(u)=M(\chi)$; thus $\max\{u,v\}\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\chi)$. Since $\{u<\max\{u,v\}\}=\{u<v\}$, we may replace $v$ by $\max\{u,v\}$ and assume that $u\leq v$. Subtracting the same constant from $u$ and $v$, we may also assume $v\leq0$. Let $m>0$ be the common mass of $u$, $v$ and $\chi$.

For $a>1$ let $\eta_a=E_a(u,v)$. By \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}, $M(\eta_a)=m$, and $\eta_a\leq u$ because its obstacle $u+(a-1)(u-v)$ is at most $u$. Put $\gamma_a=a^{-1}\eta_a+(1-a^{-1})v\leq u$ and $D_a=\{\gamma_a=u>-\infty\}$. The argument that proved \eqref{eq:extrapolation-contact-bound}, applied to the quasi-continuous obstacle of $\eta_a$, gives
\[
 \mu_{\eta_a}\leq a^n\mathbf1_{D_a}\mu_u.
\]
Since $u\leq v$, the hypothesis means that $\mu_u$ is carried by $\{u=v\}$, so $\mu_{\eta_a}$ is carried by $D_a\cap\{u=v\}$. At a point of this set, $a^{-1}\eta_a+(1-a^{-1})u=u$, that is, $\eta_a=u$. Hence $\mu_{\eta_a}$ is carried by $\{\eta_a=u\}$, and \cref{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure}, applied to $\eta_a\leq u$, gives $\mu_{\eta_a}\leq\mathbf1_{\{\eta_a=u\}}\mu_u\leq\mu_u$. Both measures have mass $m$, so they are equal, and $\eta_a=u$ holds $\mu_u$-almost everywhere. In particular
\[
 \int_X\mathrm e^{\eta_a}\,\mu_u
        =\int_X\mathrm e^u\,\mu_u>0,
\]
where the last integral is positive because $m>0$ and $\mu_u$ does not charge the pole set of $u$.

As $a$ increases, the obstacle $v+a(u-v)$ decreases, so $\eta_a$ decreases. If $\eta_a$ decreased to $-\infty$ identically as $a\to\infty$ through the integers, dominated convergence, with $0\leq\mathrm e^{\eta_a}\leq1$, would make the left side of the last display tend to zero, a contradiction. Hence the limit $\eta$ along the integers is a potential. Each $\eta_a$ satisfies $\eta_a\leq v+a(u-v)$ outside a pluripolar set; let $N$ be the union of these sets over the integers $a\geq2$. At a point outside $N$ where $u$ is finite and $u<v$, we get $\eta\leq v+a(u-v)$ for every integer $a$, hence $\eta=-\infty$. Thus $\{u<v\}$ is contained in the union of $N$ and the pole sets of $u$ and $\eta$, which is pluripolar. Therefore $u\geq v$ quasi-everywhere, and hence everywhere.
\end{proof}
\index{domination principle!relative full mass}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\leq0$ and $M(u)>0$. Then
\[
 P_\theta[u]=\max\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq0,
                         \ u\preceq v,\ M(v)=M(u)\}.
\]
In particular $P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]=P_\theta[u]$, so $P_\theta[u]$ is a model potential of mass $M(u)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ belong to the displayed family. By \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}, $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[v]$ and $M(P_\theta[u])=M(u)=M(v)=M(P_\theta[v])$; moreover $v\leq P_\theta[v]$. Hence $P_\theta[u]$ and $v$ both belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta,P_\theta[v])$, a class whose reference has positive mass. By \cref{prop:p-envelope-contact-concentration}, $\mu_{P_\theta[u]}$ is carried by $\{P_\theta[u]=0\}$, and since $v\leq0$, it gives no mass to $\{P_\theta[u]<v\}$. The domination principle, \cref{thm:relative-full-mass-domination}, yields $P_\theta[u]\geq v$. Since $P_\theta[u]$ itself belongs to the family, this proves the formula.

The potential $P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]$ is nonpositive, satisfies $u\preceq P_\theta[u]\preceq P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]$, and has mass $M(u)$ by \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}. Hence it belongs to the family, and the formula gives $P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]\leq P_\theta[u]$. Conversely, $P_\theta[u]\leq0$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]$. Since $M(P_\theta[u])=M(u)>0$, the potential $P_\theta[u]$ is a model potential.
\end{proof}
\index{model potential!characterization}

\begin{example}\label{ex:loglog-full-mass-envelope}
On $\mathbb P^1$ let $\omega=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2)$ in the affine coordinate $z$, so that $\int_{\mathbb P^1}\omega=1$; since $\omega$ is K\"ahler, $V_\omega=0$. Define
\[
 F(r)=\begin{cases}
       \max\{-\log(-r),r+2\},&r\leq-1,\\
       r+2,&r\geq-1,
      \end{cases}
 \qquad
 u(z)=F(\log|z|^2)-\log(1+|z|^2).
\]
For $r$ close to $-1$ the maximum equals $r+2$, so $F$ is convex and increasing; its slope tends to zero as $r\to-\infty$ and equals one for $r\geq-1$. Hence $F(\log|z|^2)$ is subharmonic on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$; it tends to $-\infty$ at $0$ and extends subharmonically across $0$ by \cref{thm:polar-removable}. For $|z|^2\geq\mathrm e^{-1}$ we have $u=2-\log(1+|z|^{-2})$, which is smooth near $\infty$ with $u(\infty)=2$. Thus $u\in\PSH(\mathbb P^1,\omega)$, with $\omega+\ddc u=\ddc F(\log|z|^2)$ on $\mathbb C$, and its only pole is $0$. Its Lelong number at $0$ is zero, since $\sup_{|z|<\mathrm e^{t/2}}u=F(t)-\log(1+\mathrm e^t)$ and
\[
 \lim_{r\to-\infty}\frac{F(r)}r=0.
\]
By \cref{thm:euclidean-jensen-lelong}, in one variable the mass that $\ddc F(\log|z|^2)$ gives to $0$ is this Lelong number, so the current $\omega+\ddc u$ has no atom at its pole. By \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-degree-one-restriction}, $\mu_u$ is the restriction of $\omega+\ddc u$ to $\{u>-\infty\}$, hence $M(u)=\int_{\mathbb P^1}\omega=1$. The potential $0$ has the same mass and is less singular than $u$, so \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}, applied to $u-\sup_{\mathbb P^1}u$, gives $P_\omega[u]=0$. Near $0$, however, $u=-\log(-\log|z|^2)+O(1)$ is unbounded below. Thus the singularity envelope can change the singularity type of a potential while preserving its full mass.
\end{example}
\index{singularity!log-log}

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-singularity-envelope}
Let $u,\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\preceq\phi$. The \emph{singularity envelope of $u$ relative to $\phi$} is
\[
 P_\theta[u](\phi)
       =\left(\lim_{C\to\infty}P_\theta(u+C,\phi)\right)^*.
\]
Choose $C_0$ with $u-C_0\leq\phi$. For $C\geq-C_0$, the potential $u-C_0$ is a common minorant of $u+C$ and $\phi$, so the rooftop envelope $P_\theta(u+C,\phi)$ lies between $u-C_0$ and $u+C$ and has the singularity type of $u$. These rooftop envelopes increase with $C$ and are bounded above by $\phi$, so the regularized limit is a potential between $u-C_0$ and $\phi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization}
Let $u,\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\preceq\phi$ and $M(\phi)>0$. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$;
 \item $P_\theta[u](\phi)=\phi$;
 \item $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[\phi]$.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, the relative envelope has mass $M(P_\theta[u](\phi))=M(u)$ and satisfies
\[
 \mu_{P_\theta[u](\phi)}
       \leq\mathbf1_{\{P_\theta[u](\phi)=\phi\}}\mu_\phi.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $w_C=P_\theta(u+C,\phi)$ and $w=P_\theta[u](\phi)$. Each $w_C$ with $C$ large has the singularity type of $u$, hence mass $M(u)$, and $w_C$ increases almost everywhere to $w$; by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}, $M(w)=M(u)$. By \cref{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality}, together with $w_C\leq\phi$ and $w_C\leq w\leq\phi$,
\[
 \mu_{w_C}\leq\mathbf1_{\{u+C\leq\phi\}}\mu_u
                  +\mathbf1_{\{w=\phi\}}\mu_\phi.
\]
The first term tends to zero in total variation by dominated convergence, since $u$ and $\phi$ are finite $\mu_u$-almost everywhere, and $\mu_{w_C}\to\mu_w$ weakly by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}. This proves the asserted measure bound.

If (1) holds, then $w$ and $\phi$ have the same positive mass and both belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$. By the measure bound, $\mu_w$ gives no mass to $\{w<\phi\}$, and the domination principle, \cref{thm:relative-full-mass-domination}, gives $w\geq\phi$; hence (2). Conversely, (2) gives $M(u)=M(w)=M(\phi)$, which is (1).

To prove that (2) implies (3), normalize $\phi\leq0$; this changes none of the singularity envelopes involved. Every $w_C$ is then a nonpositive potential with the singularity type of $u$, hence a competitor for $P_\theta[u]$, so $\phi=w\leq P_\theta[u]$. By (2) and (1), $M(u)=M(\phi)>0$, so $P_\theta[u]$ is a model potential by \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}. Monotonicity and $u\preceq\phi$ now give
\[
 P_\theta[\phi]\leq P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]=P_\theta[u]
                              \leq P_\theta[\phi].
\]
Finally, (3) implies $M(u)=M(P_\theta[u])=M(P_\theta[\phi])=M(\phi)$ by \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}, which is (1).
\end{proof}
\index{envelope!relative singularity envelope}\index{full mass!envelope characterization}

\section{Rooftops and mass inequalities}
\label{sec:rooftop-mass-inequalities}
Two potentials need not have a common minorant, as \cref{ex:empty-rooftop-projective-line} shows. We prove that a strict mass inequality guarantees one, and that the masses of $u$, $v$, $\max\{u,v\}$ and $P_\theta(u,v)$ satisfy the inequality of \cref{thm:rooftop-mass-diamond}. Contact concentration for model potentials gives these results for model potentials, and extrapolation transfers them to arbitrary potentials of full mass relative to model potentials. As in the preceding section, $\{\theta\}$ is big.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-mass-rooftop-existence}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy
\[
 M(u)+M(v)>M(\max\{u,v\}).
\]
Then $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $u,v\leq0$, put $m=M(\max\{u,v\})$ and $\phi=P_\theta[\max\{u,v\}]$. By monotonicity $M(u)\leq m$ and $M(v)\leq m$, so the strict inequality forces $M(u)>0$ and $M(v)>0$. Hence $m>0$, and by \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}, $\phi$ is a model potential of mass $m$. Moreover $u,v\leq\max\{u,v\}\leq\phi$.

Since $\mu_u$ and $\mu_v$ do not charge pole sets, $\mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})\to M(u)$ and $\mu_v(\{v>\phi-k\})\to M(v)$ as $k\to\infty$. Choose $k>0$ so large that
\[
 \mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})+\mu_v(\{v>\phi-k\})>m.
\]
For $C>k$ put
\[
 u_C=\max\{u,\phi-C\},\quad
 v_C=\max\{v,\phi-C\},\quad
 w_C=P_\theta(u_C,v_C).
\]
Since $\phi-C$ is a common minorant, all three potentials have the singularity type of $\phi$ and mass $m$. As $C\to\infty$, $w_C$ decreases to $P_\theta(u,v)$ by \cref{prop:rooftop-algebra}, possibly to $-\infty$. Suppose that it did collapse. Then $\sup_Xw_C\to-\infty$ by \cref{cor:global-decreasing-nets}, and \cref{lem:model-normalized-supremum} gives $w_C\leq\phi-k$ for all large $C$. On $\{w_C=u_C\}$ we then have $u_C\leq\phi-k$, and similarly for $v_C$, so \cref{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality} gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 m=M(w_C)&\leq\mu_{u_C}(\{u_C\leq\phi-k\})
                +\mu_{v_C}(\{v_C\leq\phi-k\})\\
  &=2m-\mu_u(\{u>\phi-k\})-\mu_v(\{v>\phi-k\})<m.
 \end{split}
\]
For the equality we used that, since $C>k$, $u_C=u$ on the plurifine open set $\{u>\phi-k\}$, so $\mu_{u_C}=\mu_u$ there, and similarly for $v$. This contradiction shows that $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-rooftop-fixed-point}
Let $\phi,\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$ and $P_\theta[\psi]=\psi$, and suppose that $w=P_\theta(\phi,\psi)\ne-\infty$. Then $P_\theta[w]=w$. Thus the rooftop envelope of two model potentials is a model potential whenever its mass is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $w\leq\phi$, monotonicity gives $P_\theta[w]\leq P_\theta[\phi]=\phi$, and likewise $P_\theta[w]\leq\psi$. Thus $P_\theta[w]$ is a common minorant of $\phi$ and $\psi$, and $P_\theta[w]\leq w$. Conversely, $w\leq\phi\leq0$, so $w$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[w]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rooftop-full-mass-substitution}
Let $\phi,\psi$ be model potentials whose rooftop envelope $w=P_\theta(\phi,\psi)$ has positive mass. If $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ and $v\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\psi)$, then $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$ and $M(P_\theta(u,v))=M(w)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We may shift $u$ and $v$ by constants so that $u\leq\phi$ and $v\leq\psi$. Indeed, shifting $u$ or $v$ by a constant does not affect the existence of a common minorant, since a given minorant can be shifted down by the largest absolute change. Once $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$ is known, \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member} shows that the rooftop envelopes of the original and of the shifted pair differ by a bounded amount, because their obstacles do; so they have the same mass.

For $a>1$ let $u_a=E_a(u,\phi)$. By \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}, $u_a\leq u\leq\phi$, $M(u_a)=M(\phi)$, and $a^{-1}u_a+(1-a^{-1})\phi\leq u$. Since $\max\{u_a,w\}\leq\phi$ and $M(w)>0$,
\[
 M(u_a)+M(w)>M(\phi)\geq M(\max\{u_a,w\}).
\]
By \cref{prop:positive-mass-rooftop-existence}, the rooftop envelope $P_\theta(u_a,w)$ is a potential. By associativity and $u_a\leq\phi$, it equals $P_\theta(u_a,\psi)$. The potential
\[
 a^{-1}P_\theta(u_a,\psi)+(1-a^{-1})w
\]
lies below $a^{-1}u_a+(1-a^{-1})\phi\leq u$ and below $\psi$, so it is a common minorant of $u$ and $\psi$. Hence $z=P_\theta(u,\psi)$ is a potential, and positivity and monotonicity of mass give $M(z)\geq(1-a^{-1})^nM(w)$. On the other hand $z\leq P_\theta(\phi,\psi)=w$, so $M(z)\leq M(w)$. Letting $a\to\infty$, we obtain $M(z)=M(w)>0$.

Now let $v_a=E_a(v,\psi)$, so that $v_a\leq\psi$, $M(v_a)=M(\psi)$ and $a^{-1}v_a+(1-a^{-1})\psi\leq v$. The same criterion, applied to $v_a$ and $z\leq\psi$, shows that $P_\theta(z,v_a)$ is a potential, and by associativity and $v_a\leq\psi$ it equals $P_\theta(u,v_a)$. The potential
\[
 a^{-1}P_\theta(u,v_a)+(1-a^{-1})z
\]
lies below $u$ and below $a^{-1}v_a+(1-a^{-1})\psi\leq v$, and its mass is at least $(1-a^{-1})^nM(z)$. Hence $P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$ and $M(P_\theta(u,v))\geq(1-a^{-1})^nM(w)$ for every $a>1$. Since $P_\theta(u,v)\leq w$, letting $a\to\infty$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:decreasing-zero-contact-mass}
Let $q_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be nonpositive potentials decreasing to a potential $q$. Then
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}\mu_{q_j}(\{q_j=0\})\leq\mu_q(\{q=0\}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\lambda_j=\mathbf1_{\{q_j=0\}}\mu_{q_j}$. Since $q_j\leq V_\theta\leq0$, the set $\{q_j=0\}$ is contained in $\{q_j=V_\theta\}$, and \cref{lem:ordered-potential-contact-measure} gives $\lambda_j\leq\mu_{V_\theta}$. Let $A$ be the proper analytic set of the proof of \cref{lem:bounded-lsc-obstacle-contact}: potentials with minimal singularities are locally bounded on $X\setminus A$, and $\mu_{V_\theta}(A)=0$.

For $t>0$ put $b_j=\max\{1+tq_j,0\}$ and $b=\max\{1+tq,0\}$, with value zero at the poles. Fix a coordinate ball $D\Subset X\setminus A$ and $L>1/t$, and put $\widetilde q_j=\max\{q_j,V_\theta-L\}$. On $D$ these functions are uniformly bounded, and they decrease to $\max\{q,V_\theta-L\}$. Since $V_\theta-L<-1/t$, the weight $b_j$ vanishes on $\{q_j\leq V_\theta-L\}$, and on the complementary plurifine open set $\widetilde q_j=q_j$. Plurifine locality and the weighted form of \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity} therefore give
\[
 b_j\mu_{q_j}=b_j\mu_{\widetilde q_j}
                 \longrightarrow b\mu_q
 \quad\text{weakly on }D;
\]
the weights are bounded and quasi-continuous and converge in capacity, as that theorem requires.

Let $\delta>0$. Choose a compact set $K\subset X\setminus A$ with $\mu_{V_\theta}(X\setminus K)<\delta$, and a smooth function $0\leq\zeta\leq1$ with compact support in $X\setminus A$ and $\zeta=1$ on $K$. Since $b_j=1$ on $\{q_j=0\}$ and $\lambda_j\leq\mu_{V_\theta}$,
\[
 \lambda_j(X)\leq\int_X\zeta b_j\,\mu_{q_j}+\delta.
\]
Using a finite partition of unity subordinate to balls as above, we can pass to the limit in the integral; letting then $\delta\downarrow0$ gives $\limsup_j\lambda_j(X)\leq\int_Xb\,\mu_q$. Finally, as $t\to\infty$ the weights $b$ decrease to $\mathbf1_{\{q=0\}}$, which proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rooftop-mass-diamond}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $w=P_\theta(u,v)\ne-\infty$. Then
\[
 M(u)+M(v)\leq M(\max\{u,v\})+M(w).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $M(u)=0$, the inequality follows from $M(v)\leq M(\max\{u,v\})$, and similarly if $M(v)=0$. Otherwise normalize $u,v\leq0$ and put $\phi=P_\theta[u]$ and $\psi=P_\theta[v]$; these are model potentials with $M(\phi)=M(u)$ and $M(\psi)=M(v)$. We first replace $u$ and $v$ by $\phi$ and $\psi$. The maxima of $P_\theta(u+C,V_\theta)$ and $P_\theta(v+C,V_\theta)$ have the singularity type of $\max\{u,v\}$, and they increase almost everywhere to $\max\{\phi,\psi\}$ as $C\to\infty$; by \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}, $M(\max\{\phi,\psi\})=M(\max\{u,v\})$. Moreover $P_\theta(\phi,\psi)\geq w$ is a potential. If its mass is zero, then $M(w)=0$ by monotonicity; if its mass is positive, then $M(w)=M(P_\theta(\phi,\psi))$ by \cref{prop:rooftop-full-mass-substitution}. In both cases all four masses in the inequality are unchanged when $u,v$ are replaced by $\phi,\psi$, so it suffices to prove the theorem for the model potentials $\phi,\psi$.

Put $\gamma=\max\{\phi,\psi\}$ and $m=M(\gamma)$, and for $C>0$ let
\[
 \phi_C=\max\{\phi,\gamma-C\},\quad
 \psi_C=\max\{\psi,\gamma-C\},\quad
 r_C=P_\theta(\phi_C,\psi_C).
\]
These potentials have the singularity type of $\gamma$, hence mass $m$, and $r_C$ decreases to $P_\theta(\phi,\psi)$ as $C\to\infty$. By \cref{prop:p-envelope-contact-concentration}, the measure $\mu_\phi$ is carried by $\{\phi=0\}$, which is contained in the plurifine open set $\{\phi>\gamma-C\}$, where $\phi_C=\phi$. Plurifine locality therefore gives
\[
 \mu_{\phi_C}=\mu_\phi+
                  \mathbf1_{\{\phi\leq\gamma-C\}}\mu_{\phi_C},
\]
and the last summand has mass $m-M(\phi)$; the analogous decomposition holds for $\psi_C$. Consider the bound of \cref{cor:rooftop-contact-inequality} for $\mu_{r_C}$ on the set $\{r_C<0\}$. The measure $\mu_\phi$ contributes nothing there, because at a point of $\{\phi=0\}\cap\{r_C=\phi_C\}$ we have $r_C=\phi_C=0$; the same holds for $\mu_\psi$. Consequently
\[
 \mu_{r_C}(\{r_C<0\})\leq2m-M(\phi)-M(\psi).
\]
Since $r_C\leq0$ and $M(r_C)=m$, it follows that
\[
 \mu_{r_C}(\{r_C=0\})\geq M(\phi)+M(\psi)-m.
\]
Applying \cref{lem:decreasing-zero-contact-mass} along the integers $C\to\infty$, we find that $M(P_\theta(\phi,\psi))\geq\mu_{P_\theta(\phi,\psi)}(\{P_\theta(\phi,\psi)=0\})\geq M(\phi)+M(\psi)-m$, which is the assertion for $\phi,\psi$.
\end{proof}

\section{Divisorial constraints under envelopes}
\label{sec:divisorial-envelope-constraints}
Multiplier ideals give another way of comparing singularities. By the valuative criterion of \cref{thm:general-valuative-multiplier-criterion}, inclusions between multiplier ideals are governed by generic Lelong numbers along divisors over $X$. We identify the resulting order and show that it is preserved by upper envelopes, which requires pulling envelopes back to smooth models. In this section $\{\theta\}$ may be any pseudo-effective class. A prime divisor over $X$ is a prime divisor $E$ on a smooth model of $X$, as in \cref{def:divisorial-order-discrepancy}, and $\nu(u,E)$ denotes its generic Lelong number (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}). Since multiplier ideals are local, the valuative criterion uses prime divisors over small neighbourhoods of a point; accordingly, we also allow proper smooth models of coordinate neighbourhoods in $X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:multiplier-singularity-order}
For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$ on $X$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\mathcal I(tv)\subseteq\mathcal I(tu)$ for every real $t>0$;
 \item $\mathcal I(tv)\subseteq\mathcal I(tu)$ for every positive integer $t$;
 \item $\nu(v,E)\geq\nu(u,E)$ for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$.
\end{enumerate}
We write $v\preceq_{\mathcal I}u$ when these conditions hold, and $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u$ when $v\preceq_{\mathcal I}u$ and $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}v$. In particular, equality of the multiplier ideals of all positive integer multiples is equivalent to equality for all positive real multiples, and to equality of all divisorial Lelong numbers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Clearly (1) implies (2). Assume (2) and let $E$ be a prime divisor over $X$. Nested ideals have reversed orders, so $\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(tv)\geq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(tu)$ for every positive integer $t$. Dividing by $t$ and letting $t\to\infty$, \cref{cor:divisorial-lelong-ideal-recovery} gives $\nu(v,E)\geq\nu(u,E)$; this is (3). Conversely, assume (3), let $t>0$, and let $f$ be a germ in $\mathcal I(tv)$. The valuative criterion, \cref{thm:general-valuative-multiplier-criterion}, gives $\varepsilon>0$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_E f+A_X(E)
       \geq(1+\varepsilon)t\nu(v,E)
       \geq(1+\varepsilon)t\nu(u,E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ over a small neighbourhood of the point whose center contains it. By the same criterion, $f\in\mathcal I(tu)$; this is (1). Applying the equivalence in both directions proves the assertions about equalities.
\end{proof}
\index{singularity order!multiplier ideals}\index{multiplier ideal!equivalence of potentials}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:divisorial-envelope-closure}
Let $(v_j)_{j\in J}$ be a nonempty family in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ that is uniformly bounded above, and let $v=(\sup_jv_j)^*$. Then
\[
 \nu(v,E)=\inf_j\nu(v_j,E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ over $X$. Consequently, maxima, sums and positive multiples preserve the relation $\sim_{\mathcal I}$; and if two such families are termwise equivalent for $\sim_{\mathcal I}$, their upper envelopes are equivalent as well.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a smooth model on which $E$ is a divisor. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, there is a countable subfamily with the same regularized supremum; its increasing finite maxima $w_k$ converge to $v$ almost everywhere and in $L^1$. If $Y$ is compact, the pullbacks $w_k\circ\pi$ converge to $v\circ\pi$ in $L^1$ by \cref{prop:smooth-target-psh-modification}. If $Y$ is a proper smooth model of a coordinate neighbourhood, the same conclusion holds locally. Indeed, on a relatively compact chart of $Y$, the pulled-back local plurisubharmonic functions are uniformly bounded above. Every such chart meets the dense open set over which $\pi$ is an isomorphism, and there the change of variables gives $L^1$ convergence to $v\circ\pi$. By the compactness theorem for plurisubharmonic functions, the sequence cannot converge to $-\infty$ locally uniformly on the chart, and every cluster value in $L^1$ agrees with $v\circ\pi$ on the isomorphism locus, hence almost everywhere and therefore everywhere. Thus in both cases $v\circ\pi$ is the regularized supremum of the increasing sequence $w_k\circ\pi$. Since every $v_j$ is at most $v$, every $v_j\circ\pi$ is at most $v\circ\pi$, so $v\circ\pi$ is also the regularized supremum of the whole family $(v_j\circ\pi)$. By \cref{prop:lelong-calculus}, $\nu(v\circ\pi,y)=\inf_j\nu(v_j\circ\pi,y)$ at every point $y\in E$. Taking the infimum over $y\in E$, which defines the generic Lelong number (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}) and commutes with the infimum over $j$, we obtain the formula.

For the remaining assertions, recall from \cref{prop:lelong-calculus} that Lelong numbers at a point are additive, positively homogeneous, and that the Lelong number of a maximum is the minimum of the Lelong numbers. The infimum defining a generic Lelong number is attained at very general points of $E$ (\cref{prop:generic-divisorial-lelong}), so these rules hold for generic Lelong numbers as well. Together with the formula just proved and \cref{prop:multiplier-singularity-order}, they give the stated invariance.
\end{proof}

\section{Singularity constraints and relative capacity}
\label{sec:singularity-obstacles-relative-capacity}
We now combine a singularity constraint with an obstacle. For a bounded obstacle the resulting envelope stays within a bounded distance of the singularity envelope $P_\theta[u]$, and it satisfies a contact bound. We then introduce the capacity relative to a reference potential $\phi$, defined through the potentials in the strip $\phi-1\leq v\leq\phi$, and use it to measure the deep sublevel sets of potentials of full mass relative to $\phi$. Throughout this section $\{\theta\}$ is big.

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-constrained-obstacle}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and a Borel obstacle $f$, define
\[
 P_\theta[u](f)=\left(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):
             v\preceq u,\ v\leq f\text{ quasi-everywhere}\}\right)^*
\]
when this family is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, and put $P_\theta[u](f)=-\infty$ when it is empty.
For Borel obstacles $g$ and $f$ we write $P_\theta(g,f)=P_\theta(\min\{g,f\})$, under the same conditions on the family of competitors.
\end{definition}
\index{envelope!singularity-constrained obstacle}
By the rooftop representation in \cref{prop:singularity-obstacle-calculus} below, this is consistent with \cref{def:relative-singularity-envelope} when $f$ is a potential $\phi$ with $u\preceq\phi$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singularity-obstacle-calculus}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $f$ be a Borel obstacle for which the family in \cref{def:singularity-constrained-obstacle} is nonempty and uniformly bounded above.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $P_\theta[u](f)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. The envelope increases with $f$ and with the singularity type of $u$, does not change when $f$ is modified on a pluripolar set, and satisfies $P_\theta[u](f+c)=P_\theta[u](f)+c$ for $c\in\mathbb R$.
 \item For all sufficiently large $k$, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u+k,f)$ are potentials, and
 \[
  P_\theta[u](f)=\left(\lim_{k\to\infty}P_\theta(u+k,f)\right)^*.
 \]
 \item If $|f|\leq C$ quasi-everywhere, then
 \[
  P_\theta[u]-C\leq P_\theta[u](f)\leq P_\theta[u]+C,
                     \qquad M(P_\theta[u](f))=M(u).
 \]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $P_\theta[u](0)=P_\theta[u]$ and $P_\theta[V_\theta](f)=P_\theta(f)$ whenever these envelopes are defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) As in the proof of \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, a countable subfamily and its finite maxima give an increasing sequence of competitors, each more singular than $u$ and below $f$ quasi-everywhere, with the same regularized supremum. That supremum differs from its regularization only on a pluripolar set, so $P_\theta[u](f)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. (The envelope itself need not be more singular than $u$; see \cref{ex:loglog-full-mass-envelope}.) The other assertions follow from the corresponding properties of the families of competitors.

(2) Every competitor $v$ satisfies $v\leq u+k$ for some integer $k$, hence $v\leq\min\{u+k,f\}$ quasi-everywhere and $v\leq P_\theta(u+k,f)$. Conversely, each potential $P_\theta(u+k,f)$ lies below $u+k$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, and below $f$ quasi-everywhere; so it is a competitor, and in particular the competitors for $\min\{u+k,f\}$ are uniformly bounded above. A fixed competitor shows that these rooftop envelopes are potentials for all large $k$ and bounds their increasing limit from below, while the upper bound of the family bounds it from above. This proves the representation.

(3) Normalize $u\leq0$. If $|f|\leq C$ quasi-everywhere, then $u-C$ is a competitor, and for large $k$ each rooftop envelope $P_\theta(u+k,f)$ lies between $u-C$ and $u+k$, so it has the singularity type of $u$ and mass $M(u)$. By (2) and \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}, $M(P_\theta[u](f))=M(u)$. Comparing $f$ with the constants $-C$ and $C$, and using (1) together with $P_\theta[u](0)=P_\theta[u]$, gives the two bounds.

For the final identities, note that a competitor for $P_\theta[u](0)$ is a nonpositive potential more singular than $u$, as in \cref{def:singularity-p-envelope}, and that every potential is more singular than $V_\theta$, by minimality, so that the families defining $P_\theta[V_\theta](f)$ and $P_\theta(f)$ coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singularity-obstacle-contact}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $f$ be a bounded quasi-continuous obstacle. Then $w=P_\theta[u](f)$ is a potential, and
\[
 \mu_w\leq\mathbf1_{\{w=P_\theta(f)\}}\mu_{P_\theta(f)},
                \qquad \mu_w(X\setminus\{w=f\})=0.
\]
In particular $\int_X(f-w)\,\mu_w=0$, where the integrand may be given any value at the poles of $w$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $p=P_\theta(f)$ and choose $C$ with $|f|\leq C$. Then $V_\theta-C\leq p\leq V_\theta+C$, so $p$ has minimal singularities and positive mass. A potential lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere if and only if it lies below $p$: one direction is the maximality of $p$, the other holds because $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Hence the two constrained families coincide, and $w=P_\theta[u](p)$. Since $u\preceq p$, this is the relative envelope of \cref{def:relative-singularity-envelope}, by \cref{prop:singularity-obstacle-calculus}. The measure bound in \cref{prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization}, whose proof does not use $M(u)>0$, gives the first inequality. By \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_p$ is carried by $\{p=f\}$, so $\mu_w$ is carried by $\{w=p\}\cap\{p=f\}\subset\{w=f\}$. Finally, $f-w$ is nonnegative quasi-everywhere and vanishes $\mu_w$-almost everywhere, which gives the integral identity.
\end{proof}
\index{envelope!orthogonality to the obstacle}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-mixed-comparison}
Let $u,v,\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $p$ be an integer with $1\leq p\leq n$, and let $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-p}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. For $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ put
\[
 \lambda_q=\left\langle(\theta+\ddc q)^p
                           \wedge T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{n-p}\right\rangle.
\]
If $u,v\preceq\phi$, then
\[
 \lambda_v(\{u<v\})\leq\lambda_u(\{u<v\})
                                +\lambda_\phi(X)-\lambda_u(X).
\]
If moreover $M(\phi)>0$ and $u,v\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, then $\lambda_u(X)=\lambda_\phi(X)$, and the inequality also holds with $\{u\leq v\}$ in place of $\{u<v\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\varepsilon>0$ put $q=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}$. Since $q\preceq\phi$, the monotonicity of mixed masses in \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-bounded-type-mixed-mass}, applied with the potentials of the fixed currents $T_i$ (\cref{thm:global-ddc-lemma}), gives $\lambda_q(X)\leq\lambda_\phi(X)$. On the disjoint plurifine open sets $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ and $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$, $q$ equals $v-\varepsilon$ and $u$ respectively, so plurifine locality gives
\[
 \lambda_v(\{u<v-\varepsilon\})
            \leq\lambda_\phi(X)-\lambda_u(\{u>v-\varepsilon\}).
\]
Writing $\lambda_u(\{u>v-\varepsilon\})=\lambda_u(X)-\lambda_u(\{u\leq v-\varepsilon\})$, enlarging $\{u\leq v-\varepsilon\}$ to $\{u<v\}$ up to the common pole set of $u$ and $v$, which carries no mass, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, we obtain the first inequality.

Now let $M(\phi)>0$ and $u,v\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$. By \cref{prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization}, $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[v]=P_\theta[\phi]$. Writing $T_i=\theta_i'+\ddc\varphi_i$ with smooth forms $\theta_i'$ and $\varphi_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i')$, the preservation of mixed masses in \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}, applied to the $n$ potentials $u,\ldots,u,\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_{n-p}$ and to the corresponding tuples for $v$ and $\phi$, gives $\lambda_u(X)=\lambda_v(X)=\lambda_\phi(X)$. Thus the last two terms in the inequality cancel. Applying the inequality with $u$ and $v$ interchanged, we get $\lambda_u(\{v<u\})\leq\lambda_v(\{v<u\})$, and subtracting this from $\lambda_v(X)=\lambda_u(X)$ gives $\lambda_v(\{u\leq v\})\leq\lambda_u(\{u\leq v\})$.
\end{proof}
\index{comparison principle!relative mixed products}

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-monge-ampere-capacity}
For a reference potential $\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and a Borel set $A\subset X$, the \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere capacity relative to $\phi$} is
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(A)
       =\sup\{\mu_v(A):v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ \phi-1\leq v\leq\phi\}.
\]
The potentials $v$ in this supremum are called competitors for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$. For $\phi=V_\theta$ we obtain the \emph{$\theta$-capacity}.
\end{definition}
\index{capacity!relative Monge--Ampere}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-capacity-basic}
The relative capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$ is monotone, countably subadditive, zero on pluripolar Borel sets and continuous along increasing sequences of Borel sets. Its total value is $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(X)=M(\phi)$, and it is inner regular on Borel sets:
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(A)
       =\sup_{K\subset A\text{ compact}}\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(K).
\]
It does not change when a constant is added to $\phi$. If $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfies $|\phi-\psi|\leq C$ outside the pole sets of $\phi$ and $\psi$, for a constant $C\geq0$, then
\[
 (2C+1)^{-n}\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(A)
  \leq\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(A)
  \leq(2C+1)^n\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(A).
\]
Replacing $(\theta,\phi)$ by $(\theta+\ddc g,\phi-g)$ for a smooth real function $g$ leaves the capacity unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every competitor has the singularity type of $\phi$ and hence mass $M(\phi)$; since $\phi$ itself is a competitor, the capacity is finite and $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(X)=M(\phi)$. Monotonicity, countable subadditivity and continuity along increasing sequences hold for each measure $\mu_v$ and pass to the supremum; for the last property, interchange the supremum over the sequence with the supremum over competitors. Inner regularity follows in the same way from the inner regularity of each finite measure $\mu_v$. Each $\mu_v$ gives no mass to pluripolar sets. Adding a constant $c$ to $\phi$ corresponds to the bijection $v\mapsto v+c$ of competitors, which does not change $\mu_v$.

For the comparison, note first that $\phi\leq\psi+C$ and $\psi\leq\phi+C$ hold almost everywhere, hence everywhere; in particular $\phi$ and $\psi$ have the same pole set. Put $t=(2C+1)^{-1}$. If $\phi-1\leq v\leq\phi$, the potential $q=(1-t)\psi+tv-tC$ satisfies
\[
 q\leq(1-t)\psi+t(\psi+C)-tC=\psi,\qquad
 q\geq(1-t)\psi+t(\psi-C-1)-tC=\psi-1,
\]
so it is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}$, and $\mu_q\geq t^n\mu_v$. This proves the upper comparison, and exchanging $\phi$ and $\psi$ proves the lower one. The last assertion follows from the bijection $v\mapsto v-g$ between the two families of competitors, which preserves the current $\theta+\ddc v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-capacity-sublevel-estimate}
Let $u,\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\leq\phi$, and let $s>0$ and $0<t\leq1$. Then
\[
 t^n\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(\{u<\phi-s-t\})
       \leq\mu_u(\{u<\phi-s\})+M(\phi)-M(u).
\]
In particular, if $M(\phi)>0$ and $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, then
$\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(\{u<\phi-s\})\to0$ as $s\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$, and put $q=(1-t)\phi+tv-s$. Then $\phi-s-t\leq q\leq\phi-s$, so
\[
 \{u<\phi-s-t\}\subset\{u<q\}\subset\{u<\phi-s\}.
\]
Moreover $q\sim\phi$ and $\mu_q\geq t^n\mu_v$. \Cref{prop:relative-mixed-comparison}, with $p=n$, applied to $u$ and $q$, gives
\[
 t^n\mu_v(\{u<\phi-s-t\})\leq\mu_q(\{u<q\})
           \leq\mu_u(\{u<\phi-s\})+M(\phi)-M(u),
\]
and taking the supremum over $v$ proves the inequality. If $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, then $M(u)=M(\phi)$. Taking $t=1$ and letting $s\to\infty$, the right side tends to zero, because $u$ and $\phi$ are finite outside a $\mu_u$-null set.
\end{proof}

\section{Quantitative rooftop extrapolation}
\label{sec:quantitative-rooftop-extrapolation}
In \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation} the potentials are ordered by $u\preceq v$. We now show that it suffices to order their singularity envelopes, $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[v]$. The obstacle $au+(1-a)v$ may then be unbounded above near the poles of $v$, so we must show separately that its competitors are uniformly bounded above. This follows from the uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials, applied on a set of positive volume on which the obstacle is bounded. As before, $\{\theta\}$ is big.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:model-order-rooftop-extrapolation}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $M(u),M(v)>0$ and $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[v]$, and let $a>1$ satisfy
\[
 a^n\bigl(M(v)-M(u)\bigr)<M(v).
\]
Consider the obstacle $au+(1-a)v$, evaluated where $u$ and $v$ are both finite and given arbitrary Borel values on the remaining pluripolar set. Then $\eta=P_\theta(au+(1-a)v)$ is a potential of positive mass, and
\[
 a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})v\leq u,
                   \qquad P_\theta[\eta]\leq P_\theta[u].
\]
If $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[v]$, then every $a>1$ is allowed, and $P_\theta[\eta]=P_\theta[u]$ and $M(\eta)=M(u)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\psi=P_\theta[v]$. Then $u\preceq P_\theta[u]\leq\psi$, and $M(\psi)=M(v)$ by \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}. Hence \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}, applied to $u$ and $\psi$, gives a potential $\eta'=P_\theta(au+(1-a)\psi)$ of positive mass. Choose $C$ with $v\leq\psi+C$. Since $1-a<0$, we have
$\eta'-(a-1)C\leq au+(1-a)v$ quasi-everywhere, so $\eta'-(a-1)C$ is a competitor for the obstacle $au+(1-a)v$.

We show that the competitors are uniformly bounded above. Since $u$ and $v$ are finite almost everywhere, there is a Borel set $K$ of positive $\omega^n$-volume on which both are bounded; the obstacle is then bounded above on $K$ by a constant $D$. Let $h$ be a competitor. By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}, there is a constant $B$, depending only on $X$, $\theta$ and $\omega$, such that
\[
 \int_X|h-\sup_Xh|\,\omega^n\leq B.
\]
Since $h\leq D$ almost everywhere on $K$, integration over $K$ gives $\sup_Xh\leq D+B/\int_K\omega^n$. Hence the envelope $\eta$ is a potential. It dominates $\eta'-(a-1)C$, so it has positive mass. The inequality $\eta\leq au+(1-a)v$, which holds quasi-everywhere, is equivalent there to $a^{-1}\eta+(1-a^{-1})v\leq u$; this inequality between quasi-plurisubharmonic functions therefore holds everywhere.

Put $t=a^{-1}$. For all sufficiently large integers $k$, the potential
$tP_\theta(\eta+k,0)+(1-t)P_\theta(v+k,0)$ is nonpositive and more singular than $t\eta+(1-t)v\leq u$; here $P_\theta(\cdot,0)$ is as in \cref{def:singularity-constrained-obstacle}. It is therefore a competitor for $P_\theta[u]$. By \cref{prop:singularity-obstacle-calculus}, $P_\theta(\eta+k,0)$ and $P_\theta(v+k,0)$ increase almost everywhere to $P_\theta[\eta]$ and $\psi$ as $k\to\infty$. Hence
\[
 a^{-1}P_\theta[\eta]+(1-a^{-1})\psi\leq P_\theta[u]
\]
almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere. Since $P_\theta[u]\leq\psi$, subtracting $(1-a^{-1})\psi$ where $P_\theta[u]$ is finite gives $P_\theta[\eta]\leq P_\theta[u]$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere.

Suppose finally that $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[v]$. Then $M(u)=M(v)$, so every $a>1$ is allowed, and \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation} gives $M(\eta')=M(u)$. Monotonicity of mass yields $M(\eta)\geq M(\eta')=M(u)$, while $P_\theta[\eta]\leq P_\theta[u]$ and the preservation of mass by singularity envelopes give $M(\eta)\leq M(u)$. Thus $P_\theta[\eta]$ and $P_\theta[u]$ are model potentials with the same positive mass and $P_\theta[\eta]\leq P_\theta[u]$. The characterization in \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}, applied to $P_\theta[\eta]$, shows that $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[\eta]$, so $P_\theta[\eta]=P_\theta[u]$.
\end{proof}
\index{rooftop!quantitative extrapolation}

\section{Comparison of relative capacities}
\label{sec:global-relative-capacity-comparison}
In this section we compare the relative capacities $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$ with each other and with the capacity of a K\"ahler form. All backgrounds are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms. For a K\"ahler form $\kappa$ we have $V_\kappa=0$; we write $\Omega=\int_X\kappa^n$ and $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa=\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,0}$. In each statement, $\mu_q$ and $M(q)$ refer to the background indicated there; when two backgrounds occur, a subscript such as $M_\kappa$ shows which one is meant. The main tool is the envelope $S$ of \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope}, an increasing limit of envelopes of smooth penalties that vanish exactly on a compact set $K$. Its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is carried by $K$, and its height $\sup_XS$ controls the relative capacity of $K$ from both sides.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-smooth-kahler-approximation}
Let $\kappa$ be a K\"ahler form. Every bounded $p\in\PSH(X,\kappa)$ is the pointwise limit of smooth functions $p_j\in\PSH(X,\kappa)$ with a common bound $\sup_j\sup_X|p_j|<\infty$. The approximations need not decrease.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation} with $\kappa$ as the K\"ahler form of reference. It gives potentials $\Phi_j$ decreasing to $p$ with $\kappa+\ddc\Phi_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\kappa$, where $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$, and with gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^jp))$. Since $p$ is bounded, $\mathcal I(2^jp)$ is the unit ideal, so $\Phi_j$ is bounded and the smooth function $\exp(2^j\Phi_j)$ has no zeros; thus $\Phi_j$ is smooth. Put $p_j=\Phi_j/(1+\varepsilon_j)$. Then
\[
 \kappa+\ddc p_j=\frac{(1+\varepsilon_j)\kappa+\ddc\Phi_j}{1+\varepsilon_j}\geq0,
\]
and the bounds $p\leq\Phi_j\leq\Phi_1$ give a common bound for the $p_j$ and their pointwise convergence to $p$. Subtracting $\sup_Xp_j$, we also obtain approximations with supremum zero of a potential $p$ with $\sup_Xp=0$, because the suprema converge by \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:global-singular-weighted-cln}
Let $\kappa$ be a K\"ahler form, let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\kappa)$ satisfy $v\leq u\leq v+B$ for a constant $B\geq0$, and let $p\leq0$ be a bounded function in $\PSH(X,\kappa)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-singular-weighted-cln}
 \int_Xp\,\mu_u\geq\int_Xp\,\mu_v-nB\Omega.
\end{equation}
Moreover, there is a constant $C_\kappa$, depending only on $X$ and $\kappa$, such that for every model potential $\phi$ for the background $\kappa$, every $v\in\PSH(X,\kappa)$ with $\phi-1\leq v\leq\phi$, and every $p\in\PSH(X,\kappa)$ with $\sup_Xp=0$, bounded or not,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-strip-uniform-weighted-cln}
 \int_X(-p)\,\mu_v\leq C_\kappa.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $u\sim v$, we have $M(u)=M(v)$, so both sides of \eqref{eq:global-singular-weighted-cln} change by the same amount when a constant is added to $p$. Write $\kappa_q=\kappa+\ddc q$.

\emph{A special case.} Assume first that there are $l_0>0$ and an open set $U\supset\{v<-l_0\}$ on which $u=v$. For $l>l_0$ put $u_l=\max\{u,-l\}$ and $v_l=\max\{v,-l\}$; these are bounded functions in $\PSH(X,\kappa)$ with $0\leq u_l-v_l\leq B$. On $U$ we have $u_l=v_l$ and $u=v$, so $\mu_{u_l}=\mu_{v_l}$ and $\mu_u=\mu_v$ there. The complement of $U$ lies in the plurifine open set $\{v>-l\}$, on which $u_l=u$ and $v_l=v$, because $u\geq v$; by plurifine locality, $\mu_{u_l}=\mu_u$ and $\mu_{v_l}=\mu_v$ on this set. Hence $\mu_{u_l}-\mu_{v_l}=\mu_u-\mu_v$. Expanding $\kappa_{u_l}^n-\kappa_{v_l}^n$ and integrating by parts with \cref{lem:bounded-global-integration-by-parts}, we obtain
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_Xp\,(\mu_u-\mu_v)=\int_Xp(\mu_{u_l}-\mu_{v_l})
  &=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_X(u_l-v_l)\,\ddc p
                  \wedge\kappa_{u_l}^{j}\wedge\kappa_{v_l}^{n-1-j}\\
  &\geq-nB\Omega.
 \end{split}
\]
For the last inequality write $\ddc p=(\kappa+\ddc p)-\kappa$: the first part contributes a nonnegative amount, since $u_l-v_l\geq0$, and the second contributes at least $-B\int_X\kappa\wedge\kappa_{u_l}^j\wedge\kappa_{v_l}^{n-1-j}=-B\Omega$, by \cref{prop:bounded-global-cohomological-mass}.

\emph{The general case.} Subtracting a common constant, we may assume $v\leq0$. For $0<a<1$ put $v_a=av$ and $u_a=\max\{u,av\}$; these belong to $\PSH(X,\kappa)$ because $\kappa+\ddc(av)=(1-a)\kappa+a\kappa_v$. Since $v\leq av$, we have $v_a\leq u_a\leq v_a+B$. On the open set $\{v<-B/(1-a)\}$ we have $av-v>B\geq u-v$, so $u_a=v_a$ there; thus the special case applies to $u_a$ and $v_a$. As $a\uparrow1$, $v_a$ and $u_a$ decrease to $v$ and $u$. The masses of $u_a$ and $v_a$ agree, and by multilinearity $M(v_a)$ is a polynomial in $a$ that tends to $M(v)=M(u)$. Decreasing sequences converge in extended capacity (\cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations}), so \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} shows that $\mu_{u_a}\to\mu_u$ and $\mu_{v_a}\to\mu_v$ weakly along every sequence $a\uparrow1$. For smooth $p$ we can therefore pass to the limit in \eqref{eq:global-singular-weighted-cln} for $u_a$ and $v_a$. For a bounded $p$, take the approximations $p_j$ of \cref{lem:bounded-smooth-kahler-approximation}; they converge pointwise with a common bound, and dominated convergence with respect to the finite measures $\mu_u$ and $\mu_v$ proves \eqref{eq:global-singular-weighted-cln}.

\emph{The second assertion.} Let $\phi$ be a model potential for $\kappa$. Since $V_\kappa=0$, \cref{prop:p-envelope-contact-concentration} gives $\mu_\phi\leq\kappa^n$. If $\phi-1\leq v\leq\phi$, the first assertion, applied to $\phi\leq v+1\leq\phi+1$, gives for every bounded $p$ with $\sup_Xp=0$
\[
 \int_X(-p)\,\mu_v\leq\int_X(-p)\,\mu_\phi+n\Omega
                       \leq\int_X(-p)\,\kappa^n+n\Omega.
\]
By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}, the last integral is bounded by a constant depending only on $X$ and $\kappa$. For an unbounded $p$ with $\sup_Xp=0$, apply this bound to the truncations $\max\{p,-l\}$, which are bounded, belong to $\PSH(X,\kappa)$ and have supremum zero, and let $l\to\infty$ by monotone convergence.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate!global singular weighted form}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:penalized-compact-envelope}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, let $\phi$ be a model potential of mass $m>0$, and let $K\subset X$ be a compact non-pluripolar set. There is $S\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $L=\sup_XS\geq0$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \phi\leq S\leq\phi+L,\qquad S=\phi\text{ quasi-everywhere on }K,\\
 M(S)=m,\qquad\mu_S(X\setminus K)=0.
 \end{gathered}
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-capacity-lower-bound}
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(K)
                      \geq\frac{m}{\max\{1,L\}^{n}}.
\end{equation}
If $\theta=\kappa$ is a K\"ahler form, then also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-capacity-upper-bound}
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(K)
                      \leq\frac{C}{\max\{1,L\}},
\end{equation}
where $C$ depends only on $X$ and $\kappa$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth function $g\geq0$ on $X$ whose zero set is exactly $K$. To construct it, cover $X\setminus K$ by countably many coordinate balls whose closures lie in $X\setminus K$, and choose nonnegative smooth functions supported in these balls and positive on smaller concentric balls that still cover $X\setminus K$. Multiply the $j$th function by a positive constant so small that its derivatives of order at most $j$ are bounded by $2^{-j}$ in a fixed finite atlas. The sum converges in every $C^k$ norm, is positive on $X\setminus K$ and vanishes on $K$.

Put $q_j=P_\theta(\phi+jg)$. Since $\phi$ is a competitor and every competitor is at most $\phi+j\sup_Xg$, we have $\phi\leq q_j\leq\phi+j\sup_Xg$, so $q_j\sim\phi$. The obstacle $\phi+jg$ is upper semicontinuous, so $q_j\leq\phi+jg$ everywhere by \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, and therefore $q_j=\phi$ on $K$. In particular $\sup_Kq_j\leq0$, and \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-normalization} bounds $\sup_Xq_j$ uniformly in $j$. The envelopes $q_j$ increase with $j$, since $g\geq0$, so $S=(\sup_jq_j)^*$ is a potential; put $L=\sup_XS$. Then $L\geq0$, because $S\geq\phi$ and $\sup_X\phi=0$. Since $q_j\preceq\phi$ and $\sup_Xq_j\leq L$, \cref{lem:model-normalized-supremum} gives $q_j\leq\phi+L$, hence $\phi\leq S\leq\phi+L$. The regularization changes $\sup_jq_j$ only on a pluripolar set, so $S=\phi$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Since $S\sim\phi$, $M(S)=m$.

By \cref{thm:quasicontinuous-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_{q_j}$ is carried by the contact set $\{q_j=\phi+jg\}$. At a point of this set where $\phi$ is finite, the bound $q_j\leq\phi+L$ gives $jg\leq L$. Thus $\mu_{q_j}$ is carried by $\{g\leq L/j\}$. By \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-increasing-continuity}, $\mu_{q_j}\to\mu_S$ weakly. Every compact subset of $X\setminus K$ has a neighbourhood on which $g$ has a positive lower bound, and continuous functions supported there have zero integral against $\mu_{q_j}$ for all large $j$. Hence $\mu_S$ is carried by $K$.

Set $t=1/\max\{1,L\}$ and $h=(1-t)\phi+tS-tL$. Then $\phi-1\leq\phi-tL\leq h\leq\phi$, so $h$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$, and $\mu_h\geq t^n\mu_S$. Hence $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(K)\geq t^n\mu_S(K)=t^nm$, which is \eqref{eq:penalized-capacity-lower-bound}.

In the K\"ahler case apply \eqref{eq:model-strip-uniform-weighted-cln} to the potential $p=S-L$, which has supremum zero. Quasi-everywhere on $K$ we have $-p=L-\phi\geq L$, so $L\mu_v(K)\leq C_\kappa$ for every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}$. If $L\geq1$, this gives \eqref{eq:penalized-capacity-upper-bound} with $C=C_\kappa$. If $L<1$, use $\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(K)\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(X)=m\leq\Omega$. Thus $C=\max\{C_\kappa,\Omega\}$ works.
\end{proof}

The capacity of a compact set controls the height $L$ of its penalized envelope, and the uniform exponential integrability of normalized potentials converts this height into a volume estimate. The resulting constants do not depend on the model potential.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, and let $\phi$ be a model potential of mass $m>0$. There are $\alpha,C>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta$ and $\omega$, such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}
 \int_E\omega^n\leq C\exp\left[-\alpha
       \left(\frac{m}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right],
\end{equation}
where the right side is interpreted as zero when the capacity is zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha$ and $C_0$ be the constants of \cref{cor:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound}. First let $K$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $S,L$ be as in \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope}. The potential $p=S-L$ has supremum zero and satisfies $p\leq-L$ quasi-everywhere on $K$, because $S=\phi\leq0$ there. Since pluripolar sets have zero volume, \eqref{eq:normalized-global-psh-exponential-bound} gives
\[
 \int_K\omega^n\leq\int_K\mathrm e^{-\alpha p-\alpha L}\,\omega^n\leq C_0\mathrm e^{-\alpha L}.
\]
Write $c=\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(K)$, which is positive by \eqref{eq:penalized-capacity-lower-bound}. By the same inequality,
\[
 \left(\frac mc\right)^{1/n}\leq\max\{1,L\}\leq1+L,
\]
so \eqref{eq:relative-volume-capacity-exponential} holds for $K$ with $C=C_0\mathrm e^\alpha$. If $K$ is pluripolar, its volume and its capacity both vanish.

Now let $E$ be a Borel set. If $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)>0$, the estimate for compact subsets $K\subset E$, together with the monotonicity of capacity and the inner regularity of the smooth volume, gives \eqref{eq:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}. If $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)=0$, every compact subset of $E$ has zero capacity, hence is pluripolar by \eqref{eq:penalized-capacity-lower-bound} and has zero volume; by inner regularity, $E$ has zero volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:relative-lp-capacity-domination}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}, let $p>1$, $0\leq f\in L^p(X,\omega^n)$ and $r>0$. There is $B_r>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta$, $\omega$, $p$ and $r$, such that every Borel set $E$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-lp-capacity-domination}
 \int_E f\omega^n\leq B_r\|f\|_{L^p}
           \left(\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)}m\right)^r.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $r>1$ the measure $f\omega^n$ is dominated by a power of $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$ with exponent greater than one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)=0$, then $E$ has zero volume by \cref{prop:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}, and both sides vanish. Otherwise let $q=p/(p-1)$. By H\"older's inequality and \eqref{eq:relative-volume-capacity-exponential}, the integral is at most
\[
 \|f\|_{L^p}C^{1/q}\exp\left[-\frac\alpha q
       \left(\frac{m}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right].
\]
Put $t=(m/\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E))^{1/n}$; then $t\geq1$, since the capacity is at most $M(\phi)=m$. The function $t^{nr}\mathrm e^{-\alpha t/q}$ is bounded on $[1,\infty)$: its logarithmic derivative is $nr/t-\alpha/q$, so its maximum is attained at $1$ or at $nrq/\alpha$. Hence $\mathrm e^{-\alpha t/q}\leq A\,t^{-nr}$ for a constant $A$ depending only on $n,r,q,\alpha$, and this proves \eqref{eq:relative-lp-capacity-domination}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kahler-model-capacity-powers}
Let $\kappa$ be a K\"ahler form and $\phi$ a model potential for $\kappa$ of mass $m>0$. There is $A\geq1$ such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kahler-model-capacity-powers}
 A^{-1}\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(E)^n
       \leq\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(E)
       \leq A\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(E)^{1/n}.
\end{equation}
More generally, if $\theta\leq\kappa$, $\{\theta\}$ is big and $\phi$ is a model potential for $\theta$ of mass $m>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-model-capacity-lower-power}
 \operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(E)^n
                        \leq\frac{C^n}{m}\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E),
\end{equation}
where $C$ depends only on $X$ and $\kappa$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All capacities involved are inner regular, so it suffices to treat compact sets $K$. If $K$ is pluripolar, all capacities of $K$ vanish. Otherwise, carry out the construction in the proof of \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope} with one and the same function $g$ for $(\theta,\phi)$ and for $(\kappa,0)$, and denote the resulting envelopes by $S_\phi$ and $S_0$ and their suprema by $L_\phi$ and $L_0$. Every competitor for $P_\theta(\phi+jg)$ is $\kappa$-plurisubharmonic and at most $jg$, so $P_\theta(\phi+jg)\leq P_\kappa(jg)$; hence $S_\phi\leq S_0$ and $L_\phi\leq L_0$. The two estimates of \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope} give
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(K)^n
    \leq\frac{C^n}{\max\{1,L_0\}^n}
    \leq\frac{C^n}{\max\{1,L_\phi\}^n}
    \leq\frac{C^n}{m}\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(K).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:big-model-capacity-lower-power} and, for $\theta=\kappa$, the first inequality in \eqref{eq:kahler-model-capacity-powers}.

For the upper bound let $\theta=\kappa$. By \cref{thm:positive-mass-extrapolation}, applied to $\phi\preceq0$, there is $b>1$ such that $\eta=P_\kappa(b\phi)$ is a potential; it satisfies $b^{-1}\eta\leq\phi$. Write $c=1-b^{-1}>0$. For fixed $j$ and $\delta>0$ consider
\[
 v_j=c\,P_\kappa(jg)+b^{-1}\eta-\delta,
\]
a convex combination of $\kappa$-plurisubharmonic functions minus a constant. The function $P_\kappa(jg)$ is upper semicontinuous and vanishes on $K$, because $0\leq P_\kappa(jg)\leq jg$. Hence $P_\kappa(jg)<\delta/c$ on a neighbourhood $U$ of $K$, and there $v_j<b^{-1}\eta\leq\phi$. Outside the poles of $\phi$, the difference $v_j-\phi$ is bounded above by $cj\sup_Xg$, and $g$ has a positive minimum on $X\setminus U$. Therefore $v_j\leq\phi+lg$ for a sufficiently large integer $l$, so $v_j\leq P_\kappa(\phi+lg)\leq S_\phi$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and then $j\to\infty$, and using that the increasing limit of $P_\kappa(jg)$ equals $S_0$ almost everywhere, we obtain
\[
 cS_0+b^{-1}\eta\leq S_\phi
\]
almost everywhere, hence everywhere.

Choose a Borel set $G$ of positive volume on which $b^{-1}\eta\geq-D$ for a constant $D$; such a set exists because $\eta$ is finite almost everywhere. By \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness}, there is a constant $B$, independent of $K$, with $\int_X|S_0-L_0|\,\kappa^n\leq B$. Hence $S_0\geq L_0-B/\int_G\kappa^n$ at some point of $G$, and the last display gives $L_\phi\geq cL_0-D'$, where $D'\geq0$ does not depend on $K$. If $L_0\geq\max\{1,2D'/c\}$, then $L_\phi\geq cL_0/2$, and \cref{lem:penalized-compact-envelope} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(K)\leq\frac{2C}{cL_0}
       \leq\frac{2C}{c\,\Omega^{1/n}}\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(K)^{1/n};
\]
the last inequality is the lower bound \eqref{eq:penalized-capacity-lower-bound} for the construction with reference potential $0$, which gives $\Omega/L_0^n\leq\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(K)$. In the remaining range $L_0<\max\{1,2D'/c\}$, the same lower bound shows that $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa(K)$ is bounded below by a positive constant independent of $K$, and we use $\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\phi}(K)\leq m$, enlarging $A$ if necessary. The constant $A$ in the upper bound depends on $\phi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:full-mass-capacity-downward-comparison}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, and let $\psi,\phi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $\psi\leq\phi$ and $M(\psi)=M(\phi)>0$. There is a continuous nondecreasing function $F\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ with $F(0)=0$, which may depend on $\psi$ and $\phi$, such that
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(E)
                      \leq F(\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E))
\]
for every Borel set $E\subset X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}$, so that $\psi-1\leq u\leq\psi$, and let $t\geq1$. We estimate $\mu_u(E)$ separately on $\{u>\phi-2t\}$ and on $\{u\leq\phi-2t\}$.

Put $u_t=\max\{u,\phi-2t\}$, so that $\phi-2t\leq u_t\leq\phi$. The potential $(1-(2t)^{-1})\phi+(2t)^{-1}u_t$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$, and its measure is at least $(2t)^{-n}\mu_{u_t}$. Since $u_t=u$ on the plurifine open set $\{u>\phi-2t\}$, it follows that
\[
 \mu_u(E\cap\{u>\phi-2t\})
                       \leq(2t)^n\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E).
\]

The potentials $\psi$, $u$ and $\psi-1$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$. So does the midpoint $q=(u+\phi)/2$: by \cref{prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization}, $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[\phi]$, so by \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus} every mixed term in the expansion of $\mu_q$ has total mass $M(\phi)$, and $M(q)=M(\phi)$. On the set $\{u\leq\phi-2t\}$ we have $q-t\geq u\geq\psi-1$, and on $\{\psi-1\leq q-t\}$ we have $\psi-1\leq(\psi+\phi)/2-t$, that is, $\psi\leq\phi-2t+2\leq\phi-t+1$. Thus
\[
 \{u\leq\phi-2t\}\subset\{\psi-1\leq q-t\}
                         \subset\{\psi\leq\phi-t+1\}.
\]
The non-strict comparison in \cref{prop:relative-mixed-comparison}, applied with $p=n$ to $\psi-1$ and $q-t$, together with $\mu_q\geq2^{-n}\mu_u$, gives
\[
 \mu_u(\{u\leq\phi-2t\})\leq2^n\mu_q(\{\psi-1\leq q-t\})
     \leq2^n\mu_\psi(\{\psi-1\leq q-t\})\leq2^n\mu_\psi(\{\psi\leq\phi-t+1\}).
\]
Adding the two estimates and taking the supremum over $u$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-mass-capacity-tail-bound}
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(E)
 \leq(2t)^n\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)
                 +2^n\mu_\psi(\{\psi\leq\phi-t+1\})
 \qquad(t\geq1).
\end{equation}
The last term tends to zero as $t\to\infty$, since $\psi$ and $\phi$ are finite outside a $\mu_\psi$-null set.

It remains to replace the right side by a continuous function of the capacity. Put $m=M(\psi)$, and let $H(s)$ be the minimum of $m$ and of the infimum over $t\geq1$ of the right side of \eqref{eq:full-mass-capacity-tail-bound} with $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E)$ replaced by $s$. Since $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}\leq M(\psi)=m$, we have $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(E)\leq H(\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(E))$. The function $H$ is nondecreasing, and $H(s)\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$. Choose a strictly decreasing sequence $s_k\downarrow0$ with $H(s_k)\leq m2^{-k}$. Define $F(0)=0$, $F(s_k)=m2^{1-k}$, $F$ linear on each interval $[s_{k+1},s_k]$, and $F=m$ on $[s_1,\infty)$. On $[s_{k+1},s_k]$ we have $H(s)\leq H(s_k)\leq m2^{-k}=F(s_{k+1})\leq F(s)$, and $H\leq m=F$ on $[s_1,\infty)$. Thus $F$ is a continuous nondecreasing majorant of $H$ with $F(0)=0$, as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big and that $\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive mass. There are continuous nondecreasing functions $F,G\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$, vanishing at zero, such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(E)\leq F(\operatorname{Cap}_\omega(E)),
 \qquad \operatorname{Cap}_\omega(E)\leq G(\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}(E)).
\]
Consequently, any two capacities of this kind, for possibly different big backgrounds and potentials of positive mass, are bounded by such functions of each other; in particular, they define the same notions of convergence in capacity and of uniform absolute continuity with respect to capacity on Borel sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $A\geq1$ with $\theta\leq\kappa=A\omega$. Every competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\psi}$, and its measure for $\kappa$ dominates its measure for $\theta$; hence $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\psi}$ and $M_\kappa(\psi)\geq M(\psi)>0$. Adding a constant to $\psi$ does not change the capacities, so we may assume $\psi\leq0$. Let $\Phi=P_\kappa[\psi]$. By \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}, $\Phi$ is a model potential for $\kappa$ with $\psi\leq\Phi$ and $M_\kappa(\Phi)=M_\kappa(\psi)$. Therefore \cref{lem:full-mass-capacity-downward-comparison} and \cref{prop:kahler-model-capacity-powers} bound $\operatorname{Cap}_{\kappa,\psi}$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa$. Finally, rescaling the competitors gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_\omega\leq\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa
                              \leq A^n\operatorname{Cap}_\omega:
\]
every competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$ is one for $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa$, with a larger measure, and if $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa$, then $v/A$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$ with measure $A^{-n}(\kappa+\ddc v)^n$. This proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality use the model potential $\phi=P_\theta[\psi]$ for $\theta$, which has the same positive mass as $\psi$. By \eqref{eq:big-model-capacity-lower-power}, $\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa^n\leq(C^n/M(\psi))\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$. By \cref{thm:model-order-rooftop-extrapolation} with $a=2$, applied to $\psi$ and $\phi$, whose singularity envelopes agree, the potential $\eta=P_\theta(2\psi-\phi)$ has mass $M(\psi)$ and satisfies $P_\theta[\eta]=\phi$ and $(\eta+\phi)/2\leq\psi$. Put $u=(\eta+\phi)/2$. Since $P_\theta[\eta]=P_\theta[\phi]$, the preservation of mixed masses in \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus} gives $M(u)=M(\phi)=M(\psi)$. Hence \cref{lem:full-mass-capacity-downward-comparison}, applied to $u\leq\psi$, bounds $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,u}$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\psi}$. If $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}$, then $h=(v+\eta)/2$ satisfies $u-1/2\leq h\leq u$, so it is a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,u}$, and $\mu_h\geq2^{-n}\mu_v$. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}\leq2^n\operatorname{Cap}_{\theta,u}.
\]
Combining these inequalities with $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega\leq\operatorname{Cap}_\kappa$ gives $G$; compositions, positive multiples and $n$th roots preserve continuity, monotonicity and the value zero at the origin. The last assertion follows by comparing both capacities with $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:global-local-capacity-equivalence}
Choose finitely many pairs of concentric coordinate balls $B_\alpha\Subset B'_\alpha$, with each $\overline{B'_\alpha}$ contained in a coordinate chart, such that the balls $B_\alpha$ cover $X$, and let $\operatorname{Cap}_{B'_\alpha}$ be the capacities of \cref{def:monge-ampere-capacity} in these coordinates. There is a constant $c\geq1$ such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies
\[
 c^{-1}\sum_\alpha\operatorname{Cap}_{B'_\alpha}(E\cap B_\alpha)
  \leq\operatorname{Cap}_\omega(E)
  \leq c\sum_\alpha\operatorname{Cap}_{B'_\alpha}(E\cap B_\alpha).
\]
Consequently, the relative capacities of \cref{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison} vanish precisely on the pluripolar Borel sets, convergence in each of them agrees with the local convergence in capacity, and every potential is quasi-continuous with respect to each of them.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$, so that $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ and $-1\leq v\leq0$. On a neighbourhood of $\overline{B'_\alpha}$ choose a smooth function $\rho_\alpha$ with $\ddc\rho_\alpha=\omega$, and put $c_\alpha=\sup_{B'_\alpha}\rho_\alpha-\inf_{B'_\alpha}\rho_\alpha+1$. The function $w=(v+\rho_\alpha-\inf_{B'_\alpha}\rho_\alpha+1)/c_\alpha$ is plurisubharmonic on $B'_\alpha$ with values in $[0,1]$, and $(\omega+\ddc v)^n=c_\alpha^n(\ddc w)^n$ there. Hence $\mu_v(E\cap B_\alpha)\leq c_\alpha^n\operatorname{Cap}_{B'_\alpha}(E\cap B_\alpha)$, and summing over $\alpha$ gives the upper bound.

For the lower bound fix $\alpha$ and write $B_\alpha=B(0,r)$ and $B'_\alpha=B(0,R)$ in the coordinates, with $r<R$. Choose $L>0$ with $L(R^2-r^2)>1$, and then $\delta>0$ so small that $\omega\geq\delta L\ddc|z|^2$ on $B(0,R)$ and that we can choose a number $a$ with
\[
 \delta(1+Lr^2)<a<\delta LR^2,\qquad a\leq1.
\]
Let $w$ be plurisubharmonic on $B(0,R)$ with $-1\leq w\leq0$, and put $q=\delta w-\delta L|z|^2$. Then $\omega+\ddc q\geq\delta\ddc w\geq0$ on $B(0,R)$. Near the boundary sphere $q\leq-\delta L|z|^2<-a$, so the function $v$ equal to $\max\{q,-a\}$ on $B(0,R)$ and to $-a$ elsewhere belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$. It takes values in $[-1,0]$, and on $B(0,r)$ we have $q\geq-\delta(1+Lr^2)>-a$, so $v=q$ and $\omega+\ddc v\geq\delta\ddc w$ there. Since the capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_{B(0,R)}$ may equally be computed with competitors taking values in $[-1,0]$, it follows that
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_\omega(E)\geq\delta^n
                 \operatorname{Cap}_{B(0,R)}(E)
                   \qquad(E\subset B(0,r)\text{ Borel}).
\]
Summing over the finitely many balls gives the lower bound.

The remaining assertions follow from this comparison, \cref{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison}, and the local results \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar,thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}. In particular, a finite union of local exceptional open sets has arbitrarily small capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$, and the comparison functions of \cref{thm:global-relative-capacity-comparison} transfer this to every relative capacity of a potential of positive mass.
\end{proof}
