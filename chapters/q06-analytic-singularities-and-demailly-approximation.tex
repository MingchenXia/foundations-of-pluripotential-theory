\chapter{Analytic singularities and Demailly approximation}
\label{chap:q06}

Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a domain in $\mathbb C^n$. Convolution with a smoothing kernel approximates $u$ by smooth plurisubharmonic functions, but these approximants have lost all singularities: their Lelong numbers vanish and their multiplier ideals are trivial. Many problems require approximants whose singularities are still visible but as simple as possible, namely functions locally of the form $c\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ with holomorphic $g_i$ and rational $c>0$; such functions are said to have analytic singularities. On a compact K\"ahler manifold a second difficulty appears: convolution is defined only in charts, and gluing local approximations costs positivity. This chapter presents Demailly's answer to both problems, in which convolution is replaced by weighted Bergman kernels.

For $u$ plurisubharmonic on a bounded pseudoconvex domain $\Omega$ and an integer $m\geq1$, the holomorphic functions $F$ on $\Omega$ with $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty$ form a Hilbert space, which generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$ of \cref{chap:q05}. If $K_m(z)$ denotes the supremum of $|F(z)|^2$ over its unit ball, then $u_m=m^{-1}\log K_m$ is plurisubharmonic and has analytic singularities described by $\mathcal I(mu)$. The Ohsawa--Takegoshi estimate of \cref{chap:q04} bounds $u_m$ below by $u-O(1/m)$, and the mean-value inequality bounds it above by the supremum of $u$ over a small ball, up to an error that tends to zero as $m\to\infty$. Hence $u_m\to u$, and the Lelong numbers of $u_m$ differ from those of $u$ by at most $n/m$ (\cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}).

On a compact K\"ahler manifold $(X,\omega)$ we apply this construction in finitely many coordinate balls to a function $\varphi$ with $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0$, where $\theta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form. A $\bar\partial$-estimate with a logarithmic pole shows that the kernels of two overlapping balls are comparable up to a factor that depends only on the charts (\cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}). Gluing the local potentials by a maximum gives the quantitative regularization of \cref{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization}, with a curvature loss of order $1/m$ and a lower bound by the generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Gluing them instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs gives approximants $G_m$ with gentle analytic singularities (\cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}): $\exp(mG_m)$ is smooth, and $G_m$ has a smooth remainder on every log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Comparing the kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ by extension from the diagonal, we show that the dyadic sequence $G_{2^j}$, corrected by small constants, decreases to $\varphi$ and that its multiplier ideals approximate those of $\varphi$, so that it is a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi$ in the sense of \cref{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation} (\cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}). Any two quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi$ dominate each other up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient (\cref{prop:quasi-equisingular-comparison}).

The first section introduces quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and analytic singularities and shows that blowups with smooth centers preserve the K\"ahler property. The last section studies the ideal of holomorphic germs $f$ with $|f|^2\leq C\mathrm e^v$: for $v$ with analytic singularities it is coherent, and on a compact manifold the ideal attached to $kv$ contains $\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon)v)$ for all large $k$.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-analytic-regularization}.

\section{Smooth backgrounds and analytic singularities}
\label{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}

Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq0$. Recall from \cref{sec:current-maps} that a Hermitian form on $X$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is strictly positive at every point, that is, the form associated with a Hermitian metric; it is a K\"ahler form if it is closed, and $X$ is a K\"ahler manifold if it carries a K\"ahler form. A Hermitian form $\omega$ defines a Riemannian metric on $X$ and the positive volume form $\omega^n/n!$.
\index{K\"ahler form}\index{K\"ahler manifold}\index{Hermitian form}

Continuous real $(1,1)$-forms are controlled by a Hermitian form on compact sets: if $\alpha$ is a continuous real $(1,1)$-form on a neighborhood of a compact set $K\subset X$, then $\alpha\geq-C\omega$ on $K$ for some constant $C\geq0$. Indeed, cover $K$ by finitely many compact sets $K_1,\ldots,K_N$, each contained in a chart, and write $\alpha=\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ and $\omega=\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}w_{jk}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ there, with Hermitian matrices $(a_{jk})$ and $(w_{jk})$. On $K_\iota$ the smallest eigenvalue of $(w_{jk})$, a continuous positive function, is at least some $c_\iota>0$, and the largest eigenvalue of $-(a_{jk})$ is at most some $M_\iota\geq0$. Hence $(a_{jk})\geq-M_\iota I\geq-(M_\iota/c_\iota)(w_{jk})$ on $K_\iota$, and by the matrix criterion for positivity (\cref{sec:local-complex-potentials}) $\alpha\geq-C\omega$ on $K$ with $C=\max_\iota M_\iota/c_\iota$.

The following lemma provides smooth local potentials for smooth closed forms on whole coordinate balls, not only on small polydiscs as in \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}
Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form defined on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Then there is a smooth real function $h$ on $B$ with $\ddc h=\theta$. Moreover, every real pluriharmonic function on a coordinate ball is, up to an additive constant, the real part of a holomorphic function on that ball.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In dimension zero there is nothing to prove, so let $n\geq1$. Choose concentric balls $B\Subset B'\Subset B''$ such that $\theta$ is defined on a neighborhood of $\overline{B''}$. The radial homotopy formula in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare} gives a smooth real one-form $\alpha$ on $B''$ with $\mathrm d\alpha=\theta$. Since $\theta$ has type $(1,1)$, the $(0,2)$-part $\bar\partial\alpha^{0,1}$ of $\mathrm d\alpha$ vanishes. The form $\alpha^{0,1}$ is bounded on $B'$, so \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}, applied on the pseudoconvex ball $B'$ with $p=0$ and $q=|z|^2$ (whose complex Hessian is the identity), gives a function $f\in L^2(B')$ with $\bar\partial f=\alpha^{0,1}$.

The function $f$ is smooth. Indeed, let $p\in B'$ and choose polydiscs $\Delta'\Subset\Delta\Subset B'$ centered at $p$. By \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, applied to the smooth $\bar\partial$-closed form $\alpha^{0,1}$ on $\Delta$, there is a smooth function $f_0$ on $\Delta'$ with $\bar\partial f_0=\alpha^{0,1}$. Then $\bar\partial(f-f_0)=0$ on $\Delta'$ in the sense of distributions, so $\Delta(f-f_0)=4\sum_j\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_j}(f-f_0)=0$ there, and by \cref{thm:weyl} the locally integrable function $f-f_0$ agrees almost everywhere on $\Delta'$ with a smooth function. Thus $f$ agrees almost everywhere near each point with a smooth function; two such smooth functions coincide on the overlap of their domains, being continuous and equal almost everywhere, so we may take $f$ smooth on $B'$. Since $\alpha$ is real, $\alpha^{1,0}=\overline{\alpha^{0,1}}=\partial\bar f$, and the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm d\alpha$ gives, exactly as in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare},
\[
 \theta=\bar\partial\partial\bar f+\partial\bar\partial f=\partial\bar\partial(f-\bar f)=\ddc(4\pi\Img f).
\]
Thus $h=4\pi\Img f$ has the required property on $B\subset B'$.

For the last assertion, let $h$ be real and pluriharmonic on a coordinate ball, which we center at $0$. Then $\partial h$ is a $(1,0)$-form with holomorphic coefficients, since $\bar\partial\partial h=0$, and it is closed. The radial homotopy formula in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, applied to the closed form $2\partial h$, gives a function $F$ on the whole ball with $\mathrm dF=2\partial h$. As $\mathrm dF$ has type $(1,0)$, $F$ is holomorphic, and $\mathrm d(\Real F)=\partial h+\bar\partial h=\mathrm dh$. Hence $h-\Real F$ is constant.
\end{proof}
\index{ddc@$\ddc$ operator!potentials on a ball}\index{pluriharmonic function!real part of a holomorphic function}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold. A function $\varphi\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty)$ is \emph{quasi-plurisubharmonic} if every point of $X$ has a neighborhood $V$ on which $\varphi=u+f$ pointwise, with $u\in\PSH(V)$ and $f$ a smooth real function on $V$. For a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$ we put
\[
 \PSH(X,\theta)=\{\varphi\text{ quasi-plurisubharmonic on }X:\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0\},
\]
and call its elements \emph{$\theta$-plurisubharmonic} (\emph{$\theta$-psh}) functions.
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-plurisubharmonic function}\index{theta-plurisubharmonic function}

A quasi-plurisubharmonic function is locally integrable on $X$, hence finite almost everywhere. Let $h$ be a smooth real function on an open set $V$ with $\ddc h=\theta$. Then a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ satisfies $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0$ on $V$ if and only if $\varphi+h$ is plurisubharmonic on $V$. Indeed, locally $\varphi+h$ is a plurisubharmonic function plus a smooth one, so at every point it is the limit of its ball averages, as in \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}; if $\ddc(\varphi+h)\geq0$, it is therefore the plurisubharmonic representative of its class given by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. If $X$ is compact and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form, every quasi-plurisubharmonic function satisfies $\ddc\varphi\geq-C\omega$ for some constant $C$. Indeed, by compactness there are finitely many open sets $V_\kappa\Subset V_\kappa^+$ such that the $V_\kappa$ cover $X$ and $\varphi=u_\kappa+f_\kappa$ on $V_\kappa^+$ with $u_\kappa$ plurisubharmonic and $f_\kappa$ smooth; on $V_\kappa$ we have $\ddc\varphi\geq\ddc f_\kappa\geq-C_\kappa\omega$ by the comparison at the beginning of this section, and we take $C=\max_\kappa C_\kappa$. Conversely, let $\varphi\in L^1_{\loc}(X)$ satisfy $\ddc\varphi\geq-C\omega$. On a coordinate ball whose closure lies in a chart, the same comparison, applied to $-\omega$ and the Hermitian form $\ddc|z|^2$, gives $\omega\leq C'\ddc|z|^2$, so $\ddc(\varphi+A|z|^2)\geq0$ for $A=CC'$. Hence $\varphi+A|z|^2$ has a plurisubharmonic representative by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, and subtracting $A|z|^2$ gives a representative of $\varphi$ on the ball. These representatives agree on overlaps: if $A'|z'|^2$ is the quadratic function used on a second ball, both representatives plus $A'|z'|^2$ are plurisubharmonic functions plus smooth ones, hence both equal the canonical representative of the class of $\varphi+A'|z'|^2$. Thus $\varphi$ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative.

Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic weight are defined as in \cref{def:multiplier-ideal}. Adding a smooth function to the weight multiplies the integrand by a locally bounded positive factor with locally bounded inverse, so the multiplier ideal of $\varphi=u+f$ near a point is that of $u$; in particular it is coherent, and the local results of \cref{chap:q05} apply to it. Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are defined as in \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}, by $\nu(\varphi,x)=\nu(u,x)$. Finally, if $\theta+\ddc\varphi_i\geq0$ for finitely many quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_k$, then $\theta+\ddc\max_i\varphi_i\geq0$, since locally $\max_i\varphi_i+h=\max_i(\varphi_i+h)$ with $\ddc h=\theta$, and \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} applies.

\begin{definition}\label{def:rational-analytic-singularities}
A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$ has \emph{analytic singularities} if every point of $X$ has a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ on which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-analytic-singularities}
 \varphi=c\log\sum_{i=1}^r|g_i|^2+b,
 \qquad c\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\quad b\text{ bounded},
\end{equation}
where $r\geq1$ is an integer and $g_1,\ldots,g_r\in\mathcal O(V)$ are not all identically zero. It has \emph{neat analytic singularities} if such presentations exist with $b$ smooth. Let $c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and let $\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_X$ be a coherent ideal sheaf. We say that $\varphi$ has analytic singularities of \emph{type} $(c,\mathfrak a)$ if presentations \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities} exist with this coefficient $c$, where on each $V$ the functions $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ generate $\mathfrak a$.
\end{definition}
\index{analytic singularities}\index{analytic singularities!neat}\index{analytic singularities!type}

The type condition does not depend on the choice of local generators: if $(g_i)$ and $(g'_k)$ both generate $\mathfrak a$ on $V$, each tuple is a holomorphic matrix times the other, and both matrices are bounded on smaller neighborhoods, so $\log\sum|g_i|^2-\log\sum|g'_k|^2$ is locally bounded. A type is additional data and is not unique; for example, a function of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ also has type $(c/2,\mathfrak a^2)$.

For a holomorphic map $\pi\colon Y\to X$ and an ideal sheaf $\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_X$, we write $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a\cdot\mathcal O_Y$ for the ideal generated by the pullbacks of local generators of $\mathfrak a$. Let $\mathfrak a$ be a coherent ideal on $X$, nonzero on each component. Since $X$ is smooth, the resolution step in the construction of log resolutions at the end of \cref{sec:analytic-blowups-resolution} is not needed, and \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, applied with the empty divisor, produces log resolutions of $\mathfrak a$ by blowups with smooth centers. From now on a \emph{log resolution} of $\mathfrak a$ always means a map of this form: a composite $\pi\colon Y\to X$ of blowups along closed submanifolds of positive codimension, finite over each relatively compact open subset of $X$, such that $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ whose support has simple normal crossings. We also call $\pi$ a \emph{principalization} of $\mathfrak a$, and we write $s_D$ for a local holomorphic equation of $D$.
\index{log resolution!by blowups with smooth centers}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rational-analytic-operations}
Analytic singularities are preserved by multiplication by positive rational numbers, by sums and finite maxima, and by pullback under a holomorphic map $f\colon Y\to X$ of complex manifolds whenever the composition is not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $Y$. If $\varphi$ has type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ and $\pi\colon Y\to X$ is a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ with $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_Y(-D)$, then locally on $Y$
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+O(1),
\]
and the remainder is smooth if the presentations of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ are neat.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ replaces $c$ by $qc$. For a sum or a maximum, intersect the neighborhoods of two presentations, with coefficients $c$ and $d$, and choose $c_0\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ such that $c/c_0$ and $d/c_0$ are positive integers. For an integer $k\geq1$ the multinomial formula gives
\[
 \Bigl(\sum_i|g_i|^2\Bigr)^k=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\Bigl|\sqrt{k!/\alpha!}\,g^\alpha\Bigr|^2,
\]
again a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic functions. We may therefore assume that both presentations have the coefficient $c_0$, say $c_0\log A+b$ and $c_0\log B+b'$ with $A=\sum_i|g_i|^2$ and $B=\sum_k|h_k|^2$. The sum is $c_0\log(AB)+b+b'$, and $AB=\sum_{i,k}|g_ih_k|^2$. For the maximum we use
\[
 \max(\log A,\log B)\leq\log(A+B)\leq\max(\log A,\log B)+\log2,
\]
which shows that $\max(c_0\log A+b,c_0\log B+b')$ differs from $c_0\log(A+B)$ by a bounded function. Pullback composes the tuple and the bounded remainder with $f$. By \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback} the composed function is quasi-plurisubharmonic when it is not identically $-\infty$ on any component, and then the composed tuple is not identically zero on any connected open set.

Now let $\pi$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ as in the statement. If $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ generate $\mathfrak a$, their pullbacks generate $\mathcal O_Y(-D)$, so locally $g_i\circ\pi=s_Dh_i$ with holomorphic $h_i$, and $s_D=\sum_ik_i(g_i\circ\pi)$ for holomorphic $k_i$. Since $s_D$ is not a zero divisor, $\sum_ik_ih_i=1$, so the $h_i$ have no common zero and $\log\sum_i|h_i|^2$ is smooth. Hence
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+c\log\sum_i|h_i|^2+b\circ\pi,
\]
which proves both assertions about the remainder.
\end{proof}
\index{analytic singularities!operations and pullback}

The proof uses the rationality of the coefficients for sums and maxima: two positive rational numbers are integer multiples of a common one.

If $\varphi$ has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, we also call a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ a \emph{log resolution of $\varphi$}; since types are not unique, this refers to the chosen type. On such a resolution $\varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+O(1)$ locally, by \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations}. A merely bounded remainder need not become smooth on a log resolution, which is why neat singularities, and the gentle singularities defined below on compact K\"ahler manifolds, are separate notions. When $X$ is compact, a log resolution consists of finitely many blowups with smooth centers, and if $X$ is moreover K\"ahler, so is the resolved manifold (\cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}). The proof of this fact uses the curvature of a metric on a line bundle.
\index{log resolution!of a function with analytic singularities}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quadratic-line-bundle-metric}
A holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $X$ is given by an open cover $(V_i)$ of $X$ and local frames $e_i$ over $V_i$ with transition functions $e_i=g_{ij}e_j$ on $V_i\cap V_j$, where $g_{ij}$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero and $g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}$ on triple overlaps. A smooth \emph{quadratic Hermitian metric} on $L$ assigns to each frame a smooth positive squared norm $|e_i|^2$ on $V_i$, such that $|e_i|^2=|g_{ij}|^2|e_j|^2$ on $V_i\cap V_j$. Its \emph{curvature} is the closed real $(1,1)$-form equal to $-\ddc\log|e_i|^2$ on $V_i$.
\end{definition}
\index{line bundle!holomorphic}\index{Hermitian metric!quadratic}\index{curvature!of a line bundle}

The curvature is well defined, since $\log|e_i|^2-\log|e_j|^2=\log|g_{ij}|^2$ is pluriharmonic. Smooth metrics exist: if $(\rho_k)$ is a partition of unity subordinate to $(V_k)$, write a vector $\xi$ in the fiber at $x$ as $\xi=\sigma_ke_k(x)$ for each $k$ with $x\in V_k$, and put $|\xi|^2=\sum_k\rho_k(x)|\sigma_k|^2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold and let $W\subset X$ be a closed submanifold of positive codimension. Then the blowup of $X$ along $W$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold. Consequently every log resolution of a coherent ideal on $X$, as above, is a compact K\"ahler manifold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\omega$ be a K\"ahler form on $X$, let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be the blowup along $W$ and $E=\pi^{-1}(W)$ its exceptional divisor; $Y$ is compact since $\pi$ is proper. Near components of $W$ of codimension one, $\pi$ is the identity by \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}, so the vertical spaces considered below are zero there. The second assertion follows from the first by induction on the finite number of blowups.

\emph{Step 1: a metric on $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$ that is positive along the fibers of $E$.} Let $N=TX|_W/TW$ be the normal bundle. The charts $(z,t,v)$ of \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup} identify $E$ with the projectivized normal bundle $\mathbb P(N)$, whose fibers are the projective spaces $\mathbb P(N_x)$, $x\in W$: in the chart with pivot $w_i$, built from local coordinates $(z,w)$ with $W=\{w=0\}$, the point $(z,0,v)$ corresponds to the line in $N_{(z,0)}$ spanned by the class of $\partial/\partial w_i+\sum_{j\neq i}v_j\,\partial/\partial w_j$. In these charts the local equations $t=w_i$ of $E$ change by the ratios of the fiber coordinates. Hence the restriction of the line bundle $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$ to $E$ is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(N)}(1)$, the dual of the tautological line bundle whose fiber at a line $\ell\subset N_x$ is $\ell$. A Hermitian metric on $N$ restricts to a metric on the tautological bundle and induces the dual metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(N)}(1)$. In an affine chart of a fiber, with linear coordinates orthonormal for the metric of $N_x$, the curvature of the dual metric is $\ddc\log(1+|w|^2)$, which is positive definite. Fix any smooth metric $h_0$ on $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$. The ratio of the metric just constructed to $h_0|_E$ is a smooth positive function on $E$; extend its logarithm to a smooth function on $Y$, locally in coordinates in which $E$ is a coordinate hyperplane and then by a partition of unity, and multiply $h_0$ by the exponential of the extension. Let $\eta$ be the curvature of the resulting metric. It is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$ whose restriction to each fiber of $E\to W$ is positive definite.

\emph{Step 2: positivity near $E$.} The form $P=\pi^*\omega$ is semipositive, and at a point of $E$ its kernel is the kernel of $\mathrm d\pi$, which is the tangent space of the fiber of $E\to W$. Choose finitely many compact sets $K_\alpha$ whose interiors cover $E$, each contained in one of the charts $(z,t,v)$ of \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}, in which $E=\{t=0\}$ and the fibers of $E\to W$ are the sets $\{z=\text{const},\ t=0\}$. In such a chart let $V$ be spanned by the vectors $\partial/\partial v_k$ and $H$ by the vectors $\partial/\partial z_l$ and $\partial/\partial t$, and write the real $(1,1)$-forms $P$ and $Q=\eta$, regarded as Hermitian quadratic forms on the tangent spaces, in block form with respect to the decomposition of the tangent space into $V$ and $H$; norms of tangent vectors are taken in the coordinates of the chart. At points of $E$ the vectors in $V$ are tangent to the fibers and $\mathrm d\pi|_H$ is injective, so $P_{HH}$ is positive definite and $P_{HV}=0$, while $Q_{VV}$ is positive definite by Step 1. By compactness and continuity there are constants $a,c,M>0$ and a neighborhood $\mathcal N\subset\bigcup_\alpha K_\alpha$ of $E$ such that on $\mathcal N\cap K_\alpha$, for every $\alpha$,
\[
 P_{HH}\geq c,\qquad Q_{VV}\geq a,\qquad |Q|\leq M,
\]
and the linear map $L=P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV}\colon V\to H$ is so small that $Q(\xi-L\xi,\xi-L\xi)\geq\frac a2|\xi|^2$ and $|\xi-L\xi|\leq2|\xi|$ for $\xi\in V$. Write a tangent vector at a point of $\mathcal N\cap K_\alpha$ as $\tau=\xi+\zeta$ with $\xi\in V$ and $\zeta\in H$, and put $\sigma=\zeta+L\xi\in H$. Completing the square,
\[
 P(\tau,\tau)=P_{HH}(\sigma,\sigma)+S(\xi,\xi),\qquad S=P_{VV}-P_{VH}P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV},
\]
and $S\geq0$ because $P\geq0$ (take $\sigma=0$). Hence $P(\tau,\tau)\geq c|\sigma|^2$. Since $\tau=(\xi-L\xi)+\sigma$,
\[
 Q(\tau,\tau)\geq\frac a2|\xi|^2-4M|\xi||\sigma|-M|\sigma|^2.
\]
Therefore, for $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 (P+\varepsilon Q)(\tau,\tau)\geq(c-\varepsilon M)|\sigma|^2+\frac{\varepsilon a}2|\xi|^2-4\varepsilon M|\xi||\sigma|,
\]
and the right-hand side is positive for $\tau\neq0$ as soon as $\varepsilon M<c$ and $(c-\varepsilon M)a>8\varepsilon M^2$. Thus $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is positive definite on the fixed neighborhood $\mathcal N$ of $E$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.

\emph{Step 3: positivity away from $E$.} On the compact set $Y\setminus\mathcal N$ the map $\pi$ is a local biholomorphism, so $\pi^*\omega$ is uniformly positive there, while $\eta$ is bounded. Decreasing $\varepsilon$ further, $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is positive definite on all of $Y$. It is closed, since both terms are, so it is a K\"ahler form on $Y$.
\end{proof}
\index{blowup!K\"ahler form}

\begin{definition}\label{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold. A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$ has \emph{gentle analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$} if it has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, the function $\exp(\varphi/c)$ is smooth on $X$, including at its zeros, and on every log resolution $\pi$ of $\mathfrak a$, with $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O(-D)$, one has locally
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+h,\qquad h\text{ smooth}.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{analytic singularities!gentle}

The log resolutions in this definition are compact K\"ahler manifolds by \cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}. The smoothness of $\exp(\varphi/c)$ on $X$ and that of the remainder on the log resolutions are separate requirements. Requiring the local form on every log resolution is a priori stronger than requiring it on one; we need the stronger form because later proofs use it on a resolution chosen in the course of the argument. Both requirements hold if $\varphi$ has presentations \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities} of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ with smooth remainders $b$, since then $\exp(\varphi/c)=\mathrm e^{b/c}\sum_i|g_i|^2$ locally and \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations} applies; they also hold for the cutoff sums of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}.

\section{Local Bergman kernels and ideal generators}
\label{sec:local-bergman-approximation}

In this section $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, is a bounded pseudoconvex open set, $u\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and integrals are taken with respect to Lebesgue measure unless another volume form is specified. For an integer $m\geq1$ we put
\[
 H_m=\Bigl\{F\in\mathcal O(\Omega):\|F\|_m^2
                 =\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty\Bigr\},\qquad
 K_m(z)=\sup_{\|F\|_m\leq1}|F(z)|^2.
\]
Thus $H_m$ is the space $\mathcal H_{mu}(\Omega)$ of \cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence}, now on a bounded pseudoconvex set, and the function $K_m$ is the \emph{Bergman kernel} of $H_m$ on the diagonal.

We first transfer the results of \cref{chap:q05}, proved there on polydiscs on which the weight is bounded above, to this setting. Since $u$ is locally bounded above, the mean-value inequality for holomorphic functions shows that convergence in $H_m$ implies locally uniform convergence; hence $H_m$ is a closed subspace of the weighted $L^2$ space, and it is separable by the argument in \cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence}. The cutoff-and-jet argument in the proof of that theorem also applies on $\Omega$: pseudoconvexity provides the weighted solution of the $\bar\partial$-equation, boundedness of $\Omega$ allows us to remove the auxiliary quadratic and logarithmic factors of the weight, and an upper bound for $u$ is needed only near the point where the jet is prescribed. Hence $H_m$ generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$, and near each point finitely many members of an orthonormal basis already generate it. The proof of \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation} uses only this generation property, locally uniform convergence and, for \eqref{eq:weight-versus-multiplier-generators}, \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, which applies on each connected component of $\Omega$; so that lemma also holds for $H_m$.

Let $(e_\ell)$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_m$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}
 K_m=\sum_\ell|e_\ell|^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, evaluation at $z$ is a bounded linear functional, so every $F=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_\ell$ in the unit ball satisfies $F(z)=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_\ell(z)$, and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|F(z)|^2\leq\sum_\ell|e_\ell(z)|^2$. Conversely, for finitely many indices the function $F_N=\sum_{\ell\leq N}\overline{e_\ell(z)}e_\ell$ satisfies $|F_N(z)|^2=\|F_N\|_m^2\sum_{\ell\leq N}|e_\ell(z)|^2$, so $\sum_{\ell\leq N}|e_\ell(z)|^2\leq K_m(z)$ for every $N$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}
The function $K_m$ is real analytic on $\Omega$. Near each point of $\Omega$ it is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $\sum_i|g_i|^2$, where $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ are finitely many generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$ near that point. If $\pi$ is a log resolution of $\mathcal I(mu)$ with $\pi^*\mathcal I(mu)=\mathcal O(-D)$, then locally
\[
 K_m\circ\pi=|s_D|^2b_m,\qquad b_m\text{ smooth and strictly positive}.
\]
The same conclusions hold if Lebesgue measure is replaced, in the definition of $H_m$, by a smooth positive volume form whose density with respect to Lebesgue measure is bounded above and below by positive constants on $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Evaluation is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $\Omega$: the mean-value inequality and the local upper bound for $u$ give $|F(z)|\leq C\|F\|_m$ for $z$ in the compact set. We show that the map $z\mapsto(e_\ell(z))_\ell$ into $\ell^2$ is given locally by a convergent power series with coefficients in $\ell^2$. Fix a point $z_0$ and a polydisc $P$ of multiradius $\rho$ about $z_0$ with $\overline P\subset\Omega$, and let $C$ be the evaluation bound on $\overline P$. For a finitely supported sequence $(c_\ell)$ with $\sum_\ell|c_\ell|^2\leq1$, the function $F=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_\ell$ has norm at most one, so $|F|\leq C$ on $P$, and the Cauchy estimates give $|\partial^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha!|\leq C\rho^{-\alpha}$. Taking the supremum over such sequences shows that the $\ell^2$ norm of the sequence of Taylor coefficients $(\partial^\alpha e_\ell(z_0)/\alpha!)_\ell$ is at most $C\rho^{-\alpha}$. Hence the $\ell^2$-valued Taylor series converges absolutely, together with all its derivatives, on a smaller polydisc, and it represents $(e_\ell(z))_\ell$ there. Its squared norm, which is $K_m$ by \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}, is therefore a convergent power series in $z-z_0$ and $\overline{z-z_0}$, so $K_m$ is real analytic.

Next choose members $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ of $H_m$ generating $\mathcal I(mu)$ near a given point; by the remark following \cref{def:rational-analytic-singularities}, any other finite system of generators gives a comparable sum of squares. By the definition of $K_m$, $|g_i|^2\leq\|g_i\|_m^2K_m$, so $\sum_i|g_i|^2\leq CK_m$. Conversely, \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation} gives, on a fixed smaller neighborhood, a representation $F=\sum_ig_ih_i$ with $|h_i|\leq C'$ for every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_m$. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|F|^2\leq sC'^2\sum_i|g_i|^2$, and hence $K_m\leq sC'^2\sum_i|g_i|^2$ there. These constants may depend on $m$ and $u$.

Let $\pi$ be a log resolution as in the statement. For $F\in H_m$, the germs of $F$ lie in $\mathcal I(mu)$, so $F\circ\pi$ is divisible by $s_D$. Over the neighborhood where the bounded representation holds, $(F\circ\pi)/s_D=\sum_i(h_i\circ\pi)(g_i\circ\pi)/s_D$, and the finitely many quotients $(g_i\circ\pi)/s_D$ are holomorphic and bounded on compact subsets. Hence evaluation of $(F\circ\pi)/s_D$ at a point is a bounded linear functional of $F$, uniformly on compact subsets of the resolved space. The power-series argument of the first paragraph therefore applies to the holomorphic functions $(e_\ell\circ\pi)/s_D$ and shows that $b_m=\sum_\ell|(e_\ell\circ\pi)/s_D|^2$ is smooth. It is strictly positive, because the quotients $(g_i\circ\pi)/s_D$ generate the unit ideal, as in the proof of \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations}, and $b_m\geq|(g_i\circ\pi)/s_D|^2/\|g_i\|_m^2$ for each $i$.

Finally, a volume form comparable with Lebesgue measure gives an equivalent norm on the same space of holomorphic functions. This changes only the constants in the evaluation and coefficient bounds; the proofs of coherence and bounded generation apply without change.
\end{proof}
\index{Bergman kernel!local weighted}\index{analytic singularities!resolved Bergman kernel}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-bergman-approximation}
Let $\Omega$ have diameter at most $L$, and put $u_m=m^{-1}\log K_m$. Then $u_m$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ and has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$. There are constants $C_1,C_2>0$, depending only on $n$ and $L$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-bergman-bounds}
 u(z)-\frac{\log C_1}{m}\leq u_m(z)
 \leq\sup_{B(z,r)}u+\frac1m\log\frac{C_2}{r^{2n}}
\end{equation}
for all $z\in\Omega$ and $r>0$ with $B(z,r)\Subset\Omega$. As $m\to\infty$, $u_m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1_{\loc}(\Omega)$. For every $z\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-bergman-lelong}
 \nu(u,z)-\frac nm\leq\nu(u_m,z)\leq\nu(u,z).
\end{equation}
Finally, for each fixed $m$, the function $\exp(m(u_m-u))$ is locally integrable on $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Omega_0$ be a connected component of $\Omega$; it is a bounded pseudoconvex domain of diameter at most $L$. Choose $a\in\Omega_0$ with $u(a)>-\infty$. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied on $\Omega_0$, there is a holomorphic function on $\Omega_0$ with value $1$ at $a$ and finite weighted norm; extended by zero to the other components of $\Omega$, it is an element of $H_m$ that does not vanish identically on $\Omega_0$. For every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_m$ that is not identically zero on $\Omega_0$, $\log|F|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega_0$ by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}, and $\log K_m$ is the supremum of these functions on $\Omega_0$. Since $K_m$ is continuous, this family is locally uniformly bounded above, and $\log K_m$ is upper semicontinuous with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$, so it equals its upper semicontinuous regularization. Hence \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} shows that $u_m$ is plurisubharmonic. The comparison in \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution} gives $u_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ locally, with generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$; this is the asserted type.

For the lower bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}, let $u(z)>-\infty$; at points of the pole set of $u$ there is nothing to prove. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied on the component of $\Omega$ containing $z$, and extension by zero, there is $F\in H_m$ with $F(z)=1$ and $\|F\|_m^2\leq C_1\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}$, where $C_1$ depends only on $n$ and $L$. Hence $K_m(z)\geq|F(z)|^2/\|F\|_m^2\geq C_1^{-1}\mathrm e^{mu(z)}$. For the upper bound, let $\|F\|_m\leq1$. The mean-value inequality for $|F|^2$ on $B(z,r)$ gives
\begin{align*}
 |F(z)|^2&\leq C_2r^{-2n}\int_{B(z,r)}|F|^2\\
       &\leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp\Bigl(m\sup_{B(z,r)}u\Bigr)\int_{B(z,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}
       \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp\Bigl(m\sup_{B(z,r)}u\Bigr),
\end{align*}
with $C_2=n!/\pi^n$, and taking the supremum over $F$ proves the upper bound.

Now fix $z$ and $r$, and let $m\to\infty$ in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}: we obtain $u(z)\leq\liminf_mu_m(z)\leq\limsup_mu_m(z)\leq\sup_{B(z,r)}u$. Letting $r\downarrow0$ and using the upper semicontinuity of $u$ gives $u_m(z)\to u(z)$, also at points of the pole set. On a compact set $K\subset\Omega$, a fixed $r<\operatorname{dist}(K,\partial\Omega)$ in the upper bound gives a uniform upper bound for all $u_m$, while $u_m\geq u-|\log C_1|$. Since $u\in L^1(K)$, dominated convergence gives $u_m\to u$ in $L^1(K)$.

For the Lelong numbers, let $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ generate $\mathcal I(mu)$ near $z$. We may discard the $g_i$ that vanish identically near $z$. Since $\log\sum_i|g_i|^2-\max_i\log|g_i|^2$ is bounded, \cref{prop:lelong-logarithmic-growth,prop:lelong-calculus} give $\nu(\log\sum_i|g_i|^2,z)=\nu(\max_i\log|g_i|^2,z)=\min_i\operatorname{ord}_zg_i=\operatorname{ord}_z\mathcal I(mu)$. Since Lelong numbers are invariant under bounded perturbations and scale linearly, $\nu(u_m,z)=m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_z\mathcal I(mu)$, and \eqref{eq:local-bergman-lelong} follows from \cref{lem:multiplier-order-lelong-bounds}. (The upper bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-lelong} also follows directly from $u_m\geq u-|\log C_1|$.)

Finally, near each point $K_m\leq C\sum_i|g_i|^2$ with $g_i\in H_m$, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, and each $|g_i|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is integrable on $\Omega$. Hence $\exp(m(u_m-u))=K_m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!local Bergman kernel approximation}

The kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ are compared by extending a function from the diagonal of $\Omega\times\Omega$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-diagonal-comparison}
Suppose that the spaces $H_m$ are defined with respect to a smooth positive volume form whose density with respect to Lebesgue measure is bounded above and below by positive constants on $\Omega$. There is a constant $C_\Omega>0$, depending only on $n$, on a bound for the diameter of $\Omega$ and on these density bounds, and in particular independent of $m$ and $u$, such that
\[
 K_{2m}\leq C_\Omega K_m^2\qquad\text{on }\Omega.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The product $\Omega\times\Omega$ is a bounded pseudoconvex open set in $\mathbb C^{2n}$: if $\rho$ is a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion of $\Omega$, then $\max(\rho(z),\rho(w))$ is one for $\Omega\times\Omega$. On it the weight $mu(z)+mu(w)$ is plurisubharmonic. Let $F\in H_{2m}$, and regard $F$ as a holomorphic function on the diagonal $\{z=w\}$, identified with $\Omega$; on the diagonal the weight $mu(z)+mu(w)$ restricts to $2mu(z)$, and $\int|F(z)|^2\mathrm e^{-2mu(z)}<\infty$. The induced Euclidean volume on the diagonal is $2^n$ times Lebesgue measure on $\Omega$, and the volume form differs from Lebesgue measure by the fixed density bounds. Therefore the extension statement of \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied to the affine subspace $\{z=w\}$ of codimension $n$ on each connected component $\Omega_0\times\Omega_0$ of $\Omega\times\Omega$ that meets this subspace, gives, with $G=0$ on the other components, a function $G\in\mathcal O(\Omega\times\Omega)$ with $G(z,z)=F(z)$ and
\[
 \int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)-mu(w)}\leq C_\Omega\|F\|_{2m}^2,
\]
the integral being taken with respect to the product volume form, where $C_\Omega$ depends only on $n$, the diameter bound and the density bounds.

By the Fubini--Parseval argument in the proof of \cref{thm:multiplier-restriction-subadditivity}, the products $e_j(z)e_k(w)$ form an orthonormal basis of the weighted holomorphic space on $\Omega\times\Omega$. Writing $G=\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}e_j(z)e_k(w)$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis} give
\begin{align*}
 |F(z)|^2=|G(z,z)|^2&\leq\sum_{j,k}|c_{jk}|^2\sum_{j,k}|e_j(z)|^2|e_k(z)|^2\\
 &\leq C_\Omega\|F\|_{2m}^2\Bigl(\sum_j|e_j(z)|^2\Bigr)^2=C_\Omega\|F\|_{2m}^2K_m(z)^2.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over the unit ball of $H_{2m}$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\index{Bergman kernel!diagonal comparison}

\section{Overlap interpolation and quantitative patching}
\label{sec:quantitative-regularization}

From now on $(X,\omega)$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n$. If $n=0$, then $X$ is a finite set, every function on $X$ is smooth and every multiplier ideal is the unit ideal; the constant sequence $\Phi_j=\varphi$ then has all the approximation properties proved below. We therefore assume $n\geq1$.

We fix, once and for all, finitely many concentric coordinate balls
\[
 U_j\Subset U'_j\Subset B_j\Subset B_j^+,
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,J,\qquad X=\bigcup_jU_j,
\]
and smooth functions $\chi_j\colon X\to[0,1]$ with compact support in $U'_j$ and $\chi_j=1$ on $U_j$. We require in addition that whenever $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, the union $B_j^+\cup B_l^+$ is contained in a coordinate ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ whose closure lies in a coordinate chart. Such a choice is possible. Cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate balls whose closures lie in coordinate charts, and let $\delta>0$ be a Lebesgue number of this cover for the distance $d_\omega$ of the Riemannian metric defined by $\omega$. About every point of $X$ choose concentric coordinate balls $U\Subset U'\Subset B\Subset B^+$, with $\overline{B^+}$ contained in a coordinate chart and of $d_\omega$-diameter less than $\delta/2$. By compactness finitely many of the balls $U$ cover $X$; these give the $U_j$. If $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, then $B_j^+\cup B_l^+$ has diameter less than $\delta$ and therefore lies in one ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ of the cover. Finally choose a constant $C_\chi\geq0$ with $\ddc\chi_j\geq-C_\chi\omega$ for all $j$.

All the quantities attached to these choices are now fixed: the bounds for the densities of $\omega^n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure in the charts, the coordinate changes, the diameters of the balls, the distances from $\overline{U'_j}$ to $\partial B_j$, and the derivatives of the cutoffs. We say that a constant \emph{depends only on the fixed geometry} if it depends only on these data, hence only on $(X,\omega)$ once the choices have been made.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:maximum-cutoff-patching}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $a\geq0$, and let $v_j\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$ for $j=1,\ldots,J$ satisfy $v_j\leq v_l+a$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ for all $j,l$. Then the function
\[
 v(x)=\max_{j:\,x\in\overline{U'_j}}\bigl(v_j(x)+a\chi_j(x)\bigr)
\]
is quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$ and satisfies $\theta+\ddc v\geq-aC_\chi\omega$ and $v_j\leq v\leq v_j+2a$ on $U_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since the $U_i$ cover $X$, the maximum is taken over a nonempty set at every point. Fix $x_0\in X$ and choose $i$ with $x_0\in U_i$. Indices $j$ with $x_0\notin\overline{U'_j}$ do not occur near $x_0$. If $x_0\in\partial U'_j$, then $\chi_j=0$ near $x_0$, because $\chi_j$ has compact support in $U'_j$; so near $x_0$ the $j$th term, where it is defined, equals $v_j\leq v_i+a=v_i+a\chi_i$, since $\chi_i=1$ on the neighborhood $U_i$ of $x_0$. Such a term never exceeds the $i$th term and can be omitted. Hence on a small neighborhood of $x_0$, $v$ is the maximum of $v_j+a\chi_j$ over the fixed set of indices $j$ with $x_0\in U'_j$. Each of these functions satisfies $\theta+aC_\chi\omega+\ddc(v_j+a\chi_j)\geq0$. Adding a local potential of the closed form $\theta+aC_\chi\omega$ and applying \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, we obtain that $v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic near $x_0$ with $\theta+\ddc v\geq-aC_\chi\omega$.

On $U_j$ the $j$th term is $v_j+a\geq v_j$, and every term with index $l$ is at most $v_l+a\leq v_j+2a$. This proves the two bounds, also at points of the pole sets.
\end{proof}
\index{patching!maximum with cutoffs}

Now fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$. By \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball} we can choose smooth real functions $h_j$ on $B_j^+$ with $\ddc h_j=\theta$. Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and let $m\geq1$ be an integer. Then $\varphi+h_j$ is plurisubharmonic on $B_j$, and we let $K_{j,m}$ be the Bergman kernel of \cref{sec:local-bergman-approximation} on the ball $B_j$ for the weight $m(\varphi+h_j)$ and the volume form $\omega^n/n!$:
\[
 K_{j,m}(x)=\sup\Bigl\{|F(x)|^2:F\in\mathcal O(B_j),\ \int_{B_j}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi+h_j)}\frac{\omega^n}{n!}\leq1\Bigr\}.
\]
We put
\[
 a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mh_j}K_{j,m},\qquad
 v_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log a_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}-h_j.
\]
The proof of plurisubharmonicity in \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation} does not depend on the volume form, so $m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}$ is plurisubharmonic and $v_{j,m}\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}
There is a constant $C_B\geq0$, depending only on the fixed geometry and in particular independent of $\theta$, $\varphi$ and $m$, such that
\[
 a_{j,m}\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{l,m}\qquad\text{on }\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}
\]
for all $j,l$. In particular $a_{j,m}$ and $a_{l,m}$ have the same zeros on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, and $|v_{j,m}-v_{l,m}|\leq C_B/m$ there outside these common zeros.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $j,l$ with $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, and work in the coordinates of the ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$. By \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball} choose a smooth function $g$ on $\mathcal B_{jl}$ with $\ddc g=\theta$. The functions $h_j-g$ and $h_l-g$ are pluriharmonic on the coordinate balls $B_j^+$ and $B_l^+$, so by the same lemma $h_j-g=\Real b_j$ and $h_l-g=\Real b_l$ with $b_j,b_l$ holomorphic on these balls (the constants are absorbed into $b_j,b_l$). Put $\Psi=\varphi+g$, a plurisubharmonic function on $\mathcal B_{jl}$. Since $|\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}|^2=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}$, multiplication by $\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}$ is an isometry from the weighted space defining $K_{j,m}$ onto the space of holomorphic functions on $B_j$ with weight $m\Psi$ and volume form $\omega^n/n!$. If $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}$ denotes the Bergman kernel of the latter space, then $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}K_{j,m}$, and therefore
\[
 a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{m\Psi}_{B_j},\qquad a_{l,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}.
\]
Hence it suffices to prove $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ with $C_0\geq1$ depending only on the fixed geometry.

Choose a smooth cutoff $\rho$ with compact support in $B_j\cap B_l$, equal to one on a neighborhood of $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$; this choice belongs to the fixed geometry. Let $r>0$ be the distance from this compact intersection to the support of $\bar\partial\rho$. Let $f$ be holomorphic on $B_j$ with norm at most one in the weighted space, let $x\in\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, and extend $\rho f$ by zero to $B_l$. The ball $B_l$ is pseudoconvex also in the coordinates of $\mathcal B_{jl}$, since a biholomorphism transports a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion. Apply \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} on $B_l$ to the $\bar\partial$-closed form $f\bar\partial\rho$, with the plurisubharmonic part $p=m\Psi+n\log|z-x|^2$ and smooth part $q=|z|^2$, whose complex Hessian is the identity. On the support of $\bar\partial\rho$ we have $|z-x|^{-2n}\leq r^{-2n}$, so the bound for $\bar\partial\rho$, the separation $r$ and the comparison between $\omega^n/n!$ and Lebesgue measure give a solution of $\bar\partial w=f\bar\partial\rho$ with
\[
 \int_{B_l}|w|^2|z-x|^{-2n}\mathrm e^{-m\Psi-|z|^2}\leq C,
\]
where $C$ depends only on the fixed geometry. Since $|z-x|^{2n}\mathrm e^{|z|^2}$ is bounded on the bounded set $B_l$, the weighted norm of $w$ for the weight $m\Psi$ is at most a constant $C'$ of the same kind. Near $x$ the function $w$ is holomorphic, because $\rho=1$ there, and $\Psi$ is bounded above; as in the proof of \cref{prop:logarithmic-point-interpolation}, a nonzero value $w(x)$ would make the last integral diverge, so $w(x)=0$. Hence $F=\rho f-w$ is holomorphic on $B_l$, $F(x)=f(x)$, and its weighted norm is at most a constant $C_0\geq1$ depending only on the fixed geometry. By the definition of the kernel, $|f(x)|^2=|F(x)|^2\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}(x)$, and taking the supremum over $f$ gives $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}(x)\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}(x)$.

No bound on $\Psi$, $\theta$ or $m$ enters these constants. Taking $C_B$ to be the maximum of $2\log C_0$ over the finitely many pairs $(j,l)$, with the roles of $j$ and $l$ also exchanged, proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\index{Bergman kernel!comparison on overlaps}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization}
There is a constant $c>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega)$ through the fixed geometry, with the following property. Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $m\geq1$ be an integer. Then there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi_m$ on $X$ such that
\[
 \theta+\ddc\varphi_m\geq-\frac cm\omega,
 \qquad
 \varphi_m\geq\frac1m\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)\quad\text{locally},
\]
where the $g_i$ are local generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. The bounded term may depend on $m$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ and the point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, the functions $v_{j,m}\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$ satisfy $v_{j,m}\leq v_{l,m}+C_B/m$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, also at their common zeros. Hence \cref{lem:maximum-cutoff-patching} with $a=C_B/m$ gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi_m$ with $\theta+\ddc\varphi_m\geq-(C_BC_\chi/m)\omega$, so the first conclusion holds with $c=C_BC_\chi$, increased if necessary to make it positive. On $U_j$ the function $\varphi_m$ dominates $v_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}-h_j$. Since $h_j$ is smooth, $\mathcal I(m(\varphi+h_j))=\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, and the comparison of \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution} gives $v_{j,m}\geq m^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ near each point of $U_j$. This proves the second conclusion.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!quantitative lower bound}

\section{Smooth cutoff sums and decreasing approximation}
\label{sec:gentle-decreasing-approximation}

The maximum in \cref{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization} destroys smoothness, and that theorem does not compare the functions $\varphi_m$ for different values of $m$. To obtain the regularity required of gentle singularities and, later, a decreasing sequence, we glue the local kernels instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs and control the curvature of its logarithm by a direct computation. We keep the notation of the previous section.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $m\geq1$ be an integer, and put
\[
 R_m=\sum_j\chi_j^2a_{j,m},\qquad G_m=m^{-1}\log R_m,
\]
each summand being extended by zero outside the support of $\chi_j$. There is a constant $c'>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that
$\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-(c'/m)\omega$. Moreover $R_m$ is smooth on $X$, and $G_m$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: a Hessian identity.} Let $w_j$ be smooth real functions and $\chi_j$ nonnegative smooth functions on an open set, with $S=\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}>0$, and put $\alpha_j=\chi_j\partial w_j+2\partial\chi_j$, so that $\partial S=\sum_j\chi_j\mathrm e^{w_j}\alpha_j$. Differentiating twice gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian}
\begin{aligned}
 \ddc\log S={}&\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi S^2}
 \sum_{j<l}\mathrm e^{w_j+w_l}
 (\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)
       \wedge\overline{(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)}\\
 &+\frac{\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}\ddc w_j}{S}
 +2\frac{\sum_j\mathrm e^{w_j}
  \bigl(\chi_j\ddc\chi_j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}
                         \partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\bigr)}{S}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial(\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j})=\mathrm e^{w_j}\bigl(\mathrm i\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j+\chi_j^2\,\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial w_j+2\chi_j\,\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\chi_j-2\,\mathrm i\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\bigr)$. In $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\log S=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial S/S-\mathrm i\partial S\wedge\bar\partial S/S^2$, the terms $\mathrm e^{w_j}\mathrm i\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j/S$ combine with $-\mathrm i\partial S\wedge\bar\partial S/S^2$ to the sum over pairs $j<l$ in the first line, as one sees by expanding the squares $(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)\wedge\overline{(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)}$. The first term on the right of \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian} is semipositive.

\emph{Step 2: a curvature estimate.} Choose a constant $A>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that $\chi_j\ddc\chi_j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\geq-A\omega$ for every $j$. Let $W\subset X$ be open, and for the indices $j$ with $W\cap\Supp\chi_j\neq\varnothing$ let $w_j$ be smooth real functions on $W$ such that
\[
 \ddc w_j\geq-m\theta\quad\text{on }W,\qquad
 \mathrm e^{w_j}\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}\mathrm e^{w_i}\quad\text{on }W\cap U_i\cap\Supp\chi_j
\]
for all such $i,j$. We claim that $S=\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}$ is positive on $W$ and that
\[
 \theta+\frac1m\ddc\log S\geq-\frac{2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}}m\omega\qquad\text{on }W.
\]
Let $x\in W$ and choose $i$ with $x\in U_i$. Then $\chi_i(x)=1$, so $S(x)\geq\mathrm e^{w_i(x)}>0$, and $\mathrm e^{w_j}/S\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}$ at $x$ for every $j$ with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$. If $\chi_j(x)=0$, the $j$th summand of the last term in \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian} vanishes at $x$, because the nonnegative function $\chi_j$ attains its minimum at $x$, so that $\partial\chi_j(x)=0$. Hence the last term is at least $-2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}\omega$ at $x$. The coefficients $\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}/S$ are nonnegative with sum one, so the middle term is at least $-m\theta$, and the first term is semipositive. This proves the claim.

\emph{Step 3: application to the kernels.} The kernels $a_{j,m}$ may vanish, so we approximate them. Fix $x_0\in X$ and a small coordinate ball $W$ about $x_0$ such that $W\subset U'_j$ whenever $x_0\in\Supp\chi_j$, and $W\cap\Supp\chi_j=\varnothing$ otherwise; then the indices $j$ with $W\cap\Supp\chi_j\neq\varnothing$ are those with $x_0\in\Supp\chi_j$. Choose a smooth function $g$ on $W$ with $\ddc g=\theta$, and for each such $j$ write $h_j-g=\Real b_j$ on $W$ with $b_j$ holomorphic, by \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}. As in the proof of \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, $a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}k_j$ on $W$, where, by \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}, $k_j=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}K_{j,m}=\sum_\ell|\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}e_\ell|^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell)$ of the space defining $K_{j,m}$. For $\delta>0$ the function $\log(k_j+\delta)$ is smooth, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, and plurisubharmonic: it is the increasing limit of the logarithms of the finite partial sums of these squared moduli together with $|\sqrt\delta|^2$, which are plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}, and since the limit is continuous, \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} applies. Put $w_j^\delta=\log(k_j+\delta)-mg$, so that $\ddc w_j^\delta\geq-m\theta$. On $W\cap U_i\cap\Supp\chi_j$, \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap} gives $k_j\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}k_i$, and since $\mathrm e^{C_B}\geq1$ also $k_j+\delta\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}(k_i+\delta)$. By Step 2 the function
\[
 \frac1m\log\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j^\delta}+g'
\]
is plurisubharmonic on $W$, where $g'$ is a smooth function on $W$ with $\ddc g'=\theta+(2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}/m)\omega$. As $\delta\downarrow0$, the sums $\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j^\delta}=\mathrm e^{-mg}\sum_j\chi_j^2(k_j+\delta)$ decrease to $R_m$, so these functions decrease to $G_m+g'$, which is not identically $-\infty$, since $G_m\geq v_{i,m}$ on $U_i$. By \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}, $G_m+g'$ is plurisubharmonic on $W$. Hence $G_m$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, and the curvature bound holds with $c'=2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}$, which depends only on the fixed geometry.

\emph{Step 4: gentleness.} Each summand of $R_m$ is smooth by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}. On $U_i$ we have $a_{i,m}\leq R_m\leq J\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{i,m}$ by \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, and $a_{i,m}$ is comparable with $\sum_k|g_k|^2$ for local generators $g_k$ of $\mathcal I(m(\varphi+h_i))=\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Thus $G_m$ has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$. Now let $\pi$ be any log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, with $\pi^*\mathcal I(m\varphi)=\mathcal O(-D)$. By \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, each pulled-back kernel $K_{j,m}\circ\pi$ is $|s_D|^2$ times a smooth strictly positive function. The factors $\mathrm e^{-mh_j}\circ\pi$ and $\chi_j^2\circ\pi$ are smooth, so $(R_m\circ\pi)/|s_D|^2$ is smooth, and it is strictly positive because at every point some $\chi_i$ equals one. Hence $G_m\circ\pi=m^{-1}\log|s_D|^2+h$ with $h$ smooth. Finally $\exp(mG_m)=R_m$ is smooth on $X$. These are exactly the requirements of \cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}.
\end{proof}
\index{patching!logarithmic cutoff sum}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. As $m\to\infty$, $G_m\to\varphi$ pointwise on $X$ and in $L^1(X)$. For every integer $m\geq1$ and every $x\in X$,
\[
 \nu(\varphi,x)-\frac nm\leq\nu(G_m,x)\leq\nu(\varphi,x),
 \qquad \mathrm e^{m(G_m-\varphi)}\in L^1(X).
\]
Moreover, there is a constant $D\geq0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that
$G_{2m}\leq G_m+D/m$ for every integer $m\geq1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x\in U_i$. Then $R_m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$, and the lower bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}, with the constant adjusted to the volume form $\omega^n/n!$, gives $m^{-1}\log K_{i,m}(x)\geq\varphi(x)+h_i(x)-C/m$. Since $h_i$ cancels, $G_m\geq v_{i,m}\geq\varphi-C/m$ on $U_i$, where $C\geq0$ depends only on the fixed geometry.

For an upper bound, let $r>0$ be smaller than the distance, in the coordinates of $B_j$, from $\Supp\chi_j$ to $\partial B_j$ for every $j$. If $\chi_j(x)\neq0$, the upper bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds} on $B_j$ gives
\[
 v_{j,m}(x)=\frac1m\log K_{j,m}(x)-h_j(x)\leq\sup_{B(x,r)}\varphi+\Bigl(\sup_{B(x,r)}h_j-h_j(x)\Bigr)+\frac1m\log\frac{C_2}{r^{2n}},
\]
where now $C_2$ also accounts for the volume form. The coordinate ball $B(x,r)$ is contained in the $d_\omega$-ball of radius $C_3r$ about $x$, with $C_3$ depending only on the fixed geometry, and $\sup_{B(x,r)}h_j-h_j(x)\leq C_hr$, where $C_h$ bounds the gradients of the finitely many $h_j$ and therefore depends on $\theta$. Since $R_m$ is a sum of at most $J$ terms, each at most $\max_ja_{j,m}(x)$ over the indices with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$,
\[
 G_m(x)\leq\sup_{d_\omega(x,y)<C_3r}\varphi(y)+C_hr+\frac1m\log\frac{JC_2}{r^{2n}}.
\]
Letting first $m\to\infty$ and then $r\downarrow0$, and using the lower bound and the upper semicontinuity of $\varphi$, we obtain $G_m(x)\to\varphi(x)$, also at points of the pole set. For a fixed $r$ the upper bound is uniform on $X$, and $\varphi-C\leq G_m$ with $\varphi\in L^1(X)$; dominated convergence gives $G_m\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$.

By \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}, $G_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_k|g_k|^2+O(1)$ near $x$ with generators $g_k$ of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. As in the proof of \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}, this gives $\nu(G_m,x)=m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_x\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, and the Lelong bounds follow from \cref{lem:multiplier-order-lelong-bounds}, applied to a local plurisubharmonic function differing from $\varphi$ by a smooth function. For the integrability, note that $\mathrm e^{m(G_m-\varphi)}=R_m\mathrm e^{-m\varphi}=\sum_j\chi_j^2K_{j,m}\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi+h_j)}$. Cover the compact set $\Supp\chi_j$ by finitely many open sets on which $K_{j,m}\leq C_m\sum_i|g_i|^2$ with members $g_i$ of the weighted space on $B_j$, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}. Since these $g_i$ have finite weighted norm, every summand is integrable.

For the doubling inequality, \cref{lem:bergman-diagonal-comparison} applied on each $B_j$ with $u=\varphi+h_j$ gives $K_{j,2m}\leq C_*K_{j,m}^2$, hence $a_{j,2m}\leq C_*a_{j,m}^2$, with $C_*$ depending only on the fixed geometry. Let $x\in U_i$. For every $j$ with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$, \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap} gives $a_{j,m}(x)\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{i,m}(x)$, and $R_m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$. Consequently
\[
 R_{2m}\leq C_*\sum_j\chi_j^2a_{j,m}^2
        \leq C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_B}R_m^2
\]
at $x$, also when both sides vanish. Taking logarithms and dividing by $2m$ gives $G_{2m}\leq G_m+D/m$ with $D=\frac12\log\max(1,C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_B})$.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!doubling inequality}

To obtain inclusions of multiplier ideals from an integrability estimate, we use the following elementary lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:integrable-gap-ideal-transfer}
Let $u,v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic near a point $x$ of a complex manifold, and let $m>0$ be such that $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$ is integrable near $x$. If $0<\lambda<\lambda'$ and $m\geq\lambda\lambda'/(\lambda'-\lambda)$, then
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda'v)_x\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After shrinking the neighborhood we may subtract a constant from $v$ and assume $v\leq0$; this changes neither the ideals nor the integrability of $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Put $p=\lambda'/\lambda>1$, and note that $m\geq\lambda p/(p-1)>\lambda$. Let $f\in\mathcal I(\lambda'v)_x$, defined on a small neighborhood on which $|f|^2\leq C$ for a constant $C$. All the following inequalities are taken outside the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, which have measure zero. Where $u\geq pv$, we have $\lambda u\geq\lambda'v$ and hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda'v}$. Where $u<pv$, since $m-\lambda>0$, $v\leq0$ and $(m-\lambda)p-m=m(p-1)-\lambda p\geq0$,
\[
 (m-\lambda)u-mv\leq((m-\lambda)p-m)v\leq0,
\]
that is, $-\lambda u\leq m(v-u)$; hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq C\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Both bounds are integrable near $x$, so $f\in\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x$.
\end{proof}
\index{multiplier ideal!integrable gap}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. A \emph{quasi-equisingular approximation} of $\varphi$ is a sequence $(\Phi_j)_{j\geq1}$ of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions with analytic singularities, decreasing pointwise to $\varphi$, such that $\theta+\ddc\Phi_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\omega$ for some numbers $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$, and such that for all $0<\lambda<\lambda'$
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda'\Phi_j)\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda\varphi)
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }j.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-equisingular approximation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Every $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ admits a quasi-equisingular approximation $(\Phi_j)$ in which each $\Phi_j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type
$(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^j\varphi))$, and which can be chosen with
$\varepsilon_j=c'2^{-j}$, where $c'$ is the constant of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}. Moreover $\Phi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$, and the Lelong numbers of $\Phi_j$ satisfy the bounds of \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling} with $m=2^j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Phi_j=G_{2^j}+2D/2^j$, with $D$ as in \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}. The doubling inequality gives
\[
 \Phi_{j+1}=G_{2\cdot2^j}+\frac D{2^j}\leq G_{2^j}+\frac D{2^j}+\frac D{2^j}=\Phi_j,
\]
so the sequence is decreasing, and by \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling} its pointwise and $L^1$ limit is $\varphi$. Adding a constant changes neither the curvature, nor the analytic type, nor the Lelong numbers. Moreover $\exp(2^j\Phi_j)=\mathrm e^{2D}R_{2^j}$ is smooth, and the remainder on a log resolution changes only by a constant. Hence $\Phi_j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^j\varphi))$ by \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}, and $\theta+\ddc\Phi_j\geq-c'2^{-j}\omega$.

It remains to prove the inclusions of multiplier ideals. Let $0<\lambda<\lambda'$. The function $\exp(2^j(\Phi_j-\varphi))=\mathrm e^{2D}\mathrm e^{2^j(G_{2^j}-\varphi)}$ is integrable on $X$ by \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}. If $j$ is so large that $2^j\geq\lambda\lambda'/(\lambda'-\lambda)$, then \cref{lem:integrable-gap-ideal-transfer}, with $u=\varphi$, $v=\Phi_j$ and $m=2^j$, gives $\mathcal I(\lambda'\Phi_j)_x\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda\varphi)_x$ at every point $x\in X$. Since this threshold for $j$ depends only on $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$, the inclusion holds as an inclusion of sheaves on $X$ for all such $j$.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!decreasing gentle approximation}

\section{Comparison of analytic singularities}
\label{sec:analytic-approximation-comparison}

For functions $u,v\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty)$ we write $u\preceq v$, and say that $u$ is \emph{more singular} than $v$, if $u\leq v+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$. Since $X$ is compact, it suffices that every point have a neighborhood on which such an inequality holds.
\index{more singular potential} We now show that two quasi-equisingular approximations of the same function are comparable for $\preceq$, up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient. The key is a growth estimate for the members of the multiplier ideals of a function with analytic singularities.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth}
Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a compact complex manifold $X$, and let $0<\eta<1$. There is $t_0>0$ such that for every real $t\geq t_0$ and every germ $f\in\mathcal I(tv)_x$, $x\in X$,
\[
 \log|f|^2\leq t(1-\eta)v+O(1)
\]
near $x$, where the bounded term may depend on $f$. Moreover, for every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ and every $t>0$, local generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(tu)$ satisfy
\[
 u\leq t^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cover $X$ by finitely many open sets $V_\kappa^+$ on which $v$ has a presentation \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities}, and choose open sets $V_\kappa\Subset V_\kappa^+$ that still cover $X$. By \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, the ideal generated by the presentation on $V_\kappa^+$ has a log resolution $\pi_\kappa\colon Y_\kappa\to V_\kappa^+$, a sequence of blowups that is finite over $V_\kappa$; its fibers are compact. On $Y_\kappa$ we have locally $v\circ\pi_\kappa=\sum_Ea_E\log|s_E|^2+O(1)$, where $\pi_\kappa^*(g_1,\ldots,g_r)=\mathcal O(-\sum_Ed_EE)$, $E$ runs over the components of this divisor, $s_E$ is a local equation of $E$, and $a_E=cd_E>0$. Let $A_E$ be the log discrepancy of $E$ (\cref{def:divisorial-order-discrepancy}). Only finitely many components $E$ meet the compact set $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa})$, so altogether there are finitely many pairs $(a_E,A_E)$ with $E$ meeting one of these sets. Choose $t_0>0$ with $t_0\eta a_E\geq A_E$ for all of them.

Now let $x\in V_\kappa$, let $t\geq t_0$, and let $f\in\mathcal I(tv)_x$ be defined on an open neighborhood $V'\subset V_\kappa$ of $x$. Near $x$ the function $tv$ differs by a smooth function from a plurisubharmonic function with the same multiplier ideal and of the form required in \cref{thm:analytic-log-resolution-integrability}, with coefficient $tc$. We apply that theorem over $V'$, shrunk if necessary, to the log resolution obtained by restricting $\pi_\kappa$. Each component $E'$ of the restricted divisor lies in a component $E$ as above, with the same coefficient $d_E$ and the same log discrepancy $A_E$, since the vanishing order of the Jacobian determinant of $\pi_\kappa$ along the connected submanifold $E$ is constant. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{E'}f>ta_E-A_E\geq t(1-\eta)a_E
\]
for every component $E'$ of the restricted divisor that meets $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$.

Let $p\in\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$, and choose coordinates $w$ on a neighborhood of $p$ in $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(V')$ in which the components of the divisor through $p$ are $\{w_k=0\}$, $k=1,\ldots,\ell$, with coefficients $a_k=a_E$ for the corresponding components $E$. Then $v\circ\pi_\kappa=\sum_ka_k\log|w_k|^2+O(1)$ near $p$, and by the preceding paragraph $f\circ\pi_\kappa$ is divisible by $w_k^{o_k}$ for integers $o_k>t(1-\eta)a_k$; here $o_k=\operatorname{ord}_{E'}f$ for the component $E'$ of the restricted divisor containing $\{w_k=0\}$ near $p$, since the vanishing order of $f\circ\pi_\kappa$ along the connected submanifold $E'$ is the same near all its points. Thus $f\circ\pi_\kappa=\prod_kw_k^{o_k}\cdot h$ with $h$ holomorphic, hence bounded on a smaller neighborhood of $p$. Since $|w_k|\leq1$ there, we obtain $|f\circ\pi_\kappa|^2\leq C\prod_k|w_k|^{2t(1-\eta)a_k}\leq C'\exp(t(1-\eta)v\circ\pi_\kappa)$. (If $\ell=0$, then $v\circ\pi_\kappa$ is bounded near $p$ and this bound is immediate.) Finitely many such neighborhoods cover the compact fiber $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$, and since $\pi_\kappa$ is proper, their union contains the inverse image of a neighborhood of $x$. As $\pi_\kappa$ is surjective, the inequality $|f|^2\leq C'\exp(t(1-\eta)v)$ holds near $x$.

The second assertion is \eqref{eq:weight-versus-multiplier-generators} of \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation}, applied with $m=t$ to $u+\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is a smooth local function making $u+\sigma$ plurisubharmonic; the multiplier ideal is unchanged, $\sigma$ only changes the bounded term, and any two finite systems of local generators give comparable sums of squares.
\end{proof}
\index{multiplier ideal!growth of members}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quasi-equisingular-comparison}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $(\Phi_j)$ and $(\Psi_k)$ be quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi$. Then for each $j$ and each $0<\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \Psi_k\preceq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_j
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }k.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $0<\eta<\varepsilon$ and $\delta>0$ so small that
$(1-\eta)/(1+\delta)\geq1-\varepsilon$. Subtracting one constant from $\varphi$ and from all the $\Phi_{j'}$ and $\Psi_{k'}$ preserves the hypotheses and the conclusion, and since $X$ is compact we may thus assume $\Phi_j\leq0$. Fix $t\geq t_0$, where $t_0$ is given by \cref{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth} for $v=\Phi_j$ and $\eta$. By quasi-equisingularity of $(\Psi_k)$, applied with $\lambda=t$ and $\lambda'=t(1+\delta)$, and since $\varphi\leq\Phi_j$,
\[
 \mathcal I(t(1+\delta)\Psi_k)
       \subseteq\mathcal I(t\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal I(t\Phi_j)
\]
for every sufficiently large $k$. Fix such a $k$ and choose local generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(t(1+\delta)\Psi_k)$. They belong to $\mathcal I(t\Phi_j)$, so the first part of \cref{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth} gives $\log|g_i|^2\leq t(1-\eta)\Phi_j+O(1)$. Its second part, applied to $u=\Psi_k$ and $t(1+\delta)$, then gives
\[
 \Psi_k\leq\frac1{t(1+\delta)}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)
       \leq\frac{1-\eta}{1+\delta}\Phi_j+O(1)
       \leq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_j+O(1),
\]
where the last step uses $\Phi_j\leq0$. These local bounds hold near every point, so by compactness $\Psi_k\preceq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_j$; the constant may depend on $j$ and $k$.
\end{proof}
\index{quasi-equisingular approximation!comparison}

\begin{example}\label{ex:analytic-regularization-endpoints}
Let $u=c\log|z|^2$ on a disc about $0\in\mathbb C$, with $c>0$. A germ $z^kh$ with $h(0)\neq0$ lies in $\mathcal I(mu)_0$ if and only if $|z|^{2k-2mc}$ is integrable near $0$, that is, $k>mc-1$; hence $\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $z^{\lfloor mc\rfloor}$. A potential of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$, such as the Bergman potential $u_m$ of \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}, is therefore $(\lfloor mc\rfloor/m)\log|z|^2+O(1)$ near $0$. Its coefficient $\lfloor mc\rfloor/m$ need not equal $c$ for any $m$ (take $c$ irrational), so such an approximation does not in general reproduce the multiplier ideals of $u$ at a fixed scale. This is why \cref{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation} compares two different scales $\lambda'>\lambda$ instead of requiring equality of multiplier ideals.
\end{example}
