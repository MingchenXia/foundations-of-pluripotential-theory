\chapter{Analytic singularities and Demailly approximation}
\label{chap:q06}

Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a domain in $\mathbb C^n$. Convolution with a smoothing kernel approximates $u$ by smooth plurisubharmonic functions, but these approximants have lost all singularities: their Lelong numbers vanish and their multiplier ideals are trivial. Many problems require approximants whose singularities are still visible but as simple as possible, namely functions locally of the form $c\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ with holomorphic $g_i$ and rational $c>0$; such functions are said to have analytic singularities. On a compact K\"ahler manifold a second difficulty appears: convolution is defined only in charts, and gluing local approximations costs positivity. This chapter presents Demailly's answer to both problems, in which convolution is replaced by weighted Bergman kernels.

For $u$ plurisubharmonic on a bounded pseudoconvex domain $\Omega$ and an integer $m\geq1$, the holomorphic functions $F$ on $\Omega$ with $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty$ form a Hilbert space, which generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$ of \cref{chap:q05}. If $K_m(z)$ denotes the supremum of $|F(z)|^2$ over its unit ball, then $u_m=m^{-1}\log K_m$ is plurisubharmonic and has analytic singularities described by $\mathcal I(mu)$. The Ohsawa--Takegoshi estimate of \cref{chap:q04} bounds $u_m$ below by $u-O(1/m)$, and the mean-value inequality bounds it above by the supremum of $u$ over a small ball, up to an error that tends to zero as $m\to\infty$. Hence $u_m\to u$, and the Lelong numbers of $u_m$ differ from those of $u$ by at most $n/m$ (\cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}).

On a compact K\"ahler manifold $(X,\omega)$ we apply this construction in finitely many coordinate balls to a function $\varphi$ with $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0$, where $\theta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form. A $\bar\partial$-estimate with a logarithmic pole shows that the kernels of two overlapping balls are comparable up to a factor that depends only on the charts (\cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}). Gluing the local potentials by a maximum gives the quantitative regularization of \cref{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization}, with a curvature loss of order $1/m$ and a lower bound by the generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Gluing them instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs gives approximants $G_m$ with gentle analytic singularities (\cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}): $\exp(mG_m)$ is smooth, and $G_m$ has a smooth remainder on every log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Comparing the kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ by extension from the diagonal, we show that the dyadic sequence $G_{2^j}$, corrected by small constants, decreases to $\varphi$ and that its multiplier ideals approximate those of $\varphi$, so that it is a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi$ in the sense of \cref{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation} (\cref{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}). Any two quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi$ dominate each other up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient (\cref{prop:quasi-equisingular-comparison}).

The first section introduces quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and analytic singularities and shows that blowups with smooth centers preserve the K\"ahler property. The last section studies the ideal of holomorphic germs $f$ with $|f|^2\leq C\mathrm e^v$: for $v$ with analytic singularities it is coherent, and on a compact manifold the ideal attached to $kv$ contains $\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon)v)$ for all large $k$.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-analytic-regularization}.

\section{Smooth backgrounds and analytic singularities}
\label{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}

Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq0$. Recall from \cref{sec:current-maps} that a Hermitian form on $X$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is strictly positive at every point, that is, the form associated with a Hermitian metric; it is a K\"ahler form if it is closed, and $X$ is a K\"ahler manifold if it carries a K\"ahler form. A Hermitian form $\omega$ defines a Riemannian metric on $X$ and the positive volume form $\omega^n/n!$.
\index{K\"ahler form}\index{K\"ahler manifold}\index{Hermitian form}

Continuous real $(1,1)$-forms are controlled by a Hermitian form on compact sets: if $\alpha$ is a continuous real $(1,1)$-form on a neighborhood of a compact set $K\subset X$, then $\alpha\geq-C\omega$ on $K$ for some constant $C\geq0$. Indeed, cover $K$ by finitely many compact sets $K_1,\ldots,K_N$, each contained in a chart and in the domain of $\alpha$, and write $\alpha=\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ and $\omega=\mathrm i\sum_{j,k}w_{jk}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ there, with Hermitian matrices $(a_{jk})$ and $(w_{jk})$. On $K_\iota$ the smallest eigenvalue of $(w_{jk})$, a continuous positive function, is at least some $c_\iota>0$, and the largest eigenvalue of $-(a_{jk})$ is at most some $M_\iota\geq0$. Hence $(a_{jk})\geq-M_\iota I\geq-(M_\iota/c_\iota)(w_{jk})$ on $K_\iota$, and by the matrix criterion for positivity (\cref{sec:local-complex-potentials}) $\alpha\geq-C\omega$ on $K$ with $C=\max_\iota M_\iota/c_\iota$.

The following lemma provides smooth local potentials for smooth closed forms on whole coordinate balls, not only on small polydiscs as in \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}
Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form defined on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Then there is a smooth real function $h$ on $B$ with $\ddc h=\theta$. Moreover, every real pluriharmonic function on a coordinate ball is, up to an additive constant, the real part of a holomorphic function on that ball.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In dimension zero there is nothing to prove, so let $n\geq1$. Choose concentric balls $B\Subset B'\Subset B''$ such that $\theta$ is defined on a neighborhood of $\overline{B''}$. By the last assertion of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, applied to the closed real $2$-form $\theta$ on the ball $B''$, there is a smooth real one-form $\alpha$ on $B''$ with $\mathrm d\alpha=\theta$. Since $\theta$ has type $(1,1)$, the $(0,2)$-part $\bar\partial\alpha^{0,1}$ of $\mathrm d\alpha$ vanishes. The form $\alpha^{0,1}$ is bounded on $B'$, so \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound}, applied on the ball $B'$, which is pseudoconvex by \cref{cor:pseudoconvex-sublevels-products}, with $p=0$ and $q=|z|^2$ (whose complex Hessian is the identity), gives a function $f\in L^2(B')$ with $\bar\partial f=\alpha^{0,1}$.

The function $f$ is smooth. Indeed, let $p\in B'$ and choose polydiscs $\Delta'\Subset\Delta\Subset B'$ centered at $p$. By \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, applied to the smooth $\bar\partial$-closed form $\alpha^{0,1}$ on $\Delta$, there is a smooth function $f_0$ on $\Delta'$ with $\bar\partial f_0=\alpha^{0,1}$. Then $\bar\partial(f-f_0)=0$ on $\Delta'$ in the sense of distributions, so $\Delta(f-f_0)=4\sum_j\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_j}(f-f_0)=0$ there, and by \cref{thm:weyl} the locally integrable function $f-f_0$ agrees almost everywhere on $\Delta'$ with a smooth function. Thus $f$ agrees almost everywhere near each point with a smooth function; two such smooth functions coincide on the overlap of their domains, being continuous and equal almost everywhere, so we may take $f$ smooth on $B'$. Since $\alpha$ is real, $\alpha^{1,0}=\overline{\alpha^{0,1}}=\partial\bar f$, and the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm d\alpha$ gives, exactly as in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare},
\[
 \theta=\bar\partial\partial\bar f+\partial\bar\partial f=\partial\bar\partial(f-\bar f)=\ddc(4\pi\Img f).
\]
Thus $h=4\pi\Img f$ has the required property on $B\subset B'$.

For the last assertion, let $h$ be real and pluriharmonic on a coordinate ball. Then $\partial h$ is a $(1,0)$-form with holomorphic coefficients, since $\bar\partial\partial h=0$, and it is closed. By the last assertion of \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, applied to the closed one-form $2\partial h$ on this ball, there is a smooth function $F$ on the whole ball with $\mathrm dF=2\partial h$. As $\mathrm dF$ has type $(1,0)$, $F$ is holomorphic, and $\mathrm d(\Real F)=\partial h+\bar\partial h=\mathrm dh$. Hence $h-\Real F$ is constant.
\end{proof}
\index{ddc@$\ddc$ operator!potentials on a ball}\index{pluriharmonic function!real part of a holomorphic function}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold. A function $\varphi\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty)$ is \emph{quasi-plurisubharmonic} if every point of $X$ has a neighborhood $V$ on which $\varphi=u+f$ pointwise, with $u\in\PSH(V)$ and $f$ a smooth real function on $V$. For a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$ we put
\[
 \PSH(X,\theta)=\{\varphi\text{ quasi-plurisubharmonic on }X:\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0\},
\]
and call its elements \emph{$\theta$-plurisubharmonic} (\emph{$\theta$-psh}) functions.
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-plurisubharmonic function}\index{theta-plurisubharmonic function}

A quasi-plurisubharmonic function is locally integrable on $X$, hence finite almost everywhere. Let $h$ be a smooth real function on an open set $V$ with $\ddc h=\theta$. Then a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ satisfies $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq0$ on $V$ if and only if $\varphi+h$ is plurisubharmonic on $V$. Indeed, locally $\varphi+h$ is a plurisubharmonic function plus a smooth one, so at every point it is the limit of its ball averages, as in \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}; if $\ddc(\varphi+h)\geq0$, it is therefore the plurisubharmonic representative of its class given by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. If $X$ is compact and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form, every quasi-plurisubharmonic function satisfies $\ddc\varphi\geq-C\omega$ for some constant $C$. Indeed, by compactness there are finitely many open sets $V_\kappa\Subset V_\kappa^+$ such that the $V_\kappa$ cover $X$ and $\varphi=u_\kappa+f_\kappa$ on $V_\kappa^+$ with $u_\kappa$ plurisubharmonic and $f_\kappa$ smooth; on $V_\kappa$ we have $\ddc\varphi\geq\ddc f_\kappa\geq-C_\kappa\omega$ by the comparison at the beginning of this section, and we take $C=\max_\kappa C_\kappa$. Conversely, let $\varphi\in L^1_{\loc}(X)$ satisfy $\ddc\varphi\geq-C\omega$. On a coordinate ball whose closure lies in a chart, the same comparison, applied to $-\omega$ and the Hermitian form $\ddc|z|^2$, gives $\omega\leq C'\ddc|z|^2$, so $\ddc(\varphi+A|z|^2)\geq0$ for $A=CC'$. Hence $\varphi+A|z|^2$ has a plurisubharmonic representative by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, and subtracting $A|z|^2$ gives a representative of $\varphi$ on the ball. These representatives agree on overlaps: if $A'|z'|^2$ is the quadratic function used on a second ball, both representatives plus $A'|z'|^2$ are plurisubharmonic functions plus smooth ones, hence both equal the canonical representative of the class of $\varphi+A'|z'|^2$. Thus $\varphi$ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative.

Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic weight are defined as in \cref{def:multiplier-ideal}. Adding a smooth function to the weight multiplies the integrand by a locally bounded positive factor with locally bounded inverse, so the multiplier ideal of $\varphi=u+f$ near a point is that of $u$; in particular it is coherent, and the local results of \cref{chap:q05} apply to it. Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are defined as in \cref{cor:zero-lelong-trivial-multiplier-ideal}, by $\nu(\varphi,x)=\nu(u,x)$. Finally, if $\theta+\ddc\varphi_i\geq0$ for finitely many quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_k$, then $\theta+\ddc\max_i\varphi_i\geq0$, since locally $\max_i\varphi_i+h=\max_i(\varphi_i+h)$ with $\ddc h=\theta$, and \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} applies.

\begin{definition}\label{def:rational-analytic-singularities}
A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$ has \emph{analytic singularities} if every point of $X$ has a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ on which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-analytic-singularities}
 \varphi=c\log\sum_{i=1}^r|g_i|^2+b,
 \qquad c\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\quad b\text{ bounded},
\end{equation}
where $r\geq1$ is an integer and $g_1,\ldots,g_r\in\mathcal O(V)$ are not all identically zero. It has \emph{neat analytic singularities} if such presentations exist with $b$ smooth. Let $c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and let $\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_X$ be a coherent ideal sheaf. We say that $\varphi$ has analytic singularities of \emph{type} $(c,\mathfrak a)$ if presentations \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities} exist with this coefficient $c$, where on each $V$ the functions $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ generate $\mathfrak a$.
\end{definition}
\index{analytic singularities}\index{analytic singularities!neat}\index{analytic singularities!type}

The type condition does not depend on the choice of local generators: if $(g_i)$ and $(g'_k)$ both generate $\mathfrak a$ on $V$, then near each point of $V$ each tuple is a holomorphic matrix times the other, and both matrices are bounded on smaller neighborhoods, so $\log\sum|g_i|^2-\log\sum|g'_k|^2$ is locally bounded. A type is additional data and is not unique; for example, a function of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ also has type $(c/2,\mathfrak a^2)$.

For a holomorphic map $\pi\colon Y\to X$ and an ideal sheaf $\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_X$, we write $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a\cdot\mathcal O_Y$ for the ideal generated by the pullbacks of local generators of $\mathfrak a$. Let $\mathfrak a$ be a coherent ideal on $X$, nonzero on each component. Since $X$ is smooth, the resolution step in the construction of log resolutions at the end of \cref{sec:analytic-blowups-resolution} is not needed, and \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, applied with the empty divisor, produces log resolutions of $\mathfrak a$ by blowups with smooth centers. From now on a \emph{log resolution} of $\mathfrak a$ always means a map of this form: a composite $\pi\colon Y\to X$ of blowups along closed submanifolds of positive codimension, finite over each relatively compact open subset of $X$, such that $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ whose support has simple normal crossings. We also call $\pi$ a \emph{principalization} of $\mathfrak a$, and we write $s_D$ for a local holomorphic equation of $D$.
\index{log resolution!by blowups with smooth centers}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rational-analytic-operations}
Analytic singularities are preserved by multiplication by positive rational numbers, by sums and finite maxima, and by pullback under a holomorphic map $f\colon Y\to X$ of complex manifolds whenever the composition is not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $Y$. If $\varphi$ has type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ and $\pi\colon Y\to X$ is a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ with $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_Y(-D)$, then locally on $Y$
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+O(1),
\]
and the remainder is smooth if the presentations of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ are neat.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ replaces $c$ by $qc$. Sums and finite maxima of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic: if $\varphi_i=u_i+f_i$ near a point, with $u_i$ plurisubharmonic and $f_i$ smooth, then on a small coordinate ball about the point the smooth functions $f_i+N|z|^2$ have positive semidefinite complex Hessians, hence are plurisubharmonic, for a large constant $N$, and $\varphi_1+\varphi_2$ and $\max_i\varphi_i$ are plurisubharmonic functions minus $2N|z|^2$ and $N|z|^2$, respectively, by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}. For a sum or a maximum of two functions with analytic singularities, choose about a given point presentations with coefficients $c$ and $d$, and a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ of the point contained in both domains; by the identity theorem, neither tuple vanishes identically on $V$. Choose $c_0\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ such that $c/c_0$ and $d/c_0$ are positive integers. For an integer $k\geq1$ the multinomial formula gives
\[
 \Bigl(\sum_i|g_i|^2\Bigr)^k=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\Bigl|\sqrt{k!/\alpha!}\,g^\alpha\Bigr|^2,
\]
again a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic functions, not all identically zero on $V$. We may therefore assume that both presentations have the coefficient $c_0$, say $c_0\log A+b$ and $c_0\log B+b'$ with $A=\sum_i|g_i|^2$ and $B=\sum_k|h_k|^2$. The sum is $c_0\log(AB)+b+b'$, and $AB=\sum_{i,k}|g_ih_k|^2$, where some product $g_ih_k$ is not identically zero on the connected set $V$. For the maximum we use
\[
 \max(\log A,\log B)\leq\log(A+B)\leq\max(\log A,\log B)+\log2,
\]
which shows that $\max(c_0\log A+b,c_0\log B+b')$ differs from $c_0\log(A+B)$ by a bounded function.

For the pullback, let $f\colon Y\to X$ be holomorphic, and suppose that $\varphi\circ f$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any connected component of $Y$. Near each point of $Y$, write $\varphi\circ f=u\circ f+h\circ f$ on a connected coordinate neighborhood $W$, where $\varphi=u+h$ near the image point with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $h$ smooth; by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, $u\circ f$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on $W$. Let $S$ be the open set of points of $Y$ near which $\varphi\circ f$ is identically $-\infty$. If $y$ lies in the closure of $S$ and $W$ is as above for $y$, then $u\circ f=-\infty$ on the nonempty open set $W\cap S$; since plurisubharmonic functions are locally integrable, $u\circ f$ is identically $-\infty$ on $W$, so $y\in S$. Thus $S$ is open and closed, and by hypothesis it contains no component of $Y$; hence $S=\varnothing$, and $\varphi\circ f$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. If $\varphi=c\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+b$ on $V$, then on every connected coordinate neighborhood $W\subset f^{-1}(V)$ we have $\varphi\circ f=c\log\sum_i|g_i\circ f|^2+b\circ f$, with $b\circ f$ bounded, and the functions $g_i\circ f$ do not all vanish identically on $W$, because $S=\varnothing$.

Now let $\pi$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ as in the statement. If $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ generate $\mathfrak a$, their pullbacks generate $\mathcal O_Y(-D)$, so locally $g_i\circ\pi=s_Dh_i$ with holomorphic $h_i$, and $s_D=\sum_ik_i(g_i\circ\pi)$ for holomorphic $k_i$. Since $s_D$ is not a zero divisor, $\sum_ik_ih_i=1$, so the $h_i$ have no common zero and $\log\sum_i|h_i|^2$ is smooth. Hence
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+c\log\sum_i|h_i|^2+b\circ\pi,
\]
which proves both assertions about the remainder.
\end{proof}
\index{analytic singularities!operations and pullback}

The proof uses the rationality of the coefficients for sums and maxima: two positive rational numbers are integer multiples of a common one.

If $\varphi$ has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, we also call a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ a \emph{log resolution of $\varphi$}; since types are not unique, this refers to the chosen type. On such a resolution $\varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+O(1)$ locally, by \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations}. A merely bounded remainder need not become smooth on a log resolution. For instance, on the unit disc in $\mathbb C$ the function $\log|z|^2+|\Real z|$ is subharmonic, since $|\Real z|=\max(\Real z,-\Real z)$ is a maximum of harmonic functions (\cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}), and it has analytic singularities of type $(1,(z))$; in dimension one a closed submanifold of positive codimension is a hypersurface, so every blowup in a log resolution is the identity by \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}, and the remainder $|\Real z|$ is not smooth on the imaginary axis. This is why neat singularities, and the gentle singularities defined below on compact K\"ahler manifolds, are separate notions. When $X$ is compact, a log resolution consists of finitely many blowups with smooth centers, and if $X$ is moreover K\"ahler, so is the resolved manifold (\cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}). The proof of this fact uses the curvature of a metric on a line bundle.
\index{log resolution!of a function with analytic singularities}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quadratic-line-bundle-metric}
A holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $X$ is given by an open cover $(V_i)$ of $X$ and local frames $e_i$ over $V_i$ with transition functions $e_i=g_{ij}e_j$ on $V_i\cap V_j$, where $g_{ij}$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero and $g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}$ on triple overlaps. A smooth \emph{quadratic Hermitian metric} on $L$ assigns to each frame a smooth positive squared norm $|e_i|^2$ on $V_i$, such that $|e_i|^2=|g_{ij}|^2|e_j|^2$ on $V_i\cap V_j$. Its \emph{curvature} is the closed real $(1,1)$-form equal to $-\ddc\log|e_i|^2$ on $V_i$.
\end{definition}
\index{line bundle!holomorphic}\index{Hermitian metric!quadratic}\index{curvature!of a line bundle}

The curvature is well defined, since $\log|e_i|^2-\log|e_j|^2=\log|g_{ij}|^2$ is pluriharmonic, $g_{ij}$ being holomorphic and nowhere zero (\cref{sec:local-complex-potentials}). Smooth metrics exist: let $(\rho_k)$ be a smooth partition of unity with $\Supp\rho_k\subset V_k$, obtained from \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes} by grouping the members of a partition of unity subordinate to $(V_k)$, write a vector $\xi$ in the fiber at $x$ as $\xi=\sigma_ke_k(x)$ for each $k$ with $x\in V_k$, and put $|\xi|^2=\sum_k\rho_k(x)|\sigma_k|^2$. Then $|e_i|^2=\sum_k\rho_k|g_{ik}|^2$ is smooth and positive on $V_i$, and the cocycle relation $g_{ik}=g_{ij}g_{jk}$ gives $|e_i|^2=|g_{ij}|^2|e_j|^2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold with K\"ahler form $\omega$, let $W\subset X$ be a closed submanifold of positive codimension, and let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be the blowup of $X$ along $W$, with exceptional divisor $E=\pi^{-1}(W)$. Then $Y$ is a compact complex manifold, and there are a smooth function $\rho\colon X\to[0,\infty)$ with $\rho^{-1}(0)=W$ and a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ on $Y$ with $\eta=\ddc\log(\rho\circ\pi)$ on $Y\setminus E$, such that $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is a K\"ahler form on $Y$ for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. The form $\eta$ is the curvature of a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on the line bundle $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$, whose local frames are the function $1$ on $Y\setminus E$ and the local equations of $E$ near $E$. In particular $Y$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold. Consequently every log resolution of a coherent ideal on $X$, as above, is a compact K\"ahler manifold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}, $Y$ is a complex manifold and $E$ is a smooth hypersurface with $\mathcal I_W\mathcal O_Y=\mathcal O_Y(-E)$; moreover $Y$ is compact, since $\pi$ is proper (\cref{prop:analytic-blowup-universal}), and $\pi$ restricts to a biholomorphism $Y\setminus E\to X\setminus W$. The second assertion follows from the first by induction: since $X$ is compact, a log resolution is a composite of finitely many blowups along closed submanifolds of positive codimension, and each intermediate manifold is compact K\"ahler by the first assertion.

We use the charts of \cref{prop:smooth-center-blowup}. Near a point of $W$ at which $W$ has codimension $r$, choose coordinates $(z,w)$ on $X$, $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_r)$, with $W=\{w=0\}$. For each pivot $i$ the blowup has a chart with coordinates $(z,t,v)$, $v=(v_j)_{j\neq i}$, in which
\[
 \pi(z,t,v)=(z,t\tilde v),\qquad \tilde v_i=1,\quad\tilde v_j=v_j\ (j\neq i);
\]
in it $E=\{t=0\}$, and the fibers of $E\to W$ are the sets $\{z=\text{const},\ t=0\}$. If $r=1$ there is no variable $v$, and $\pi$ is the identity near the point.

\emph{Step 1: a closed $(1,1)$-form that is positive along the fibers of $E$.} Choose finitely many charts $V_1,\ldots,V_N$ of $X$ covering $W$, with coordinates $(z^\alpha,w^\alpha)$ such that $W\cap V_\alpha=\{w^\alpha=0\}$, put $V_0=X\setminus W$, and let $(\lambda_\alpha)_{0\leq\alpha\leq N}$ be a smooth partition of unity on $X$ with $\Supp\lambda_\alpha\subset V_\alpha$, obtained from \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes} by grouping the members of a partition of unity subordinate to this cover according to a set $V_\alpha$ containing their supports. Put
\[
 \rho=\lambda_0+\sum_{\alpha=1}^N\lambda_\alpha|w^\alpha|^2,
\]
each term extended by zero outside $V_\alpha$. This is a smooth function on $X$, positive on $X\setminus W$: at a point $x\notin W$ some $\lambda_\alpha(x)$ is positive, and $w^\alpha(x)\neq0$ if $\alpha\geq1$.

We describe $\rho$ near a point $x_0\in W$, in coordinates $(z,w)$ as above on a polydisc $\Delta$ centered at $x_0$. Shrinking $\Delta$, we may assume that $\Delta$ does not meet $\Supp\lambda_0$, nor $\Supp\lambda_\alpha$ for the indices $\alpha$ with $x_0\notin V_\alpha$, and that $\Delta\subset V_\alpha$ for the others. For such an $\alpha$, each component $w^\alpha_k$ vanishes on $W=\{w=0\}$, so
\[
 w^\alpha_k(z,w)=\sum_{l=1}^rw_l\int_0^1\frac{\partial w^\alpha_k}{\partial w_l}(z,sw)\,\mathrm ds,
\]
that is, $w^\alpha=A_\alpha w$ with a holomorphic $r\times r$ matrix $A_\alpha$ on $\Delta$. At points of $W$ the matrix $A_\alpha(z,0)=(\partial w^\alpha_k/\partial w_l)(z,0)$ is invertible: since $w^\alpha$ vanishes on $W$, its derivatives in the $z$-directions vanish there, and the Jacobian matrix of the coordinate change $(z,w)\mapsto(z^\alpha,w^\alpha)$ is invertible. Hence, on $\Delta$,
\[
 \rho=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha|A_\alpha w|^2=w^*\mathcal Q\,w,\qquad \mathcal Q=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha A_\alpha^*A_\alpha,
\]
where the sums run over the indices $\alpha\geq1$ with $x_0\in V_\alpha$, and $w^*$ is the conjugate transpose of the column vector $w$. The Hermitian matrix $\mathcal Q$ is smooth and positive semidefinite, and it is positive definite at $x_0$, because the coefficients $\lambda_\alpha(x_0)$ of the invertible matrices $A_\alpha(x_0)$ add up to one; shrinking $\Delta$, we may assume that $\mathcal Q$ is positive definite on $\Delta$. In a chart $(z,t,v)$ over $\Delta$ we obtain
\[
 \rho\circ\pi=|t|^2h_E,\qquad h_E(z,t,v)=\tilde v^*\mathcal Q(z,t\tilde v)\,\tilde v,
\]
and $h_E$ is smooth and positive, since $\tilde v\neq0$.

Define a real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ on $Y$ by $\eta=\ddc\log(\rho\circ\pi)$ on $Y\setminus E$ and $\eta=\ddc\log h_E$ on each chart $(z,t,v)$ as above. These definitions agree. Off $E$ we have $\log(\rho\circ\pi)=\log|t|^2+\log h_E$, and $\log|t|^2$ is pluriharmonic there. On the overlap of two charts, the local equations $t$ and $t'$ of $E$ generate the same ideal $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$, so $t'=ut$ with $u$ holomorphic and nowhere zero; then $\log h_E-\log h'_E=\log|u|^2$ for the corresponding functions $h_E$ and $h'_E$ off $E$; both sides are continuous functions on the overlap and $E$ is nowhere dense, so the identity holds on the whole overlap, and $\log|u|^2$ is pluriharmonic. Thus $\eta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$. (It is the curvature, in the sense of \cref{def:quadratic-line-bundle-metric}, of the metric on the line bundle $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$ with the frame $1$ on $Y\setminus E$, the frames $t$ on the charts and the transition functions $t$ and $t'/t$, for which $|1|^2=1/(\rho\circ\pi)$ and $|t|^2=1/h_E$.)

We show that $\eta$ is positive definite along the fibers of $E\to W$. Fix a point $(z_0,0,v_0)$ of $E$ in a chart, and put $\mathcal Q_0=\mathcal Q(z_0,0)$. On the fiber $\{z=z_0,\ t=0\}$ we have $h_E=\tilde v^*\mathcal Q_0\tilde v$. Write $\mathcal Q_0=\Gamma^*\Gamma$ with an invertible matrix $\Gamma$, for instance the positive square root of $\mathcal Q_0$, and let $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb C^r$. Then $h_E=|F(v)|^2$ on the fiber, where $F(v)=\Gamma\tilde v=\Gamma e_i+\sum_{j\neq i}v_j\Gamma e_j$ is an affine map whose linear part $\Lambda\xi=\sum_{j\neq i}\xi_j\Gamma e_j$ is injective, and $F(v)$ never lies in the image of $\Lambda$, because the columns $\Gamma e_1,\ldots,\Gamma e_r$ are linearly independent. Writing $\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_ka_k\bar b_k$ on $\mathbb C^r$, we have $\partial_{\bar v_l}|F|^2=\langle F,\Gamma e_l\rangle$ and $\partial_{v_k}|F|^2=\langle\Gamma e_k,F\rangle$, so for $\xi\in\mathbb C^{r-1}$
\[
 \sum_{k,l\neq i}\frac{\partial^2\log|F|^2}{\partial v_k\partial\bar v_l}\,\xi_k\bar\xi_l
 =\frac{|\Lambda\xi|^2}{|F|^2}-\frac{|\langle\Lambda\xi,F\rangle|^2}{|F|^4}
 =\frac{|F|^2|\Lambda\xi|^2-|\langle\Lambda\xi,F\rangle|^2}{|F|^4}.
\]
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality this is nonnegative, and it vanishes only if $\Lambda\xi=\mu F$ for some $\mu\in\mathbb C$. Since $F$ does not lie in the image of $\Lambda$, this forces $\mu=0$, so $\Lambda\xi=0$ and $\xi=0$. By the matrix criterion, the restriction of $\eta$ to each fiber of $E\to W$ is positive definite.

\emph{Step 2: positivity near $E$.} The form $P=\pi^*\omega$ is a smooth closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $Y$. We regard the real $(1,1)$-forms $P$ and $Q=\eta$ in a chart $(z,t,v)$ as Hermitian forms on holomorphic tangent vectors, given by their coefficient matrices, so that $P(\tau,\tau)=\omega(\mathrm d\pi\,\tau,\mathrm d\pi\,\tau)$ by the chain rule; norms of tangent vectors are taken in the coordinates of the chart. Let $V$ be spanned by the vectors $\partial/\partial v_k$ and $H$ by the vectors $\partial/\partial z_l$ and $\partial/\partial t$, and write $P$ and $Q$ in block form with respect to the decomposition of the tangent space into $V$ and $H$. At points of $E$ we have $\mathrm d\pi(\partial/\partial v_k)=t\,\partial/\partial w_k=0$, while $\mathrm d\pi(\partial/\partial z_l)=\partial/\partial z_l$ and $\mathrm d\pi(\partial/\partial t)=\sum_j\tilde v_j\,\partial/\partial w_j$ are linearly independent. Since $\omega$ is positive definite, $P_{HV}=0$ and $P_{HH}$ is positive definite at points of $E$, while $Q_{VV}$ is positive definite there by Step 1.

Choose finitely many compact sets $K_\iota$ whose interiors cover $E$, each contained in a chart $(z,t,v)$. The smallest eigenvalues of $P_{HH}$ and of $Q_{VV}$ are continuous and positive on the compact sets $E\cap K_\iota$, so there are $a,c>0$ such that they exceed $2c$ and $2a$, respectively, on $E\cap K_\iota$ for every $\iota$; let $M>0$ bound the operator norm of $Q$ on every $K_\iota$, so that $|Q(\tau,\tau')|\leq M|\tau||\tau'|$; and choose $\ell\in(0,1]$ with $2M\ell+M\ell^2\leq a/2$. Where $P_{HH}$ is invertible, the linear map $L=P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV}\colon V\to H$ depends continuously on the point, and it vanishes on $E$. Hence every point of $E\cap K_\iota$ has a neighborhood in which $P_{HH}\geq c$, $Q_{VV}\geq a$ and $\|L\|\leq\ell$ on $K_\iota$; let $O_\iota$ be the union of these neighborhoods. Then
\[
 \mathcal N=\Int\Bigl(\bigcup_\iota K_\iota\Bigr)\cap\bigcap_\iota\bigl(O_\iota\cup(Y\setminus K_\iota)\bigr)
\]
is an open neighborhood of $E$ with $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota\subset O_\iota$ for every $\iota$. On $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota$ we have, for $\xi\in V$,
\[
 Q(\xi-L\xi,\xi-L\xi)\geq\bigl(a-2M\|L\|-M\|L\|^2\bigr)|\xi|^2\geq\frac a2|\xi|^2,\qquad|\xi-L\xi|\leq2|\xi|.
\]
Write a tangent vector at a point of $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota$ as $\tau=\xi+\zeta$ with $\xi\in V$ and $\zeta\in H$, and put $\sigma=\zeta+L\xi\in H$. Completing the square,
\[
 P(\tau,\tau)=P_{HH}(\sigma,\sigma)+S(\xi,\xi),\qquad S=P_{VV}-P_{VH}P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV},
\]
and $S\geq0$ because $P\geq0$ (take $\zeta=-L\xi$, so that $\sigma=0$). Hence $P(\tau,\tau)\geq c|\sigma|^2$. Since $\tau=(\xi-L\xi)+\sigma$,
\[
 Q(\tau,\tau)\geq\frac a2|\xi|^2-4M|\xi||\sigma|-M|\sigma|^2.
\]
Therefore, for $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 (P+\varepsilon Q)(\tau,\tau)\geq(c-\varepsilon M)|\sigma|^2+\frac{\varepsilon a}2|\xi|^2-4\varepsilon M|\xi||\sigma|.
\]
The right-hand side is a quadratic form in $(|\sigma|,|\xi|)$, which is positive definite as soon as $\varepsilon M<c$ and $(c-\varepsilon M)a>8\varepsilon M^2$; and $(\sigma,\xi)\neq0$ for $\tau\neq0$. Thus $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is positive definite on the fixed neighborhood $\mathcal N$ of $E$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.

\emph{Step 3: positivity away from $E$.} On the compact set $Y\setminus\mathcal N\subset Y\setminus E$ the map $\pi$ is a local biholomorphism, so $\pi^*\omega$ is positive definite at every point. Cover $Y\setminus\mathcal N$ by finitely many compact sets contained in charts. On each of them the smallest eigenvalue of the coefficient matrix of $\pi^*\omega$ is bounded below by a positive constant $c'$, and the operator norm of the coefficient matrix of $\eta$ is bounded by a constant $M'$, with $c'$ and $M'$ independent of the set. Hence $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is positive definite on $Y\setminus\mathcal N$ if $\varepsilon M'<c'$. Decreasing $\varepsilon$ accordingly, $\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon\eta$ is positive definite on all of $Y$. It is closed, since both terms are, so it is a K\"ahler form on $Y$.
\end{proof}
\index{blowup!K\"ahler form}

\begin{definition}\label{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold. A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ on $X$ has \emph{gentle analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$} if it has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, the function $\exp(\varphi/c)$ is smooth on $X$, including at its zeros, and on every log resolution $\pi$ of $\mathfrak a$, with $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O(-D)$, one has locally
\[
 \varphi\circ\pi=c\log|s_D|^2+h,\qquad h\text{ smooth}.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{analytic singularities!gentle}

The log resolutions in this definition are compact K\"ahler manifolds by \cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup}. The smoothness of $\exp(\varphi/c)$ on $X$ and that of the remainder on the log resolutions are separate requirements. Requiring the local form on every log resolution is a priori stronger than requiring it on one; we need the stronger form because later proofs use it on a resolution chosen in the course of the argument. Both requirements hold if $\varphi$ has presentations \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities} of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ with smooth remainders $b$, since then $\exp(\varphi/c)=\mathrm e^{b/c}\sum_i|g_i|^2$ locally and \cref{prop:rational-analytic-operations} applies; they also hold for the cutoff sums of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}.

\section{Local Bergman kernels and ideal generators}
\label{sec:local-bergman-approximation}

In this section $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, is a bounded pseudoconvex open set, $u\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and integrals are taken with respect to Lebesgue measure unless another measure is specified. For an integer $m\geq1$ we put
\[
 H_m=\Bigl\{F\in\mathcal O(\Omega):\|F\|_m^2
                 =\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty\Bigr\},\qquad
 K_m(z)=\sup_{\|F\|_m\leq1}|F(z)|^2.
\]
Thus $H_m$ is the space $\mathcal H_{mu}(\Omega)$ of \cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence}, now on a bounded pseudoconvex set, and the function $K_m$ is the \emph{Bergman kernel} of $H_m$ on the diagonal.

We first show that $H_m$ is a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions that generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$, with bounded coefficients near each point. In \cref{chap:q05} this was proved on polydiscs on which the weight is bounded above (\cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence,lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation}). We prove it on bounded pseudoconvex open sets, for an arbitrary weight and for a measure comparable with Lebesgue measure; the measure is needed in \cref{sec:quantitative-regularization}, where the kernels are computed with the volume form of a K\"ahler form.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, be a bounded pseudoconvex open set, let $\psi\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and let $\varrho$ be a measurable function on $\Omega$ with $c_\varrho\leq\varrho\leq C_\varrho$ for positive constants $c_\varrho$ and $C_\varrho$. Put
\[
 \mathcal H=\Bigl\{F\in\mathcal O(\Omega):\|F\|^2=\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}\varrho\,\mathrm dV<\infty\Bigr\},\qquad\langle F,G\rangle=\int_\Omega F\bar G\,\mathrm e^{-\psi}\varrho\,\mathrm dV.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every compact set $K\subset\Omega$ there is a constant $C_K$ such that $|F(z)|\leq C_K\|F\|$ for all $F\in\mathcal H$ and $z\in K$. The space $\mathcal H$ is a separable Hilbert space, and it has an orthonormal basis, that is, an orthonormal family $(e_\ell)$, finite or countable, whose linear span is dense. For every such family and every $F\in\mathcal H$, the partial sums of $\sum_\ell\langle F,e_\ell\rangle e_\ell$ converge to $F$ in norm and locally uniformly on $\Omega$, and $\sum_\ell|\langle F,e_\ell\rangle|^2=\|F\|^2$.
\item The germs of the elements of $\mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi)$ at every point of $\Omega$, and near each point finitely many elements of $\mathcal H$ already generate $\mathcal I(\psi)$.
\item Let $x\in\Omega$, and let $g_1,\ldots,g_s\in\mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi)$ on a neighborhood of $x$. Then there are a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ and a constant $C$ such that every $F\in\mathcal H$ can be written as $F=\sum_ig_ih_i$ on $V$ with $h_i\in\mathcal O(V)$ and $\sup_V|h_i|\leq C\|F\|$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Let $K\subset\Omega$ be compact, let $r>0$ be half the distance from $K$ to $\mathbb C^n\setminus\Omega$, and let $M$ be the maximum of the upper semicontinuous function $\psi$ on the compact set $K_r=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,K)\leq r\}\subset\Omega$. For $z\in K$ and $F\in\mathcal H$, the mean-value property of the harmonic function $F$ on $B(z,r)$ and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give
\[
 |F(z)|^2\leq\frac1{|B(z,r)|}\int_{B(z,r)}|F|^2\,\mathrm dV\leq\frac{\mathrm e^M}{c_\varrho|B(z,r)|}\|F\|^2,
\]
which is the evaluation bound. Consequently a Cauchy sequence $(F_k)$ in $\mathcal H$ converges locally uniformly on $\Omega$, and its limit $F$ is holomorphic, by Cauchy's integral formula on polydiscs. The sequence also converges to some $G$ in the complete space $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\psi}\varrho\,\mathrm dV)$, and a subsequence converges to $G$ almost everywhere; hence $F=G$ almost everywhere, $F\in\mathcal H$, and $F_k\to F$ in $\mathcal H$. Thus $\mathcal H$ is a Hilbert space. Multiplication by $\mathrm e^{-\psi/2}\varrho^{1/2}$ maps $L^2(\Omega,\mathrm e^{-\psi}\varrho\,\mathrm dV)$ isometrically into $L^2(\Omega)$. The space $L^2(\Omega)$ is separable: by \cref{lem:mollifier-density}(5), $C_c(\Omega)$ is dense in it, and each $g\in C_c(\Omega)$, being uniformly continuous with compact support, is the uniform limit, with supports in a fixed compact subset of $\Omega$, of step functions that take values in $\mathbb Q+\mathrm i\mathbb Q$ on finitely many half-open cubes with dyadic rational vertices contained in $\Omega$ and vanish elsewhere; these step functions form a countable set. Since a subset of a separable metric space is separable, $\mathcal H$ is separable. Applying the Gram--Schmidt process to a countable dense subset of $\mathcal H$, and discarding the vectors that depend linearly on the previous ones, we obtain an orthonormal basis. Now let $(e_\ell)$ be any orthonormal basis, let $F\in\mathcal H$, and put $c_\ell=\langle F,e_\ell\rangle$ and $S_N=\sum_{\ell\leq N}c_\ell e_\ell$. Orthonormality gives
\[
 \|F-S_N\|^2=\|F\|^2-\sum_{\ell\leq N}|c_\ell|^2,\qquad\|S_N-S_{N'}\|^2=\sum_{N'<\ell\leq N}|c_\ell|^2\quad(N'<N).
\]
Hence $\sum_\ell|c_\ell|^2\leq\|F\|^2$, and $(S_N)$ is a Cauchy sequence; let $S$ be its limit. Then $F-S$ is orthogonal to every $e_\ell$, hence to their dense linear span, hence to itself, so $S=F$. The first identity now gives $\sum_\ell|c_\ell|^2=\|F\|^2$, and the evaluation bound shows that $S_N\to F$ locally uniformly.

(2) Let $(e_\ell)$ be an orthonormal basis, and let $\mathcal J$ be the ideal sheaf on $\Omega$ generated by the $e_\ell$. By \cref{lem:strong-analytic-noetherian}, $\mathcal J$ is coherent, and near each point it is generated by finitely many $e_\ell$. For $F\in\mathcal H$, the partial sums $S_N$ of (1) have their germs in $\mathcal J$ and converge locally uniformly to $F$, so the germs of $F$ lie in $\mathcal J$ by \cref{lem:analytic-krull-closure}. Thus $\mathcal J$ is the ideal sheaf generated by $\mathcal H$. It is contained in $\mathcal I(\psi)$, because $\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}\,\mathrm dV\leq c_\varrho^{-1}\|F\|^2$ for $F\in\mathcal H$.

Conversely, let $x\in\Omega$, $f\in\mathcal I(\psi)_x$ and let $s\geq0$ be an integer; we construct $F\in\mathcal H$ with $f-F\in\mathfrak m_x^{s+1}$, where $\mathfrak m_x$ is the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{\Omega,x}$. Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$, equal to one near $x$, such that $f$ is holomorphic and $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}$ is integrable on a neighborhood of $\Supp\chi$. The $(0,1)$-form $\alpha=f\bar\partial\chi$, extended by zero, is smooth and $\bar\partial$-closed, and it vanishes near $x$. We apply \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} on $\Omega$ with the plurisubharmonic function $p=\psi+(n+s)\log|z-x|^2$ (\cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm,prop:psh-max-gluing}) and the smooth function $q=|z|^2$, whose complex Hessian matrix is the identity. Since $|z-x|^{-2(n+s)}$ and $|\bar\partial\chi|$ are bounded on the support of $\alpha$, we have $\int_\Omega|\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV\leq C\int_{\Supp\chi}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. Hence there is $u$ with $\bar\partial u=\alpha$ and
\[
 \int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi-|z|^2}|z-x|^{-2(n+s)}\,\mathrm dV<\infty.
\]
Near $x$ we have $\bar\partial u=0$, so $\Delta u=4\sum_j\partial_{z_j}(\partial u/\partial\bar z_j)=0$ there; by \cref{thm:weyl}, $u$ is smooth near $x$, hence holomorphic. Since $\psi$ is bounded above near $x$, the last display gives $\int_{B(x,r)}|u|^2|z-x|^{-2(n+s)}\,\mathrm dV<\infty$ for small $r>0$. Hence $u$ vanishes to order at least $s+1$ at $x$: otherwise the germ of $u$ at $x$ is nonzero and its first nonzero homogeneous term has some degree $k_0\leq s$, and \eqref{eq:sphere-lowest-term}, integrated in polar coordinates \eqref{eq:polar-coordinates}, gives $\int_{B(x,r)}|u|^2|z-x|^{-2(n+s)}\,\mathrm dV\geq c\int_0^rt^{2k_0-2s-1}\,\mathrm dt=\infty$ for small $r>0$, with a constant $c>0$. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $\mathrm e^{-|z|^2}|z-x|^{-2(n+s)}$ is bounded below on $\Omega$ by a positive constant; as $\varrho\leq C_\varrho$, it follows that $\int_\Omega|u|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}\varrho\,\mathrm dV<\infty$. The function $F=\chi f-u$ satisfies $\bar\partial F=0$ on $\Omega$, so it is holomorphic by \cref{thm:weyl} as before, and $F\in\mathcal H$. Near $x$ we have $f-F=u\in\mathfrak m_x^{s+1}$. Thus $f\in\mathcal J_x+\mathfrak m_x^{s+1}$ for every $s$, and \cref{lem:analytic-krull-closure} gives $f\in\mathcal J_x$. Hence $\mathcal J=\mathcal I(\psi)$, which proves (2).

(3) The proof of the first assertion of \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation} uses, about the space $\mathcal H_\varphi(\Omega)$ there, only that it is a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on $\Omega$ in which convergence in norm implies locally uniform convergence, and that the germ at $x$ of each of its elements lies in the ideal generated near $x$ by $g_1,\ldots,g_s$. By (1) and (2) these properties hold for $\mathcal H$, so that argument applies verbatim.
\end{proof}
\index{Bergman kernel!weighted space on a pseudoconvex set}\index{multiplier ideal!generation on pseudoconvex sets}

By \cref{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}, applied with $\psi=mu$ and $\varrho=1$, the space $H_m$ is a Hilbert space on which evaluation at each point is a bounded linear functional; in particular $K_m$ is finite. Let $(e_\ell)$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_m$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}
 K_m=\sum_\ell|e_\ell|^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_m$ and put $c_\ell=\langle F,e_\ell\rangle$. By \cref{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}(1), $F(z)=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_\ell(z)$ and $\sum_\ell|c_\ell|^2=\|F\|_m^2\leq1$, so the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|F(z)|^2\leq\sum_\ell|e_\ell(z)|^2$. Conversely, for each $N$ the function $F_N=\sum_{\ell\leq N}\overline{e_\ell(z)}e_\ell$ satisfies $F_N(z)=\|F_N\|_m^2=\sum_{\ell\leq N}|e_\ell(z)|^2$; if this number is positive, then $F_N/\|F_N\|_m$ lies in the unit ball, and $\sum_{\ell\leq N}|e_\ell(z)|^2=|F_N(z)|^2/\|F_N\|_m^2\leq K_m(z)$. The same argument applies to the kernel of the space defined with $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$ in place of Lebesgue measure, for an orthonormal basis of that space.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}
The function $K_m$ is real analytic on $\Omega$. Near each point of $\Omega$ it is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $\sum_i|g_i|^2$, where $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ are finitely many generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$ near that point. If $\pi\colon Y\to\Omega$ is a holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$ such that $\pi^*\mathcal I(mu)=\mathcal O_Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ on $Y$, for instance a log resolution of $\mathcal I(mu)$, then locally on $Y$
\[
 K_m\circ\pi=|s_D|^2b_m,\qquad b_m\text{ smooth and strictly positive},
\]
where $s_D$ is a local equation of $D$. The same conclusions hold if Lebesgue measure is replaced, in the definition of $H_m$, by $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$, where $\varrho$ is a measurable function on $\Omega$ with $c_\varrho\leq\varrho\leq C_\varrho$ for positive constants $c_\varrho$ and $C_\varrho$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the proof for the measure $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$; Lebesgue measure is the case $\varrho=1$. By \cref{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}, applied with $\psi=mu$, the space $H_m$ is a Hilbert space with an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell)$, evaluation is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $\Omega$, and \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis} holds.

We show that the map $z\mapsto(e_\ell(z))_\ell$ into $\ell^2$ is given locally by a convergent power series with coefficients in $\ell^2$. Fix a point $z_0$ and a polydisc $P$ of multiradius $\rho$ about $z_0$ with $\overline P\subset\Omega$, and let $C$ be the evaluation bound on $\overline P$. For a finitely supported sequence $(c_\ell)$ with $\sum_\ell|c_\ell|^2\leq1$, the function $F=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_\ell$ has norm at most one, so $|F|\leq C$ on $P$, and the Cauchy estimates give $|\partial^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha!|\leq C\rho^{-\alpha}$. Since $\partial^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha!=\sum_\ell c_\ell A_{\alpha,\ell}$ with $A_{\alpha,\ell}=\partial^\alpha e_\ell(z_0)/\alpha!$, taking the supremum over such sequences shows that the sequence $A_\alpha=(A_{\alpha,\ell})_\ell$ has $\ell^2$ norm $\|A_\alpha\|\leq C\rho^{-\alpha}$. Let $0<\theta<1$, and let $P_\theta$ be the concentric polydisc of multiradius $\theta\rho$. For $z\in P_\theta$ we have $\sum_\alpha\|A_\alpha\||(z-z_0)^\alpha|\leq C\sum_\alpha\theta^{|\alpha|}<\infty$, so the $\ell^2$-valued series $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha(z-z_0)^\alpha$ converges absolutely, and its $\ell$th component is the Taylor series of $e_\ell$, which converges to $e_\ell(z)$. By \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis},
\[
 K_m(z)=\Bigl\|\sum_\alpha A_\alpha(z-z_0)^\alpha\Bigr\|^2=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\langle A_\alpha,A_\beta\rangle(z-z_0)^\alpha\overline{(z-z_0)^\beta},\qquad z\in P_\theta,
\]
where $|\langle A_\alpha,A_\beta\rangle|\leq C^2\rho^{-\alpha-\beta}$. Hence this double power series, and each series obtained from it by differentiating term by term, converge uniformly on $P_{\theta'}$ for every $\theta'<\theta$, since a derivative of order $k$ only introduces factors bounded by a polynomial of degree $k$ in $|\alpha|+|\beta|$. Therefore $K_m$ is real analytic, in particular smooth.

Next choose members $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ of $H_m$ generating $\mathcal I(mu)$ near a given point, by \cref{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}(2); by the remark following \cref{def:rational-analytic-singularities}, any other finite system of generators gives a comparable sum of squares. By the definition of $K_m$, $|g_i|^2\leq\|g_i\|_m^2K_m$, so $\sum_i|g_i|^2\leq CK_m$. Conversely, \cref{lem:pseudoconvex-weighted-bergman-space}(3) gives, on a fixed smaller neighborhood, a representation $F=\sum_ig_ih_i$ with $|h_i|\leq C'$ for every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_m$. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|F|^2\leq sC'^2\sum_i|g_i|^2$, and hence $K_m\leq sC'^2\sum_i|g_i|^2$ there. These constants may depend on $m$, $u$ and $\varrho$.

Let $\pi$ and $D$ be as in the statement, let $y\in Y$, let $V$ be a neighborhood of $\pi(y)$ on which the bounded representation of the previous paragraph holds, and let $s_D$ be a local equation of $D$ on a neighborhood $W\subset\pi^{-1}(V)$ of $y$. Since $\pi^*\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $s_D$ on $W$, we may write $g_i\circ\pi=s_Dk_i$ and $s_D=\sum_il_i\,(g_i\circ\pi)$ with holomorphic $k_i$ and $l_i$, after shrinking $W$; since $s_D$ does not vanish identically on any open set, $\sum_il_ik_i=1$. For $F\in H_m$, the representation $F=\sum_ig_ih_i$ on $V$ gives $F\circ\pi=s_D\sum_i(h_i\circ\pi)k_i$ on $W$, so
\[
 \frac{F\circ\pi}{s_D}=\sum_i(h_i\circ\pi)\,k_i,\qquad\Bigl|\frac{F\circ\pi}{s_D}\Bigr|\leq C'\|F\|_m\sum_i|k_i|.
\]
Hence evaluation of $(F\circ\pi)/s_D$ is bounded on each compact subset of $W$, uniformly for $F$ in the unit ball of $H_m$. The power-series argument above therefore applies, in local coordinates on $Y$, to the holomorphic functions $(e_\ell\circ\pi)/s_D$, and shows that $b_m=\sum_\ell|(e_\ell\circ\pi)/s_D|^2$ is smooth; by \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}, $K_m\circ\pi=|s_D|^2b_m$. To see that $b_m$ is strictly positive, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_m$, with coefficients $c_\ell=\langle F,e_\ell\rangle$ and partial sums $S_N$. The estimate in the last display, applied to $F-S_N$, shows that $(S_N\circ\pi)/s_D\to(F\circ\pi)/s_D$ pointwise on $W$; hence $(F\circ\pi)/s_D=\sum_\ell c_\ell(e_\ell\circ\pi)/s_D$, and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $|(F\circ\pi)/s_D|^2\leq b_m$. Taking $F=g_i/\|g_i\|_m$ for the indices with $g_i\neq0$, we obtain $b_m\geq|k_i|^2/\|g_i\|_m^2$, and the functions $k_i$ have no common zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-bergman-approximation}
Let $\Omega$ have diameter at most $L$, and put $u_m=m^{-1}\log K_m$. Then $u_m$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ and has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$. There are constants $C_1,C_2>0$, depending only on $n$ and $L$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-bergman-bounds}
 u(z)-\frac{\log C_1}{m}\leq u_m(z)
 \leq\sup_{B(z,r)}u+\frac1m\log\frac{C_2}{r^{2n}}
\end{equation}
for all $z\in\Omega$ and $r>0$ with $B(z,r)\Subset\Omega$. As $m\to\infty$, $u_m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1_{\loc}(\Omega)$. For every $z\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-bergman-lelong}
 \nu(u,z)-\frac nm\leq\nu(u_m,z)\leq\nu(u,z).
\end{equation}
Finally, for each fixed $m$, the function $\exp(m(u_m-u))$ is locally integrable on $\Omega$. All these assertions remain true if Lebesgue measure is replaced, in the definition of $H_m$, by $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$, where $\varrho$ is a measurable function on $\Omega$ with $c_\varrho\leq\varrho\leq C_\varrho$ for positive constants $c_\varrho$ and $C_\varrho$, provided $C_1$ and $C_2$ are replaced by $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Omega_0$ be a connected component of $\Omega$; it is a bounded pseudoconvex domain of diameter at most $L$, since the restriction of a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function of $\Omega$ to the closed subset $\Omega_0$ is one. Choose $a\in\Omega_0$ with $u(a)>-\infty$. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied on $\Omega_0$, there is a holomorphic function on $\Omega_0$ with value $1$ at $a$ and finite weighted norm; extended by zero to the other components of $\Omega$, it is an element of $H_m$ that does not vanish identically on $\Omega_0$. For every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_m$ that is not identically zero on $\Omega_0$, $\log|F|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega_0$ by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}, and $\log K_m$ is the supremum of these functions on $\Omega_0$. Since $K_m$ is continuous by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, this family is locally uniformly bounded above, and $\log K_m$ is upper semicontinuous with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$, so it equals its upper semicontinuous regularization. Hence \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} shows that $u_m$ is plurisubharmonic. The comparison in \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution} gives $u_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ locally, with generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$; this is the asserted type.

For the lower bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}, let $u(z)>-\infty$; at points of the pole set of $u$ there is nothing to prove. By \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound}, applied on the component of $\Omega$ containing $z$, and extension by zero, there is $F\in H_m$ with $F(z)=1$ and $\|F\|_m^2\leq C_1\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}$, where $C_1$ depends only on $n$ and $L$. Hence $K_m(z)\geq|F(z)|^2/\|F\|_m^2\geq C_1^{-1}\mathrm e^{mu(z)}$. For the upper bound, let $\|F\|_m\leq1$. The mean-value inequality for $|F|^2$ on $B(z,r)$ gives
\begin{align*}
 |F(z)|^2&\leq C_2r^{-2n}\int_{B(z,r)}|F|^2\\
       &\leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp\Bigl(m\sup_{B(z,r)}u\Bigr)\int_{B(z,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}
       \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp\Bigl(m\sup_{B(z,r)}u\Bigr),
\end{align*}
with $C_2=n!/\pi^n$, and taking the supremum over $F$ proves the upper bound.

Now fix $z$ and $r$, and let $m\to\infty$ in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}: we obtain $u(z)\leq\liminf_mu_m(z)\leq\limsup_mu_m(z)\leq\sup_{B(z,r)}u$. Letting $r\downarrow0$ and using the upper semicontinuity of $u$ gives $u_m(z)\to u(z)$, also at points of the pole set. On a compact set $K\subset\Omega$, a fixed $r<\operatorname{dist}(K,\partial\Omega)$ in the upper bound gives a uniform upper bound for all $u_m$, while $u_m\geq u-|\log C_1|$. Since $u\in L^1(K)$, dominated convergence gives $u_m\to u$ in $L^1(K)$.

For the Lelong numbers, let $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ generate $\mathcal I(mu)$ near $z$. We may discard the $g_i$ that vanish identically near $z$; at least one generator remains, because the germ at $z$ of the element of $H_m$ constructed in the first paragraph for the component containing $z$ is a nonzero element of $\mathcal I(mu)_z$. Since $\log\sum_i|g_i|^2-\max_i\log|g_i|^2$ is bounded, \cref{prop:lelong-logarithmic-growth,prop:lelong-calculus} give $\nu(\log\sum_i|g_i|^2,z)=\nu(\max_i\log|g_i|^2,z)=\min_i\operatorname{ord}_zg_i=\operatorname{ord}_z\mathcal I(mu)$. Since Lelong numbers are invariant under bounded perturbations and scale linearly, $\nu(u_m,z)=m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_z\mathcal I(mu)$, and \eqref{eq:local-bergman-lelong} follows from \cref{lem:multiplier-order-lelong-bounds}. (The upper bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-lelong} also follows directly from $u\leq u_m+|\log C_1|$: since $u_m\leq\nu(u_m,z)\log|\zeta-z|^2+O(1)$ near $z$ by \eqref{eq:lelong-attained-growth}, the same holds for $u$, and the characterization of $\nu(u,z)$ as the largest such coefficient in \cref{prop:lelong-logarithmic-growth} gives $\nu(u_m,z)\leq\nu(u,z)$.)

Finally, near each point $K_m\leq C\sum_i|g_i|^2$ with $g_i\in H_m$, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, and each $|g_i|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is integrable on $\Omega$. Hence $\exp(m(u_m-u))=K_m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable.

Now replace Lebesgue measure by $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$, and let $K^\varrho_m$ be the corresponding kernel. The space of holomorphic functions is the same, and the two norms satisfy $c_\varrho\|F\|_m^2\leq\|F\|_{m,\varrho}^2\leq C_\varrho\|F\|_m^2$. If $\|F\|_m\leq1$, then $F/C_\varrho^{1/2}$ lies in the unit ball for $\varrho$, and if $\|F\|_{m,\varrho}\leq1$, then $c_\varrho^{1/2}F$ lies in the unit ball for Lebesgue measure; hence
\[
 C_\varrho^{-1}K_m\leq K^\varrho_m\leq c_\varrho^{-1}K_m.
\]
The proof of plurisubharmonicity applies verbatim to $m^{-1}\log K^\varrho_m$, the unit ball now being taken for $\varrho$ and the continuity of $K^\varrho_m$ being given by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}; the lower and upper bounds follow from the last display with the constants $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho$; the convergence and the Lelong numbers then follow as before, since \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution} applies to $K^\varrho_m$; and $K^\varrho_m\mathrm e^{-mu}\leq c_\varrho^{-1}K_m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!local Bergman kernel approximation}

The kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ are compared by extending a function from the diagonal of $\Omega\times\Omega$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-diagonal-comparison}
Suppose that the spaces $H_m$ are defined with respect to $\varrho\,\mathrm dV$, where $\varrho$ is a measurable function on $\Omega$ with $c_\varrho\leq\varrho\leq C_\varrho$ for positive constants $c_\varrho$ and $C_\varrho$; for instance $\varrho=1$. There is a constant $C_\Omega>0$, depending only on $n$, on a bound $L$ for the diameter of $\Omega$ and on $c_\varrho$ and $C_\varrho$, and in particular independent of $m$ and $u$, such that
\[
 K_{2m}\leq C_\Omega K_m^2\qquad\text{on }\Omega.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We write $\|\cdot\|_m$ for the norm of $H_m$. By the definition of $K_m$, every $h\in H_m$ satisfies $|h(\zeta)|^2\leq\|h\|_m^2K_m(\zeta)$ for all $\zeta\in\Omega$; if $\|h\|_m=0$, then $h$ vanishes almost everywhere, hence identically.

Each connected component $\Omega_0$ of $\Omega$ is pseudoconvex, as noted at the beginning of the proof of \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}. By \cref{cor:pseudoconvex-sublevels-products}, $\Omega_0\times\Omega_0$ is a bounded pseudoconvex domain in $\mathbb C^{2n}$, of diameter at most $\sqrt2L$, and the function $p(z,w)=mu(z)+mu(w)$ is plurisubharmonic on it (\cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback,prop:psh-max-gluing}). Let $F\in H_{2m}$. The diagonal $\Lambda=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb C^{2n}:z=w\}$ is a complex linear subspace of codimension $n$, and the map $z\mapsto(z,z)$ multiplies Euclidean lengths by $\sqrt2$, so Lebesgue measure on $\Lambda$ corresponds to $2^n$ times Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb C^n$. On $\Lambda\cap(\Omega\times\Omega)$, identified with $\Omega$, the weight $p$ restricts to $2mu$, so
\[
 \int_{\Lambda\cap(\Omega\times\Omega)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{2n}=2^n\int_\Omega|F|^2\mathrm e^{-2mu}\,\mathrm dV\leq2^nc_\varrho^{-1}\|F\|_{2m}^2.
\]
The set $\Lambda$ meets only the components of $\Omega\times\Omega$ of the form $\Omega_0\times\Omega_0$. Applying the extension statement of \cref{cor:ot-point-value-bound} on each of them, and putting $G=0$ on the other components, we obtain $G\in\mathcal O(\Omega\times\Omega)$ with $G(z,z)=F(z)$ and $\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|G|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\,\mathrm dV_{4n}\leq C^n(\sqrt2L)^{2n}2^nc_\varrho^{-1}\|F\|_{2m}^2$, where $C$ is the constant of \cref{thm:local-ot-extension}. Hence
\[
 \|G\|^2=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)-mu(w)}\varrho(z)\varrho(w)\,\mathrm dV(z)\,\mathrm dV(w)\leq C_\Omega\|F\|_{2m}^2
\]
with $C_\Omega=4^nC^nL^{2n}C_\varrho^2/c_\varrho$.

We now bound $G$ on the diagonal by $K_m$. For $w\in\Omega$ put
\[
 N(w)=\int_\Omega|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}\varrho(z)\,\mathrm dV(z)\in[0,\infty].
\]
By Tonelli's theorem, $N$ is measurable and $\int_\Omega N(w)\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho(w)\,\mathrm dV(w)=\|G\|^2<\infty$; since $\mathrm e^{-mu}\varrho>0$ almost everywhere, $N(w)<\infty$ for almost every $w$. For such $w$, the function $G(\cdot,w)$ belongs to $H_m$ with $\|G(\cdot,w)\|_m^2=N(w)$, so $|G(z,w)|^2\leq N(w)K_m(z)$ for every $z$. Fix $z\in\Omega$. Integrating this inequality in $w$, we obtain
\[
 \int_\Omega|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho(w)\,\mathrm dV(w)\leq K_m(z)\|G\|^2,
\]
so $G(z,\cdot)\in H_m$ with $\|G(z,\cdot)\|_m^2\leq K_m(z)\|G\|^2$, and therefore $|G(z,w)|^2\leq K_m(z)K_m(w)\|G\|^2$ for every $w$. Taking $w=z$,
\[
 |F(z)|^2=|G(z,z)|^2\leq K_m(z)^2\|G\|^2\leq C_\Omega\|F\|_{2m}^2K_m(z)^2.
\]
Taking the supremum over the unit ball of $H_{2m}$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Overlap interpolation and quantitative patching}
\label{sec:quantitative-regularization}

From now on $(X,\omega)$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n$. If $n=0$, then $X$ is a finite set, every quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is real-valued and hence smooth, and every multiplier ideal is the unit ideal; the constant sequence $\Phi_j=\varphi$ then has all the approximation properties proved below. We therefore assume $n\geq1$.

We fix, once and for all, finitely many concentric coordinate balls
\[
 U_j\Subset U'_j\Subset B_j\Subset B_j^+,
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,J,\qquad X=\bigcup_jU_j,
\]
and smooth functions $\chi_j\colon X\to[0,1]$ with compact support in $U'_j$ and $\chi_j=1$ on $U_j$. We require in addition that whenever $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, the union $B_j^+\cup B_l^+$ is contained in a coordinate ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ whose closure lies in a coordinate chart. Such a choice is possible. We first fix a metric $d$ defining the topology of $X$. Choose finitely many charts $\kappa_k\colon V_k\to\mathbb C^n$, $1\leq k\leq N$, and compact sets $L_k\subset V_k$ whose interiors cover $X$, and, by \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}, smooth functions $\psi_k\colon X\to[0,1]$ with compact support in $V_k$ and $\psi_k=1$ on $L_k$. The map $\Psi=(\psi_k,\psi_k\kappa_k)_{1\leq k\leq N}$ from $X$ to $\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb C^{nN}$, where $\psi_k\kappa_k$ is extended by zero outside $V_k$, is continuous and injective: if $\Psi(x)=\Psi(y)$ and $x\in L_k$, then $\psi_k(y)=\psi_k(x)=1$, so $y\in V_k$ and $\kappa_k(y)=\kappa_k(x)$, whence $y=x$. A continuous injective map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image, so $d(x,y)=|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|$ is a metric defining the topology of $X$. Now cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate balls whose closures lie in coordinate charts, and let $\delta>0$ be a Lebesgue number of this cover for $d$, so that every subset of $X$ of $d$-diameter less than $\delta$ lies in one of these balls. About every point of $X$ choose concentric coordinate balls $U\Subset U'\Subset B\Subset B^+$, with $\overline{B^+}$ contained in a coordinate chart and of $d$-diameter less than $\delta/2$. By compactness finitely many of the balls $U$ cover $X$; these give the $U_j$, and \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes} gives the cutoff functions $\chi_j$. If $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, then $B_j^+\cup B_l^+$ is a union of two intersecting sets of diameter less than $\delta/2$, so it has diameter less than $\delta$ and lies in one ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ of the cover. Finally, by the comparison of real $(1,1)$-forms with $\omega$ in \cref{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}, there is a constant $C_\chi\geq0$ with $\ddc\chi_j\geq-C_\chi\omega$ for all $j$.

All the quantities attached to these choices are now fixed: the bounds for the densities of $\omega^n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure in the charts, the coordinate changes, the diameters of the balls, the distances from $\overline{U'_j}$ to $\partial B_j$, and the derivatives of the cutoffs. We say that a constant \emph{depends only on the fixed geometry} if it depends only on these data, hence only on $(X,\omega)$ once the choices have been made.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:maximum-cutoff-patching}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $a\geq0$, and let $v_j\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$ for $j=1,\ldots,J$ satisfy $v_j\leq v_l+a$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ for all $j,l$. Then the function
\[
 v(x)=\max_{j:\,x\in\overline{U'_j}}\bigl(v_j(x)+a\chi_j(x)\bigr)
\]
is quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$ and satisfies $\theta+\ddc v\geq-aC_\chi\omega$ and $v_j\leq v\leq v_j+2a$ on $U_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since the $U_i$ cover $X$, the maximum is taken over a nonempty set at every point. Fix $x_0\in X$ and choose $i$ with $x_0\in U_i$. Indices $j$ with $x_0\notin\overline{U'_j}$ do not occur near $x_0$. If $x_0\in\partial U'_j$, then $\chi_j=0$ near $x_0$, because $\chi_j$ has compact support in $U'_j$; so near $x_0$ the $j$th term, where it is defined, equals $v_j\leq v_i+a=v_i+a\chi_i$, since $\chi_i=1$ on the neighborhood $U_i$ of $x_0$. Such a term never exceeds the $i$th term and can be omitted. Hence on a small neighborhood of $x_0$, $v$ is the maximum of $v_j+a\chi_j$ over the fixed set of indices $j$ with $x_0\in U'_j$. Each of these functions satisfies $\theta+aC_\chi\omega+\ddc(v_j+a\chi_j)\geq0$. Adding a local potential of the closed form $\theta+aC_\chi\omega$ on a coordinate ball about $x_0$ (\cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}), which makes these functions plurisubharmonic by the remarks following \cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}, and applying \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, we obtain that $v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic near $x_0$ with $\theta+\ddc v\geq-aC_\chi\omega$.

On $U_j$ the $j$th term is $v_j+a\geq v_j$, and every term with index $l$ is at most $v_l+a\leq v_j+2a$. This proves the two bounds, also at points of the pole sets.
\end{proof}
\index{patching!maximum with cutoffs}

Now fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$. By \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball} we can choose smooth real functions $h_j$ on $B_j^+$ with $\ddc h_j=\theta$. Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and let $m\geq1$ be an integer. Then $\varphi+h_j$ is plurisubharmonic on $B_j$, and we let $K_{j,m}$ be the Bergman kernel of \cref{sec:local-bergman-approximation} on the ball $B_j$ for the weight $m(\varphi+h_j)$ and the volume form $\omega^n/n!$:
\[
 K_{j,m}(x)=\sup\Bigl\{|F(x)|^2:F\in\mathcal O(B_j),\ \int_{B_j}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi+h_j)}\frac{\omega^n}{n!}\leq1\Bigr\}.
\]
We put
\[
 a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mh_j}K_{j,m},\qquad
 v_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log a_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}-h_j.
\]
The closure of $B_j$ lies in a chart, so in the coordinates of $B_j$ the density of $\omega^n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure is bounded above and below by positive constants on $B_j$. Hence \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}, in the version with the measure $\varrho\,\mathrm dV=\omega^n/n!$, applies on $B_j$: $m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}$ is plurisubharmonic, and $v_{j,m}\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}
There is a constant $C_B\geq0$, depending only on the fixed geometry and in particular independent of $\theta$, $\varphi$ and $m$, such that
\[
 a_{j,m}\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{l,m}\qquad\text{on }\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}
\]
for all $j,l$. In particular $a_{j,m}$ and $a_{l,m}$ have the same zeros on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, and $|v_{j,m}-v_{l,m}|\leq C_B/m$ there outside these common zeros.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $j,l$ with $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}\neq\varnothing$, and work in the coordinates of the ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$. By \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball} choose a smooth function $g$ on $\mathcal B_{jl}$ with $\ddc g=\theta$. The functions $h_j-g$ and $h_l-g$ are pluriharmonic on the coordinate balls $B_j^+$ and $B_l^+$, so by the same lemma $h_j-g=\Real b_j$ and $h_l-g=\Real b_l$ with $b_j,b_l$ holomorphic on these balls (the constants are absorbed into $b_j,b_l$). Put $\Psi=\varphi+g$, a plurisubharmonic function on $\mathcal B_{jl}$. Since $|\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}|^2=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}$, multiplication by $\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}$ is an isometry from the weighted space defining $K_{j,m}$ onto the space of holomorphic functions on $B_j$ with weight $m\Psi$ and volume form $\omega^n/n!$. If $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}$ denotes the Bergman kernel of the latter space, then $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}K_{j,m}$, and therefore
\[
 a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{m\Psi}_{B_j},\qquad a_{l,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}.
\]
Hence it suffices to prove $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ with $C_0\geq1$ depending only on the fixed geometry.

Choose a smooth function $\rho\colon X\to[0,1]$ with compact support in $B_j\cap B_l$, equal to one on a neighborhood of the compact set $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}); this choice belongs to the fixed geometry. Let $r>0$ be the distance, in the coordinates of $\mathcal B_{jl}$, from $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$ to the support of $\bar\partial\rho$, and let $c>0$ and $C''$ be lower and upper bounds for the density of $\omega^n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure on $B_j\cup B_l$ in these coordinates. Let $f$ be holomorphic on $B_j$ with norm at most one in the weighted space, let $x\in\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, and extend $\rho f$ by zero to a smooth function on $B_l$. The ball $B_l$ is pseudoconvex also in the coordinates of $\mathcal B_{jl}$, since a biholomorphism transports a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function (\cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}). We apply \cref{thm:hormander-singular-lower-bound} on $B_l$ to the smooth $\bar\partial$-closed form $f\bar\partial\rho$, with the plurisubharmonic function $p=m\Psi+n\log|z-x|^2$ and the smooth function $q=|z|^2$, whose complex Hessian matrix is the identity. On the support of $\bar\partial\rho$ we have $|z-x|^{-2n}\leq r^{-2n}$, so
\[
 \int_{B_l}|f\bar\partial\rho|^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\,\mathrm dV\leq r^{-2n}\sup|\bar\partial\rho|^2\int_{B_j}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-m\Psi}\,\mathrm dV\leq\frac{\sup|\bar\partial\rho|^2}{cr^{2n}}.
\]
Hence there is a solution of $\bar\partial w=f\bar\partial\rho$ on $B_l$ with
\[
 \int_{B_l}|w|^2|z-x|^{-2n}\mathrm e^{-m\Psi-|z|^2}\,\mathrm dV\leq C=\frac{\sup|\bar\partial\rho|^2}{cr^{2n}},
\]
and $C$ depends only on the fixed geometry. Since $|z-x|^{2n}\mathrm e^{|z|^2}$ is bounded on the bounded set $B_l$ by a constant of the same kind, and the density of $\omega^n/n!$ is at most $C''$, the squared weighted norm of $w$ for the weight $m\Psi$ and the volume form $\omega^n/n!$ is at most a constant $C'$ depending only on the fixed geometry.

Near $x$ we have $\bar\partial w=f\bar\partial\rho=0$, because $\rho=1$ there; hence $\Delta w=0$ near $x$, and by \cref{thm:weyl} $w$ is smooth, hence holomorphic, near $x$. Suppose that $w(x)\neq0$. Then $|w|^2\geq|w(x)|^2/4$ on a ball $B(x,\delta)$ on which $\Psi\leq M$ for some constant $M$, since $\Psi$ is upper semicontinuous, and the last integral is at least
\[
 \frac{|w(x)|^2}4\,\mathrm e^{-mM-\sup_{B_l}|z|^2}\int_{B(x,\delta)}|z-x|^{-2n}\,\mathrm dV=\infty,
\]
because $\int_{B(x,\delta)}|z-x|^{-2n}\,\mathrm dV=s_{2n-1}\int_0^\delta t^{-1}\,\mathrm dt$ in polar coordinates. Hence $w(x)=0$. The function $F=\rho f-w$ satisfies $\bar\partial F=0$ on $B_l$, so it is holomorphic by \cref{thm:weyl}, and $F(x)=f(x)$. Since $0\leq\rho\leq1$ and $\rho$ vanishes outside $B_j$, the weighted norm of $F$ on $B_l$ is at most $1+C'^{1/2}=C_0$. By the definition of the kernel, $|f(x)|^2=|F(x)|^2\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}(x)$, and taking the supremum over $f$ gives $K^{m\Psi}_{B_j}(x)\leq C_0^2K^{m\Psi}_{B_l}(x)$.

No bound on $\Psi$, $\theta$ or $m$ enters these constants. Taking $C_B$ to be the maximum of $2\log C_0$ over the finitely many pairs $(j,l)$, with the roles of $j$ and $l$ also exchanged, proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\index{Bergman kernel!comparison on overlaps}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization}
There is a constant $c>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega)$ through the fixed geometry, with the following property. Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and let $m\geq1$ be an integer. Then there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi_m$ on $X$ such that
\[
 \theta+\ddc\varphi_m\geq-\frac cm\omega,
 \qquad
 \varphi_m\geq\frac1m\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)\quad\text{locally},
\]
where the $g_i$ are local generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. The bounded term may depend on $m$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ and the point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, the functions $v_{j,m}\in\PSH(B_j,\theta)$ satisfy $v_{j,m}\leq v_{l,m}+C_B/m$ on $\overline{U'_j}\cap\overline{U'_l}$, also at their common zeros. Hence \cref{lem:maximum-cutoff-patching} with $a=C_B/m$ gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi_m$ with $\theta+\ddc\varphi_m\geq-(C_BC_\chi/m)\omega$, so the first conclusion holds with $c=C_BC_\chi$, increased if necessary to make it positive. On $U_j$ the function $\varphi_m$ dominates $v_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}-h_j$. Since $h_j$ is smooth, $\mathcal I(m(\varphi+h_j))=\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, and the comparison of \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution} gives $v_{j,m}\geq m^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ near each point of $U_j$. This proves the second conclusion.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!quantitative lower bound}

\section{Smooth cutoff sums and decreasing approximation}
\label{sec:gentle-decreasing-approximation}

The maximum in \cref{thm:quantitative-lower-bound-regularization} destroys smoothness, and that theorem does not compare the functions $\varphi_m$ for different values of $m$. To obtain the regularity required of gentle singularities and, later, a decreasing sequence, we glue the local kernels instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs and control the curvature of its logarithm by a direct computation. We keep the notation of the previous section.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $m\geq1$ be an integer, and put
\[
 R_m=\sum_j\chi_j^2a_{j,m},\qquad G_m=m^{-1}\log R_m,
\]
each summand being extended by zero outside the support of $\chi_j$. There is a constant $c'>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that
$\theta+\ddc G_m\geq-(c'/m)\omega$. Moreover $R_m$ is smooth on $X$, and $G_m$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: a Hessian identity.} Let $w_j$ be smooth real functions and $\chi_j$ nonnegative smooth functions on an open set, with $S=\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}>0$, and put $\alpha_j=\chi_j\partial w_j+2\partial\chi_j$, so that $\partial S=\sum_j\chi_j\mathrm e^{w_j}\alpha_j$. Differentiating twice gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian}
\begin{aligned}
 \ddc\log S={}&\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi S^2}
 \sum_{j<l}\mathrm e^{w_j+w_l}
 (\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)
       \wedge\overline{(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)}\\
 &+\frac{\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}\ddc w_j}{S}
 +2\frac{\sum_j\mathrm e^{w_j}
  \bigl(\chi_j\ddc\chi_j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}
                         \partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\bigr)}{S}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, put $f_j=\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}$. Since $\chi_j$ and $w_j$ are real, $\bar\partial f_j=\overline{\partial f_j}=\chi_j\mathrm e^{w_j}\bar\alpha_j$ with $\bar\alpha_j=\chi_j\bar\partial w_j+2\bar\partial\chi_j$, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \partial\bar\partial f_j&=\partial(\chi_j\mathrm e^{w_j})\wedge\bar\alpha_j+\chi_j\mathrm e^{w_j}\,\partial\bar\alpha_j\\
 &=\mathrm e^{w_j}\bigl((\partial\chi_j+\chi_j\partial w_j)\wedge(\chi_j\bar\partial w_j+2\bar\partial\chi_j)\\
 &\qquad+\chi_j\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial w_j+\chi_j^2\partial\bar\partial w_j+2\chi_j\partial\bar\partial\chi_j\bigr)\\
 &=\mathrm e^{w_j}\bigl(\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j+\chi_j^2\partial\bar\partial w_j+2\chi_j\partial\bar\partial\chi_j-2\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\bigr),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last line follows by expanding $\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j=\chi_j^2\partial w_j\wedge\bar\partial w_j+2\chi_j\partial w_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j+2\chi_j\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial w_j+4\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j$. Next, $\partial\bar\partial\log S=\partial\bar\partial S/S-\partial S\wedge\bar\partial S/S^2$, and since $\sum_l\chi_l^2\mathrm e^{w_l}=S$,
\[
 \sum_j\frac{\mathrm e^{w_j}\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j}S-\frac{\partial S\wedge\bar\partial S}{S^2}
 =\frac1{S^2}\sum_{j,l}\mathrm e^{w_j+w_l}\bigl(\chi_l^2\,\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_j-\chi_j\chi_l\,\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_l\bigr).
\]
In the last sum the terms with $j=l$ vanish, and for $j<l$ the terms with indices $(j,l)$ and $(l,j)$ add up to $\mathrm e^{w_j+w_l}(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)\wedge\overline{(\chi_j\alpha_l-\chi_l\alpha_j)}$. Multiplying by $\mathrm i/2\pi$ and using $\ddc=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial$, we obtain \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian}. The first term on the right of \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian} is semipositive, because for a $(1,0)$-form $\gamma=\sum_k\gamma_k\,\mathrm dz_k$ the coefficient matrix $(\gamma_k\bar\gamma_{k'})$ of $\mathrm i\gamma\wedge\bar\gamma$ is positive semidefinite.

\emph{Step 2: a curvature estimate.} By the comparison of real $(1,1)$-forms with $\omega$ at the beginning of \cref{sec:analytic-singularity-definitions}, there is a constant $A>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that $\chi_j\ddc\chi_j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\chi_j\wedge\bar\partial\chi_j\geq-A\omega$ for every $j$. Let $W\subset X$ be open, and for the indices $j$ with $W\cap\Supp\chi_j\neq\varnothing$ let $w_j$ be smooth real functions on $W$ such that
\[
 \ddc w_j\geq-m\theta\quad\text{on }W,\qquad
 \mathrm e^{w_j}\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}\mathrm e^{w_i}\quad\text{on }W\cap U_i\cap\Supp\chi_j
\]
for all such $i,j$. We claim that $S=\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}$ is positive on $W$ and that
\[
 \theta+\frac1m\ddc\log S\geq-\frac{2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}}m\omega\qquad\text{on }W.
\]
Let $x\in W$ and choose $i$ with $x\in U_i$. Then $\chi_i(x)=1$, so $S(x)\geq\mathrm e^{w_i(x)}>0$, and $\mathrm e^{w_j}/S\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}$ at $x$ for every $j$ with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$. If $\chi_j(x)=0$, the $j$th summand of the last term in \eqref{eq:cutoff-sum-hessian} vanishes at $x$, because the nonnegative function $\chi_j$ attains its minimum at $x$, so that $\partial\chi_j(x)=0$. Hence the last term is at least $-2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}\omega$ at $x$. The coefficients $\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j}/S$ are nonnegative with sum one, so the middle term is at least $-m\theta$, and the first term is semipositive. This proves the claim.

\emph{Step 3: application to the kernels.} The kernels $a_{j,m}$ may vanish, so we approximate them. Fix $x_0\in X$ and a small coordinate ball $W$ about $x_0$ such that $W\subset U'_j$ whenever $x_0\in\Supp\chi_j$, and $W\cap\Supp\chi_j=\varnothing$ otherwise; then the indices $j$ with $W\cap\Supp\chi_j\neq\varnothing$ are those with $x_0\in\Supp\chi_j$. Choose a smooth function $g$ on $W$ with $\ddc g=\theta$, and for each such $j$ write $h_j-g=\Real b_j$ on $W$ with $b_j$ holomorphic, by \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}. As in the proof of \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, $a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}k_j$ on $W$, where, by \eqref{eq:bergman-kernel-basis}, $k_j=\mathrm e^{-m\Real b_j}K_{j,m}=\sum_\ell|\mathrm e^{-mb_j/2}e_\ell|^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell)$ of the space defining $K_{j,m}$. For $\delta>0$ the function $\log(k_j+\delta)$ is smooth, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, and plurisubharmonic: it is the increasing limit of the logarithms of the finite partial sums of these squared moduli together with $|\sqrt\delta|^2$, which are plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}, and since the limit is continuous, \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} applies. Put $w_j^\delta=\log(k_j+\delta)-mg$, so that $\ddc w_j^\delta\geq-m\theta$. On $W\cap U_i\cap\Supp\chi_j$, \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap} gives $k_j\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}k_i$, and since $\mathrm e^{C_B}\geq1$ also $k_j+\delta\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}(k_i+\delta)$. By Step 2 the function
\[
 \frac1m\log\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j^\delta}+g'
\]
is plurisubharmonic on $W$, where $g'$ is a smooth function on $W$ with $\ddc g'=\theta+(2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}/m)\omega$, given by \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}; indeed, this function is smooth with positive semidefinite complex Hessian. As $\delta\downarrow0$, the sums $\sum_j\chi_j^2\mathrm e^{w_j^\delta}=\mathrm e^{-mg}\sum_j\chi_j^2(k_j+\delta)$ decrease to $R_m$, so these functions decrease to $G_m+g'$, which is not identically $-\infty$, since $G_m\geq v_{i,m}$ on $U_i$. By \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}, $G_m+g'$ is plurisubharmonic on $W$. Hence $G_m$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, and the curvature bound holds with $c'=2JA\mathrm e^{C_B}$, which depends only on the fixed geometry.

\emph{Step 4: gentleness.} Each summand of $R_m$ is smooth by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}. On $U_i$ we have $a_{i,m}\leq R_m\leq J\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{i,m}$ by \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap}, and $a_{i,m}$ is comparable with $\sum_k|g_k|^2$ for local generators $g_k$ of $\mathcal I(m(\varphi+h_i))=\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Thus $G_m$ has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi))$. Now let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be any log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, with $\pi^*\mathcal I(m\varphi)=\mathcal O(-D)$. Its restriction $\pi^{-1}(B_j)\to B_j$ satisfies $\pi^*\mathcal I(m(\varphi+h_j))=\mathcal O(-D)$ on $\pi^{-1}(B_j)$, so by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}, applied with the volume form $\omega^n/n!$, the pulled-back kernel $K_{j,m}\circ\pi$ is locally $|s_D|^2$ times a smooth strictly positive function on $\pi^{-1}(B_j)$. The factors $\mathrm e^{-mh_j}\circ\pi$ and $\chi_j^2\circ\pi$ are smooth, and the latter vanishes outside the closed set $\pi^{-1}(\Supp\chi_j)\subset\pi^{-1}(B_j)$; hence $(R_m\circ\pi)/|s_D|^2$ is smooth on $Y$, and it is strictly positive because at every point some $\chi_i\circ\pi$ equals one. Hence $G_m\circ\pi=m^{-1}\log|s_D|^2+h$ with $h$ smooth. Finally $\exp(mG_m)=R_m$ is smooth on $X$. These are exactly the requirements of \cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}.
\end{proof}
\index{patching!logarithmic cutoff sum}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. As $m\to\infty$, $G_m\to\varphi$ pointwise on $X$ and in $L^1(X)$. For every integer $m\geq1$ and every $x\in X$,
\[
 \nu(\varphi,x)-\frac nm\leq\nu(G_m,x)\leq\nu(\varphi,x),
 \qquad \mathrm e^{m(G_m-\varphi)}\in L^1(X).
\]
Moreover, there is a constant $D\geq0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that
$G_{2m}\leq G_m+D/m$ for every integer $m\geq1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x\in U_i$. Then $R_m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$. The density of $\omega^n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure in the coordinates of $B_i$ is bounded above and below by positive constants on $B_i$, so the lower bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds}, in the version with this measure in \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}, gives $m^{-1}\log K_{i,m}(x)\geq\varphi(x)+h_i(x)-C/m$. Since $h_i$ cancels, $G_m\geq v_{i,m}\geq\varphi-C/m$ on $U_i$, where $C\geq0$ depends only on the fixed geometry.

For an upper bound, let $r>0$ be smaller than the distance, in the coordinates of $B_j$, from $\Supp\chi_j$ to $\partial B_j$ for every $j$, and for $x\in\Supp\chi_j$ let $B_j(x,r)\subset B_j$ denote the ball of radius $r$ about $x$ in these coordinates. If $\chi_j(x)\neq0$, the upper bound in \eqref{eq:local-bergman-bounds} on $B_j$, again in the version with the measure $\omega^n/n!$, gives
\[
 v_{j,m}(x)=\frac1m\log K_{j,m}(x)-h_j(x)\leq\sup_{B_j(x,r)}\varphi+\Bigl(\sup_{B_j(x,r)}h_j-h_j(x)\Bigr)+\frac1m\log\frac{C_2}{r^{2n}},
\]
where $C_2$ depends only on the fixed geometry. By the mean value theorem, $\sup_{B_j(x,r)}h_j-h_j(x)\leq C_hr$, where $C_h$ bounds the gradients of the finitely many functions $h_j$ on the compact sets $\overline{B_j}$ and therefore depends on $\theta$. Since $R_m(x)$ is a sum of at most $J$ terms, each at most $\max_ja_{j,m}(x)$ over the indices with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$,
\[
 G_m(x)\leq\max_{j:\,\chi_j(x)\neq0}\sup_{B_j(x,r)}\varphi+C_hr+\frac1m\log\frac{JC_2}{r^{2n}}.
\]
Fix $x$. Letting first $m\to\infty$ and then $r\downarrow0$, and using that the balls $B_j(x,r)$ shrink to $x$, the upper semicontinuity of $\varphi$ and the lower bound, we obtain $G_m(x)\to\varphi(x)$, also at points of the pole set. For a fixed $r$, the upper bound shows that $G_m\leq\sup_X\varphi+C_hr+|\log(JC_2r^{-2n})|$ on $X$ for every $m$, where $\sup_X\varphi<\infty$ because $\varphi$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact space $X$. Since moreover $\varphi-C\leq G_m$ and $\varphi\in L^1(X)$, dominated convergence gives $G_m\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$.

By \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}, $G_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_k|g_k|^2+O(1)$ near $x$ with generators $g_k$ of $\mathcal I(m\varphi)$. Write $G_m=\psi+f$ near $x$ with $\psi$ plurisubharmonic and $f$ smooth; then $\psi$ differs from $m^{-1}\log\sum_k|g_k|^2$ by a bounded function, and the computation of Lelong numbers in the proof of \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation} gives $\nu(G_m,x)=\nu(\psi,x)=m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_x\mathcal I(m\varphi)$, and the Lelong bounds follow from \cref{lem:multiplier-order-lelong-bounds}, applied to a local plurisubharmonic function differing from $\varphi$ by a smooth function. For the integrability, note that $\mathrm e^{m(G_m-\varphi)}=R_m\mathrm e^{-m\varphi}=\sum_j\chi_j^2K_{j,m}\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi+h_j)}$. Cover the compact set $\Supp\chi_j$ by finitely many open sets on which $K_{j,m}\leq C_m\sum_i|g_i|^2$ with members $g_i$ of the weighted space on $B_j$, by \cref{lem:bergman-smoothness-and-resolution}. Since these $g_i$ have finite weighted norm, every summand is integrable.

For the doubling inequality, \cref{lem:bergman-diagonal-comparison} applied on each $B_j$ with $u=\varphi+h_j$ gives $K_{j,2m}\leq C_*K_{j,m}^2$, hence $a_{j,2m}\leq C_*a_{j,m}^2$, with $C_*$ depending only on the fixed geometry. Let $x\in U_i$. For every $j$ with $\chi_j(x)\neq0$, \cref{lem:geometry-uniform-kernel-overlap} gives $a_{j,m}(x)\leq\mathrm e^{C_B}a_{i,m}(x)$, and $R_m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$. Consequently
\[
 R_{2m}\leq C_*\sum_j\chi_j^2a_{j,m}^2
        \leq C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_B}R_m^2
\]
at $x$, also when both sides vanish. Taking logarithms and dividing by $2m$ gives $G_{2m}\leq G_m+D/m$ with $D=\frac12\log\max(1,C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_B})$.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!doubling inequality}

To obtain inclusions of multiplier ideals from an integrability estimate, we use the following elementary lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:integrable-gap-ideal-transfer}
Let $u,v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic near a point $x$ of a complex manifold, and let $m>0$ be such that $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$ is integrable near $x$. If $0<\lambda<\lambda'$ and $m\geq\lambda\lambda'/(\lambda'-\lambda)$, then
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda'v)_x\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After shrinking the neighborhood we may subtract a constant from $v$ and assume $v\leq0$; this changes neither the ideals nor the integrability of $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Put $p=\lambda'/\lambda>1$, and note that $m\geq\lambda p/(p-1)>\lambda$. Let $f\in\mathcal I(\lambda'v)_x$, defined on a small neighborhood on which $|f|^2\leq C$ for a constant $C$. All the following inequalities are taken outside the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, which have measure zero. Where $u\geq pv$, we have $\lambda u\geq\lambda'v$ and hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda'v}$. Where $u<pv$, since $m-\lambda>0$, $v\leq0$ and $(m-\lambda)p-m=m(p-1)-\lambda p\geq0$,
\[
 (m-\lambda)u-mv\leq((m-\lambda)p-m)v\leq0,
\]
that is, $-\lambda u\leq m(v-u)$; hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq C\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Both bounds are integrable near $x$, so $f\in\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x$.
\end{proof}
\index{multiplier ideal!integrable gap}

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. A \emph{quasi-equisingular approximation} of $\varphi$ is a sequence $(\Phi_j)_{j\geq1}$ of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions with analytic singularities, decreasing pointwise to $\varphi$, such that $\theta+\ddc\Phi_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\omega$ for some numbers $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$, and such that for all $0<\lambda<\lambda'$
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda'\Phi_j)\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda\varphi)
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }j.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-equisingular approximation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gentle-quasi-equisingular-approximation}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Every $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ admits a quasi-equisingular approximation $(\Phi_j)$ in which each $\Phi_j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type
$(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^j\varphi))$, and which can be chosen with
$\varepsilon_j=c'2^{-j}$, where $c'$ is the constant of \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}. Moreover $\Phi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$, and the Lelong numbers of $\Phi_j$ satisfy the bounds of \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling} with $m=2^j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Phi_j=G_{2^j}+2D/2^j$, with $D$ as in \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}. The doubling inequality gives
\[
 \Phi_{j+1}=G_{2\cdot2^j}+\frac D{2^j}\leq G_{2^j}+\frac D{2^j}+\frac D{2^j}=\Phi_j,
\]
so the sequence is decreasing, and by \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling} its pointwise and $L^1$ limit is $\varphi$. Adding a constant changes neither the curvature, nor the analytic type, nor the Lelong numbers. Moreover $\exp(2^j\Phi_j)=\mathrm e^{2D}R_{2^j}$ is smooth, and the remainder on a log resolution changes only by a constant. Hence $\Phi_j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^j\varphi))$ by \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum}, and $\theta+\ddc\Phi_j\geq-c'2^{-j}\omega$.

It remains to prove the inclusions of multiplier ideals. Let $0<\lambda<\lambda'$. The function $\exp(2^j(\Phi_j-\varphi))=\mathrm e^{2D}\mathrm e^{2^j(G_{2^j}-\varphi)}$ is integrable on $X$ by \cref{lem:global-bergman-limit-and-doubling}. If $j$ is so large that $2^j\geq\lambda\lambda'/(\lambda'-\lambda)$, then \cref{lem:integrable-gap-ideal-transfer}, with $u=\varphi$, $v=\Phi_j$ and $m=2^j$, gives $\mathcal I(\lambda'\Phi_j)_x\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda\varphi)_x$ at every point $x\in X$. Since this threshold for $j$ depends only on $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$, the inclusion holds as an inclusion of sheaves on $X$ for all such $j$.
\end{proof}
\index{Demailly regularization!decreasing gentle approximation}

\section{Comparison of analytic singularities}
\label{sec:analytic-approximation-comparison}

For functions $u,v\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty)$ we write $u\preceq v$, and say that $u$ is \emph{more singular} than $v$, if $u\leq v+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$. Since $X$ is compact, it suffices that every point have a neighborhood on which such an inequality holds.
\index{more singular potential} We now show that two quasi-equisingular approximations of the same function are comparable for $\preceq$, up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient. The key is a growth estimate for the members of the multiplier ideals of a function with analytic singularities.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth}
Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a compact complex manifold $X$, and let $0<\eta<1$. There is $t_0>0$ such that for every real $t\geq t_0$ and every germ $f\in\mathcal I(tv)_x$, $x\in X$,
\[
 \log|f|^2\leq t(1-\eta)v+O(1)
\]
near $x$, where the bounded term may depend on $f$. Moreover, for every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ and every $t>0$, local generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(tu)$ satisfy
\[
 u\leq t^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cover $X$ by finitely many open sets $V_\kappa^+$ on which $v$ has a presentation \eqref{eq:rational-analytic-singularities}, and choose open sets $V_\kappa\Subset V_\kappa^+$ that still cover $X$. By \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, the ideal generated by the presentation on $V_\kappa^+$ has a log resolution $\pi_\kappa\colon Y_\kappa\to V_\kappa^+$, a sequence of blowups that is finite over $V_\kappa$; it is proper and surjective, so $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa})$ and the fibers of $\pi_\kappa$ are compact. On $Y_\kappa$ we have locally $v\circ\pi_\kappa=\sum_Ea_E\log|s_E|^2+O(1)$, where $\pi_\kappa^*(g_1,\ldots,g_r)=\mathcal O(-\sum_Ed_EE)$, $E$ runs over the components of this divisor, $s_E$ is a local equation of $E$, and $a_E=cd_E>0$. Let $A_E$ be the log discrepancy of $E$ (\cref{def:divisorial-order-discrepancy}). Only finitely many components $E$ meet the compact set $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa})$, so altogether there are finitely many pairs $(a_E,A_E)$ with $E$ meeting one of these sets. Choose $t_0>0$ with $t_0\eta a_E\geq A_E$ for all of them.

Now let $x\in V_\kappa$, let $t\geq t_0$, and let $f\in\mathcal I(tv)_x$ be defined on an open neighborhood $V'\subset V_\kappa$ of $x$. Near $x$ the function $tv$ differs by a smooth function from a plurisubharmonic function with the same multiplier ideal and of the form required in \cref{thm:analytic-log-resolution-integrability}, with coefficient $tc$. We apply that theorem over $V'$, shrunk if necessary, to the log resolution obtained by restricting $\pi_\kappa$. Each component $E'$ of the restricted divisor is a connected component of $E\cap\pi_\kappa^{-1}(V')$ for a component $E$ as above, and it has the same coefficient $d_E$ and the same log discrepancy $A_E$, because the vanishing orders along $E$ of a local generator of $\pi_\kappa^*(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$ and of the Jacobian determinant of $\pi_\kappa$ do not depend on the point of $E$ at which they are computed (\cref{def:divisorial-order-discrepancy}). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{E'}f>ta_E-A_E\geq t(1-\eta)a_E
\]
for every component $E'$ of the restricted divisor that meets $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$.

If $f=0$ there is nothing to prove, so let $f\neq0$; shrinking $V'$, we may assume that $V'$ is connected, so that $f$ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open subset of $V'$, and $f\circ\pi_\kappa$ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open subset of $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(V')$, because $\pi_\kappa$ is a biholomorphism off the nowhere dense set $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(V(g_1,\ldots,g_r))$. Let $p\in\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$, and choose coordinates $w$ on a neighborhood of $p$ in $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(V')$ in which the components of the divisor through $p$ are $\{w_k=0\}$, $k=1,\ldots,\ell$, with coefficients $a_k=a_E$ for the corresponding components $E$. Then $v\circ\pi_\kappa=\sum_ka_k\log|w_k|^2+O(1)$ near $p$, and by the preceding paragraph $f\circ\pi_\kappa$ is divisible by $w_k^{o_k}$ for integers $o_k>t(1-\eta)a_k$; here $o_k=\operatorname{ord}_{E'}f$ for the component $E'$ of the restricted divisor containing $\{w_k=0\}$ near $p$, since the vanishing order of $f\circ\pi_\kappa$ along the connected submanifold $E'$ is the same near all its points. Divisibility by each $w_k^{o_k}$ separately implies divisibility by their product. Indeed, suppose that $F=w_1^{o_1}\cdots w_{k-1}^{o_{k-1}}F'$ is also divisible by $w_k^{o_k}$, and expand $F'=\sum_jc_jw_k^j$ on a polydisc about $p$, with coefficients $c_j$ holomorphic in the other variables. Since $F$ is divisible by $w_k^{o_k}$, the coefficients $w_1^{o_1}\cdots w_{k-1}^{o_{k-1}}c_j$ of $w_k^j$ in the expansion of $F$ vanish for $j<o_k$; as $w_1^{o_1}\cdots w_{k-1}^{o_{k-1}}$ is not identically zero, $c_j=0$ for $j<o_k$, and $F'$ is divisible by $w_k^{o_k}$. By induction on $k$, $f\circ\pi_\kappa=\prod_kw_k^{o_k}\cdot h$ with $h$ holomorphic, hence bounded on a smaller neighborhood of $p$. Since $|w_k|\leq1$ there, we obtain $|f\circ\pi_\kappa|^2\leq C\prod_k|w_k|^{2t(1-\eta)a_k}\leq C'\exp(t(1-\eta)v\circ\pi_\kappa)$. (If $\ell=0$, then $v\circ\pi_\kappa$ is bounded near $p$ and this bound is immediate.) Finitely many such neighborhoods cover the compact fiber $\pi_\kappa^{-1}(x)$, and since $\pi_\kappa$ is proper, their union contains the inverse image of a neighborhood of $x$. As $\pi_\kappa$ is surjective, the inequality $|f|^2\leq C'\exp(t(1-\eta)v)$ holds near $x$.

For the second assertion, fix a point and write $u=u_0-\sigma$ near it, with $u_0$ plurisubharmonic and $\sigma$ smooth. Then $\mathcal I(tu)=\mathcal I(tu_0)$ near the point. On a bounded polydisc $\Omega$ about the point on which $u_0$ is bounded above, \cref{thm:multiplier-ideal-coherence} provides finitely many elements of $\mathcal H_{tu_0}(\Omega)$ that generate $\mathcal I(tu_0)$ near the point, and \eqref{eq:weight-versus-multiplier-generators} of \cref{lem:bounded-hilbert-ideal-generation}, applied with $m=t$ to the weight $u_0$, gives $u_0\leq t^{-1}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)$ for these generators. Since $\sigma$ is bounded near the point, and any two finite systems of local generators give comparable sums of squares (the remark following \cref{def:rational-analytic-singularities}), the assertion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quasi-equisingular-comparison}
Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold, let $\varphi$ and $\Phi$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ with $\varphi\leq\Phi$, where $\Phi$ has analytic singularities, and let $(\Psi_k)_{k\geq1}$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ such that for all $0<\lambda<\lambda'$
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda'\Psi_k)\subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda\varphi)
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }k.
\]
Then for each $0<\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \Psi_k\preceq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }k.
\]
In particular, if $(X,\omega)$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold, $\theta$ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and $(\Phi_j)$ and $(\Psi_k)$ are quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi$, then for each $j$ and each $0<\varepsilon<1$ we have $\Psi_k\preceq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_j$ for every sufficiently large $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $0<\eta<\varepsilon$ and $\delta>0$ so small that $(1-\eta)/(1+\delta)\geq1-\varepsilon$. Fix $t\geq t_0$, where $t_0$ is given by \cref{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth} for $v=\Phi$ and $\eta$. By the hypothesis on $(\Psi_k)$, applied with $\lambda=t$ and $\lambda'=t(1+\delta)$, and since $\varphi\leq\Phi$,
\[
 \mathcal I(t(1+\delta)\Psi_k)
       \subseteq\mathcal I(t\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal I(t\Phi)
\]
for every sufficiently large $k$. Fix such a $k$ and choose local generators $g_i$ of $\mathcal I(t(1+\delta)\Psi_k)$ near a point of $X$. They belong to $\mathcal I(t\Phi)$, so the first part of \cref{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth} gives $\log|g_i|^2\leq t(1-\eta)\Phi+O(1)$ near the point, and hence $\log\sum_i|g_i|^2\leq t(1-\eta)\Phi+O(1)$. Its second part, applied to $u=\Psi_k$ and $t(1+\delta)$, then gives
\[
 \Psi_k\leq\frac1{t(1+\delta)}\log\sum_i|g_i|^2+O(1)
       \leq\frac{1-\eta}{1+\delta}\Phi+O(1).
\]
The upper semicontinuous function $\Phi$ is bounded above on the compact space $X$, say by $M$. Since $\Phi-M\leq0$ and $(1-\eta)/(1+\delta)\geq1-\varepsilon$, we have $\frac{1-\eta}{1+\delta}(\Phi-M)\leq(1-\varepsilon)(\Phi-M)$, and therefore $\Psi_k\leq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi+O(1)$ near every point. By compactness $\Psi_k\preceq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi$; the constant may depend on $k$.

For the last assertion, recall from \cref{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation} that each $\Phi_j$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic with analytic singularities and that $\varphi\leq\Phi_j$, since the sequence decreases to $\varphi$; and $(\Psi_k)$ satisfies the hypothesis on the inclusions of multiplier ideals. Hence the first part applies with $\Phi=\Phi_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:analytic-regularization-endpoints}
Let $u=c\log|z|^2$ on a disc about $0\in\mathbb C$, with $c>0$. A germ $z^kh$ with $h(0)\neq0$ lies in $\mathcal I(mu)_0$ if and only if $|z|^{2k-2mc}$ is integrable near $0$, that is, $k>mc-1$; hence $\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $z^{\lfloor mc\rfloor}$ (\cref{ex:multiplier-monomial-threshold}). A potential of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$, such as the Bergman potential $u_m$ of \cref{thm:local-bergman-approximation}, is therefore $(\lfloor mc\rfloor/m)\log|z|^2+O(1)$ near $0$. Its coefficient $\lfloor mc\rfloor/m$ need not equal $c$ for any $m$, and then such an approximation does not reproduce the multiplier ideals of $u$ at a fixed scale. For instance, let $c$ be irrational, so that $\lfloor mc\rfloor/m<c$ for every $m$, and take the scale $1/c$. Since $u_m/c$ differs by a bounded function from $c_m\log|z|^2$ with $c_m=\lfloor mc\rfloor/(mc)<1$, the same computation gives $\mathcal I(u_m/c)_0=\mathcal O_0$ for every $m$, whereas $\mathcal I(u/c)_0=\mathcal I(\log|z|^2)_0$ is generated by $z$. This is why \cref{def:quasi-equisingular-approximation} compares two different scales $\lambda'>\lambda$ instead of requiring equality of multiplier ideals.
\end{example}
