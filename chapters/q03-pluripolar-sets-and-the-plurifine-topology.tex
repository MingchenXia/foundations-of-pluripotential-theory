\chapter{Pluripolar sets and the plurifine topology}
\label{chap:q03}

Upper regularization repairs a supremum of plurisubharmonic functions, but can change its value at individual points. To use such envelopes in measure identities, we must understand the exceptional set. The capacity constructed in the preceding chapter gives the answer: its zero sets are exactly the sets contained in plurisubharmonic poles. Relative extremal functions connect the two descriptions. Their total Monge--Amp\`ere mass measures capacity, and a sequence of extremals with vanishing mass produces a pole function. A logarithmic gluing argument then proves Josefson's theorem on $\mathbb C^n$.

There is a second consequence of this exceptional-set theory. The region where an unbounded potential exceeds a truncation level need not be Euclidean open, although it is open for the topology making plurisubharmonic functions continuous. We develop that plurifine topology far enough to prove locality of bounded products there. A countable selection outside a pluripolar set lets us pass from local superlevel identities to identities of completed measures. The resulting compatibility of truncations is the local foundation of the non-pluripolar products constructed later. We also establish how to compare two potentials across their separate pole sets, without assigning a value to their undefined difference at a common pole.

\section{Pluripolar sets and relative extremal functions}
\label{sec:pluripolar-extremals}

\begin{definition}\label{def:pluripolar-set}
A subset $E$ of a complex manifold is pluripolar if every point has a connected coordinate neighborhood $U$ and a plurisubharmonic $V$ on $U$ such that $E\cap U\subset\{V=-\infty\}$. It is globally pluripolar on a connected domain $D$ if one $V\in\PSH(D)$ has $E\subset\{V=-\infty\}$. A statement holds quasi-everywhere if its exceptional set is pluripolar. These definitions do not require $E$ to be Borel.
\end{definition}
\index{pluripolar set}\index{quasi-everywhere!pluripolar exception}

Pluripolar sets have Lebesgue measure zero in charts, by local integrability and a countable cover within each chart. In complex dimension one the local notion agrees with polarity. In dimension zero a plurisubharmonic function is finite on each point, so the only pluripolar set is empty. The arguments below concern $n\ge1$.

Let $B$ be a bounded ball in $\mathbb C^n$. For any $E\subset B$ define
\[
 h_E(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in\PSH(B),\ v\le0,\ v|_E\le-1\},
 \qquad H_E=h_E^*.
\]
The constant $-1$ is a competitor. Taking the maximum with it allows all competitors to lie in $[-1,0]$, without changing the envelope. Thus $-1\le h_E\le H_E\le0$, and $H_E$ is plurisubharmonic by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}. The exceptional set $\{h_E<H_E\}$ has zero outer capacity by \cref{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity}. We call $H_E$ the relative extremal function.
\index{relative extremal function!plurisubharmonic}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-extremal-compact}
If $K\Subset B$ is compact, then $H_K$ tends to zero at $\partial B$, its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is carried by $K\cap\{H_K=-1\}$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_B(K)=\int_B(\ddc H_K)^n.
\]
For decreasing compact sets $K_j$ with intersection $K$, the $H_{K_j}$ increase almost everywhere to $H_K$ and their capacities decrease to $\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)$. In particular $\operatorname{Cap}_B^*(K)=\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $B=B(a,R)$. A sufficiently large multiple $\rho=A(|z-a|^2-R^2)$ is at most $-1$ on $K$, so $\rho\le H_K\le0$. This proves the boundary limit.

Choquet selection gives bounded competitors $v_j$ increasing almost everywhere to $H_K$. For a ball $D\Subset B\setminus K$, replace each $v_j$ on $D$ by its canonical homogeneous balayage from \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}. The replacements are still competitors for $K$, dominate $v_j$, and increase. They consequently have the same almost-everywhere limit $H_K$. Their measures vanish on $D$; increasing continuity gives $(\ddc H_K)^n=0$ there. A countable ball cover shows that this measure is carried by $K$. On $K$, the raw envelope equals $-1$. Thus $K\cap\{H_K>-1\}$ lies in the zero-outer-capacity discrepancy set. The competitor $H_K+1$ shows that its measure gives this set zero mass, proving the support assertion.

Since $H_K+1$ is a capacity competitor, the total mass, which is carried by $K$, is at most $\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)$. Conversely take any competitor $0\le v\le1$ and put $w=(1-2\varepsilon)(v-1)-\varepsilon$ for $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Then $-1<w<0$. Outside the discrepancy set, $H_K=-1<w$ on $K$. The measure of $w$ does not charge that discrepancy, by its zero capacity. The boundary limit of $H_K$ permits comparison with $w$, giving
\[
 (1-2\varepsilon)^n\int_K(\ddc v)^n
 \le\int_{\{H_K<w\}}(\ddc w)^n
 \le\int_B(\ddc H_K)^n.
\]
Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and take the supremum over $v$.

For decreasing $K_j$, the extremals increase and their canonical almost-everywhere limit $H$ satisfies $H\le H_K$. For a competitor $v$ for $K$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, the open set $\{v<-1+\varepsilon\}$ contains $K$ and hence contains $K_j$ eventually. Therefore $v/(1-\varepsilon)$ is a competitor for those $K_j$, so $v\le(1-\varepsilon)H_{K_j}\le(1-\varepsilon)H$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and then take the upper regularization of the supremum over $v$. It follows that $H_K\le H$, proving equality. Increasing continuity gives weak convergence of their measures. All are carried by the fixed compact $K_1$, so their total masses converge. The mass identity proves capacity convergence. Finally choose compact thickenings of $K$ decreasing to it and with interiors containing it. Their capacities bound the outer capacity from above and tend to $\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)$, proving the last equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-extremal-open}
If $G\Subset B$ is open, then $h_G=H_G$, it equals $-1$ on $G$, tends to zero on $\partial B$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_B(G)=\int_B(\ddc H_G)^n.
\]
The measure is carried by $\overline G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exhaust $G$ by increasing compact neighborhoods $K_j$ with $K_j\subset\Int K_{j+1}$. The $H_{K_j}$ decrease to a bounded plurisubharmonic $H$. Every point of $G$ eventually lies in the interior of $K_j$, where the raw envelope and its regularization both equal $-1$. Thus $H=-1$ on $G$ and belongs to its competitor class. Conversely every competitor for $G$ is a competitor for every $K_j$, so $h_G\le H$. Hence $h_G=H=H_G$. A quadratic barrier for $\overline G$ gives the boundary value. Decreasing continuity passes the measures to the limit; their supports lie in the common compact $\overline G$. Taking total masses and using Borel capacity continuity from below gives the formula and the support assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}
For a subset $E$ of a bounded ball $B$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ has zero outer Monge--Amp\`ere capacity in $B$;
\item $E$ is contained in the pole set of a nonpositive plurisubharmonic function on $B$;
\item $H_E=0$ on $B$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently local pluripolarity on $B$ also implies one global pole function on $B$. Countable unions of pluripolar sets are pluripolar on every complex manifold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The implication from a pole to zero outer capacity is \cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity} and a countable compact exhaustion. First suppose $E\Subset B$ and its outer capacity is zero. Choose decreasing open sets $G_j$ containing $E$, with closures in one fixed compact subset of $B$, and $\operatorname{Cap}_B(G_j)\to0$. The extremals $H_{G_j}$ increase almost everywhere to a bounded plurisubharmonic $H$. By \cref{prop:relative-extremal-open} their measures have total masses tending to zero; increasing continuity gives $(\ddc H)^n=0$. Their common quadratic lower barrier makes $H$ tend to zero at the boundary. Comparison with the constant zero gives $H=0$. Since $H_{G_j}\le H_E\le0$, it follows that $H_E=0$ almost everywhere; canonical uniqueness gives equality everywhere.

Whenever $H_E=0$, Choquet selection supplies bounded competitors $v_j$ increasing almost everywhere to zero. Choose a point where this convergence holds and a subsequence $v_{j_l}$ with $v_{j_l}(a)>-2^{-l}$. The decreasing partial sums converge to a nonpositive plurisubharmonic function, since their limit is finite at $a$. Each summand is at most $-1$ on $E$, so that function is $-\infty$ on $E$.

For general zero-capacity $E$, intersect it with a compact exhaustion of $B$. The preceding argument gives nonpositive pole functions $V_l$ for these intersections. Choose a point $a$ where all $V_l$ are finite; their pole sets form a countable Lebesgue null union. Positive coefficients $c_l$ with $c_l|V_l(a)|\le2^{-l}$ make $\sum_lc_lV_l$ a nonpositive plurisubharmonic pole function for $E$, finite at $a$. If $V$ is any such nonpositive pole function, then $\varepsilon V\le h_E\le H_E\le0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $H_E=0$ almost everywhere, and canonical uniqueness gives equality everywhere. This proves all three equivalences.

A locally pluripolar set has zero outer capacity on compact neighborhoods by \cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity}; a countable cover and domain restriction give zero outer capacity in $B$. Finally a countable union of locally pluripolar sets has zero outer capacity in each coordinate ball, by subadditivity. The equivalence supplies a pole function there, proving local pluripolarity of the union.
\end{proof}
\index{capacity!zero and pluripolarity}

\section{Global poles on Euclidean space}
\label{sec:josefson}

A pole function on a ball cannot simply be extended by zero: that operation generally violates the submean inequality. A negative logarithmic collar allows gluing while keeping the sum integrable on every fixed compact set.

\begin{theorem}[Josefson]\label{thm:josefson}
For every pluripolar $E\subset\mathbb C^n$ there is a global plurisubharmonic $V$ such that $E\subset\{V=-\infty\}$. One can arrange $V(z)\le\log^+|z|$ for all $z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose countably many subsets $E_m$ covering $E$, each relatively compact in a bounded ball $D_m$ and of zero outer capacity there. This follows from local pluripolarity, \cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity}, and a countable ball cover with compact neighborhoods. Choose a sequence $m_j$ in which every positive integer occurs infinitely often. Choose $R_j\uparrow\infty$, $R_j>1$, such that $D_{m_j}\Subset B_j=B(0,R_j)$ and
\[
 2^{-j}(\log^+|z|-\log R_j)\le-1\quad\hbox{on }E_{m_j}.
\]
Domain restriction gives zero outer capacity of $E_{m_j}$ in $B_j$. Its extremal is zero by \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}. Bounded Choquet competitors increasing almost everywhere to zero and bounded convergence therefore supply $u_j\in\PSH(B_j)$ with
\[
 -1\le u_j\le0,\qquad u_j=-1\ \hbox{on }E_{m_j},
 \qquad\int_{B_j}(-u_j)\,\mathrm dV<2^{-j}.
\]
The function $\log^+|z|$ is plurisubharmonic: the Hessian of $\log(|z|^2+\varepsilon)$ is nonnegative by Cauchy--Schwarz, and decreasing limits followed by a maximum with zero prove the assertion. Put $\rho_j=2^{-j}(\log^+|z|-\log R_j)$, paste $\max\{u_j,\rho_j\}$ inside $B_j$ to $\rho_j$ outside, and call the result $V_j$. The upper boundary limit of $u_j$ is at most zero, which equals $\rho_j$ on the sphere, so \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} applies. Also $V_j=-1$ on $E_{m_j}$ and $V_j\le2^{-j}\log^+|z|$ everywhere.

On any fixed compact ball, all sufficiently late $V_j$ are nonpositive and their negative integrals are at most $2^{-j}$. Their partial sums decrease to a nontrivial plurisubharmonic limit there, with finite $L^1$ norm by Tonelli. The finitely many earlier terms are locally bounded, so these local limits define $V=\sum_jV_j\in\PSH(\mathbb C^n)$. Every point of $E_m$ has infinitely many summands equal to $-1$, and all sufficiently late summands are nonpositive at that point. Thus $V=-\infty$ on $E$. Summing the upper growth bounds gives $V\le\log^+|z|$.
\end{proof}
\index{Josefson's theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}
The discrepancy between a nonempty locally uniformly bounded-above plurisubharmonic supremum and its upper regularization is pluripolar. The pointwise supremum of an increasing locally bounded-above sequence equals its canonical plurisubharmonic limit quasi-everywhere. Bounded-potential products and their trace measures give every pluripolar set zero outer measure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity} and \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar} prove the discrepancy assertion in coordinate balls. The increasing sequence is an envelope of its terms. Local pole-zero capacity and \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination} prove the trace assertion. Coefficient variations are controlled by that trace, so the full order-zero products do not charge the set either.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:real-polar-not-pluripolar}
The product torus $\mathbb T^2=\{|z_1|=|z_2|=1\}\subset\mathbb C^2$ is real polar but not pluripolar. For real polarity, let $\mu$ be its normalized product arc measure. By \cref{prop:fundamental-potential}, the function
\[
 u(z)=-\int_{\mathbb T^2}|z-\zeta|^{-2}\,\mathrm d\mu(\zeta)
\]
is subharmonic on $\mathbb R^4$ and finite off the torus. Near a point of the torus, its two angle parameters satisfy $|z-\zeta|\le C|\theta|$. Thus the integral is bounded below by a positive multiple of $\int_{|\theta|<r}|\theta|^{-2}\,\mathrm d\theta=\infty$, proving $u=-\infty$ there.

If the torus were pluripolar, Josefson's theorem would give a global plurisubharmonic $V$ with those poles. Fix $|z_2|=1$. Its first-coordinate restriction is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$, and is $-\infty$ on the unit circle. Upper semicontinuity and compactness make every finite negative constant an upper bound near that circle; comparison makes the restriction $-\infty$ throughout the unit disc. Now fix $|z_1|<1$ and apply the same argument in the second coordinate. It makes $V=-\infty$ on the bidisc, contradicting local integrability. This proves the distinction.
\end{example}
\index{polar set!not pluripolar}

\section{Thinness and plurifine neighborhoods}
\label{sec:plurifine-neighborhoods}

\begin{definition}\label{def:plurifine-topology}
The plurifine topology on a complex manifold is the coarsest topology containing the Euclidean chart topology and making every local plurisubharmonic function continuous with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$. A set $E$ is plurithin at $a$ if $a$ is not an accumulation point of $E\setminus\{a\}$, or if some plurisubharmonic $\psi$, finite at $a$ and defined near it, satisfies
\[
 \limsup_{E\setminus\{a\}\ni z\to a}\psi(z)<\psi(a).
\]
The limits in this definition use the Euclidean topology.
\end{definition}
\index{plurifine topology}\index{thinness!plurithin}

Only finite plurisubharmonic generators are needed locally. If $u(a)>t$, truncation below $t$ preserves the superlevel set. If $u(a)=-\infty$, a neighborhood of that value is a sublevel set, which is Euclidean open by upper semicontinuity. On a Euclidean domain, local bounded truncations can moreover be extended to finite global plurisubharmonic functions on $\mathbb C^n$: on a ball where the truncation is bounded, paste its maximum with a quadratic function below its lower bound near the chosen point and above its upper bound on a boundary collar. Outside the ball use that quadratic function. The maximum gluing theorem proves the extension. Thus on $\mathbb C^n$ the finite global plurisubharmonic functions generate this same topology; real coordinate functions also generate the Euclidean topology.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:plurifine-base}
A set $U$ containing $a$ is a plurifine neighborhood of $a$ if and only if its complement is plurithin there. A local base consists of sets $B\cap\{\psi>0\}$ containing $a$, where $B$ is a sufficiently small Euclidean ball and $\psi$ is bounded plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. This topology restricts to the same topology on Euclidean open subdomains, is preserved by biholomorphic charts, and makes holomorphic maps plurifinely continuous.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A finite intersection of generating neighborhoods at $a$ imposes finitely many lower bounds $u_i>c_i$; upper bounds are Euclidean open and can be incorporated into a small ball. Truncate the $u_i$ below their thresholds. Put $\delta_i=u_i(a)-c_i>0$. Upper semicontinuity permits a smaller ball on which $(u_i-u_i(a))/\delta_i<1/(2k)$, where $k$ is the number of lower bounds. The bounded plurisubharmonic
\[
 \psi=\tfrac14+\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{u_i-u_i(a)}{\delta_i}
\]
is positive at $a$. If any lower bound fails, its value is at most $1/4-1+(k-1)/(2k)<0$. Thus its positive superlevel set in that ball lies in the given intersection. If there are no lower bounds, use the constant one. This proves the stated base. On the complement of such a base neighborhood, near $a$ the witness is at most zero and has positive value at $a$, so the complement is plurithin.

Conversely a strict thinness gap gives, after choosing a threshold between its two sides and shrinking the ball, a positive superlevel neighborhood avoiding the complement. A nonaccumulation point has an ordinary ball neighborhood. Restriction to subdomains is built into the local generators, and biholomorphic invariance follows from composition. For a holomorphic map the pullback of a generating potential is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on a component by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}; in the latter case it is a continuous constant. Ordinary continuity handles the Euclidean generators. This proves continuity for the generated topologies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:plurifine-positive-volume}
Every nonempty plurifine open set contains a Borel subset of positive Lebesgue measure in a chart. The complement of any pluripolar set is plurifinely dense. The finite locus of any plurisubharmonic function is plurifine open.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take a base neighborhood $B\cap\{\psi>0\}$ at a point where $\psi>0$. If it were Lebesgue null, $\psi\le0$ almost everywhere on $B$. Canonical uniqueness applied to $\max\{\psi,0\}$ would give $\psi\le0$ everywhere, a contradiction. The neighborhood is Borel and has positive volume. A pluripolar set is Lebesgue null, so cannot contain a nonempty fine open set; its complement is dense. The finite locus is $\bigcup_m\{u>-m\}$, a union of generating open sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:pluripolar-hyperplane-not-discrete}
For $n\ge2$, the hyperplane $\{z_1=0\}$ is pluripolar, with pole function $\log|z_1|^2$, but has no isolated point in its induced plurifine topology. A base witness positive at such a point restricts to a plurisubharmonic function on the hyperplane; its positive superlevel neighborhood has positive volume there by the preceding proposition. Thus the classical fine discreteness of polar sets from \cref{chap:p05} does not transfer to arbitrary complex pluripolar sets.
\end{example}

\section{Countable selections and plurifine locality}
\label{sec:plurifine-selection-locality}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:plurifine-quasi-lindelof}
On a complex manifold with a countable basis, for any family of plurifine open sets, a countable subfamily has the same union outside a pluripolar set. Each plurifine open set differs from a Borel subset of itself by a pluripolar set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use a countable Euclidean coordinate-ball base. The preceding local base theorem expresses the union by positive superlevel sets of bounded plurisubharmonic functions on these balls, each superlevel set contained in an original member. The ball can be chosen with closure in the witness's domain. For each fixed ball divide every witness by a positive constant and truncate below $-1$, so all lie in $[-1,1]$ without changing their positive superlevel sets.

Choquet selection and \cref{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy} give a countable selection of these witnesses whose supremum and the full supremum equal their common regularization outside a pluripolar set. There the full supremum is positive if and only if the selected supremum is positive, which is equivalent to positivity of some selected witness. Choose the corresponding original open sets. Repeat over the countably many balls. The union of all exceptional sets is pluripolar by \cref{thm:zero-capacity-pluripolar}, proving the first assertion. The union of the selected superlevel sets is Borel, lies in the original union and differs from it only within that exceptional set. This proves the second assertion. The empty family uses the empty selection.
\end{proof}
\index{quasi-Lindel\"of principle!plurifine}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bt-superlevel-locality}
Let $u,v,t$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a Euclidean neighborhood, $t\ge0$, and suppose $u=v$ on $\{t>0\}$. For a fixed positive closed current $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ and every integer $0\le p\le r$,
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{t>0\}}(\ddc u)^p\wedge T
       =\mathbf1_{\{t>0\}}(\ddc v)^p\wedge T.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $w_\varepsilon=\max\{u+\varepsilon t,v\}$. On $\{t>0\}$, $u=v$ makes the first branch strictly larger, so maximum locality gives equality there with $(\ddc(u+\varepsilon t))^p\wedge T$. Multiply by $t$; the equality then holds everywhere, since this weight vanishes on the complement. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ both potentials decrease, and weighted continuity yields
$t(\ddc\max\{u,v\})^p\wedge T=t(\ddc u)^p\wedge T$.
Interchange $u,v$ and obtain the analogous identity with $v$. Dividing by $t+\delta$ and then letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives the assertion against coefficient variations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-plurifine-locality}
Bounded-potential products with base current $1$ are local in the plurifine topology. Precisely, on a complex manifold of dimension $n$, let $u_l,v_l$, $1\le l\le q$, be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions equal on a plurifine open $W$, where $1\le q\le n$ is an integer. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_W\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q
     =\mathbf1_W\ddc v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc v_q.
\]
The restriction uses the completed coefficient measures; it has an unambiguous Borel representative modulo their null sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work in a coordinate ball; equality of currents can be checked locally. Write $W$ for its intersection with that ball. Cover $W$ by local base sets $\{\psi>0\}$ in coordinate balls, and put $t=\max\{\psi,0\}$. On each such set the preceding lemma proves equality of powers. Polarization gives equality of mixed products: expand powers of the sums of the $q$ potentials and take the alternating sum over subsets, which isolates $q!$ times their mixed product. The same argument applies after restricting to a smaller ball where every potential and $t$ is bounded.

The quasi-Lindel\"of theorem selects countably many such base sets covering $W$ outside a pluripolar set. Their union is Borel. Bounded-product coefficient variations do not charge the exception by \cref{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}. Thus $W$ is measurable for their completions, and equality on the countable union gives equality on $W$. The base current is $1$ in this last argument: an arbitrary singular current could charge the pluripolar exception.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!plurifine locality}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-locus-truncation}
For plurisubharmonic $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on a coordinate domain in $\mathbb C^n$, with integer $1\le q\le n$, and for each integer $m\ge1$, put $u_l^{(m)}=\max\{u_l,-m\}$ and $E_m=\bigcap_l\{u_l>-m\}$. If $k\ge m$ is an integer, then
\[
 \mathbf1_{E_m}\ddc u_1^{(k)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q^{(k)}
 =\mathbf1_{E_m}\ddc u_1^{(m)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q^{(m)}.
\]
The trace measures of the products restricted to $E_m$ increase with $m$ and give every pluripolar set zero mass. Their increasing limit is a measure, potentially of infinite mass on compact sets.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Borel $E_m$ is plurifine open. On it all the specified truncations equal the original finite potentials, so \cref{thm:bt-plurifine-locality} gives the equality. Since $E_m\subset E_k$, positivity gives increase of the trace measures. Each bounded product avoids pluripolar sets, and restriction preserves that property. Monotone convergence defines the limiting measure and proves countable additivity, by interchange of the increasing limit and the nonnegative countable sum. It also preserves zero mass on every pluripolar set. Local finiteness requires a separate mass estimate before this limit can define an order-zero current.
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!finite locus}\index{truncation!plurifine compatibility}

\section{Common poles and quasi-extrema}
\label{sec:plurifine-quasi-extrema}

The difference of two plurisubharmonic functions is undefined where both are $-\infty$. Removing that common pole set makes it a continuous extended-real function for the plurifine topology. Further pluripolar deletions do not change its extrema on a fine open set.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:plurifine-difference-continuity}
For plurisubharmonic $\varphi,\psi$ on a coordinate domain, put $P=\{\varphi=\psi=-\infty\}$. Then $P$ is plurifine closed and pluripolar, and $\varphi-\psi$ is continuous with values in $[-\infty,\infty]$ on its plurifine open complement. This remains true after adding a smooth real function.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each pole set is the inverse image of the closed singleton $\{-\infty\}$ under a generating continuous function. Their intersection is closed and lies in a plurisubharmonic pole set. At a point where both potentials are finite, subtraction is continuous. If only $\varphi$ is $-\infty$, a finite bound for $\psi$ from both sides in a fine neighborhood, together with arbitrarily small upper bounds for $\varphi$, proves continuity to $-\infty$. If only $\psi$ is $-\infty$, the same argument gives continuity to $+\infty$. A smooth function is Euclidean continuous and hence plurifinely continuous, so addition preserves these conclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:pluripolar-quasi-extrema}
If an extended-real $f$ is defined outside a pluripolar $Z$ in a nonempty plurifine open $W$, define
\[
 \operatorname{q\!\sup}_W f
   =\inf_{N\supset Z,\ N\text{ pluripolar}}\sup_{W\setminus N}f,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{q\!\inf}_W f
   =\sup_{N\supset Z,\ N\text{ pluripolar}}\inf_{W\setminus N}f.
\]
The sets over which these extrema are taken are nonempty by positive volume. All extrema use the extended-real order.
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-supremum!pluripolar exception}\index{quasi-infimum!pluripolar exception}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:quasi-extrema-common-poles}
Let $\varphi,\psi$ be plurisubharmonic on a coordinate domain and $f=\varphi-\psi+b$, where $b$ is real and smooth, and let $Z$ be any pluripolar set containing their common poles. For a nonempty plurifine open $W$,
\[
 \operatorname{q\!\sup}_W f=\sup_{W\setminus Z}f,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{q\!\inf}_W f=\inf_{W\setminus Z}f.
\]
The values are independent of the chosen $Z$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The common-pole complement $W\setminus P$ is fine open and the difference is continuous there. For any pluripolar $N\supset P$, its complement is dense in this open set by \cref{prop:plurifine-positive-volume}. A continuous extended-real function has the same supremum and infimum on a dense subset: any strict finite level below the supremum or above the infimum is attained in a nonempty open level set, which meets the dense subset. Thus all the displayed extrema equal the extrema on $W\setminus P$. This proves both identities and their independence. No value is assigned to the undefined subtraction at common poles.
\end{proof}
