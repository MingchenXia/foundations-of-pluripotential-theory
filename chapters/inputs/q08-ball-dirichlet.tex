\section{The ball Dirichlet problem with continuous density}
\label{sec:bt-continuous-ball-dirichlet}

We now solve the Dirichlet problem
\[
 (\ddc u)^n=f\eta^n\quad\text{on }B,\qquad u=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial B,
\]
on a ball $B$, for a continuous density $f\ge0$ and continuous boundary values $\varphi$. As for the homogeneous equation in \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}, the solution is the upper envelope of subsolutions. By the linear characterization of density lower bounds in \cref{prop:bounded-monge-ampere-linear-density}, subsolutions are stable under maxima, convex combinations and the addition of multiples of $|z|^2$ (\cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}). Stability under maxima makes the envelope a subsolution, and stability under averages allows us to compose with the automorphisms of \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms} once more to bound its second differences; since a biholomorphism multiplies the density by the squared modulus of its Jacobian determinant, this factor now enters the estimate.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}
Let $u,v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$, and let $F,G\ge0$ be continuous functions on $\Omega$ with $(\ddc u)^n\ge F^n\eta^n$ and $(\ddc v)^n\ge G^n\eta^n$. Then, for $0\le t\le1$ and $c\ge0$,
\[
 \bigl(\ddc((1-t)u+tv+c|z|^2)\bigr)^n
 \ge\bigl((1-t)F+tG+c\bigr)^n\eta^n.
\]
Moreover, $(\ddc\max\{u,v\})^n\ge\min\{F,G\}^n\eta^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $B$ be a positive definite Hermitian matrix with $\det B=1$. By the implication from (1) to (3) in \cref{prop:bounded-monge-ampere-linear-density}, $L_Bu\ge nF$ and $L_Bv\ge nG$ in the sense of distributions. Moreover $L_B|z|^2=\operatorname{tr}B\ge n(\det B)^{1/n}=n$ by the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for the eigenvalues of $B$. Hence
\[
 L_B\bigl((1-t)u+tv+c|z|^2\bigr)
 \ge n\bigl((1-t)F+tG\bigr)+c\operatorname{tr}B
 \ge n\bigl((1-t)F+tG+c\bigr),
\]
and the implication from (3) to (1), applied with the continuous density $((1-t)F+tG+c)^n$, gives the first assertion.

For the second, let $q$ be an upper test function for $\max\{u,v\}$ at a point $x$. If $u(x)\ge v(x)$, then $q(x)=u(x)$ and $q\ge\max\{u,v\}\ge u$ near $x$, so $q$ is an upper test function for $u$ at $x$, and the implication from (1) to (2) gives $\det\mathcal H(q)(x)\ge F(x)^n\ge\min\{F,G\}(x)^n$. The case $v(x)\ge u(x)$ is symmetric. The implication from (2) to (1) now gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:continuous-density-ball-dirichlet}
Let $B=B(a,R)\subset\mathbb C^n$ with $n\ge1$ and $R>0$. Let $f\in C(\overline B)$ be nonnegative, and let $\varphi\in C(\partial B)$ be real. There is a unique $H_{f,\varphi}\in\PSH(B)\cap C(\overline B)$ such that
\[
 (\ddc H_{f,\varphi})^n=f\eta^n\quad\text{on }B,
 \qquad H_{f,\varphi}|_{\partial B}=\varphi.
\]
It is the pointwise supremum of the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $v$ on $B$ such that $(\ddc v)^n\ge f\eta^n$ and $\varlimsup_{B\ni z\to\zeta}v(z)\le\varphi(\zeta)$ for every $\zeta\in\partial B$. If $g\in C(\overline B)$ is another nonnegative density and $\chi\in C(\partial B)$ another real boundary function, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability}
 \|H_{f,\varphi}-H_{g,\chi}\|_{L^\infty(B)}
 \le\|\varphi-\chi\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}
       +R^2\|f^{1/n}-g^{1/n}\|_{L^\infty(B)}.
\end{equation}
If $f^{1/n}$ is the restriction of a real $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and $\varphi$ that of a real $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$, then $H_{f,\varphi}\in C^{1,1}_{\loc}(B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For data of class $C^2$ (defined below) we show that the envelope of subsolutions is a locally $C^{1,1}$ solution (Steps 1--3). A stability estimate then gives a solution for continuous data (Step 4), and a comparison argument identifies every continuous solution with the envelope (Step 5).

The affine map $\tau(\zeta)=a+R\zeta$ carries the unit ball onto $B$. Since $|\tau(\zeta)|^2-R^2|\zeta|^2=2R\Real\sum_j\zeta_j\bar a_j+|a|^2$ is the real part of a holomorphic polynomial plus a constant, its complex Hessian vanishes, so $\tau$ pulls $\eta=\ddc|z|^2$ back to $R^2\eta$ and $\eta^n$ back to $R^{2n}\eta^n$. Since Bedford--Taylor products are invariant under biholomorphisms (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(5)), a function $u$ on $B$ satisfies $(\ddc u)^n\ge f\eta^n$ if and only if $\zeta\mapsto u(a+R\zeta)$ satisfies the same inequality with the density $R^{2n}f(a+R\zeta)$, whose $n$th root is $R^2f^{1/n}(a+R\zeta)$; the same holds with equality. It therefore suffices to prove the theorem for the unit ball $B=B(0,1)$, with $R=1$ in \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability}. We write $z$ again for the variable and put
\[
 \psi=|z|^2-1,\qquad F=f^{1/n}.
\]
In this proof a \emph{subsolution for $(f,\varphi)$} is a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ with $(\ddc v)^n\ge f\eta^n$ and $\varlimsup_{B\ni z\to\zeta}v(z)\le\varphi(\zeta)$ for all $\zeta\in\partial B$. We say that the data $(f,\varphi)$ are \emph{of class $C^2$} if $F$ and $\varphi$ are restrictions of real $C^2$ functions on neighborhoods of $\overline B$ and $\partial B$, respectively.

\emph{Step 1. The envelope of subsolutions for data of class $C^2$.}
Assume that $(f,\varphi)$ is of class $C^2$. We first construct a subsolution with boundary values $\varphi$. Multiplying a $C^2$ extension of $\varphi$ by a cutoff function supported in its domain and equal to one near $\partial B$, we obtain a real $C^2$ function $b$ on $\mathbb C^n$ with compact support and $b=\varphi$ on $\partial B$. Let $\lambda\ge0$ bound the operator norm of the complex Hessian of $b$, and choose $c_0>\lambda$ with $(c_0-\lambda)^n\ge\max_{\overline B}f$. The complex Hessian of $s=b+c_0\psi$ is at least $(c_0-\lambda)I$, so $s$ is plurisubharmonic (\cref{prop:smooth-psh-criterion}) and $\det(s_{j\bar k})\ge(c_0-\lambda)^n\ge f$ on $B$; by the formula for plurisubharmonic $C^2$ functions at the beginning of \cref{sec:bt-density-lower-bounds}, $(\ddc s)^n\ge f\eta^n$ on $B$. Moreover $s$ is continuous on $\overline B$ and equals $\varphi$ on $\partial B$; in particular, $s$ is a subsolution. On the other hand, let $P\varphi$ be the harmonic extension of $\varphi$, that is, its Poisson integral (\cref{thm:ball-dirichlet}). Every subsolution is subharmonic by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, with boundary upper limits at most $\varphi$, so the harmonic comparison principle (\cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison} and the remark following it) shows that it is bounded above by $P\varphi$.

Let $H$ be the pointwise supremum of all subsolutions, so that $s\le H\le P\varphi$. By \cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}, the maximum of finitely many subsolutions is a subsolution. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, some countable family of subsolutions has the same upper semicontinuous regularization $H^*$ as the whole family, and its supremum equals $H^*$ almost everywhere. Taking finite maxima of $s$ and the members of this family, we obtain subsolutions $v_j$ which increase to a function equal to $H^*$ almost everywhere, and $s\le v_j\le P\varphi$. By \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}, $(\ddc v_j)^n\to(\ddc H^*)^n$ weakly, and hence $(\ddc H^*)^n\ge f\eta^n$. Since $P\varphi$ is continuous, $s\le H\le H^*\le P\varphi$; as $s$ and $P\varphi$ are continuous on $\overline B$ and equal $\varphi$ on $\partial B$, the function $H^*$ has boundary limits $\varphi$. Thus $H^*$ is a subsolution, so $H^*\le H$ and $H=H^*$. In particular, $H$ is a subsolution with $\lim_{B\ni z\to\zeta}H(z)=\varphi(\zeta)$ for all $\zeta\in\partial B$.

\emph{Step 2. Second differences for data of class $C^2$.}
We show that $H$ satisfies the hypothesis of \cref{lem:second-difference-regularity} on $B$. Fix a compact set $K\subset B$, and let $h_0>0$ and the automorphisms $S_{a,h}$, $a\in K$, $|h|\le h_0$, be as in \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms}. Thus the map $(a,h,z)\mapsto S_{a,h}(z)$ is smooth, and holomorphic in $z$, on an open set $O$ containing the compact set $L=K\times\overline{B(0,h_0)}\times\overline B$. Consequently the complex Jacobian determinant $J_{a,h}(z)=\det_{\mathbb C}DS_{a,h}(z)$ of $z\mapsto S_{a,h}(z)$ is a smooth function of $(a,h,z)$ on $O$. Since $S_{a,0}$ is the identity, $J_{a,0}=1$ on $B$, and hence on $\overline B$, as $z\mapsto J_{a,0}(z)$ is continuous. The open set $W=\{(a,h,z)\in O:|J_{a,h}(z)-1|<1/2\}$ therefore contains the compact set $K\times\{0\}\times\overline B$, and hence all points within some positive distance of it; decreasing $h_0$, we may assume that $L\subset W$. Then $|J_{a,h}|^{2/n}=(J_{a,h}\overline{J_{a,h}})^{1/n}$ is smooth and positive on the neighborhood $W$ of $L$.

Since $S_{a,h}$ is holomorphic, $S_{a,h}^*(\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n)=J_{a,h}\,\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n$, and the conjugate identity holds for $\mathrm d\bar z_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_n$. As $\eta^n$ is a constant multiple of $\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_n$, it follows that $S_{a,h}^*\eta^n=|J_{a,h}|^2\eta^n$. Pulling back the inequality $(\ddc H)^n\ge F^n\eta^n$ by $S_{a,h}$, and using again the invariance of Bedford--Taylor products under biholomorphisms, we obtain
\[
 \bigl(\ddc(H\circ S_{a,h})\bigr)^n
 \ge (F\circ S_{a,h})^n|J_{a,h}|^2\eta^n
 =\bigl((F\circ S_{a,h})|J_{a,h}|^{2/n}\bigr)^n\eta^n.
\]
Put
\[
 \Phi_{a,h}=\tfrac12\bigl((F\circ S_{a,h})|J_{a,h}|^{2/n}
 +(F\circ S_{a,-h})|J_{a,-h}|^{2/n}\bigr),
\]
a nonnegative continuous function on $\overline B$. We claim that there are constants $C_1,C_2\ge0$ such that, for all $a\in K$ and $|h|\le h_0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_{a,h}&\ge F-C_1|h|^2 &&\text{on }\overline B,\\
 \tfrac12(\varphi\circ S_{a,h}+\varphi\circ S_{a,-h})
 &\le\varphi+C_2|h|^2 &&\text{on }\partial B.
 \end{aligned}
\]

To prove the first inequality, fix $a\in K$ and $z\in\overline B$, and consider $\gamma(h)=F(S_{a,h}(z))|J_{a,h}(z)|^{2/n}$ as a function of the real variable $h\in\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$, $|h|\le h_0$. Since $S_{a,h}(\overline B)=\overline B$, $F$ is of class $C^2$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and the maps $(a,h,z)\mapsto S_{a,h}(z)$ and $(a,h,z)\mapsto|J_{a,h}(z)|^{2/n}$ are smooth near the compact set $L$, the second derivatives of $\gamma$ are bounded by a constant $2C_1$ which depends neither on $a$ nor on $z$. Taylor's formula with integral remainder, applied at $h$ and at $-h$, gives $|\gamma(h)+\gamma(-h)-2\gamma(0)|\le2C_1|h|^2$, since the first-order terms cancel. As $\gamma(0)=F(z)$, it follows that $\Phi_{a,h}(z)=\frac12(\gamma(h)+\gamma(-h))\ge F(z)-C_1|h|^2$. The second inequality follows in the same way from a $C^2$ extension of $\varphi$ to a neighborhood of $\partial B$, since $S_{a,\pm h}$ map $\partial B$ onto itself and $S_{a,0}$ is the identity. The constants depend only on $K$, $h_0$, $n$ and the $C^2$ extensions of $F$ and $\varphi$.

Now consider
\[
 V_{a,h}=\tfrac12(H\circ S_{a,h}+H\circ S_{a,-h})
            +C_1|h|^2\psi-C_2|h|^2.
\]
Since additive constants do not change Monge--Amp\`ere measures, \cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}, applied with $t=1/2$ and the quadratic term $C_1|h|^2|z|^2$, gives
\[
 (\ddc V_{a,h})^n\ge\bigl(\Phi_{a,h}+C_1|h|^2\bigr)^n\eta^n\ge F^n\eta^n=f\eta^n.
\]
Since $S_{a,\pm h}$ preserve $\partial B$ and $H$ has boundary limits $\varphi$, the boundary limits of $V_{a,h}$ are $\frac12(\varphi\circ S_{a,h}+\varphi\circ S_{a,-h})-C_2|h|^2\le\varphi$. Thus $V_{a,h}$ is a subsolution, and $V_{a,h}\le H$. Evaluating at $a$, where $S_{a,\pm h}(a)=a\pm h$, and using $-1\le\psi(a)\le0$, we obtain
\[
 H(a+h)+H(a-h)-2H(a)\le2(C_1+C_2)|h|^2
\]
for all $a\in K$ and $|h|\le h_0$; here the points $a\pm h=S_{a,\pm h}(a)$ lie in $B$.

\emph{Step 3. The equation for data of class $C^2$.}
Since $K$ was arbitrary and $H$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic, \cref{lem:second-difference-regularity} applies on $B$. Thus $H$ is continuous and locally $C^{1,1}$ on $B$, and its distributional real Hessian is a locally bounded measurable matrix $A$. Almost every $x\in B$ satisfies
\[
 H(x+h)=H(x)+\nabla H(x)\cdot h+\tfrac12h^tA(x)h+o(|h|^2)\qquad(h\to0),
\]
and the complex Hessian $(H_{j\bar k})$ computed from $A$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere and satisfies $(\ddc H)^n=n!\pi^{-n}\det(H_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm dV_{2n}=\det(H_{j\bar k})\,\eta^n$. Since $(\ddc H)^n\ge f\eta^n$, we have $\det(H_{j\bar k})\ge f$ almost everywhere, and it remains to prove the reverse inequality.

Suppose, to the contrary, that $\det(H_{j\bar k}(x))>f(x)$ at a point $x$ at which the Taylor expansion holds and $(H_{j\bar k}(x))$ is positive semidefinite. The quadratic Taylor polynomial $Q(x+h)=H(x)+\nabla H(x)\cdot h+\frac12h^tA(x)h$ has the constant complex Hessian $(H_{j\bar k}(x))$, which is positive semidefinite with positive determinant, hence positive definite. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ so small that the matrix $(H_{j\bar k}(x))-\varepsilon I$ is positive definite and its determinant exceeds $f(x)$. By the continuity of $f$ and the Taylor expansion at $x$, there is $r>0$ such that $\overline{B(x,r)}\subset B$, this determinant exceeds $f$ on $B(x,r)$, and $|H-Q|<\varepsilon r^2/2$ on $\partial B(x,r)$. The function
\[
 q=Q-\varepsilon|z-x|^2+\varepsilon r^2/4
\]
is plurisubharmonic (\cref{prop:smooth-psh-criterion}), and $(\ddc q)^n=\det\bigl((H_{j\bar k}(x))-\varepsilon I\bigr)\eta^n\ge f\eta^n$ on $B(x,r)$. Moreover $q<H-\varepsilon r^2/4$ on $\partial B(x,r)$, while $q(x)=H(x)+\varepsilon r^2/4>H(x)$.

Let $\widehat H=\max\{H,q\}$ on $B(x,r)$ and $\widehat H=H$ on $B\setminus B(x,r)$. Since $q$ and $H$ are continuous and $q<H$ on the sphere $\partial B(x,r)$, we have $q<H$ on a neighborhood of this sphere. Hence $\widehat H$ is plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, and it coincides with $H$ near $\partial B(x,r)$. On $B(x,r)$, \cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}, applied with $F=G=f^{1/n}$, gives $(\ddc\widehat H)^n\ge f\eta^n$. Every other point of $B$ has a neighborhood on which $\widehat H=H$, so $(\ddc\widehat H)^n\ge f\eta^n$ near it as well. Since every nonnegative test function on $B$ is a finite sum of nonnegative test functions supported in such neighborhoods (\cref{lem:smooth-partition-unity}), the inequality holds on all of $B$. Thus $\widehat H$ is a subsolution with $\widehat H(x)>H(x)$, a contradiction. Consequently $\det(H_{j\bar k})=f$ almost everywhere, that is, $(\ddc H)^n=f\eta^n$. As $H$ is continuous on $B$ with boundary limits $\varphi$ (Step 1), it extends to a solution in $\PSH(B)\cap C(\overline B)$, which is locally $C^{1,1}$ on $B$.

\emph{Step 4. Stability and continuous data.}
Let $(f,\varphi)$ and $(g,\chi)$ be data of class $C^2$, with envelopes $H_{f,\varphi}$ and $H_{g,\chi}$ as in Step 1. Put $\delta=\|\varphi-\chi\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}$ and $\sigma=\|f^{1/n}-g^{1/n}\|_{L^\infty(B)}$. By \cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra},
\[
 \bigl(\ddc(H_{g,\chi}+\sigma\psi-\delta)\bigr)^n\ge(g^{1/n}+\sigma)^n\eta^n\ge f\eta^n,
\]
and $H_{g,\chi}+\sigma\psi-\delta$ has boundary limits $\chi-\delta\le\varphi$. It is therefore a subsolution for $(f,\varphi)$, and since $\psi\ge-1$ we get $H_{g,\chi}-\sigma-\delta\le H_{f,\varphi}$. Exchanging the roles of the two data gives \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability} with $R=1$ for data of class $C^2$.

Now let $f$ and $\varphi$ be continuous. Extend $F$ to $\mathbb C^n$ by $F(z)=F(z/|z|)$ for $|z|>1$ and convolve with mollifiers; this gives smooth functions $F_j\ge0$ with $F_j\to F$ uniformly on $\overline B$ (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}(1)). Similarly, extending $\varphi$ to a neighborhood of $\partial B$ by $\varphi(z)=\varphi(z/|z|)$ and mollifying, we obtain smooth functions $\varphi_j$ on a neighborhood of $\partial B$ with $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ uniformly on $\partial B$. The data $(F_j^n,\varphi_j)$ are of class $C^2$, and by Steps 1--3 the corresponding envelopes $H_j=H_{F_j^n,\varphi_j}$ are solutions which are continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $\varphi_j$. By the estimate just proved,
\[
 \|H_j-H_k\|_{L^\infty(B)}\le\|\varphi_j-\varphi_k\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}+\|F_j-F_k\|_{L^\infty(B)},
\]
so $(H_j)$ is a uniform Cauchy sequence on $\overline B$. Its uniform limit $U$ is continuous on $\overline B$ and equal to $\varphi$ on $\partial B$. By \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, $U$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$ and $(\ddc H_j)^n\to(\ddc U)^n$ weakly; since $F_j^n\to f$ uniformly on $\overline B$, we obtain $(\ddc U)^n=f\eta^n$.

\emph{Step 5. The envelope, uniqueness and stability.}
Let $U$ be a solution of the problem that is continuous on $\overline B$, for instance the one constructed in Step 4, and let $v$ be a subsolution for $(f,\varphi)$. For $\varepsilon>0$ put $v_\varepsilon=v+\varepsilon\psi$. By \cref{lem:continuous-density-subsolution-algebra}, $(\ddc v_\varepsilon)^n\ge(F+\varepsilon)^n\eta^n$, while $(\ddc U)^n=F^n\eta^n$. Since $\varliminf_{z\to\zeta}(U(z)-v_\varepsilon(z))\ge0$ for every $\zeta\in\partial B$, the comparison principle \cref{thm:bt-comparison} gives
\[
 \int_{\{U<v_\varepsilon\}}(F+\varepsilon)^n\eta^n
 \le\int_{\{U<v_\varepsilon\}}(\ddc v_\varepsilon)^n
 \le\int_{\{U<v_\varepsilon\}}F^n\eta^n.
\]
These integrals are finite, because $F$ is bounded on $B$, and $(F+\varepsilon)^n-F^n\ge\varepsilon^n$, so the Borel set $\{U<v_\varepsilon\}$ is Lebesgue null. Hence $\max\{U,v_\varepsilon\}=U$ almost everywhere, and the uniqueness assertion of \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative} gives $U\ge v_\varepsilon$ everywhere. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, we get $U\ge v$. Since $U$ is itself a subsolution, it is the pointwise supremum of all subsolutions. In particular, any two solutions in $\PSH(B)\cap C(\overline B)$ coincide with this supremum, which proves uniqueness. For data of class $C^2$ the solution is therefore the envelope of Step 1, which is locally $C^{1,1}$ by Step 3.

Finally, if $(f,\varphi)$ and $(g,\chi)$ are continuous data, approximate both as in Step 4. By uniqueness, the corresponding solutions converge uniformly to $H_{f,\varphi}$ and $H_{g,\chi}$, and \eqref{eq:ball-dirichlet-root-stability} for the approximating data of class $C^2$ passes to the limit. Undoing the affine change of variables proves all assertions of the theorem.
\end{proof}
