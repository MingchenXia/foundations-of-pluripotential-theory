\section{Gradients against positive currents}
\label{sec:bt-current-gradients}
Several later arguments differentiate the product $v\Theta$ of a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a closed positive current $\Theta$; examples are the proof that non-pluripolar products are closed (\cref{thm:nonpluripolar-product-closed}) and cutoff arguments on compact manifolds. For smooth $v$ the derivative is $\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$, and its size is controlled by the gradient measure $\mathrm dv\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v\wedge\Theta$, with $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ as in \eqref{eq:dc-operator}. We extend both objects, together with the chain rule, to locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions by decreasing approximation. In this section $\eta$ denotes a real one-form, not the form $\ddc|z|^2$ of the preceding sections.

Throughout, $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ is open and $\Theta$ is a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on $U$, with trace measure $\mu=\Theta\wedge\beta$. We first describe $\Theta$ by a matrix of densities with respect to $\mu$. For $1\leq j,k\leq n$ let $\nu_{jk}$ be the complex Radon measure $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\mathrm dz_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j\wedge\Theta$. Up to a constant factor, $\nu_{jk}$ is a coefficient measure of $\Theta$, so by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero} there is a constant $C$, depending only on $n$, with $|\nu_{jk}|\leq C\mu$. Hence $C\mu+\Real\nu_{jk}$ and $C\mu+\Img\nu_{jk}$ are positive measures bounded by $2C\mu$, and \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density}, applied to them, gives bounded Borel functions $h_{jk}$ with
\[
 \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\mathrm dz_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j\wedge\Theta=h_{jk}\,\mu
 \qquad(1\leq j,k\leq n).
\]

Since $\Theta$ is real (\cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}), the complex conjugate of $\nu_{jk}$ is $\frac{-\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\mathrm d\bar z_k\wedge\mathrm dz_j\wedge\Theta=\nu_{kj}$; by the uniqueness of densities in \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density}, $h_{kj}=\bar h_{jk}$ $\mu$-almost everywhere, so the matrix $H=(h_{jk})$ is Hermitian $\mu$-almost everywhere. Since $\sum_j\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j=\beta/\pi$, summing the identities with $j=k$ gives $\operatorname{tr}H=1/\pi$ $\mu$-almost everywhere.

We identify a $(1,0)$-form $\xi=\sum_k\xi_k\,\mathrm dz_k$ with its coefficient vector $(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\in\mathbb C^n$, and write $\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_ja_j\bar b_j$ on $\mathbb C^n$. For a constant $(1,0)$-form $\xi$, the measure $(\mathrm i/2\pi)\,\xi\wedge\bar\xi\wedge\Theta=\langle H\xi,\xi\rangle\mu$ is positive, because $\mathrm i\xi\wedge\bar\xi$ is a strongly positive form and $\Theta$ is positive; so $\langle H\xi,\xi\rangle\geq0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. Applying this to the countably many $\xi$ with rational real and imaginary parts, and using that $\xi\mapsto\langle H\xi,\xi\rangle$ is continuous, we see that $H$ is positive semidefinite $\mu$-almost everywhere. Changing $H$ on a $\mu$-null set, we may assume that it is Hermitian and positive semidefinite with trace $1/\pi$ everywhere. Its eigenvalues then lie in $[0,1/\pi]$, so that $\langle H\xi,\xi\rangle\leq|\xi|^2/\pi$.

Let $H^{1/2}$ be the positive semidefinite square root of $H$. It is a bounded Borel function on $U$. Indeed, by the Weierstrass approximation theorem there are real polynomials $p_m$ with $p_m(t)\to\sqrt t$ uniformly on $[0,1/\pi]$. Diagonalizing $H(x)$ by a unitary matrix, we see that every entry of $p_m(H(x))-H(x)^{1/2}$ is bounded by $\sup_{[0,1/\pi]}|p_m(t)-\sqrt t|$. The entries of $p_m(H)$ are polynomials in the $h_{jk}$, hence Borel functions, and so are their pointwise limits, the entries of $H^{1/2}$. Moreover $|H^{1/2}\xi|^2=\langle H\xi,\xi\rangle\leq|\xi|^2/\pi$.

For a real $C^1$ function $w$, the coefficient vector of the form $\partial w$ is $(\partial w/\partial z_k)_{k=1}^n$, and we denote it also by $\partial w$. Since $\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w=(\mathrm i/2\pi)\,\partial w\wedge\bar\partial w$ by \eqref{eq:bt-gradient-form}, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-gradient-density}
 \mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta=\bigl|H^{1/2}\partial w\bigr|^2\mu.
\end{equation}
Thus, for a compact set $K\subset U$, the square of the \emph{energy seminorm}\index{gradient measure!energy seminorm} $\|H^{1/2}\partial w\|_{L^2(K,\mu)}$ is the mass that $\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta$ gives to $K$.

Similarly, let $\eta$ be a real one-form with continuous coefficients and $(1,0)$-part $\sum_ke_k\,\mathrm dz_k$, and put $e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ and $w_k=\partial w/\partial z_k$. Only the $(1,1)$-part
\[
 \sum_{j,k}(e_k\bar w_j-\bar e_jw_k)\,\mathrm dz_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j
\]
of $\eta\wedge\mathrm dw$ contributes to $\eta\wedge\mathrm dw\wedge\Theta$, so that $\eta\wedge\mathrm dw\wedge\Theta=-2\pi\mathrm i\bigl(\langle He,\partial w\rangle-\langle H\partial w,e\rangle\bigr)\mu$. Since $H$ is Hermitian, the second inner product is the complex conjugate of the first, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-gradient-pairing}
 \eta\wedge\mathrm dw\wedge\Theta=4\pi\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,H^{1/2}\partial w\bigr\rangle\,\mu.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-psh-current-chain-rule}
Let $\Theta$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on an open set $U\subset\mathbb C^n$, and let $v$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $U$. Then the current $\mathrm d(v\Theta)$ has measure coefficients; we denote it by $\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$. The current
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-current-gradient-measure}
 E_\Theta(v)=\mathrm dv\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v\wedge\Theta
       =\tfrac12\ddc(v^2\Theta)-v\,\ddc v\wedge\Theta
\end{equation}
is a positive measure, the \emph{gradient measure} of $v$ with respect to $\Theta$. If smooth plurisubharmonic functions $v_l$ decrease to $v$ on a neighborhood of a compact set $K\subset U$, then their gradients $\partial v_l$ converge in the energy seminorm on $K$, that is, $H^{1/2}\partial v_l$ converges in $L^2(K,\mu)$. For every $f\in C^1(\mathbb R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-current-chain-rule}
 \mathrm d(f(v)\Theta)=f'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta.
\end{equation}
Finally, let $\eta$ be a compactly supported smooth real one-form on $U$, and let $\tau\in C_c(U)$, $\tau\geq0$, be equal to one on $\Supp\eta$. There is a constant $C_\eta$, depending only on $\eta$, such that for every bounded Borel function $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-gradient-cauchy-schwarz}
 \left|\int a\eta\wedge\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta\right|^2
 \leq C_\eta\left(\int\tau E_\Theta(v)\right)
                 \left(\int_{\Supp\eta}|a|^2\,
                                         \Theta\wedge\beta\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We approximate $v$ by smooth plurisubharmonic functions $v_l$ and pass to the limit, first in the gradients (Step 1) and then in each assertion of the lemma (Steps 2--5). All assertions are local, so we fix a compact set $K\subset U$ and an open neighborhood $W\subset U$ of $K$ on which smooth plurisubharmonic functions $v_l$ decrease to $v$. For the convergence assertion, $W$ and the $v_l$ are given. Otherwise we take for $W$ a relatively compact open neighborhood of $K$ in $U$ and for $v_l$ the radial mollifications $v*\rho_{\varepsilon_l}$ with $\varepsilon_l\downarrow0$; after discarding finitely many indices, they are smooth and plurisubharmonic on $W$ and decrease to $v$ (\cref{prop:smooth-psh-criterion}). Choose a compact set $K_1\subset W$ with $K\subset\Int K_1$, and a function $\sigma\in C^\infty_c(W)$ with $\sigma\geq0$ which equals one on a neighborhood of $K_1$. Since $v\leq v_l\leq v_1$, the $v_l$ are uniformly bounded on $\Supp\sigma$.

\emph{Step 1. Convergence of the gradients.}
For a smooth real function $w$, we have $\ddc(w^2)=2\,\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w+2w\,\ddc w$, and $\ddc(w^2\Theta)=\ddc(w^2)\wedge\Theta$ because $\Theta$ is closed. Hence
\[
 \mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta
       =\tfrac12\ddc(w^2\Theta)-w\,\ddc w\wedge\Theta.
\]
Apply this identity to $w=v_l-v_m$ and integrate against $\sigma$:
\[
 \int\sigma\,\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta
 =\tfrac12\int(v_l-v_m)^2\ddc\sigma\wedge\Theta
  -\int\sigma(v_l-v_m)\,\ddc(v_l-v_m)\wedge\Theta.
\]
Both terms on the right tend to zero as $l,m\to\infty$. For the first, $|v_l-v_m|\leq v_N-v$ for $l,m\geq N$, and $v_N-v\to0$ pointwise and boundedly on $\Supp\sigma$; since $\ddc\sigma\wedge\Theta$ is a signed measure with total variation bounded by a multiple of $\mu$ on $\Supp\sigma$, dominated convergence applies. For the second, expand it into the four integrals $\int\sigma v_p\,\ddc v_q\wedge\Theta$ with $p,q\in\{l,m\}$, taken with the signs $+,-,-,+$. Given indices $l_k,m_k\to\infty$, pass to a subsequence along which both are strictly increasing; then $v_{l_k}$ and $v_{m_k}$ decrease to $v$, and the weighted form of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} on $W$, with the base current $\Theta$, shows that each of the four integrals tends to $\int\sigma v\,\ddc v\wedge\Theta$. Their signed sum therefore tends to zero, and since this holds for a subsequence of every such pair of index sequences, the second term tends to zero as $l,m\to\infty$. Since $\sigma\geq0$, $\sigma=1$ on $K_1$ and $\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta$ is the positive measure $|H^{1/2}\partial w|^2\mu$, by \eqref{eq:current-gradient-density}, the left side bounds $\|H^{1/2}\partial v_l-H^{1/2}\partial v_m\|_{L^2(K_1,\mu)}^2$. Consequently $H^{1/2}\partial v_l$ is a Cauchy sequence in $L^2(K_1,\mu)$; let $\Gamma\in L^2(K_1,\mu)$ be its limit. In particular, $H^{1/2}\partial v_l$ converges in $L^2(K,\mu)$, which is the convergence assertion.

\emph{Step 2. The first derivative.}
Let $\eta$ be a smooth real one-form with compact support in $\Int K_1$, with $(1,0)$-part $\sum_ke_k\,\mathrm dz_k$. By \eqref{eq:current-gradient-pairing},
\[
 \int\eta\wedge\mathrm dv_l\wedge\Theta
 =4\pi\int\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,H^{1/2}\partial v_l\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm d\mu
 \longrightarrow4\pi\int\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,\Gamma\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm d\mu,
\]
since $H^{1/2}e$ is bounded and vanishes outside $K_1$, and $\mu(K_1)<\infty$. On the other hand, $\mathrm dv_l\wedge\Theta=\mathrm d(v_l\Theta)$ since $\Theta$ is closed, and $v_l\Theta\to v\Theta$ weakly on $W$ by dominated convergence on the coefficient measures; hence $\mathrm d(v_l\Theta)\to\mathrm d(v\Theta)$ weakly. Therefore the pairing of $\mathrm d(v\Theta)$ with $\eta$, written $\int\eta\wedge\mathrm d(v\Theta)$, equals the last integral. Write $\langle H^{1/2}e,\Gamma\rangle=\sum_ke_kG_k$, where $G_k=\sum_j(H^{1/2})_{jk}\bar\Gamma_j$ belongs to $L^2(K_1,\mu)\subset L^1(K_1,\mu)$, so that
\[
 4\pi\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,\Gamma\bigr\rangle
 =\sum_k\bigl(-2\pi\mathrm i\,G_k\,e_k+2\pi\mathrm i\,\bar G_k\,\bar e_k\bigr).
\]
Thus, on real test one-forms $\eta$ supported in $\Int K_1$, the current $\mathrm d(v\Theta)$ acts by integrating against $\mu$ the coefficients $e_k$ and $\bar e_k$ of $\eta$, multiplied by the functions $-2\pi\mathrm i\,G_k$ and $2\pi\mathrm i\,\bar G_k$, which are integrable on $K_1$. Every test one-form is a complex linear combination of two real ones, so on $\Int K_1$ the current $\mathrm d(v\Theta)$ has measure coefficients, which are absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$ and finite on $K_1$. In particular, for a bounded Borel function $a$,
\[
 \int a\eta\wedge\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta=4\pi\int a\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,\Gamma\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm d\mu.
\]

\emph{Step 3. The gradient measure.}
Since $H^{1/2}\partial v_l\to\Gamma$ in $L^2(K_1,\mu)$, the densities $|H^{1/2}\partial v_l|^2$ converge to $|\Gamma|^2$ in $L^1(K_1,\mu)$, so the measures $\mathrm dv_l\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v_l\wedge\Theta=|H^{1/2}\partial v_l|^2\mu$ converge weakly on $\Int K_1$ to the positive measure $|\Gamma|^2\mu$. On the other hand, by the identity of Step 1,
\[
 \mathrm dv_l\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v_l\wedge\Theta
 =\tfrac12\ddc(v_l^2\Theta)-v_l\,\ddc v_l\wedge\Theta
 \longrightarrow\tfrac12\ddc(v^2\Theta)-v\,\ddc v\wedge\Theta
\]
weakly on $W$: the first term converges by dominated convergence on the coefficient measures of $v_l^2\Theta$ and the weak continuity of $\ddc$, and the second by \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}. Hence the right side of \eqref{eq:bounded-current-gradient-measure} is the positive measure $|\Gamma|^2\mu$ on $\Int K_1$. Since $K$ was arbitrary and $K\subset\Int K_1$, the current $E_\Theta(v)$, which does not involve the approximations, is a positive measure on $U$.

\emph{Step 4. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.}
Let $\eta$, $\tau$ and $a$ be as in the statement, and apply Steps 1--3 with $K=\Supp\tau$; then $\Supp\eta\subset K\subset\Int K_1$. For a smooth real function $w$ on $W$, \eqref{eq:current-gradient-pairing} and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give
\[
 \left|\int a\eta\wedge\mathrm dw\wedge\Theta\right|^2
 \leq16\pi^2\int_{\Supp\eta}|a|^2\bigl|H^{1/2}e\bigr|^2\,\mathrm d\mu
      \int_{\Supp\eta}\bigl|H^{1/2}\partial w\bigr|^2\,\mathrm d\mu.
\]
Since $|H^{1/2}e|^2\leq|e|^2/\pi$, we have $16\pi^2|H^{1/2}e|^2\leq C_\eta$ with $C_\eta=16\pi\sup|e|^2$, which depends only on $\eta$. The second integral is at most $\int\tau\,\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge\Theta$, because $\tau\geq0$ and $\tau=1$ on $\Supp\eta$. Apply this to $w=v_l$ and let $l\to\infty$. The left side converges to $|\int a\eta\wedge\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta|^2$ by Step 2, since $a\,\Img\langle H^{1/2}e,H^{1/2}\partial v_l\rangle\to a\,\Img\langle H^{1/2}e,\Gamma\rangle$ in $L^1(K_1,\mu)$, and $\int\tau\,\mathrm dv_l\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v_l\wedge\Theta\to\int\tau E_\Theta(v)$ by Step 3, since $\tau$ is continuous with compact support in $\Int K_1$. This proves \eqref{eq:current-gradient-cauchy-schwarz}.

\emph{Step 5. The chain rule.}
Both sides of \eqref{eq:bounded-current-chain-rule} are complex-linear in $f$, so we may assume that $f$ is real. Let first $f$ be smooth. The functions $v_l$ and $v$ take values in a compact interval $J$ on $\Supp\sigma$. Since $f(v_l)\to f(v)$ boundedly, $f(v_l)\Theta\to f(v)\Theta$ weakly on $W$ by dominated convergence, and hence $\mathrm d(f(v_l)\Theta)\to\mathrm d(f(v)\Theta)$. By the chain rule for smooth functions and the closedness of $\Theta$, $\mathrm d(f(v_l)\Theta)=f'(v_l)\,\mathrm dv_l\wedge\Theta$. Moreover $f'(v_l)H^{1/2}\partial v_l\to f'(v)\Gamma$ in $L^2(K_1,\mu)$: the difference is
\[
 f'(v_l)\bigl(H^{1/2}\partial v_l-\Gamma\bigr)+\bigl(f'(v_l)-f'(v)\bigr)\Gamma,
\]
where the first term tends to zero because $f'$ is bounded on $J$, and the second by dominated convergence. Now let $\eta$ be as in Step 2. Identity \eqref{eq:current-gradient-pairing}, multiplied by the continuous function $f'(v_l)$, and Step 2 with $a=f'(v)$ give
\[
 \int\eta\wedge f'(v_l)\,\mathrm dv_l\wedge\Theta
 =4\pi\int\Img\bigl\langle H^{1/2}e,f'(v_l)H^{1/2}\partial v_l\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm d\mu
 \longrightarrow\int\eta\wedge f'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta.
\]
Since the left side also tends to $\int\eta\wedge\mathrm d(f(v)\Theta)$, the currents $\mathrm d(f(v)\Theta)$ and $f'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$ agree on $\Int K_1$. As $K$ was arbitrary, this proves \eqref{eq:bounded-current-chain-rule} for smooth $f$.

Now let $f\in C^1(\mathbb R)$, and let $f_m=f*\rho_{1/m}$ be its mollifications on $\mathbb R$. By \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing} and \cref{lem:mollifier-density}, $f_m\to f$ and $f_m'=f'*\rho_{1/m}\to f'$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb R$. Since $v$ is locally bounded, $f_m(v)\to f(v)$ and $f_m'(v)\to f'(v)$ uniformly on compact subsets of $U$. As $\Theta$ and $\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$ are currents of order zero, it follows that $f_m(v)\Theta\to f(v)\Theta$ and $f_m'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta\to f'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$ weakly. Since $\mathrm d$ is weakly continuous, the identity $\mathrm d(f_m(v)\Theta)=f_m'(v)\,\mathrm dv\wedge\Theta$ passes to the limit.
\end{proof}
