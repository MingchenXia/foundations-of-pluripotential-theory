\subsection{Ordered error sets, monotone nets and weights}
\label{sec:capacity-nets-weighted-limits}
Convergence locally in capacity in the sense of \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence} controls the two one-sided error sets $\{u_a>u+\delta\}$ and $\{u_a<u-\delta\}$, while extended capacity convergence controls the arctangent distance. For plurisubharmonic functions the two notions coincide, even when the limit has poles; the comparison uses the quasi-continuity of the limit. We then show that monotone nets of plurisubharmonic functions converge in this sense, and we extend \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} to varying bounded weights.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}
Let $(u_a)$ be a net of plurisubharmonic functions and $u$ a plurisubharmonic function on a bounded open set $D\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$. Then $u_a\to u$ locally in capacity in the sense of \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence} if and only if $u_a\to u$ in extended capacity. A decreasing net whose pointwise limit $u$ is plurisubharmonic converges to $u$ in this sense. So does an increasing net whose regularized upper envelope $u=(\sup_au_a)^*$ is plurisubharmonic; in particular, an increasing net that converges almost everywhere to a plurisubharmonic function $u$ converges to $u$ in this sense.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Equivalence.} Fix a compact set $K\subset D$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity} there is an open set $G$ of outer capacity less than $\varepsilon$, containing the poles of $u$ near $K$, such that $u$ is finite and continuous on the compact set $F=K\setminus G$. Then $u$ is bounded on $F$, say $-C\leq u\leq M$. For $\delta>0$, the two continuous positive functions
\[
 s\longmapsto\arctan(s+\delta)-\arctan s,
 \qquad s\longmapsto\arctan s-\arctan(s-\delta)
\]
have a common positive lower bound $b$ on $[-C,M]$. Hence, at a point of $F$, an ordered error of size $\delta$ in either direction implies $d_*(u_a,u)>b/2$. By subadditivity, the outer capacities of the two ordered error sets in $K$ are at most $\varepsilon$ plus the outer capacity of $K\cap\{d_*(u_a,u)>b/2\}$. Thus extended capacity convergence, followed by $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, gives convergence in the ordered sense. Conversely, $|\arctan s-\arctan t|\leq|s-t|$ for finite $s,t$; if one value is $-\infty$ and the other finite, there is an ordered error for every finite threshold; and equal infinite values have extended distance zero. Hence the set where $d_*(u_a,u)>\delta$ is contained in the union of the two ordered error sets of size $\delta$, and subadditivity proves the converse.

\emph{Decreasing nets.} Let $u_a$ decrease to $u$, so that $u_a\geq u$. With $F$ and $G$ as above, the functions $u_a-u$ are finite and upper semicontinuous on the compact set $F$, and decrease pointwise to zero. Hence the closed sets $\{u_a-u\geq\delta\}\cap F$ have empty intersection, so finitely many of them already have empty intersection, and since the net is decreasing, all sets with a sufficiently large index are empty. Consequently, for large $a$ the only ordered error in $K$ lies in $G$, whose capacity is less than $\varepsilon$. This proves convergence. Only compactness is used, so the argument applies to nets and not only to sequences.

\emph{Increasing nets.} Let the net increase, with $u=(\sup_au_a)^*$ plurisubharmonic. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, there are countably many indices $b_1,b_2,\ldots$ such that $(\sup_iu_{b_i})^*=u$. Since the index set is directed, there is an increasing sequence of indices $a_k$ with $a_k\geq b_1,\ldots,b_k$, so that $u_{a_k}\geq\max\{u_{b_1},\ldots,u_{b_k}\}$. Since also $u_{a_k}\leq\sup_au_a\leq u$, the regularized supremum of the sequence $(u_{a_k})$ is $u$, and by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} the increasing limit $\lim_ku_{a_k}=\sup_ku_{a_k}$ equals $u$ almost everywhere. Each member of the net satisfies $u_a\leq u$. Hence, for every $C>0$, the truncations $\max\{u_{a_k},-C\}$ are locally uniformly bounded and increase almost everywhere to $\max\{u,-C\}$, so they converge in capacity by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}. By \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations} and the equivalence just proved, the sequence $(u_{a_k})$ converges to $u$ locally in capacity in the ordered sense. For $a\geq a_k$, monotonicity gives
\[
 \{u_a<u-\delta\}\subseteq\{u_{a_k}<u-\delta\},
 \qquad\{u_a>u+\delta\}=\varnothing,
\]
so the estimates for the sequence give convergence of the whole net; the indices $a_k$ need not be cofinal. Finally, suppose that the increasing net converges to a plurisubharmonic function $u$ outside a Lebesgue null set $N$. For $x\notin N$ we have $\sup_au_a(x)=\lim_au_a(x)=u(x)$, so $u_a\leq u$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere: $\max\{u_a,u\}$ and $u$ are plurisubharmonic and agree almost everywhere, so they agree everywhere (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative,thm:subharmonic-distribution}). Thus $F=\sup_au_a\leq u$, so that $F^*\leq u$, as $u$ is upper semicontinuous. Moreover $F^*$ is plurisubharmonic and equals $F$, hence $u$, almost everywhere, by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}; therefore $F^*=u$, and the previous case applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weighted-nonpluripolar-capacity-limits}
Let $T_{j,k}$, $T_j$, $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and $R_k$, $R$ be as in \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}. Let $\chi_k$ and $\chi$ be Borel functions on $X$ with $0\leq\chi_k,\chi\leq A<\infty$, where $\chi$ is quasi-continuous, and suppose that $\chi_k\to\chi$ locally in capacity. Then for every smooth strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta$ on $X$,
\[
 \liminf_k\int_X\chi_k R_k\wedge\eta
                          \geq\int_X\chi R\wedge\eta.
\]
If moreover
\[
 \limsup_k\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}
                         \leq\int_XR\wedge\omega^{n-p},
\]
then $\chi_kR_k\to\chi R$ weakly as currents, and weakly as Radon measures when $p=n$; this holds in particular when $\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}\to\int_XR\wedge\omega^{n-p}$. The lower inequality also holds locally for finite local products whose potentials are locally uniformly bounded above. By \cref{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}, the hypothesis of extended capacity convergence is satisfied when, for each $j$, the potentials $u_{j,k}$ decrease to $u_j$, or increase to $u_j$ almost everywhere.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower inequality.} On a relatively compact coordinate ball, let $v_{j,k}$, $v_j$ be the local plurisubharmonic potentials, and $v_{j,k}^C$, $v_j^C$ and $a_k$, $a$ the truncations and cutoff weights, depending on $C,\varepsilon>0$, of the proof of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}. We may assume $A>0$. The bounded Borel weights $\chi_ka_k$ converge locally in capacity to the bounded quasi-continuous function $\chi a$, because $|\chi_ka_k-\chi a|\leq A|a_k-a|+|\chi_k-\chi|$, so that the set where $|\chi_ka_k-\chi a|>\delta$ is contained in the union of the sets where $|a_k-a|>\delta/(2A)$ and where $|\chi_k-\chi|>\delta/2$. (The product $\chi a$ is quasi-continuous, because a finite union of open sets of small capacity has small capacity.) As in that proof, plurifine locality and $0\leq a_k\leq1$ give
\[
 \chi_kR_k\geq\chi_ka_kR_k
              =\chi_ka_k\bigwedge_{j=1}^p\ddc v_{j,k}^C.
\]
By the weighted capacity continuity of bounded products (\cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}), the right side converges weakly to $\chi a\bigwedge_j\ddc v_j^C=\chi aR$. Test with a nonnegative smooth function with compact support times $\eta$, and then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and $C\to\infty$. The weight $a$ increases to one outside the union of the pole sets, which $R$ does not charge, so monotone convergence gives the local lower inequality, and a finite partition of unity gives the global one. The local argument uses only the local upper bounds and the finiteness of the limiting products.

\emph{Convergence.} Under the additional mass hypothesis, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} gives $R_k\to R$ weakly, and in particular $\int_XR_k\wedge\eta\to\int_XR\wedge\eta$. Apply the lower inequality also to the weights $A-\chi_k$, which converge in capacity to $A-\chi$. Since
\[
 \int_X\chi_kR_k\wedge\eta
       =A\int_XR_k\wedge\eta
                     -\int_X(A-\chi_k)R_k\wedge\eta,
\]
this gives the matching upper limit, so $\int_X\chi_kR_k\wedge\eta\to\int_X\chi R\wedge\eta$. Every smooth real $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta$ on $X$ is the difference of two smooth strongly positive forms. Indeed, let $(\rho_i)$ be a finite partition of unity subordinate to coordinate charts. By \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, the constant strongly positive forms span the real forms, so $\rho_i\eta=\sum_lf_le_l$ with constant strongly positive forms $e_l$ and smooth real functions $f_l$ supported in $\Supp\rho_i$; if $\kappa_i\geq0$ is a test function of the chart equal to one on $\Supp\rho_i$ and $c_l\geq\sup|f_l|$, then $(f_l+c_l\kappa_i)e_l$ and $c_l\kappa_ie_l$ are smooth and strongly positive. Hence the convergence holds for all smooth real forms, and by linearity for all smooth forms, so $\chi_kR_k\to\chi R$ weakly as currents. Since $\chi_k\leq A$ and the trace masses of $R_k$ are bounded, the coefficient measures of $\chi_kR_k$ have bounded total variation; when $p=n$, approximating continuous functions on the compact manifold $X$ uniformly by smooth ones, using a partition of unity and \cref{lem:mollifier-density}, upgrades the convergence to weak convergence of Radon measures. Finally, for monotone sequences the extended capacity hypothesis follows from \cref{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}, applied to the local potentials.
\end{proof}
