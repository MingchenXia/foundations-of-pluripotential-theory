\subsection{Ordered error sets, monotone nets and weights}
\label{sec:capacity-nets-weighted-limits}
The arctangent metric permits bounded truncation arguments. For a psh limit it gives exactly the ordered-error notion of \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence}. Quasi-continuity makes this comparison possible even when the limit has poles.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}
Let $u_a,u$ be plurisubharmonic on $D\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$. The net $u_a$ converges locally in capacity to $u$ in the ordered-error sense if and only if it converges in extended capacity. A decreasing net with psh pointwise limit converges in this topology. An increasing net with psh canonical upper envelope $u=(\sup_a u_a)^*$ also converges in this topology. In particular the increasing conclusion holds if the net converges almost everywhere to a psh function $u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $K\Subset D$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, \Cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity} supplies an open $G$ with outer capacity below $\varepsilon$, containing the poles of $u$ near $K$, such that $u$ is finite and continuous on $F=K\setminus G$. It is bounded there, say $-C\leq u\leq M$. For $\delta>0$, the two continuous positive functions
\[
 s\longmapsto\arctan(s+\delta)-\arctan s,
 \qquad s\longmapsto\arctan s-\arctan(s-\delta)
\]
have a common positive lower bound $b$ on $[-C,M]$. Thus either ordered error of size $\delta$ on $F$ implies $d_*(u_a,u)>b/2$. The error capacities are at most $\varepsilon$ plus the capacity of that extended error set. Extended convergence, followed by $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, gives ordered-error convergence. Conversely $|\arctan s-\arctan t|\leq|s-t|$ for finite $s,t$; if one value is $-\infty$ and the other finite, one ordered error occurs for every finite threshold. Equal infinite values have extended distance zero. These observations show that an extended error of size $\delta$ lies in the union of the two ordered errors of size $\delta$. Subadditivity proves the converse.

For a decreasing net, $u_a\geq u$. On the compact $F$ above, $u_a-u$ is finite, upper semicontinuous and decreases pointwise to zero. The closed sets $\{u_a-u\geq\delta\}\cap F$ have empty intersection. Compactness gives finitely many with empty intersection, and a common upper index makes every later set empty. Hence the only ordered error lies in $G$ eventually. Its capacity is below $\varepsilon$, proving convergence without applying an integral convergence theorem to a net.

For an increasing net, the upper-envelope theorem supplies countably many members with regularized supremum $u$. Directedness gives an increasing sequence of indices $a_k$ whose members dominate the first $k$ selected functions. Its regularized supremum is $u$, and \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} makes $u_{a_k}\uparrow u$ almost everywhere. For each fixed $C>0$, the truncations are locally uniformly bounded because $u_{a_k}\leq u$; they increase almost everywhere to $\max\{u,-C\}$. They converge in capacity by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}. The arctangent/truncation equivalence in \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations} gives extended convergence of this sequence, and the first part of the present proof gives its ordered-error convergence. Every member of the original net is at most $u$ pointwise. For $a\geq a_k$,
\[
 \{u_a<u-\delta\}\subseteq\{u_{a_k}<u-\delta\},
 \qquad\{u_a>u+\delta\}=\varnothing.
\]
The sequence estimates therefore give convergence of the full net. No cofinal sequence is required. If the original net has psh almost-everywhere limit $u$, monotonicity and canonical representatives give $u_a\leq u$ pointwise, and its pointwise supremum equals $u$ almost everywhere. Its regularization is consequently $u$, so the proved case applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weighted-nonpluripolar-capacity-limits}
Keep the currents $T_{j,k},T_j$, $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and products $R_k,R$ of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}. Let $0\leq\chi_k,\chi\leq A<\infty$ be quasi-continuous Borel functions with $\chi_k\to\chi$ locally in capacity. Then for every smooth strongly positive test form $\eta$ of complementary bidegree,
\[
 \liminf_k\int_X\chi_k R_k\wedge\eta
                          \geq\int_X\chi R\wedge\eta.
\]
If
\[
 \limsup_k\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}
                         \leq\int_XR\wedge\omega^{n-p},
\]
then $\chi_kR_k\to\chi R$ weakly as currents, and as Radon measures when $p=n$. In particular this holds when the raw mixed trace masses converge. The lower assertion also holds locally for finite local products with common local upper bounds. Monotone psh sequences with psh limits satisfy the capacity hypothesis.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On a relatively compact coordinate ball use the local psh potentials $v_{j,k},v_j$ and the soft cutoff of the preceding theorem:
\[
 v_{j,k}^C=\max\{v_{j,k},-C\},\qquad
 a_k=\prod_{j=1}^p\frac{v_{j,k}^C+C}{v_{j,k}^C+C+\varepsilon},
 \qquad C,\varepsilon>0.
\]
Define $a$ from the limits in the same way. The bounded quasi-continuous weights $\chi_ka_k$ converge in capacity to $\chi a$: their difference is bounded by $A|a_k-a|+|\chi_k-\chi|$. Locality and $0\leq a_k\leq1$ give
\[
 \chi_kR_k\geq\chi_ka_kR_k
              =\chi_ka_k\bigwedge_{j=1}^p\ddc v_{j,k}^C.
\]
Bounded weighted capacity continuity in \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity} passes the last expression to
$\chi a\bigwedge_j\ddc v_j^C=\chi aR$.
Test with a nonnegative compact cutoff times $\eta$, then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and $C\to\infty$. The cutoff $a$ increases to one off the pole union, which $R$ annihilates. Monotone convergence gives the local lower inequality. A finite nonnegative partition of unity gives the global assertion. This proof only uses common local upper bounds and finite limiting products for its local form.

Under the trace-mass hypothesis the preceding theorem gives $R_k\to R$, and hence convergence of the raw trace masses. Apply the proved lower inequality also to $A-\chi_k$, with limit $A-\chi$. Since
\[
 \int_X\chi_kR_k\wedge\eta
       =A\int_XR_k\wedge\eta
                     -\int_X(A-\chi_k)R_k\wedge\eta,
\]
it gives the matching upper limit. Hence the weighted pairings converge. Strongly positive tests span all smooth tests locally by \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, so this is weak current convergence. The uniform bound $\chi_k\leq A$ and bounded raw trace masses give bounded coefficient variations; in top degree smooth approximation of continuous tests on compact $X$ upgrades this to weak Radon-measure convergence. Finally the proposition gives extended-capacity convergence for both kinds of monotone psh sequence, so the same argument applies.
\end{proof}
