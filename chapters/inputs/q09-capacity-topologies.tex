\subsection{Ordered error sets, monotone nets and weights}
\label{sec:capacity-nets-weighted-limits}
Convergence locally in capacity in the sense of \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence} controls the two one-sided error sets $\{u_a>u+\delta\}$ and $\{u_a<u-\delta\}$, while extended capacity convergence controls the arctangent distance. For plurisubharmonic functions the two notions coincide, even when the limit has poles; the comparison uses the quasi-continuity of the limit. We then show that monotone nets of plurisubharmonic functions converge in this sense, and we extend \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} to varying bounded weights.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}
Let $(u_a)$ be a net of plurisubharmonic functions and $u$ a plurisubharmonic function on an open set $D\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$. Then $u_a\to u$ locally in capacity in the sense of \cref{def:local-capacity-convergence} if and only if $u_a\to u$ in extended capacity. A decreasing net whose pointwise limit $u$ is plurisubharmonic converges to $u$ in this sense. So does an increasing net whose regularized upper envelope $u=(\sup_au_a)^*$ is plurisubharmonic; in particular, an increasing net that converges almost everywhere to a plurisubharmonic function $u$ converges to $u$ in this sense.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Equivalence.} Fix a compact set $K\subset D$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity} there is an open set $G$ of outer capacity less than $\varepsilon$, containing the poles of $u$ near $K$, such that $u$ is finite and continuous on the compact set $F=K\setminus G$. Then $u$ is bounded on $F$, say $-C\leq u\leq M$. For $\delta>0$, the two continuous positive functions
\[
 s\longmapsto\arctan(s+\delta)-\arctan s,
 \qquad s\longmapsto\arctan s-\arctan(s-\delta)
\]
have a common positive lower bound $b$ on $[-C,M]$. Hence, at a point of $F$, an ordered error of size $\delta$ in either direction implies $d_*(u_a,u)>b/2$. By subadditivity, the outer capacities of the two ordered error sets in $K$ are at most $\varepsilon$ plus the outer capacity of $K\cap\{d_*(u_a,u)>b/2\}$. Thus extended capacity convergence, followed by $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, gives convergence in the ordered sense. Conversely, $|\arctan s-\arctan t|\leq|s-t|$ for finite $s,t$; if one value is $-\infty$ and the other finite, there is an ordered error for every finite threshold; and equal infinite values have extended distance zero. Hence the set where $d_*(u_a,u)>\delta$ is contained in the union of the two ordered error sets of size $\delta$, and subadditivity proves the converse.

\emph{Decreasing nets.} Let $u_a$ decrease to $u$, so that $u_a\geq u$. With $F$ and $G$ as above, the functions $u_a-u$ are finite and upper semicontinuous on the compact set $F$, and decrease pointwise to zero. Hence the closed sets $\{u_a-u\geq\delta\}\cap F$ have empty intersection, so finitely many of them already have empty intersection, and since the net is decreasing, all sets with a sufficiently large index are empty. Consequently, for large $a$ the only ordered error in $K$ lies in $G$, whose capacity is less than $\varepsilon$. This proves convergence. The argument uses only compactness; no integral convergence theorem, which would require a sequence, is applied to the net.

\emph{Increasing nets.} Let the net increase, with $u=(\sup_au_a)^*$ plurisubharmonic. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, countably many members of the net have the same regularized supremum $u$. Since the index set is directed, there is an increasing sequence of indices $a_k$ such that $u_{a_k}$ dominates the first $k$ selected functions. The regularized supremum of the sequence $(u_{a_k})$ is then also $u$, and by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, $u_{a_k}\uparrow u$ almost everywhere. Each member of the net satisfies $u_a\leq u$. Hence, for every $C>0$, the truncations $\max\{u_{a_k},-C\}$ are locally uniformly bounded and increase almost everywhere to $\max\{u,-C\}$, so they converge in capacity by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}. By \cref{lem:extended-capacity-truncations} and the equivalence just proved, the sequence $(u_{a_k})$ converges to $u$ locally in capacity in the ordered sense. For $a\geq a_k$, monotonicity gives
\[
 \{u_a<u-\delta\}\subseteq\{u_{a_k}<u-\delta\},
 \qquad\{u_a>u+\delta\}=\varnothing,
\]
so the estimates for the sequence give convergence of the whole net; the indices $a_k$ need not be cofinal. Finally, if the increasing net converges almost everywhere to a plurisubharmonic function $u$, then $u_a\leq u$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere, by uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives, and $\sup_au_a=u$ almost everywhere. Therefore $(\sup_au_a)^*=u$, and the previous case applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weighted-nonpluripolar-capacity-limits}
Let $T_{j,k}$, $T_j$, $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and $R_k$, $R$ be as in \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}. Let $\chi_k,\chi$ be quasi-continuous Borel functions on $X$ with $0\leq\chi_k,\chi\leq A<\infty$, and suppose that $\chi_k\to\chi$ locally in capacity. Then for every smooth strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta$ on $X$,
\[
 \liminf_k\int_X\chi_k R_k\wedge\eta
                          \geq\int_X\chi R\wedge\eta.
\]
If moreover
\[
 \limsup_k\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}
                         \leq\int_XR\wedge\omega^{n-p},
\]
then $\chi_kR_k\to\chi R$ weakly as currents, and weakly as Radon measures when $p=n$; this holds in particular when $\int_XR_k\wedge\omega^{n-p}\to\int_XR\wedge\omega^{n-p}$. The lower inequality also holds locally for finite local products whose potentials are locally uniformly bounded above. By \cref{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}, the hypothesis of extended capacity convergence is satisfied when, for each $j$, the potentials $u_{j,k}$ decrease to $u_j$, or increase to $u_j$ almost everywhere.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower inequality.} On a relatively compact coordinate ball, use the local plurisubharmonic potentials $v_{j,k},v_j$ and the cutoff weights of the proof of \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit}:
\[
 v_{j,k}^C=\max\{v_{j,k},-C\},\qquad
 a_k=\prod_{j=1}^p\frac{v_{j,k}^C+C}{v_{j,k}^C+C+\varepsilon},
 \qquad C,\varepsilon>0,
\]
and define $a$ from the limits in the same way. The bounded quasi-continuous weights $\chi_ka_k$ converge in capacity to $\chi a$, because $|\chi_ka_k-\chi a|\leq A|a_k-a|+|\chi_k-\chi|$. As in that proof, plurifine locality and $0\leq a_k\leq1$ give
\[
 \chi_kR_k\geq\chi_ka_kR_k
              =\chi_ka_k\bigwedge_{j=1}^p\ddc v_{j,k}^C.
\]
By the weighted capacity continuity of bounded products (\cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}), the right side converges weakly to $\chi a\bigwedge_j\ddc v_j^C=\chi aR$. Test with a nonnegative smooth function with compact support times $\eta$, and then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and $C\to\infty$. The weight $a$ increases to one outside the union of the pole sets, which $R$ does not charge, so monotone convergence gives the local lower inequality, and a finite partition of unity gives the global one. The local argument uses only the local upper bounds and the finiteness of the limiting products.

\emph{Convergence.} Under the additional mass hypothesis, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-capacity-lower-limit} gives $R_k\to R$ weakly, and in particular $\int_XR_k\wedge\eta\to\int_XR\wedge\eta$. Apply the lower inequality also to the weights $A-\chi_k$, which converge in capacity to $A-\chi$. Since
\[
 \int_X\chi_kR_k\wedge\eta
       =A\int_XR_k\wedge\eta
                     -\int_X(A-\chi_k)R_k\wedge\eta,
\]
this gives the matching upper limit, so $\int_X\chi_kR_k\wedge\eta\to\int_X\chi R\wedge\eta$. Strongly positive forms span all smooth forms locally (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}), so $\chi_kR_k\to\chi R$ weakly as currents. Since $\chi_k\leq A$ and the trace masses of $R_k$ are bounded, the coefficient measures of $\chi_kR_k$ have bounded total variation; when $p=n$, approximating continuous functions on the compact manifold $X$ uniformly by smooth ones upgrades the convergence to weak convergence of Radon measures. Finally, for monotone sequences the extended capacity hypothesis follows from \cref{prop:psh-capacity-topologies}, applied to the local potentials.
\end{proof}
