\subsection{Obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian}
\label{sec:metric-laplacian-obstacles}
The results above show that the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an envelope is carried by its contact set with the obstacle, but they do not identify the measure there. For regular obstacles we can compute it on the contact set from the second derivatives of the obstacle. The regularity we use is a bound on the Laplacian. Recall from \cref{sec:global-trace-equation} that $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$ is the nonnegative Laplacian of the Riemannian metric of the fixed K\"ahler form $\omega$; we call it the \emph{metric Laplacian}. A function $f$ on an open set $U\subset X$ has \emph{(locally) bounded metric Laplacian} if the distribution $Lf$ on $U$ is a (locally) bounded measurable function.
\index{Laplacian!bounded metric Laplacian}

By the trace identity $\Lambda\ddc f=-Lf/(4\pi)$ of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}, where $\Lambda$ is contraction with $\omega$, a bound for $Lf$ is a bound for the trace of the complex Hessian of $f$. For a $\theta$-psh function $u$ the current $\theta+\ddc u$ is positive, and positivity turns this trace bound into bounds for all complex Hessian coefficients. A general obstacle has no such positivity, and its complex Hessian need not be bounded. Nevertheless it has square-integrable weak second derivatives (\cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}), and on the contact set these coincide almost everywhere with the second derivatives of the potential lying below it. This gives the contact formula of \cref{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}. The $L^p$ estimate of \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} shows moreover that such an obstacle has weak second derivatives in every $L^p$ with $p<\infty$, so that wedge products of the corresponding weak forms are integrable on all of $X$ (\cref{cor:metric-laplacian-barrier-determinants}).

The first lemma is the variable-coefficient analogue of \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}. Here $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$ consists of the functions that belong to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}$ in every holomorphic chart of $U$, and Sobolev norms on open subsets of $X$ are computed in the charts of a fixed finite atlas, as in the definition of $W^{k,p}(X)$ in \cref{sec:hessian-singular-integrals}.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}
Let $U\subset X$ be open, and let $f\in L^2_{\loc}(U)$ be such that its distributional metric Laplacian $Lf$ belongs to $L^2_{\loc}(U)$. Then $f\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$. More precisely, for open sets $U'\Subset V\Subset U$ there is a constant $C$, depending only on $U'$, $V$ and $\omega$, such that
\[
 \|f\|_{W^{2,2}(U')}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L^2(V)}+\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}\bigr).
\]
In particular, every continuous function on $U$ with locally bounded metric Laplacian belongs to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use the Sobolev spaces $H^r$ on $X$ and the operator $P=L+c$, with $c>0$ fixed, from the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} (on functions, $\mathcal L=L$). That proof shows that $P$ has an inverse which is bounded from $H^{-1}$ to $H^1$ and from $L^2$ to $H^2$, and that the identities $PP^{-1}=P^{-1}P=1$ extend by density and duality to all distributions on $X$. Since no derivative of $f$ is known in advance, we gain regularity in two steps, using two cutoff functions.

Choose $\chi_0\in C_c^\infty(V)$ equal to one near $\overline{U'}$, and $\chi_1\in C_c^\infty(V)$ supported in the interior of $\{\chi_0=1\}$ and also equal to one near $\overline{U'}$. Extend $\chi_0f$ by zero to a function on $X$. The product rule for distributions gives
\[
 P(\chi_0f)=\chi_0(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi_0]f.
\]
The commutator $[L,\chi_0]$ is a first-order differential operator whose coefficients are smooth and supported in $V$. Pairing $[L,\chi_0]f$ with a test function $\phi\in H^1$ and moving the derivative onto $\phi$, we bound the pairing by $C\|f\|_{L^2(V)}\|\phi\|_{H^1}$. Hence $[L,\chi_0]f\in H^{-1}$, and the right side of the last identity has $H^{-1}$ norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. Applying $P^{-1}$, we obtain $\chi_0f\in H^1$ with the same bound.

In particular $f$ has square-integrable first derivatives on the interior of $\{\chi_0=1\}$, which contains the support of $\chi_1$. Therefore, in the identity
\[
 P(\chi_1f)=\chi_1(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi_1]f,
\]
the commutator term involves only $f$ and its first derivatives on the support of $\mathrm d\chi_1$, and the right side lies in $L^2$, with norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. Since $P^{-1}$ is bounded from $L^2$ to $H^2$, it follows that $\chi_1f\in H^2$ with this bound. By \cref{thm:plancherel}, the space $H^2$ coincides in each chart with $W^{2,2}$, as observed in the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}. Since $\chi_1=1$ near $\overline{U'}$, this proves the estimate, and covering $U$ by such sets $U'$ proves that $f\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$.
\end{proof}
\index{Laplacian!metric weak Hessian}

For the contact formula we work in a coordinate ball. Recall from \cref{sec:positive-currents} that $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ denotes the mixed discriminant of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, and that $\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Euclidean volume in the coordinates.

\Needspace{28\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}
Let $U\subset X$ be open. For $1\leq j\leq n$, let $\theta_j$ be a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$, let $u_j$ be a continuous $\theta_j$-psh function on $U$, and let $f_j$ be a continuous real function on $U$. Suppose that $u_j$ and $f_j$ have locally bounded metric Laplacian, and put
\[
 E=\bigcap_{j=1}^n\{u_j=f_j\}.
\]
Let $W\Subset U$ be a coordinate ball, choose smooth real functions $\rho_j$ on $W$ with $\ddc\rho_j=\theta_j$, and let
\[
 A_j=\bigl(\partial_a\bar\partial_b(\rho_j+u_j)\bigr)_{a,b},
 \qquad
 B_j=\bigl(\partial_a\bar\partial_b(\rho_j+f_j)\bigr)_{a,b}
\]
be the weak complex Hessian matrices, defined almost everywhere on $W$. Then each $A_j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere and locally bounded, and on $W$
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n},\\
 \mathbf1_E\bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\mathbf1_E D(B_1,\ldots,B_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular the density $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\ldots,B_n)$ is nonnegative and locally bounded, although the $f_j$ need not be $\theta_j$-psh.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The potentials $\rho_j$ exist by \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}. By \cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}, all $u_j$ and $f_j$ belong to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$, so the matrices $A_j$ and $B_j$ are defined almost everywhere, and the positive current $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc u_j$ has coefficients in $L^2_{\loc}$.

We first show that $A_j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere. For each $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ the distribution $\sum_{a,b}\partial_a\bar\partial_b(\rho_j+u_j)\xi_a\bar\xi_b$ is positive, because $T_j$ is a positive current; since it is a locally integrable function, it is nonnegative almost everywhere. We apply this to the countably many $\xi$ with rational real and imaginary parts. Outside the union of the corresponding null sets, $\sum_{a,b}(A_j)_{ab}\xi_a\bar\xi_b\geq0$ for all such $\xi$, hence for all $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ by continuity in $\xi$.

Next we bound the coefficients. By the trace identity \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star},
\[
 \Lambda T_j=\Lambda\theta_j-\frac{Lu_j}{4\pi},
\]
which is locally bounded by hypothesis. On a compact subset of $W$, the matrix of $\omega$ in the coordinates is bounded above and below by positive multiples of the identity. Hence the trace of the positive semidefinite matrix $A_j$, which is a fixed multiple of the coefficient matrix of $T_j$, is bounded by a constant times $\Lambda T_j$ there. For a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix every diagonal entry is at most the trace, and every entry satisfies $|a_{ab}|^2\leq a_{aa}a_{bb}$. Therefore $A_j$ is locally bounded.

The functions $\rho_j+u_j$ are continuous and plurisubharmonic on $W$, they have bounded complex Hessian in the sense of \cref{sec:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}, and $\ddc(\rho_j+u_j)=T_j$. The first identity is therefore the first part of \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density}. The functions $\rho_j+f_j$ are real, continuous and belong to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(W)$, and adding $\rho_j$ to both $u_j$ and $f_j$ does not change the contact set $\{u_j=f_j\}$. Hence the second part of \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density} gives the second identity. Its proof shows, through \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}, that $A_j=B_j$ almost everywhere on $\{u_j=f_j\}$, hence on $E$. Consequently $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\ldots,B_n)=\mathbf1_ED(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ almost everywhere. This density is nonnegative, since by the first identity $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is a positive measure, and it is locally bounded, since $D$ is a polynomial in the entries of the locally bounded matrices $A_j$. Only the values of the $B_j$ on $E$ enter here, so no integrability of $D(B_1,\ldots,B_n)$ off $E$ is needed.
\end{proof}
