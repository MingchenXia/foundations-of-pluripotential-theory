The scalar Laplacian controls the trace of a complex Hessian. For a plurisubharmonic potential positivity turns that trace bound into bounds for all complex coefficients. A general obstacle has no such positivity, but its weak Hessian can still be compared with the potential on their contact set.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}
Let $U\subset X$ be open and let $f\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ have $Lf\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ distributionally, where $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$ is the Laplacian of $\omega$. Then $f\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. For $U'\Subset V\Subset U$ there is a constant depending only on these sets and the metric such that
\[
 \|f\|_{W^{2,2}(U')}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L^2(V)}+\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}\bigr).
\]
In particular every continuous function with locally bounded distributional metric Laplacian has this weak Hessian.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} constructs the inverse of $P=L+c$ on $X$, for a fixed $c>0$, with bounded maps $H^{-1}\to H^1$ and $L^2\to H^2$. Its distributional inverse identities hold by density and duality. We use these maps with two cutoffs, so that no derivative of $f$ is assumed in advance.

Choose $\chi_0\in C_c^\infty(V)$ and $\chi_1\in C_c^\infty(\{\chi_0=1\}^{\circ})$, both equal to one near $\overline{U'}$. Extend $\chi_0f$ by zero to $X$. The distributional product rule gives
\[
 P(\chi_0f)=\chi_0(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi_0]f.
\]
The commutator is a first-order operator with smooth compactly supported coefficients. Its action on $f\in L^2(V)$ belongs to $H^{-1}$: moving its derivatives to an $H^1$ test bounds the pairing by $C\|f\|_{L^2(V)}$ times the test norm. The right side consequently has $H^{-1}$ norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. Applying $P^{-1}$ proves that $\chi_0f\in H^1$, with this bound.

On the support of $\chi_1$ we now have $f\in H^1$. The identity with $\chi_1$ therefore has its right side in $L^2$, with norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. The second inverse estimate gives $\chi_1f\in H^2$ with the stated bound. The chart characterization of $H^2$ in the same lemma is $W^{2,2}$, proving the local assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}
Let $U\subset X$ be open. For $1\leq j\leq n$, let $\theta_j$ be closed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms, let $u_j$ be continuous $\theta_j$-psh functions on $U$, and let $f_j$ be continuous real functions there. Suppose $Lu_j$ and $Lf_j$ are locally bounded distributionally. In a coordinate ball choose smooth real $\rho_j$ with $\ddc\rho_j=\theta_j$ and put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_j&=\bigl(\partial_a\bar\partial_b(\rho_j+u_j)\bigr)_{a,b},
 &B_j&=\bigl(\partial_a\bar\partial_b(\rho_j+f_j)\bigr)_{a,b},\\
 E&=\bigcap_{j=1}^n\{u_j=f_j\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
These are weak Hessians defined almost everywhere. The matrices $A_j$ are positive semidefinite and locally bounded. Writing $D$ for the polarized determinant in \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density} and $\mathrm d\lambda$ for coordinate Lebesgue measure, one has
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\,\mathrm d\lambda,\\
 \mathbf1_E\bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\mathbf1_E D(B_1,\ldots,B_n)\,\mathrm d\lambda.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The density on the right of the contact identity is nonnegative and locally bounded. No plurisubharmonicity of the $f_j$ is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lemma gives local $W^{2,2}$ regularity for every $u_j,f_j$. Thus $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc u_j$ has $L^2$ coefficients. Positivity of the current makes its coefficient matrix positive semidefinite almost everywhere: test nonnegative scalar functions against each constant rational complex direction, then take a countable dense set of directions. The trace identity \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star} gives
\[
 \Lambda_\omega T_j=\Lambda_\omega\theta_j-\frac{Lu_j}{4\pi}.
\]
This is locally bounded. For a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix every diagonal entry is at most its trace, and $|a_{ab}|^2\leq a_{aa}a_{bb}$. Smooth metric changes on a compact subball preserve coefficient bounds. Hence $T_j$, and therefore $A_j$, have locally bounded coefficients.

The local psh functions $\rho_j+u_j$ are continuous, so \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density} gives the first identity. By \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}, the weak Hessians of $u_j$ and $f_j$ coincide almost everywhere on $\{u_j=f_j\}$. Adding $\rho_j$ and intersecting the contact sets gives $A_j=B_j$ almost everywhere on $E$. The second identity and its positivity and boundedness follow from the first. The expression $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\ldots,B_n)$ uses only this restriction; no integrability of the unrestricted barrier determinant is needed.
\end{proof}
