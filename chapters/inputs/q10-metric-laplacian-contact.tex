\subsection{Obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian}
\label{sec:metric-laplacian-obstacles}
The results above show that the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an envelope is carried by the contact set, but they do not identify the measure there. For obstacles with bounded Laplacian we now compute the measure on the contact set from the second derivatives of the obstacle. Recall from \cref{sec:global-trace-equation} that $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$ is the Laplace--Beltrami operator of the Riemannian metric defined by the fixed K\"ahler form $\omega$; with our sign convention it is nonnegative, and for the Euclidean metric it equals $-\Delta$. We call $L$ the \emph{metric Laplacian}. A function $f$ on an open set $U\subset X$ has \emph{(locally) bounded metric Laplacian} if the distribution $Lf$ on $U$ is a (locally) bounded measurable function.
\index{Laplacian!metric}\index{Laplacian!bounded metric Laplacian}

By the trace identity $\Lambda\ddc f=-Lf/(4\pi)$ of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}, where $\Lambda$ is the trace with respect to $\omega$, a bound for $Lf$ is a bound for the trace of the complex Hessian of $f$. In local holomorphic coordinates, write $\omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{i,j}h_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ and let $(h^{i\bar j})$ be the inverse matrix, normalized by $\sum_jh^{i\bar j}h_{l\bar j}=\delta_{il}$; then \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}, applied with $\omega'=\omega$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-laplacian-coordinates}
 Lf=-4\pi\sum_{i,j}h^{i\bar j}f_{i\bar j}
\end{equation}
for every distribution $f$ in the coordinate domain. For a $\theta$-psh function $u$ the current $\theta+\ddc u$ is positive, and positivity turns this trace bound into bounds for all complex Hessian coefficients. A general obstacle has no such positivity, and if $n\geq2$ its complex Hessian need not be bounded (\cref{ex:metric-laplacian-unbounded-hessian}). Nevertheless it has square-integrable weak second derivatives (\cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}), and on the set where it agrees with a potential that has bounded metric Laplacian, these coincide almost everywhere with the second derivatives of the potential. This gives the contact formula of \cref{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}. The $L^p$ estimate of \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} shows moreover that such an obstacle has weak second derivatives in every $L^p$ with $p<\infty$, so that wedge products of the corresponding weak forms are integrable on all of $X$ (\cref{cor:metric-laplacian-barrier-determinants}).

\begin{example}\label{ex:metric-laplacian-unbounded-hessian}
Let $n\geq2$ and $x_0\in X$. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z$ centered at $x_0$ in which $h_{i\bar j}(0)=\delta_{ij}$, which is possible after a complex-linear change of coordinates, and choose $0<r_0<\mathrm e^{-1}$ such that the coordinates are defined on a neighborhood of $\{|z|\leq r_0\}$. On $0<|z|<1$ put
\[
 \ell(z)=\log(-\log|z|),\qquad P(z)=|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2,\qquad \phi=P\ell,
\]
and $\phi(0)=0$. Write $r=|z|$. Since $\partial_{z_i}\log r=\bar z_i/(2r^2)$ and $\partial_{\bar z_j}(r^2\log r)=z_j(\log r+\frac12)$, we have
\[
 \partial_{z_i}\ell=\frac{\bar z_i}{2r^2\log r},\qquad
 \partial_{z_i}\partial_{z_j}\ell=-\frac{(\log r+\frac12)\bar z_i\bar z_j}{2r^4(\log r)^2},
\]
\[
 \ell_{i\bar j}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2r^2\log r}-\frac{(\log r+\frac12)\bar z_iz_j}{2r^4(\log r)^2}.
\]
For $0<r<r_0$ we have $\log r<-1$, hence $|\log r+\frac12|\leq|\log r|$, and these formulas give $|\nabla\ell|\leq C/(r|\log r|)$ and $|D^2\ell|\leq C/(r^2|\log r|)$ for the real gradient and Hessian, with a constant $C$ depending only on $n$. Moreover $|P|\leq r^2$, $|\nabla P|\leq2r$, the real Hessian of $P$ is constant, $P_{1\bar1}=1$, $P_{2\bar2}=-1$, and all other $P_{i\bar j}$ vanish. By the Leibniz rule, on $0<r<r_0$,
\[
 |\nabla\phi|\leq2r\ell+\frac{Cr}{|\log r|},\qquad
 |D^2\phi-\ell D^2P|\leq\frac{C}{|\log r|},\qquad
 \Bigl|\phi_{i\bar j}-\ell P_{i\bar j}\Bigr|\leq\frac{C}{|\log r|}.
\]
In particular $\phi$ is continuously differentiable on $\{|z|<r_0\}$, with $\nabla\phi(0)=0$, since $|\phi|\leq r^2\ell$ and $\nabla\phi\to0$ as $z\to0$; and its pointwise second derivatives off the origin are bounded by $C(1+\ell)\leq C(1-\log|z|)$, hence locally integrable. These pointwise derivatives are its distributional second derivatives. Indeed, let $\eta$ be a smooth function on $\mathbb R$ with $\eta=0$ on $(-\infty,1]$ and $\eta=1$ on $[2,\infty)$, put $\eta_\varepsilon(z)=\eta(|z|/\varepsilon)$, and let $\psi$ be a test function on $\{|z|<r_0\}$. Since $\phi$ is $C^1$, we have $\int\phi\,\partial_a\partial_b\psi=-\int\partial_a\phi\,\partial_b\psi$ for real coordinate derivatives $\partial_a,\partial_b$. Splitting $\psi=\eta_\varepsilon\psi+(1-\eta_\varepsilon)\psi$ and integrating by parts once more in the first term, where $\phi$ is smooth, we get
\[
 \int\phi\,\partial_a\partial_b\psi=\int\partial_b\partial_a\phi\,\eta_\varepsilon\psi-\int\partial_a\phi\,\partial_b\bigl((1-\eta_\varepsilon)\psi\bigr).
\]
The last integral is bounded by $\sup_{|z|\leq2\varepsilon}|\nabla\phi|\cdot C_\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}+1)\varepsilon^{2n}$, which tends to zero, and the first tends to $\int\partial_b\partial_a\phi\,\psi$ by dominated convergence.

Choose a smooth function $\chi$ on $X$ with support in $\{|z|<r_0\}$ that equals $1$ on $\{|z|\leq r_0/2\}$, and put $f=\chi\phi$, extended by zero. Then $f$ is continuous. On $\{|z|<r_0/2\}$, \eqref{eq:metric-laplacian-coordinates} and the estimates above give
\[
 \Bigl|\frac{Lf}{4\pi}\Bigr|\leq\ell\,|h^{1\bar1}-h^{2\bar2}|+\frac{C'}{|\log r|}\leq C''\bigl(r\ell+1\bigr),
\]
because $h^{1\bar1}-h^{2\bar2}$ is smooth and vanishes at the origin; elsewhere $f$ is smooth. Hence $f$ has bounded metric Laplacian. On the other hand $f_{1\bar1}=\phi_{1\bar1}\geq\ell-C/|\log r|$ near $x_0$, which is unbounded. Thus the complex Hessian of $f$ is not locally bounded.
\end{example}

We use the Sobolev spaces $W^{k,p}$ of \cref{sec:hessian-singular-integrals}. For an open set $U\subset X$, a function on $U$ belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\loc}(U)$ if it belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\loc}$ in every holomorphic chart of $U$. By \cref{lem:sobolev-leibniz-chain}(2), applied to the coordinate changes, it suffices to check this in the charts of one atlas of $U$, because membership in $W^{k,p}_{\loc}$ is a local property. Indeed, let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be open, covered by open sets $D_1,D_2,\ldots$ (an open cover of $D$ has a countable subcover), and let $v\in W^{k,p}_{\loc}(D_i)$ for every $i$. For $|\alpha|\leq k$, the $L^p_{\loc}$ functions representing $\partial^\alpha v$ on $D_i$ and on $D_j$ agree almost everywhere on $D_i\cap D_j$ (\cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}(4)), so they define a measurable function $g_\alpha$ on $D$; it belongs to $L^p_{\loc}(D)$, since every compact subset of $D$ is covered by finitely many compact balls, each contained in some $D_i$; and the distribution $\partial^\alpha v-g_\alpha$ vanishes on every $D_i$, hence on $D$ (see the remark after \cref{lem:smooth-partition-unity}).

Let $(U_i,\varphi_i)$ and $(\chi_i)$ be the charts and the partition of unity that define the norm of $W^{k,p}(X)$. For an open set $U'\subset X$ we put $\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(U')}=\sum_i\|(\chi_if)\circ\varphi_i^{-1}\|_{W^{k,p}(\varphi_i(U'\cap U_i))}$. Every $v\in W^{k,p}(X)$ belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\loc}(X)$, with a bound: for every holomorphic chart $\psi\colon U_0\to\mathbb C^n$ and every open set $U'\Subset U_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction}
 \|v\circ\psi^{-1}\|_{W^{k,p}(\psi(U'))}\leq C\|v\|_{W^{k,p}(X)},
\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of $v$. Indeed, let $\eta$ be a smooth function on $X$ with compact support in $U_0$ that equals $1$ on $U'$ (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}). The charts $\psi,\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\ldots$ with the partition of unity $\eta,(1-\eta)\chi_1,(1-\eta)\chi_2,\ldots$ are another admissible choice in \cref{sec:hessian-singular-integrals}, so the norm they define is at most a constant times $\|v\|_{W^{k,p}(X)}$; one of its terms is $\|(\eta v)\circ\psi^{-1}\|_{W^{k,p}(\psi(U_0))}$, which dominates the left side of \eqref{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction}.

Finally, the Hilbert spaces $H^r$, $r\geq0$, of \cref{sec:global-trace-equation} are contained in $W^{r,2}(X)$, with $\|u\|_{W^{r,2}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^r}$. Indeed, by Step 1 of the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, on smooth functions the norm of $H^r$ is equivalent to the sum, over the charts used to define it, of the $L^2$ norms of the derivatives of order at most $r$ of the products with the cutoff functions, that is, to a norm of $W^{r,2}(X)$. An element $u\in H^r$ is the limit in $H^r$ of smooth functions $u_j$. In each chart the products of the $u_j$ with the cutoff function therefore form a Cauchy sequence in $W^{r,2}(\mathbb R^{2n})$; their derivatives of order at most $r$ converge in $L^2$, the limits are the weak derivatives of the limit, by testing against smooth functions, and the limit is the product of $u$ with the cutoff function, because $u_j\to u$ in $L^2$.

The first lemma is the variable-coefficient analogue of \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}
Let $U\subset X$ be open, and let $f\in L^2_{\loc}(U)$ be such that its distributional metric Laplacian $Lf$ belongs to $L^2_{\loc}(U)$. Then $f\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$. More precisely, for open sets $U'\Subset V\Subset U$ there is a constant $C$, depending only on $U'$, $V$ and $\omega$ (and on the fixed charts defining the norms), such that
\[
 \|f\|_{W^{2,2}(U')}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L^2(V)}+\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}\bigr).
\]
In particular, every continuous function on $U$ with locally bounded metric Laplacian belongs to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use the Sobolev spaces $H^r$ of \cref{sec:global-trace-equation}. Fix $c>0$. By \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}(3), applied to functions, on which $\mathcal L=L$, the operator $P=L+c$ is a bijection of the space of distributions on $X$ onto itself, and its inverse maps $H^{-1}$ boundedly into $H^1$ and $H^0=L^2$ boundedly into $H^2$. Since no derivative of $f$ is known in advance, we gain regularity in two steps, using two cutoff functions.

For a smooth function $\chi$ on $X$, the Leibniz rules $\mathrm d(\chi u)=\chi\,\mathrm du+u\,\mathrm d\chi$ and $\mathrm d^*(\chi\alpha)=\chi\,\mathrm d^*\alpha-\langle\alpha,\mathrm d\chi\rangle$, for smooth functions $u$ and real $1$-forms $\alpha$, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-laplacian-commutator}
 L(\chi u)=\chi Lu+[L,\chi]u,\qquad [L,\chi]u=-2\langle\mathrm d\chi,\mathrm du\rangle+(L\chi)u,
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the pointwise inner product of real $1$-forms, extended bilinearly. Since $L$ and multiplication by $\chi$ are formally self-adjoint, the transpose of $[L,\chi]$ is $-[L,\chi]$, and \eqref{eq:metric-laplacian-commutator} extends to distributions $u$, with $\langle[L,\chi]u,\phi\rangle=-\langle u,[L,\chi]\phi\rangle$ for smooth $\phi$.

By \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}, choose smooth functions $\chi_0,\chi_1$ on $X$ with compact supports in $V$ such that $\chi_1=1$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{U'}$ and $\chi_0=1$ on a neighborhood of $\Supp\chi_1$. Extend $\chi_0f$ by zero to a function on $X$. By \eqref{eq:metric-laplacian-commutator},
\[
 P(\chi_0f)=\chi_0(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi_0]f.
\]
The first term lies in $L^2$, with norm at most $\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+c\|f\|_{L^2(V)}$, hence in $H^{-1}$ with a comparable norm. For smooth $\phi$, the function $[L,\chi_0]\phi$ is a first-order expression in $\phi$ with smooth coefficients supported in $V$, so
\[
 |\langle[L,\chi_0]f,\phi\rangle|=|\langle f,[L,\chi_0]\phi\rangle|\leq C\|f\|_{L^2(V)}\|\phi\|_{H^1},
\]
because the norm of $H^1$ dominates the $L^2$ norms of the first derivatives in the charts (Step 1 of the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}). Hence $[L,\chi_0]f\in H^{-1}$, and the right side of the last identity has $H^{-1}$ norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. Applying $P^{-1}$, we obtain $\chi_0f\in H^1$ with the same bound.

In particular $f$ has square-integrable first derivatives on the interior of $\{\chi_0=1\}$, which contains $\Supp\chi_1$, with $L^2$ norm bounded in the same way. Therefore the distribution $[L,\chi_1]f=-2\langle\mathrm d\chi_1,\mathrm df\rangle+(L\chi_1)f$ is a square-integrable function, and in the identity
\[
 P(\chi_1f)=\chi_1(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi_1]f
\]
the right side lies in $L^2$, with norm at most $C(\|Lf\|_{L^2(V)}+\|f\|_{L^2(V)})$. Since $P^{-1}$ is bounded from $L^2$ to $H^2$, it follows that $\chi_1f\in H^2\subset W^{2,2}(X)$, with this bound for its norm in $W^{2,2}(X)$. Since $\chi_1=1$ on $U'$, this proves the estimate.

Finally, let $\psi\colon U_0\to\mathbb C^n$ be a holomorphic chart with $U_0\subset U$, and let $U''\Subset U_0$ be open. Applying the preceding argument with $U'=U''$ and some open $V$ with $U''\Subset V\Subset U$, we find $\chi_1f\in W^{2,2}(X)$ with $\chi_1=1$ on $U''$, so $f\circ\psi^{-1}\in W^{2,2}(\psi(U''))$ by \eqref{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction}. Hence $f\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)$. The last assertion holds because continuous functions and locally bounded measurable functions are locally square-integrable.
\end{proof}
\index{Laplacian!metric weak Hessian}

For the contact formula we work in a coordinate ball. Recall that $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ denotes the mixed discriminant of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices (\cref{sec:positive-currents}), that $\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Lebesgue measure in the coordinates, and that $(u_{a\bar b})$, with $u_{a\bar b}=\partial_{z_a}\partial_{\bar z_b}u$, is the complex Hessian matrix of $u$ (\cref{sec:complex-exterior-calculus}).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}
Let $U\subset X$ be open. For $1\leq j\leq n$, let $\theta_j$ be a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$, let $u_j$ be a continuous $\theta_j$-psh function on $U$ with locally bounded metric Laplacian, and let $f_j$ be a real Borel function on $U$ that belongs to $W^{2,1}_{\loc}(U)$; by \cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}, this holds, for instance, if $f_j$ is continuous and has locally bounded metric Laplacian. Put
\[
 E=\bigcap_{j=1}^n\{u_j=f_j\}.
\]
Let $W\Subset U$ be a coordinate ball, choose smooth real functions $\rho_j$ on $W$ with $\ddc\rho_j=\theta_j$, and let
\[
 A_j=\bigl((\rho_j+u_j)_{a\bar b}\bigr)_{a,b},
 \qquad
 B_j=\bigl((\rho_j+f_j)_{a\bar b}\bigr)_{a,b}
\]
be the weak complex Hessian matrices, defined almost everywhere on $W$. Then each $A_j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere and locally bounded, and on $W$
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n},\\
 \mathbf1_E\bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)
       &=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\mathbf1_E D(B_1,\ldots,B_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular the density $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\ldots,B_n)$ is nonnegative and locally bounded, although the $f_j$ need not be $\theta_j$-psh.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The potentials $\rho_j$ exist by \cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}. By \cref{lem:metric-laplacian-local-weak-hessian}, all $u_j$ belong to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(U)\subset W^{2,1}_{\loc}(U)$, so the matrices $A_j$ and $B_j$ are defined almost everywhere, and the positive current $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc u_j=\ddc(\rho_j+u_j)$ has coefficients in $L^2_{\loc}$.

We first show that $A_j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere. For each $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ the distribution $\sum_{a,b}(\rho_j+u_j)_{a\bar b}\xi_a\bar\xi_b$ is positive, because $T_j$ is a positive current; since it is a locally integrable function, it is nonnegative almost everywhere, by \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}(3),(4) and \cref{lem:mollifier-density}(2) (its mollifications are nonnegative and converge to it in $L^1_{\loc}$). We apply this to the countably many $\xi$ with rational real and imaginary parts. Outside the union of the corresponding null sets, $\sum_{a,b}(A_j)_{ab}\xi_a\bar\xi_b\geq0$ for all such $\xi$, hence for all $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$, because these $\xi$ are dense and a quadratic form on $\mathbb C^n$ is continuous.

Next we bound the coefficients. By the trace identity \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star},
\[
 \Lambda T_j=\Lambda\theta_j-\frac{Lu_j}{4\pi},
\]
which is bounded on $W$, since $Lu_j$ is locally bounded and $\overline W$ is compact. Since $T_j=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{a,b}(A_j)_{ab}\,\mathrm dz_a\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_b$, the function $\Lambda T_j$ is the trace $\sum_{a,b}h^{a\bar b}(A_j)_{ab}=\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}A_j)$ of $A_j$ relative to $h$, as in \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}, where $H=(h_{a\bar b})$. As $\overline W$ is compact, $H\leq CI$ on $W$ for a constant $C$, so $H^{-1}\geq C^{-1}I$; since $A_j$ is positive semidefinite, $\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}A_j)=\operatorname{tr}(A_j^{1/2}H^{-1}A_j^{1/2})\geq C^{-1}\operatorname{tr}A_j$ almost everywhere on $W$. For a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix every diagonal entry is at most the trace, and every entry satisfies $|a_{ab}|^2\leq a_{aa}a_{bb}$, by the positivity of the principal $2\times2$ minors. Therefore $A_j$ is bounded on $W$.

The functions $\rho_j+u_j$ are continuous and plurisubharmonic on $W$, and they have bounded complex Hessian in the sense of \cref{sec:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}. The first identity is therefore the first part of \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density}. The functions $\rho_j+f_j$ are real Borel functions in $W^{2,1}_{\loc}(W)$, and adding $\rho_j$ to both $u_j$ and $f_j$ does not change the contact set $\{u_j=f_j\}$. Hence the second part of \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density} gives the second identity. Moreover \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}, applied to $\rho_j+u_j$ and $\rho_j+f_j$, shows that $A_j=B_j$ almost everywhere on $\{u_j=f_j\}$, hence on $E$. Consequently $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\ldots,B_n)=\mathbf1_ED(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ almost everywhere. This density is nonnegative, since the mixed discriminant of positive semidefinite matrices is nonnegative (\cref{sec:positive-currents}), and it is bounded, since $D$ is a polynomial in the entries of the bounded matrices $A_j$.
\end{proof}
