\section{Global poles on compact complex manifolds}
\label{sec:compact-complex-josefson}
We end the chapter by dropping the K\"ahler assumption. Recall that a subset of a complex manifold is pluripolar if it is locally contained in pole sets of plurisubharmonic functions (\cref{def:pluripolar-set}), and that by Josefson's theorem (\cref{thm:josefson}) a pluripolar subset of $\mathbb C^n$ lies in the pole set of a single plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^n$. On a compact connected complex manifold every plurisubharmonic function is constant: it attains its maximum, and the set where it does so is closed by upper semicontinuity and open by the maximum principle (\cref{prop:subharmonic-maximum}), applied in coordinate balls, where plurisubharmonic functions are subharmonic (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}). A global pole function must therefore be quasi-plurisubharmonic, and we prove that every pluripolar subset of a compact complex manifold, K\"ahler or not, lies in the pole set of one quasi-plurisubharmonic function (\cref{thm:compact-complex-global-josefson}).

Throughout this section $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$ (\cref{sec:current-maps}), which need not be closed. Then \cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh} does not apply to $\omega$, but its formula still makes sense: we write $\PSH(X,\omega)$ for the set of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u$ on $X$ with $\omega+\ddc u\geq0$ in the sense of currents, and call its elements \emph{$\omega$-plurisubharmonic}; for an open set $D\subset X$ the class $\PSH(D,\omega)$ is defined in the same way. When $\omega$ is K\"ahler, this is the class of \cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}. We put $\omega_u=\omega+\ddc u$. By the remarks following \cref{def:quasi-psh-and-theta-psh}, every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\Phi$ on $X$ satisfies $\ddc\Phi\geq-C\omega$ for some $C>0$, so $t\Phi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ for $0<t\leq1/C$. In local coordinates $\beta$ denotes the Euclidean form \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}.
\index{plurisubharmonic function!relative to a Hermitian form}

Recall from \cref{def:pluripolar-set} that a subset of a connected complex manifold is globally pluripolar if it lies in the pole set of a single plurisubharmonic function on the manifold. Since every plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is constant, only the empty set is globally pluripolar in $X$ in this sense. On the compact manifold $X$ we therefore replace plurisubharmonic by quasi-plurisubharmonic functions: in this section, a set $A\subset X$ is called \emph{globally pluripolar} if $A\subset\{\Phi=-\infty\}$ for some quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\Phi$ on $X$. Subsets of globally pluripolar sets are globally pluripolar.
\index{pluripolar set!globally pluripolar on compact manifolds}

The proof adapts the capacity argument of \cref{sec:pluripolar-extremals}. For a set $A\subset X$ we consider the global extremal function $T_A$, the upper envelope of the $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions that are nonpositive on $A$; its regularization is bounded unless $A$ is globally pluripolar (\cref{lem:hermitian-extremal-pole-alternative}). If a piece of a pluripolar set were not globally pluripolar, we would approximate it by compact sublevel sets of small capacity. The Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the extremal function of a compact set is carried by that set, and its mass is controlled by the capacity (\cref{prop:hermitian-extremal-mass-support}), so in the limit we would obtain a bounded potential with zero Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Since $\omega$ need not be closed, the total mass $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ is not a cohomological quantity, and we must prove separately that it is positive. This follows from a comparison principle for small gaps (\cref{prop:hermitian-small-gap-comparison}), in which the error terms produced by $\mathrm d\omega$ and $\ddc\omega$ are absorbed by the size of the gap. To show that the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an extremal function vanishes off its set, we freeze the coefficients of $\omega$ on small balls, where the homogeneous balayage of \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} is available, and control the resulting error by a local estimate for nearby bounded potentials (\cref{lem:nearby-bounded-psh-traces}).

We first define products of the currents $\omega_u$ for bounded $u$. Let $u_1,\ldots,u_p\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be bounded, where $1\leq p\leq n$. On a coordinate ball $B$ choose a smooth function $q$ with $\ddc q\geq\omega$ on $B$, for instance a large multiple of $|z|^2$. Then the functions $u_l+q$ are bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, and $\omega_{u_l}=\ddc(u_l+q)+\sigma$ with the smooth form $\sigma=\omega-\ddc q$. On $B$ we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}
 \omega_{u_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{u_p}
 =\sum_{J\subset\{1,\ldots,p\}}\Bigl(\bigwedge_{l\in J}\ddc(u_l+q)\Bigr)\wedge\sigma^{p-|J|},
\end{equation}
where the products of the currents $\ddc(u_l+q)$ are Bedford--Taylor products (\cref{def:bedford-taylor-product}). These products are symmetric (\cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}), and their recursive definition, together with the identity $\ddc(hR)=\ddc h\wedge R$ for a smooth function $h$ and a closed current $R$ of pure bidegree, shows by induction on the number of factors that adding a smooth function $h$ to one potential adds to the product the same product with that factor replaced by the smooth closed form $\ddc h$. If $q$ is replaced by another such function $q'$, each factor $\ddc(u_l+q)$ therefore changes by $\ddc(q'-q)$ and $\sigma$ changes by $-\ddc(q'-q)$; since the right side of \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion} is the expansion of the product of the sums $\ddc(u_l+q)+\sigma$, it does not change. Hence the local definitions agree on overlaps and define a current of order zero on $X$; by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion} it is the exterior product of the forms $\omega_{u_l}$ when the $u_l$ are smooth. The product is symmetric in its factors and affine in each $u_l$. For $u_1=\cdots=u_p=u$ we write $\omega_u^p$, and similarly for mixed powers; the factor $\omega$ is the case $u_l=0$. By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2) below, $\omega_u^n$ is a positive measure; we call it the \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere measure} of $u$ with respect to $\omega$. It is the global counterpart of the measure $(\ddc u)^n$ of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product}.
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!Hermitian background}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}
The following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A finite maximum of functions in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$, and so does the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of a nonempty family in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ that is uniformly bounded above. If $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, $D\subset X$ is open, $v\in\PSH(D,\omega)$, and $\limsup_{D\ni y\to x}v(y)\leq u(x)$ for every $x\in\partial D$, then the function equal to $\max\{u,v\}$ on $D$ and to $u$ on $X\setminus D$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$.
\item Let $u_1,\ldots,u_p\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be bounded, $1\leq p\leq n$. The current $\omega_{u_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{u_p}$ is positive. The total mass of its trace measure with respect to $\omega$ (\cref{sec:current-maps}) is bounded by a constant depending only on $(X,\omega)$ and $\max_l\sup_X|u_l|$, and every pluripolar set has outer measure zero for this trace measure. Moreover, if $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ is bounded and $g$ is a smooth function on a coordinate ball $D$ with $\ddc g\geq\omega$, then $(\ddc(u+g))^n\geq\omega_u^n$ on $D$.
\item For $1\leq l\leq p$ let $(u_{l,j})_{j\geq1}$ be sequences in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\sup_{l,j}\sup_X|u_{l,j}|<\infty$. Suppose that either each sequence decreases pointwise to a function $u_l$, or each sequence increases and $u_l$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of its limit. Then the $u_l$ are bounded functions in $\PSH(X,\omega)$, and $\omega_{u_{1,j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{u_{p,j}}\to\omega_{u_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{u_p}$ weakly.
\item The set of $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\sup_Xu=0$ is compact in $L^1(X)$, hence bounded in $L^1(X)$, and $u\mapsto\sup_Xu$ is continuous on $\PSH(X,\omega)$ for the $L^1$ topology.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) We freeze the coefficients of $\omega$ on small balls. Fix $\varepsilon>0$, a coordinate chart and a compact subset $L$ of it. For $a\in L$ let $\omega(a)$ be the form with constant coefficients equal to those of $\omega$ at $a$. By uniform continuity of the coefficients, there is $r>0$ such that $\gamma_a=\omega(a)+\tfrac\varepsilon2\beta$ satisfies $\omega\leq\gamma_a\leq\omega+\varepsilon\beta$ on $B(a,r)$ for every $a\in L$. Let $g_a$ be a quadratic polynomial with $\ddc g_a=\gamma_a$. If $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, then $\ddc(u+g_a)\geq\gamma_a-\omega\geq0$, so $u+g_a$ is plurisubharmonic on $B(a,r)$. Apply \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} (for maxima and for the pasted function) or \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} (for regularized suprema) to the functions $u+g_a$ on $B(a,r)$, and subtract $g_a$. Since $g_a$ is continuous, this gives the function $w$ in question on $B(a,r)$; for instance $(\sup_iu_i)^*+g_a=(\sup_i(u_i+g_a))^*$. Hence $w$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and $\ddc w\geq-\gamma_a\geq-\omega-\varepsilon\beta$ on each ball $B(a,r)$ with $a\in L$. Positivity of currents is a local property, so $\omega+\ddc w\geq-\varepsilon\beta$ on a neighborhood of $L$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $\omega+\ddc w\geq0$ on the interior of $L$, and since the chart and $L$ were arbitrary, $w\in\PSH(X,\omega)$.

(2) We first prove positivity on a coordinate ball $B$, choosing $q=C|z|^2$ in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}. Let $\rho_k$ be radial mollifiers whose radii decrease to zero, and put $u_{l,k}=(u_l+q)*\rho_k-q$ on a smaller concentric ball $B'$. Since $q$ is quadratic, $\ddc u_{l,k}=(\ddc u_l)*\rho_k$, where the convolution acts on the coefficients, and $(\omega+\ddc u_l)*\rho_k\geq0$ because mollification preserves positivity. By uniform continuity of the coefficients of $\omega$, there are $\tau_k\to0$ with $\omega*\rho_k\leq\omega+\tau_k\beta$ on $B'$. Hence the smooth $(1,1)$-forms $\omega_{u_{l,k}}+\tau_k\beta$ are positive, and so is their exterior product. Expanding this product as in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}, with $\sigma$ replaced by $\sigma+\tau_k\beta$, we obtain
\[
 \sum_J\Bigl(\bigwedge_{l\in J}\ddc\bigl((u_l+q)*\rho_k\bigr)\Bigr)\wedge(\sigma+\tau_k\beta)^{p-|J|}.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, the functions $(u_l+q)*\rho_k$ decrease to $u_l+q$, so the Bedford--Taylor products converge weakly by \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}; their masses are locally bounded by \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, and $\sigma+\tau_k\beta\to\sigma$ uniformly. Hence the product \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion} is a weak limit of positive forms on $B'$, and it is positive.

Cover $X$ by finitely many such balls, with $q$ defined on larger balls. On compact subsets of each ball, \cref{thm:cln-bounded} bounds the trace masses of the Bedford--Taylor products in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion} in terms of $\max_l\sup|u_l+q|$; since $\sigma$ is smooth, the same holds for the total variation of the coefficient measures of the product, and the finite cover gives the asserted bound for its trace measure with respect to $\omega$. By \cref{cor:psh-pluripolar-discrepancy}(3), every pluripolar set has outer measure zero for the total variations of the coefficient measures of Bedford--Taylor products of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions; it is also Lebesgue null. Hence it has outer measure zero for every term of \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}, and so for the trace measure of the product. Finally, if $\ddc g\geq\omega$ on $D$, we may take $q=g$ in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion} on $D$. Put $\gamma=\ddc g-\omega\geq0$, so that $\sigma=-\gamma$ and \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion} reads $\omega_u^k=\sum_i\binom ki(\ddc(u+g))^i\wedge(-\gamma)^{k-i}$. The binomial formula therefore gives
\[
 (\ddc(u+g))^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\omega_u^k\wedge\gamma^{n-k}\geq\omega_u^n,
\]
because the currents $\omega_u^k$ are positive and the forms $\gamma^{n-k}$ are strongly positive.

(3) On a coordinate ball, add a function $q$ as in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}. In the decreasing case, $u_l+q$ is plurisubharmonic and $u_{l,j}+q\to u_l+q$ in $L^1_{\loc}$ by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}, since $u_l+q$ is bounded below; hence $\omega+\ddc u_l$ is the weak limit of the positive currents $\omega+\ddc u_{l,j}$, and $u_l\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. The Bedford--Taylor products of the functions $u_{l,j}+q$ converge weakly by \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}. In the increasing case, $u_l\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ by (1), the functions $u_{l,j}+q$ increase to $u_l+q$ almost everywhere by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, and \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity} gives the same convergence. In both cases we multiply by the fixed smooth forms $\sigma^{p-|J|}$ and sum; a partition of unity subordinate to finitely many balls gives weak convergence on $X$.

(4) The proof of \cref{prop:normalized-global-psh-compactness} uses the background form only through smooth functions $h_k$ on a finite cover by coordinate balls $B_k$ such that $\varphi+h_k$ is plurisubharmonic on $B_k$ for every $\varphi$ in the class. Replacing $h_k$ by functions $q_k$ with $\ddc q_k\geq\omega$ on $B_k$, as in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}, for which $u+q_k$ is plurisubharmonic whenever $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, we can repeat that proof. In particular, if $u_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ converge in $L^1(X)$ to $u$, then $\omega+\ddc u\geq0$ in the sense of currents, and the canonical plurisubharmonic representatives of $u+q_k$ on the balls give the representative of $u$ in $\PSH(X,\omega)$; continuity of $u\mapsto\sup_Xu$ follows from the same Hartogs argument as there, after adding $q_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hermitian-one-factor-comparison}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be bounded, put $E=\{u<v\}$, and let $T$ be a product of $n-1$ factors, each equal to $\omega$, $\omega_u$ or $\omega_v$. Then $\ddc T$ is a real measure on $X$, that is, a real current of order zero and degree $2n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-one-factor-comparison}
 \int_E(\omega_v-\omega_u)\wedge T
       \leq\int_E(v-u)\,\ddc T,
\end{equation}
where $(\omega_v-\omega_u)\wedge T=\omega_v\wedge T-\omega_u\wedge T$. Moreover, there is a constant $C$ depending only on $(X,\omega)$ such that the total variation $|\ddc T|$ of this measure satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-ddc-product-variation}
 |\ddc T|\leq C\Bigl(\sum_iT_{\hat\imath}\wedge\omega^2+\sum_{i<i'}T_{\hat\imath\hat\imath'}\wedge\omega^3\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $T_{\hat\imath}$ denotes the product of the factors of $T$ other than the $i$th one, $T_{\hat\imath\hat\imath'}$ the product of those other than the $i$th and the $i'$th, and empty sums are zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $n=1$, then $T=1$ and $\ddc T=0$, so \eqref{eq:hermitian-ddc-product-variation} holds; the proof of \eqref{eq:hermitian-one-factor-comparison} below covers this case as well.

\emph{Step 1. The measure $\ddc T$.} Work on a coordinate ball with $q$ and $\sigma$ as in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}. Each factor of $T$ has the form $\Theta_i+\sigma$, where $\Theta_i$ is $\ddc q$, $\ddc(u+q)$ or $\ddc(v+q)$; thus $T=\sum_J\Theta_J\wedge\sigma^{n-1-|J|}$, where $\Theta_J$ is the Bedford--Taylor product of the $\Theta_i$ with $i\in J$. The currents $\Theta_J$ are closed of pure bidegree, so $\mathrm d\Theta_J=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Theta_J=0$, where $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ is the operator \eqref{eq:dc-operator}; hence $\ddc(\Theta_J\wedge\sigma^m)=\Theta_J\wedge\ddc(\sigma^m)$. Since $\mathrm d$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ annihilate $\ddc q$, we have $\mathrm d\sigma=\mathrm d\omega$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\sigma=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega$, and the Leibniz rule gives
\[
 \ddc(\sigma^m)=m\,\sigma^{m-1}\wedge\ddc\omega+m(m-1)\,\sigma^{m-2}\wedge\mathrm d\omega\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega.
\]
Expand $T_{\hat\imath}$ and $T_{\hat\imath\hat\imath'}$ in the same way. For a fixed $J$, with $m=n-1-|J|$, there are $m$ indices $i\notin J$ and $m(m-1)/2$ pairs $i<i'$ outside $J$; comparing coefficients, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-ddc-product}
 \ddc T=\sum_i\ddc\omega\wedge T_{\hat\imath}
      +2\sum_{i<i'}\mathrm d\omega\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega\wedge T_{\hat\imath\hat\imath'}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\mathrm dT$, $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}T$ and $\ddc T$ are currents of order zero, being sums of Bedford--Taylor products multiplied by smooth forms. The forms $\ddc\omega$ and $\mathrm d\omega\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\omega\wedge\bar\partial\omega$ are real, of bidegrees $(2,2)$ and $(3,3)$. At every point, $\omega^2$ and $\omega^3$ are interior points of the cones of strongly positive forms, by \cref{prop:positivity-cones} applied after a linear change of coordinates that takes $\omega$ at that point to $\beta$; since $X$ is compact, there is $C$ such that $C\omega^2\pm\ddc\omega$ and $C\omega^3\pm\mathrm d\omega\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega$ are strongly positive on $X$. The currents $T_{\hat\imath}$ and $T_{\hat\imath\hat\imath'}$ are positive by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2), so their products with these forms are positive measures, and \eqref{eq:hermitian-ddc-product-variation} follows from \eqref{eq:hermitian-ddc-product}.

\emph{Step 2. A convex cutoff.} Put $w=v-u$, a bounded Borel function on $X$. Fix a smooth increasing function $\lambda\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ with $\lambda=0$ on $(-\infty,0]$ and $\lambda=1$ on $[1,\infty)$, and for $\delta>0$ put $F_\delta(t)=\int_0^t\lambda(s/\delta)\,\mathrm ds$. Then $F_\delta$ is smooth and convex, $F_\delta=0$ on $(-\infty,0]$, $0\leq F_\delta'\leq1$, and as $\delta\downarrow0$ we have $F_\delta(t)\to t^+$ and $F_\delta'(t)\to\mathbf1_{(0,\infty)}(t)$ for every real $t$. We shall prove that, for every $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-convex-cutoff-comparison}
 \int_XF_\delta'(w)\,(\omega_v-\omega_u)\wedge T\leq\int_XF_\delta(w)\,\ddc T.
\end{equation}
Granting this, let $\delta\downarrow0$. The integrands converge pointwise and boundedly to $\mathbf1_E$ and to $(v-u)\mathbf1_E$; note that $F_\delta'(0)=0$, so the first limit is the indicator of $\{w>0\}=E$. Both measures have finite total variation, so dominated convergence turns \eqref{eq:hermitian-convex-cutoff-comparison} into \eqref{eq:hermitian-one-factor-comparison}.

To prove \eqref{eq:hermitian-convex-cutoff-comparison}, choose finitely many coordinate balls $B_i$ covering $X$, smooth functions $q_i$ with $\ddc q_i\geq\omega$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_i}$, and a smooth partition of unity $(\chi_i)$ on $X$ with $\Supp\chi_i\subset B_i$. Fix $i$. On a neighborhood of $\Supp\chi_i$, radial convolution of $u+q_i$ and $v+q_i$ gives uniformly bounded smooth plurisubharmonic functions, which we write as $u_l+q_i$ and $v_l+q_i$, decreasing to $u+q_i$ and $v+q_i$ as $l\to\infty$. Then $w_l=v_l-u_l\to w$ pointwise and boundedly on $\Supp\chi_i$. The function $f=F_\delta(w_l)$ is smooth near $\Supp\chi_i$, which contains the support of $\chi_iT$, so the definition of derivatives of currents and the Leibniz rule give
\[
 \int f\,\ddc(\chi_iT)=\int\chi_i\,\ddc f\wedge T,\qquad
 \ddc(\chi_iT)=\chi_i\ddc T+\Lambda_i,
\]
where $\Lambda_i=\ddc\chi_i\wedge T+\mathrm d\chi_i\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}T-\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi_i\wedge\mathrm dT$, and the chain rule gives
\[
 \ddc f=F_\delta'(w_l)\,\ddc w_l+F_\delta''(w_l)\,\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\partial w_l\wedge\bar\partial w_l.
\]
The last term is a smooth strongly positive $(1,1)$-form, since $F_\delta''\geq0$, and $T$ is positive by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2), so the product of this term with $\chi_iT$ is a positive measure. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-local-cutoff-inequality}
 \int\chi_iF_\delta'(w_l)\,\ddc w_l\wedge T\leq\int F_\delta(w_l)\bigl(\chi_i\ddc T+\Lambda_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
Now let $l\to\infty$. By Step 1, $\ddc T$ and $\Lambda_i$ are measures of finite total variation, so the right side tends to $\int F_\delta(w)(\chi_i\ddc T+\Lambda_i)$ by dominated convergence. On the left, $\ddc w_l\wedge T=\ddc(v_l+q_i)\wedge T-\ddc(u_l+q_i)\wedge T$. By \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}, each of these two currents is a finite sum of products of smooth forms with currents $\ddc(v_l+q_i)\wedge S$ or $\ddc(u_l+q_i)\wedge S$, where $S$ is a fixed Bedford--Taylor product of some of the functions $q_i$, $u+q_i$ and $v+q_i$. The weight $F_\delta'(w_l)$ is a continuous function of $(u_l+q_i,v_l+q_i)$, and these pairs take values in a fixed compact square. Approximating this function uniformly on the square by polynomials, and using \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} with base current $S$ together with the mass bounds of \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, we find that the left side of \eqref{eq:hermitian-local-cutoff-inequality} tends to $\int\chi_iF_\delta'(w)\,\bigl(\ddc(v+q_i)-\ddc(u+q_i)\bigr)\wedge T=\int\chi_iF_\delta'(w)\,(\omega_v-\omega_u)\wedge T$. Finally we sum the limiting inequalities over $i$. Since $\sum_i\chi_i=1$, we have $\sum_i\Lambda_i=0$, and \eqref{eq:hermitian-convex-cutoff-comparison} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-small-gap-comparison}
There are constants $s_0,C>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega)$, with the following property. Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be bounded with $\omega_v\geq\tfrac12\omega$, put $E=\{u<v\}$, and suppose that $v-u\leq s$ on $E$ for some $s\in(0,s_0)$. Then
\[
 \int_E\omega_v^n\leq(1+Cs)\int_E\omega_u^n.
\]
In particular $\int_X\omega_u^n>0$ for every bounded $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a product $T$ of $n-1$ factors chosen among $\omega,\omega_u,\omega_v$, let $\mathcal P(T)$ be the set of products obtained from $T$ by deleting one factor and inserting $\omega^2$, or by deleting two factors and inserting $\omega^3$. Each element of $\mathcal P(T)$ occurs at most $n^2$ times in \eqref{eq:hermitian-ddc-product-variation}, and $0<v-u\leq s$ on $E$. Hence \cref{lem:hermitian-one-factor-comparison} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-small-gap-error}
 \int_E\omega_v\wedge T-\int_E\omega_u\wedge T\leq sB\sum_{P\in\mathcal P(T)}\int_EP,
\end{equation}
with a constant $B\geq1$ depending only on $(X,\omega)$.

\emph{Step 1. Pure traces.} For $0\leq k\leq n$ put $a_k=\int_E\omega_u^k\wedge\omega^{n-k}$, and apply \cref{lem:hermitian-one-factor-comparison} to $T=\omega_u^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}$, $0\leq k<n$. Since $\omega_v-\tfrac12\omega=\tfrac12\omega_{2v}$ with $2v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ bounded, and the products are affine in each potential, the current $(\omega_v-\tfrac12\omega)\wedge T$ is positive; hence $\int_E\omega_v\wedge T\geq\tfrac12a_k$. Moreover $\int_E\omega_u\wedge T=a_{k+1}$, and the elements of $\mathcal P(T)$ are among the products $\omega_u^h\wedge\omega^{n-h}$ with $h\in\{k-2,k-1,k\}$. Thus \eqref{eq:hermitian-small-gap-error} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-pure-trace-recurrence}
 \tfrac12a_k\leq a_{k+1}+sB\sum_{h=0}^k a_h,
                  \qquad 0\leq k<n.
\end{equation}
Choose $s_0>0$ with $s_0B(n+1)4^n\leq1/4$; in particular $sB\leq1$. We prove $a_k\leq4a_{k+1}$ for $0\leq k<n$ by induction on $k$. If it holds for all smaller indices, then $a_h\leq4^{k-h}a_k$ for $h\leq k$, so $\sum_{h=0}^ka_h\leq(k+1)4^ka_k$, and the error term in \eqref{eq:hermitian-pure-trace-recurrence} is at most $s_0B(n+1)4^na_k\leq\tfrac14a_k$. Hence $\tfrac12a_k\leq a_{k+1}+\tfrac14a_k$, that is, $a_k\leq4a_{k+1}$. Consequently
\[
 a_k\leq4^{n-k}a_n,\qquad 0\leq k\leq n.
\]

\emph{Step 2. Mixed traces.} For $j,k\geq0$ with $j+k\leq n$ put $a_{j,k}=\int_E\omega_u^j\wedge\omega_v^k\wedge\omega^{n-j-k}$, so that $a_{j,0}=a_j$. If $k\geq1$, apply \cref{lem:hermitian-one-factor-comparison} to $T=\omega_u^j\wedge\omega_v^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-j-k}$. Then $\int_E\omega_v\wedge T=a_{j,k}$ and $\int_E\omega_u\wedge T=a_{j+1,k-1}$, while every element of $\mathcal P(T)$ has fewer than $j+k$ factors different from $\omega$. Thus \eqref{eq:hermitian-small-gap-error} gives
\[
 a_{j,k}\leq a_{j+1,k-1}+sB\sum_{p+q<j+k}a_{p,q}\qquad(k\geq1).
\]
We show by induction on $m=j+k$ that $a_{j,k}\leq C_m\sum_{h=0}^ma_h$ with $C_m$ depending only on $n$. For $k=0$ this is clear. For $k\geq1$, since $sB\leq1$, applying the last inequality $k$ times, which replaces the factors $\omega_v$ one at a time by $\omega_u$, gives
\[
 a_{j,k}\leq a_{j+k,0}+k\sum_{p+q<m}a_{p,q},
\]
and by the induction hypothesis the last sum is at most a constant depending only on $n$ times $\sum_{h<m}a_h$. Together with Step 1 this shows $a_{j,k}\leq C'_na_n$ for all $j,k$, where $C'_n$ depends only on $n$.

\emph{Step 3. Conclusion.} By symmetry of the products,
\[
 \omega_v^n-\omega_u^n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\omega_v-\omega_u)\wedge\omega_v^i\wedge\omega_u^{n-1-i}.
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:hermitian-small-gap-error} to each of the $n$ products $T=\omega_v^i\wedge\omega_u^{n-1-i}$. The resulting error terms are $sB$ times sums of traces $a_{p,q}$, hence at most a constant times $sa_n$ by Step 2. This proves $\int_E\omega_v^n\leq(1+Cs)\int_E\omega_u^n$ with $C$ depending only on $(X,\omega)$.

\emph{Step 4. Positivity.} Let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be bounded, put $c=\inf_Xu$, fix $s\in(0,s_0)$, and apply the inequality to the constant function $v=c+s$. Then $\omega_v=\omega\geq\tfrac12\omega$, and $E=\{u<c+s\}$ is nonempty by the definition of the infimum and open because $u$ is upper semicontinuous; moreover $0<v-u\leq s$ on $E$ since $u\geq c$. Hence $0<\int_E\omega^n\leq(1+Cs)\int_E\omega_u^n$, and therefore $\int_X\omega_u^n\geq\int_E\omega_u^n>0$.
\end{proof}
\index{comparison principle!Hermitian small gaps}

We now introduce the extremal functions and the capacity. For a set $A\subset X$, the \emph{global extremal function} of $A$ is
\[
 T_A=\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\omega):u\leq0\text{ on }A\},
\]
and the functions $u$ in this supremum are called \emph{competitors} for $T_A$. For a Borel set $A\subset X$, the \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere capacity} of $A$ with respect to $\omega$ is
\[
 c_\omega(A)=\sup\Bigl\{\int_A\omega_v^n:v\in\PSH(X,\omega),\ 0\leq v\leq1\Bigr\}.
\]
This is a global analogue of the capacity $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ of a bounded open set $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ (\cref{def:monge-ampere-capacity}). The functions $v$ in the supremum satisfy the same normalization $0\leq v\leq1$ as the competitors for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$, but they are now $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions on the whole compact manifold, and the measure $(\ddc v)^n$ is replaced by $\omega_v^n$. In particular $v=0$ occurs in the supremum, so that $c_\omega(A)\geq\int_A\omega^n$, whereas constants contribute nothing to $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2), $c_\omega(A)\leq c_\omega(X)<\infty$.
\index{extremal function!global}\index{competitor!global extremal function}\index{capacity!Monge--Amp\`ere, Hermitian background}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hermitian-extremal-pole-alternative}
For every set $A\subset X$, exactly one of the following holds: $A$ is globally pluripolar, or $T_A^*$ is a bounded nonnegative function in $\PSH(X,\omega)$. A compact set $K\subset X$ with nonempty interior is not globally pluripolar. A countable union of globally pluripolar sets is globally pluripolar.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal F_A$ be the family of competitors for $T_A$. It contains the constant $0$, so $T_A\geq0$.

Suppose first that $\sup\{\sup_Xu:u\in\mathcal F_A\}=\infty$. Choose $u_j\in\mathcal F_A$ with $b_j=\sup_Xu_j\geq2^j$ for $j\geq1$, and put $\psi_j=u_j-b_j$. Then $\psi_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, $\sup_X\psi_j=0$, $\psi_j\leq-2^j$ on $A$, and $\|\psi_j\|_{L^1(X)}\leq M$ for a constant $M$, by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(4). The partial sums $\Phi_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}\psi_j$ decrease with $N$, and $\ddc\Phi_N\geq-\sum_j2^{-j}\omega\geq-\omega$. On a coordinate ball with a function $q$ as in \eqref{eq:hermitian-product-expansion}, the functions $\Phi_N+q$ are plurisubharmonic and decrease, so by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets} their limit $\Phi+q$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on the ball. By monotone convergence $\Phi_N\to\Phi$ in $L^1(X)$ and $\int_X|\Phi|\leq\sum_j2^{-j}M\leq M$, so $\Phi$ is finite almost everywhere and $\Phi+q$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any ball. Hence $\Phi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, and $\Phi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ because the inequality $\omega+\ddc\Phi_N\geq0$ passes to the limit. Moreover $\Phi=-\infty$ on $A$, because every term $2^{-j}\psi_j$ is at most $-1$ there. Thus $A$ is globally pluripolar.

Otherwise $T_A\leq C$ for a constant $C$, and by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(1) the regularization $T_A^*$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$ and satisfies $0\leq T_A^*\leq C$. Conversely, if $A\subset\{\Phi=-\infty\}$ for a quasi-plurisubharmonic $\Phi$, choose $t>0$ with $t\Phi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. Then $t\Phi+c\in\mathcal F_A$ for every real $c$, and $\sup_X(t\Phi+c)=t\sup_X\Phi+c$ is unbounded in $c$. Hence the two alternatives exclude each other.

A quasi-plurisubharmonic function is locally integrable, so its pole set has Lebesgue measure zero in every chart and contains no nonempty open set. Therefore a compact set with nonempty interior is not globally pluripolar.

Finally let $A_j\subset\{\Phi_j=-\infty\}$, $j\geq1$, with $\Phi_j$ quasi-plurisubharmonic. After multiplying by small positive constants and subtracting constants, we may assume that $\Phi_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ and $\sup_X\Phi_j=0$. The argument of the second paragraph, applied to the $\Phi_j$ in place of the $\psi_j$, shows that $\sum_j2^{-j}\Phi_j$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$; it equals $-\infty$ on every $A_j$.
\end{proof}

The next estimate is purely local: it compares the mixed Monge--Amp\`ere masses of two bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a ball whose difference is small compared with the square of the radius.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nearby-bounded-psh-traces}
Let $U,V$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a ball $B(a,r)\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, with $|V-U|\leq\kappa r^2$ for some $\kappa\geq0$. Put $A=\ddc U$ and $D=\ddc V$. For every integer $0\leq k\leq n$,
\[
 \int_{B(a,r/2)}\sum_{j=0}^k A^j\wedge D^{k-j}\wedge\beta^{n-k}
 \leq C_n\sum_{l=0}^k \kappa^{k-l}
                         \int_{B(a,r)}A^l\wedge\beta^{n-l},
\]
where $C_n$ depends only on $n$ and $\kappa^0=1$, also when $\kappa=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\rho_i=r/2+ir/(2n)$ and $B_i=B(a,\rho_i)$ for $0\leq i\leq n$, so that $B_0=B(a,r/2)$ and $B_n=B(a,r)$. Choose cutoff functions $\chi_i\in C^\infty_c(B_{i+1})$, $0\leq i<n$, with $0\leq\chi_i\leq1$, $\chi_i=1$ on $B_i$ and $-C_nr^{-2}\beta\leq\ddc\chi_i\leq C_nr^{-2}\beta$; this is possible because the gaps between consecutive radii are $r/(2n)$. For integers $j,m\geq0$ with $j+m\leq n$ and $0\leq i\leq n$, put
\[
 I_i(j,m)=\int_{B_i}A^j\wedge D^m\wedge\beta^{n-j-m}.
\]
Let $m\geq1$, $i<n$, and let $R=A^j\wedge D^{m-1}\wedge\beta^{n-j-m}$, a closed positive current. By the definition of Bedford--Taylor products, $D\wedge R-A\wedge R=\ddc((V-U)R)$, so
\[
 \int\chi_i\,D\wedge R=\int\chi_i\,A\wedge R+\int(V-U)\,R\wedge\ddc\chi_i.
\]
Since $R$ is positive and $|V-U|\leq\kappa r^2$, the last integral is at most $C_n\kappa\int_{B_{i+1}}R\wedge\beta$ in absolute value. Using $\mathbf1_{B_i}\leq\chi_i\leq\mathbf1_{B_{i+1}}$ and the positivity of the currents involved, we obtain
\[
 I_i(j,m)\leq I_{i+1}(j+1,m-1)+C_n\kappa\,I_{i+1}(j,m-1).
\]
Both terms on the right have one factor $D$ fewer. Applying this inequality $m$ times, starting from $i=0$, we find
\[
 I_0(j,m)\leq\sum_{t=0}^m\binom mt(C_n\kappa)^{m-t}I_m(j+t,0)
 \leq\sum_{t=0}^m\binom mt(C_n\kappa)^{m-t}\int_{B(a,r)}A^{j+t}\wedge\beta^{n-j-t}.
\]
Take $m=k-j$ and sum over $0\leq j\leq k$. With $l=j+t$ we have $m-t=k-l$, so every term with $A^l$ carries the factor $\kappa^{k-l}$, and the coefficients depend only on $n$. This proves the lemma; for $l=k$ the factor is $\kappa^0=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-extremal-mass-support}
Let $K\subset X$ be compact, and suppose that $u=T_K^*$ is bounded. Then the measure $\omega_u^n$ is carried by $K$. Moreover, with $S=\max\{1,\sup_Xu\}$ and $M=\int_X\omega_u^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-extremal-capacity-bound}
                    0<M\leq S^n c_\omega(K).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1. Increasing competitors.} The competitors for $T_K$ are bounded above by $\sup_Xu$. By Choquet's lemma (\cref{lem:choquet-selection}), applied in finitely many coordinate charts, there are competitors $v_k$ for $T_K$ such that $(\sup_kv_k)^*=u$. The functions $u_j=\max\{0,v_1,\ldots,v_j\}$ belong to $\PSH(X,\omega)$ by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(1), increase with $j$, vanish on $K$, satisfy $0\leq u_j\leq T_K\leq u$, and $(\sup_ju_j)^*=u$.

\emph{Step 2. Frozen balayage.} Let $G\subset X\setminus K$ be the domain of a coordinate chart, identified with an open subset of $\mathbb C^n$, and let $L\subset G$ be compact. Since $X\setminus K$ is a countable union of such sets $L$, it suffices to show that $\omega_u^n(L)=0$. Choose open sets $G_2\Subset G_1\Subset G_0\Subset G$ with $L\subset G_2$. For $a\in G_2$ let $\omega(a)$ be the form with constant coefficients equal to those of $\omega$ at $a$, and let $h_a$ be a quadratic polynomial with $\ddc h_a=\omega(a)$. The coefficients of $\omega$ are Lipschitz on $G_1$, so there are $r_0>0$ and $C_1$ such that, for $a\in G_2$ and $0<r\leq r_0$, the ball $B(a,2r)$ lies in $G_1$ and
\[
 -\delta\beta\leq\omega-\omega(a)\leq\delta\beta\quad\text{on }B(a,2r),\qquad\delta=C_1r.
\]
Fix such $a$ and $r$, put $q=\pi|z-a|^2$, so that $\ddc q=\beta$ by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}, and let $B=B(a,r)$. The functions
\[
 U_j=u_j+h_a+\delta q
\]
are bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B(a,2r)$, since $\ddc U_j=\omega_{u_j}+(\omega(a)-\omega+\delta\beta)\geq0$. Let $V_j$ be the homogeneous balayage of $U_j$ in $B$ given by \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}: it equals $U_j$ outside $B$, satisfies $V_j\geq U_j$ and $(\ddc V_j)^n=0$ in $B$, and its restriction to $B$ has upper boundary limits at most $U_j$ on $\partial B$.

We compare $V_j$ with $u$. Put $\rho=q-\pi r^2$, which is nonpositive on $B$ and vanishes on $\partial B$, and let $Z_j=V_j-h_a-\delta q+2\delta\rho$ on $B$. Then
\[
 \omega+\ddc Z_j=\ddc V_j+(\omega-\omega(a)+\delta\beta)\geq0,
\]
so $Z_j\in\PSH(B,\omega)$, and for $x\in\partial B$ we have $\limsup_{B\ni y\to x}Z_j(y)\leq U_j(x)-h_a(x)-\delta q(x)=u_j(x)$. By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(1), the function equal to $\max\{u_j,Z_j\}$ on $B$ and to $u_j$ on $X\setminus B$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$. It vanishes on $K$, because $B\cap K=\varnothing$, so it is a competitor for $T_K$; hence $\max\{u_j,Z_j\}\leq T_K\leq u$ on $B$. Therefore
\[
 U_j\leq V_j\leq u+h_a+\delta q-2\delta\rho\quad\text{on }B.
\]
The balayage is increasing in the data, so $V_j$ increases with $j$. Let $V$ be the upper semicontinuous regularization of $\lim_jV_j$ on $B$. Since $U_1\leq V_j\leq u+h_a+\delta q+2\pi\delta r^2$, the $V_j$ are uniformly bounded, $V$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic, and $V_j\to V$ almost everywhere by \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}; hence $(\ddc V)^n=0$ on $B$ by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}. Put $U=u+h_a+\delta q$. Since $\sup_jU_j=\sup_ju_j+h_a+\delta q$ and $h_a+\delta q$ is continuous, upper regularization gives $V\geq U$. The upper bound for $V_j$ is upper semicontinuous, so it also holds for $V$. As $-2\delta\rho\leq2\pi\delta r^2$ on $B$, we conclude
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-frozen-balayage-error}
                  0\leq V-U\leq2\pi\delta r^2\quad\text{on }B.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3. A local mass estimate.} Put $A=\ddc U$ and $D=\ddc V$ on $B$. The function $g=h_a+\delta q$ satisfies $\ddc g=\omega(a)+\delta\beta\geq\omega$ on $B(a,2r)$, so \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2) gives $A^n=(\ddc(u+g))^n\geq\omega_u^n$ on $B$. Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B(a,r/2))$ with $0\leq\chi\leq1$, $\chi=1$ on $B(a,r/4)$ and $-C_2r^{-2}\beta\leq\ddc\chi\leq C_2r^{-2}\beta$. By the definition of Bedford--Taylor products,
$A^n-D^n=\ddc\bigl((U-V)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge D^{n-1-j}\bigr)$. Using $D^n=0$, integration by parts against $\chi$ and \eqref{eq:hermitian-frozen-balayage-error}, we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{B(a,r/4)}\omega_u^n
 &\leq\int\chi A^n
 =\int(U-V)\,\ddc\chi\wedge\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge D^{n-1-j}\\
 &\leq2\pi C_2\delta\int_{B(a,r/2)}
                   \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge D^{n-1-j}\wedge\beta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Apply \cref{lem:nearby-bounded-psh-traces} with $k=n-1$ and $\kappa=2\pi\delta=2\pi C_1r$; for small $r$ we have $\kappa\leq1$, and the last integral is at most $C_n\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\int_{B(a,r)}A^l\wedge\beta^{n-l}$. Choose $C_0$ such that $C_0\beta\geq\omega$ on $G_0$ and $C_0\beta\geq\omega(a)+\delta\beta$ for all $a\in G_2$ and $r\leq r_0$. Then $\Gamma=\ddc u+C_0\beta$ is the Hessian of the bounded plurisubharmonic function $u+C_0\pi|z|^2$ on $G_0$, and it does not depend on $a$ and $r$. On $B$ the difference $\Gamma-A=C_0\beta-\omega(a)-\delta\beta$ is a positive form with constant coefficients, so expanding $\Gamma^l=(A+(\Gamma-A))^l$ gives $A^l\wedge\beta^{n-l}\leq\Gamma^l\wedge\beta^{n-l}$. Altogether, for $a\in G_2$ and all sufficiently small $r$,
\[
 \int_{B(a,r/4)}\omega_u^n\leq C_3\,r\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\int_{B(a,r)}\Gamma^l\wedge\beta^{n-l},
\]
with $C_3$ independent of $a$ and $r$.

\emph{Step 4. Zero mass off $K$.} For small $r$, the points of a cubical grid of mesh $r/(4\sqrt{2n})$ that lie within distance $r/4$ of $L$ belong to $G_2$. Every point of $\mathbb C^n$ lies within distance $r/8$ of a grid point, so the balls $B(a,r/4)$ centered at these points cover $L$, and each point of $\mathbb C^n$ lies in at most $N_n$ of the balls $B(a,r)$, where $N_n$ depends only on $n$. Summing the local estimate over these balls gives
\[
 \omega_u^n(L)\leq C_3N_n\,r\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\int_{\overline{G_1}}\Gamma^l\wedge\beta^{n-l}.
\]
The integrals on the right are finite by \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, applied in $G_0$, and do not depend on $r$. Letting $r\downarrow0$ gives $\omega_u^n(L)=0$. Hence $\omega_u^n$ is carried by $K$.

\emph{Step 5. The mass bound.} Since $T_K\geq0$, we have $0\leq u\leq S$. The function $v=u/S$ satisfies $0\leq v\leq1$ and belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$ because $S\geq1$. Moreover $\omega_v=S^{-1}\omega_u+(1-S^{-1})\omega$, so expanding $\omega_v^n$ and using the positivity of the mixed products gives $\omega_v^n\geq S^{-n}\omega_u^n$. As $\omega_u^n$ is carried by $K$,
\[
 M=\int_K\omega_u^n\leq S^n\int_K\omega_v^n\leq S^nc_\omega(K).
\]
Finally $M>0$ by \cref{prop:hermitian-small-gap-comparison}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact-complex-global-josefson}
Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold, and let $A\subset X$ be pluripolar (\cref{def:pluripolar-set}). Then there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\Phi$ on $X$ with $A\subset\{\Phi=-\infty\}$; that is, $A$ is globally pluripolar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compact manifold $X$ has finitely many connected components, and it suffices to construct $\Phi$ on each of them. On a component of dimension zero, which is a point, pluripolar sets are empty and $\Phi=0$ works. We may therefore assume that $X$ is connected of dimension $n\geq1$, and we fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ (one exists by \cref{sec:current-maps}), so that the notation of this section applies.

Every point of $X$ has a coordinate ball $U$ and a plurisubharmonic function on $U$ whose pole set contains $A\cap U$; choose a smaller ball $U_1\Subset U$ around the point. By compactness finitely many of the balls $U_1$ cover $X$. By the last assertion of \cref{lem:hermitian-extremal-pole-alternative} it therefore suffices to prove the following: if $U$ is a coordinate ball, $\varphi$ is plurisubharmonic on $U$ and $U_1\Subset U$ is a smaller ball, then $E=\{\varphi=-\infty\}\cap U_1$ is globally pluripolar. If $E$ is empty, there is nothing to prove, so let $E\neq\varnothing$. Choose balls $U_1\Subset U_2\Subset U_3\Subset U$ and subtract a constant from $\varphi$ so that $\varphi\leq0$ on $U_3$. Suppose, to reach a contradiction, that $E$ is not globally pluripolar. By \cref{lem:hermitian-extremal-pole-alternative}, there is a constant $C$ with $0\leq T_E^*\leq C$.

\emph{Step 1. Sublevel sets and their extremal functions.} Radial convolution gives smooth plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi_j$ on $U_2$ that decrease to $\varphi$ as $j\to\infty$. Since $\varphi\leq0$ on $U_3$, we have $\varphi\leq\varphi_j\leq0$ on $U_2$, so $\|\varphi_j\|_{L^1(U_2)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^1(U_2)}$. For integers $N,j\geq1$ put
\[
 K_{j,N}=\{\varphi_j\leq-N\}\cap\overline{U_1},\qquad O_{j,N}=\{\varphi_j<-N\}\cap U_1.
\]
Then $K_{j,N}$ is compact and contains the open set $O_{j,N}$. Fix $x_0\in E$. Since $\varphi_j(x_0)\downarrow-\infty$, there is $j_0(N)$ such that $x_0\in O_{j,N}$ for $j\geq j_0(N)$. For these $j$ the set $K_{j,N}$ has nonempty interior, so $u_{j,N}=T_{K_{j,N}}^*$ is a bounded nonnegative function in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ by \cref{lem:hermitian-extremal-pole-alternative}. Every competitor for $T_{K_{j,N}}$ is nonpositive on the open set $O_{j,N}$; hence so is $T_{K_{j,N}}$, and its upper semicontinuous regularization satisfies $u_{j,N}=0$ on $O_{j,N}$.

For fixed $N$, the sets $K_{j,N}$ increase with $j$, because $\varphi_j$ decreases. Hence $u_{j,N}$ decreases with $j\geq j_0(N)$; let $u_N$ be its limit. By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(3), $u_N\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, and $0\leq u_N\leq u_{j_0(N),N}$ is bounded. If $x\in U_1$ and $\varphi(x)<-N$, then $x\in O_{j,N}$ for all large $j$, so $u_N(x)=0$. In particular $u_N=0$ on $E$, so $u_N$ is a competitor for $T_E$, and
\[
                    0\leq u_N\leq T_E^*\leq C.
\]
For fixed $j$, the sets $K_{j,N}$ decrease as $N$ increases, so $u_{j,N}$, and hence $u_N$, increase with $N$. Let $u_\infty$ be the upper semicontinuous regularization of $\lim_Nu_N$. By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(1), $u_\infty\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ and $0\leq u_\infty\leq C$.

\emph{Step 2. Masses.} Put
\[
 M_{j,N}=\int_X\omega_{u_{j,N}}^n,\qquad
 M_N=\int_X\omega_{u_N}^n,\qquad
 M_\infty=\int_X\omega_{u_\infty}^n.
\]
By \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(3), applied to the uniformly bounded decreasing sequence $(u_{j,N})_{j\geq j_0(N)}$ and to the uniformly bounded increasing sequence $(u_N)_{N\geq1}$, and testing the weak convergence with the constant function $1$ on the compact manifold $X$, we obtain
\[
 M_{j,N}\longrightarrow M_N\quad(j\to\infty),\qquad
 M_N\longrightarrow M_\infty\quad(N\to\infty).
\]
Moreover $M_\infty>0$ by \cref{prop:hermitian-small-gap-comparison}, since $u_\infty$ is bounded.

\emph{Step 3. The capacity of $K_{j,N}$.} Choose a smooth function $h$ on $U_2$ with $\ddc h\geq\omega$ and $\sup_{U_2}|h|<\infty$, for instance a multiple of $|z|^2$. If $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ and $0\leq v\leq1$, then $v+h$ is plurisubharmonic on $U_2$ with $|v+h|\leq1+\sup_{U_2}|h|$, and $\omega_v^n\leq(\ddc(v+h))^n$ on $U_2$ by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(2). Since $-\varphi_j\geq N$ on $K_{j,N}$, the weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality (\cref{thm:cln-weighted}), applied with a compact neighborhood $L\subset U_2$ of $\overline{U_1}$, gives
\[
 \int_{K_{j,N}}\omega_v^n
 \leq\frac1N\int_{\overline{U_1}}(-\varphi_j)\,(\ddc(v+h))^n
 \leq\frac{C'}N.
\]
The constant $C'$ depends on $\|\varphi_j\|_{L^1(L)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^1(U_2)}$ and on the bound for $|v+h|$, but not on $v$, $j$ or $N$. Taking the supremum over $v$, we obtain $c_\omega(K_{j,N})\leq C'/N$.

\emph{Step 4. Conclusion.} By \cref{prop:hermitian-extremal-mass-support},
\[
 M_{j,N}\leq\max\{1,\sup_Xu_{j,N}\}^n\,\frac{C'}N\qquad(j\geq j_0(N)).
\]
For fixed $N$, the uniformly bounded functions $u_{j,N}$ decrease to $u_N$, so $u_{j,N}\to u_N$ in $L^1(X)$ by dominated convergence, and $\sup_Xu_{j,N}\to\sup_Xu_N$ by \cref{lem:hermitian-bounded-products}(4). Letting $j\to\infty$ and using $u_N\leq C$, we obtain $M_N\leq\max\{1,C\}^nC'/N$. Letting $N\to\infty$ gives $M_\infty=0$, which contradicts $M_\infty>0$. Hence $E$ is globally pluripolar, and the theorem is proved.
\end{proof}
