\subsection{Smoothing and its joint complex Hessian}
\label{sec:chern-envelope-smoothing}
We now turn to envelopes in a big class $\{\theta\}$. The background $\theta$ is no longer positive, so the penalized equations of \cref{sec:penalized-kahler-envelopes} are not available. Instead we compare the envelope $u$ with a smoothing $\Psi_u(x,r)$, an average of $u$ over a neighborhood of $x$ of size $r$, and the metric-Laplacian bound will follow from an estimate $\Psi_u(x,r)-u(x)=O(r^2)$. Convolution in coordinates does not define a global family on $X$, so we build the smoothing kernel from finite holomorphic jets of the exponential map of $\omega$. Smoothing loses a small amount of positivity. This loss will be corrected by minimizing over the radius, as in Kiselman's minimum principle, and for that we need the complex Hessian of the smoothing jointly in the base point and the radius. This joint estimate is \cref{lem:chern-joint-smoothing}.

For the calculations in this and the next two subsections we put
\[
 \mathcal D=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial=2\pi\ddc,
 \qquad \alpha=2\pi\theta,\qquad \widehat\omega=2\pi\omega.
\]
Thus $\alpha+\mathcal Du\geq0$ is precisely the condition that $u$ is $\theta$-psh, and multiplying forms by $2\pi$ only changes the constants in the estimates. Let $h$ be the coefficient matrix of $\widehat\omega$ in local holomorphic coordinates, as in \cref{sec:penalized-kahler-envelopes}, and write $|v|_h$ for the corresponding norm of a tangent vector $v$. For a real $(1,1)$-form $T=\mathrm i\sum_{l,m}t_{l\bar m}\,\mathrm dz_l\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_m$ we write $T[y]^2=\sum_{l,m}t_{l\bar m}y_l\bar y_m$ for the value of the associated Hermitian quadratic form on a vector $y$; thus $\widehat\omega[v]^2=|v|_h^2$. For a real $(1,1)$-form on a product of an open subset of $X$ with an open subset of $\mathbb C$, we write $T[v,\eta]^2$ for its value on the product tangent vector $(v,\eta)$. If $P=(p_{l\bar m})$ and $M=(M_{lm})$ are Hermitian matrices, we write $P[M]=\sum_{l,m}p_{l\bar m}M_{lm}$ for their contraction, so that $P[y]^2=P[yy^*]$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:chern-joint-smoothing}
There are $r_0>0$ and, for every integrable function $u$ on $X$, a function $\Psi_u(x,w)$ of $x\in X$ and $w\in\mathbb C$ with $0<|w|<r_0$, obtained by integrating $u$ against a positive kernel of total mass one, such that $\Psi_u$ is smooth, invariant under rotations of $w$ and linear in $u$, and has the following properties. We write $\Psi_u(x,r)$ for its value at $|w|=r$. In the assertions about $H_u$ and its joint Levi form we assume that $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$; constants denoted by $C$ may then depend on the fixed geometry, $\sup_Xu$ and $\|u\|_{L^1}$, but not on the radius, and the constant $A$ depends only on the fixed geometry.

For a sufficiently large constant $K$ of the same kind as $C$, put
\[
 H_u(x,r)=\Psi_u(x,r)+Kr^2,\qquad
 \lambda_u(x,r)=r\partial_rH_u(x,r).
\]
Then $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)$ is a strictly convex nondecreasing function of $s$ which tends to $u(x)$ as $s\to-\infty$; in particular $\lambda_u\geq0$. If $u$ is bounded near $x$, then $\lambda_u(x,r)\to0$ as $r\to0$.

At each point $x$ we use holomorphic normal coordinates for $\omega$ centered at $x$ (\cref{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates}), defined on a ball of a fixed size, as explained in the proof. Let $q$ be a smooth function there with $\mathcal Dq=\alpha$, so that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic, and let $\mu_p$ be the trace of the complex Hessian of $p$ in these coordinates, a positive measure. Put $d=2n$ and
\[
 m_u(x,r)=1+r^{-d}\mu_p(B(x,4r)).
\]
All estimates below hold uniformly over these fixed normal charts. For a tangent vector $v$ at $x$ and a tangent vector $\eta\in\mathbb C$ in the radius variable, the joint Levi form of $H_u$ in $(x,w)$ satisfies
\begin{align}\label{eq:chern-joint-mass-estimate}
 (\alpha+\mathcal D_{x,w}H_u)[v,\eta]^2
 \geq{}&-\bigl(A\lambda_u+C(r^2+r^3m_u)\bigr)|v|_h^2\notag\\
       &-C(r+r^2m_u)|v|_h|\eta|-Cr^2|\eta|^2,
       \qquad r=|w|.
\end{align}
Since $m_u\leq Cr^{-2}$, as we show in the proof, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-initial-joint-bound}
 (\alpha+\mathcal D_{x,w}H_u)[v,\eta]^2
 \geq-(A\lambda_u+Cr)|v|_h^2-C|v|_h|\eta|-C|\eta|^2.
\end{equation}
If $m_u(x,r)\leq M(x)/r$ with $M(x)\geq1$, then the stronger estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-improved-joint-bound}
 (\alpha+\mathcal D_{x,w}H_u)[v,\eta]^2
 \geq-(A\lambda_u+CM(x)r^2)|v|_h^2
                    -CM(x)r|v|_h|\eta|-CM(x)r|\eta|^2
\end{equation}
holds. Moreover, if $u$ is bounded on the ball $B(x,2r)$ of the normal coordinates at $x$, then $\Psi_u(x,r)$ differs from the ordinary radial convolution $\int u(x+r\zeta)\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta$ in these coordinates by at most $Cr^2\sup_{B(x,2r)}|u|$, where $C$ depends only on the fixed geometry; here $\chi$ is the radial weight used in the construction of the kernel. For a $C^1$ function $f$ with bounded weak complex Hessian,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-regular-obstacle-error}
 |\Psi_f(x,r)-f(x)|\leq C_f r^2
\end{equation}
uniformly on $X$, where $C_f$ is controlled by the bounds for the value, the gradient and the complex Hessian of $f$. Finally, for every integrable function $u$ on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-smoothing-generator}
 \frac{\Psi_u(\cdot,r)-u}{r^2}\longrightarrow a_\chi D_hu
 \quad\text{in the sense of distributions as }r\to0,
\end{equation}
where $D_hu=\sum_{i,j}h^{i\bar j}u_{i\bar j}$ is the trace of the complex Hessian, as in \cref{sec:penalized-kahler-envelopes}, and $a_\chi=\frac1n\int|\zeta|^2\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first construct the kernel and then compute its joint complex Hessian. All bounds below are uniform over the centers, because the constructions are made in the charts of a fixed finite atlas, with coefficients bounded uniformly in the center.

\emph{Normal coordinates.} Fix the jet order $N=8$. For $x\in X$ we use the normal coordinates for $\omega$ at $x$ of \cref{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates}, applied in one of the charts of a fixed finite atlas, chosen so that $x$ lies in a fixed compact subset of it: in them $h(0)=I$ and $\mathrm dh(0)=0$. The coordinate change there is a polynomial map of degree two whose coefficients, and the inverse of whose linear part, are bounded uniformly in $x$; hence these coordinates are defined on balls of a fixed size, and all finite jets of $h$ in them are bounded uniformly in $x$. Put $c_{jklm}=-\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}h_{l\bar m}(0)$. Since $h$ is Hermitian, $\overline{c_{jklm}}=c_{kjml}$; hence, for every vector $v$, the matrix $C^v$ with entries $C^v_{ml}=\sum_{j,k}c_{jklm}v_j\bar v_k$ is Hermitian, and $\|C^v\|\leq C|v|^2$ for its operator norm.

\emph{The jet map.} In holomorphic coordinates define $\Gamma^m_{jl}$ by $\sum_m\Gamma^m_{jl}h_{m\bar k}=\partial_jh_{l\bar k}$. Thus $\Gamma(z)$ is a bilinear map $\mathbb C^n\times\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^n$, symmetric because $\widehat\omega$ is closed, depending smoothly on $z$, and $\Gamma(0)=0$ in normal coordinates. Consider the geodesic equation
\[
 \ddot\gamma+\Gamma(\gamma)(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0
\]
for formal power series $\gamma(t)=z+\sum_{k\geq1}c_kt^k/k!$ in a real variable $t$, with $c_k\in\mathbb C^n$; here $\Gamma(\gamma(t))$ is the composition of the Taylor series of $\Gamma$ at $z$ with the series $\gamma(t)-z$, which has no constant term. Prescribe $c_1=\zeta$. Comparing the coefficients of $t^k$ in the equation determines $c_{k+2}$ as a polynomial in $c_1,\ldots,c_{k+1}$ whose coefficients are derivatives of $\Gamma$ at $z$ of order at most $k$. By induction, $c_k$ is a polynomial in $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta$ with coefficients depending smoothly on $z$, and it is homogeneous of degree $k$: for real $\lambda$ the series $\gamma(\lambda t)$ is the formal solution with initial velocity $\lambda\zeta$, so that $c_k(\lambda\zeta)=\lambda^kc_k(\zeta)$. Let $c_k^{\mathrm h}(z,\zeta)$ be the sum of the monomials of $c_k$ that contain no $\bar\zeta$, and define
\[
 J_z(\zeta)=z+\zeta+\sum_{k=2}^N\frac{c_k^{\mathrm h}(z,\zeta)}{k!},
\]
a polynomial in $\zeta$ that is holomorphic in $\zeta$. Since $c_2=-\Gamma(z)(\zeta,\zeta)$, in normal coordinates at $x$ we have $J_0(\zeta)=\zeta+O(|\zeta|^3)$.

These jets do not depend on the coordinates. Let $y=\phi(z)$ be a holomorphic change of coordinates, with derivative $\Phi=D\phi$, and let $\widetilde h$, $\widetilde\Gamma$ and $\widetilde J$ be the corresponding objects in the coordinates $y$. Then $h_{j\bar k}=\sum_{a,b}\Phi^a_j\,\widetilde h_{a\bar b}(\phi)\,\overline{\Phi^b_k}$, and since $\Phi$ is holomorphic,
\[
 \partial_jh_{l\bar k}=\sum_{a,b}\Bigl(\partial_j\Phi^a_l+\sum_{c,e}\Phi^c_j\Phi^e_l\,\widetilde\Gamma^a_{ce}(\phi)\Bigr)\widetilde h_{a\bar b}(\phi)\,\overline{\Phi^b_k}.
\]
Comparing with the definition of $\Gamma$, and using that the matrix $(\sum_b\widetilde h_{a\bar b}\overline{\Phi^b_k})_{a,k}$ is invertible, we obtain $\Phi\,\Gamma(u,u')=D^2\phi(u,u')+\widetilde\Gamma(\phi)(\Phi u,\Phi u')$. Let $\gamma$ be a formal solution in the coordinates $z$. The second derivative of $\phi\circ\gamma$ is $\Phi(\gamma)\ddot\gamma+D^2\phi(\gamma)(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)$, because $\Phi$ is holomorphic, and therefore
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d^2(\phi\circ\gamma)}{\mathrm dt^2}+\widetilde\Gamma(\phi\circ\gamma)\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm d(\phi\circ\gamma)}{\mathrm dt},\frac{\mathrm d(\phi\circ\gamma)}{\mathrm dt}\Bigr)=\Phi(\gamma)\bigl(\ddot\gamma+\Gamma(\gamma)(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\bigr)=0.
\]
By uniqueness, $\phi\circ\gamma$ is the formal solution with initial point $\phi(z)$ and initial velocity $\Phi(z)\zeta$. By homogeneity we may put $t=1$ degree by degree, and we obtain an identity between formal power series in $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta$. Call a monomial in $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta$ \emph{discardable} if it contains a factor $\bar\zeta_m$ or has degree at least $N+1$; the product of a discardable monomial with any monomial is discardable. The two series differ from $\widetilde J_{\phi(z)}(\Phi(z)\zeta)$ and from $\phi(J_z(\zeta))$, respectively, by series of discardable monomials; for the second one we use the Taylor expansion of the holomorphic map $\phi$ at $J_z(\zeta)$, each of whose terms beyond the first contains the discardable difference as a factor. The two holomorphic functions of $\zeta$ just named therefore have the same Taylor coefficients of degree at most $N$ at $\zeta=0$, and Taylor's formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-jet-compatibility}
 \widetilde J_{\phi(z)}\bigl(\Phi(z)\zeta\bigr)=\phi\bigl(J_z(\zeta)\bigr)+O(|\zeta|^{N+1}),
\end{equation}
where the remainder is a smooth function vanishing to order $N+1$ at $\zeta=0$, with bounds for its derivatives that are locally uniform in $z$.

\emph{The global map.} Choose finitely many holomorphic charts $\kappa_\nu\colon U_\nu\to\mathbb C^n$, $1\leq\nu\leq\nu_0$, and open sets $V_\nu\Subset U_\nu$ covering $X$. For $x\in U_\nu$ and small $\zeta\in T_xX$ put $F_\nu(x,\zeta)=\kappa_\nu^{-1}\bigl(J^\nu_{\kappa_\nu(x)}(D\kappa_\nu(x)\zeta)\bigr)$, where $J^\nu$ is the jet map in the coordinates $\kappa_\nu$. By \eqref{eq:chern-jet-compatibility}, in every holomorphic chart, $F_\nu$ and $F_\mu$ differ by $O(|\zeta|^{N+1})$ where both are defined. We say that a smooth map $G(x,\zeta)$, defined for $x$ in an open set $O$ and $|\zeta|<\epsilon$, has the \emph{geodesic jets of order $N$} if in every chart $\kappa_\nu$ we have $G-F_\nu=O(|\zeta|^{N+1})$, with bounds that are uniform on compact subsets of $O\cap U_\nu$. We construct inductively such maps $G_k$ on open sets $O_k\supset\overline{V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_k}$. Let $G_1=F_1$ and $O_1=U_1$. Given $G_{k-1}$, choose an open set $O'$ with $\overline{V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_{k-1}}\subset O'\Subset O_{k-1}$, a function $\vartheta\in C^\infty_c(U_k)$ with $0\leq\vartheta\leq1$ that equals one on a relatively compact open neighborhood $N_k\subset U_k$ of the compact set $\overline{V_k}\setminus O'$ (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}(4)), and an open set $W$ with $\Supp\vartheta\subset W\Subset U_k$. Put $O_k=O'\cup N_k$, and define $G_k=G_{k-1}$ on $O'\setminus\Supp\vartheta$, $G_k=F_k$ on $N_k$, and
\[
 G_k=\kappa_k^{-1}\bigl((1-\vartheta)\,\kappa_k\circ G_{k-1}+\vartheta\,\kappa_k\circ F_k\bigr)\qquad\text{on }O'\cap W.
\]
For $x$ in the compact set $\overline{O'}\cap\overline W$ and small $\zeta$, the points $G_{k-1}(x,\zeta)$ and $F_k(x,\zeta)$ lie in a small ball about $\kappa_k(x)$ in the chart $\kappa_k$, so the last formula is defined. The three sets cover $O_k$, and the three definitions agree on the overlaps, because $\vartheta=0$ outside $\Supp\vartheta$ and $\vartheta=1$ on $N_k$. In the chart $\kappa_k$ the last formula gives $G_k-F_k=(1-\vartheta)(G_{k-1}-F_k)=O(|\zeta|^{N+1})$, so $G_k$ has the geodesic jets of order $N$. Finally, $F=G_{\nu_0}$ is defined on $X$ for $|\zeta|<r_1$, with a fixed $r_1>0$. Since $F(x,0)=x$ and $D_\zeta F(x,0)$ is the identity, we may decrease $r_1$ so that, in each chart, $\|D_\zeta F-I\|\leq\frac12$ for $|\zeta|<r_1$; then each fiber map $\zeta\mapsto F(x,\zeta)$ is injective, because $|F(x,\zeta)-F(x,\zeta')-(\zeta-\zeta')|\leq\frac12|\zeta-\zeta'|$, and by the inverse function theorem it is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

In the normal coordinates at a center $x$, \eqref{eq:chern-jet-compatibility} shows that $F(x',\zeta)$ differs from the jet map $J_{z'}(\zeta)$ of these coordinates by $O(|\zeta|^{N+1})$, where $z'$ is the coordinate of $x'$. Since $J_0(\zeta)=\zeta+O(|\zeta|^3)$, we obtain at the center
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-normal-kernel-map}
 F(x,\zeta)=x+\zeta+O(|\zeta|^3),
 \qquad D_\zeta F=I+O(|\zeta|^2).
\end{equation}

\emph{The kernel.} Choose
\[
 \chi(t)=\frac{C_n}{(1-t)^2}\mathrm e^{-1/(1-t)}\quad(t<1),
 \qquad \chi(t)=0\quad(t\geq1),
\]
with $\int_{\mathbb C^n}\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta=1$, and let $\chi_1(t)=-C_n\mathrm e^{-1/(1-t)}$ for $t<1$ and $\chi_1(t)=0$ otherwise. Both functions are smooth on $\mathbb R$, $\chi_1'=\chi$, and $-\chi_1/\chi=(1-t)^2$ for $t<1$. Define
\[
 \Psi_u(x,w)=\int_{T_xX}u(F(x,w\zeta))
                       \chi(|\zeta|_h^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta_h,
\]
where $\mathrm d\zeta_h$ is the Lebesgue measure of the Hermitian space $(T_xX,h)$; in coordinates it is $\det h$ times the Lebesgue measure of the components of $\zeta$, and in unitary coordinates of $T_xX$ the kernel has total mass $\int\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta=1$. The substitution $\zeta\mapsto\mathrm e^{\mathrm it}\zeta$ shows that $\Psi_u$ is invariant under rotations of $w$. For $w\neq0$, the change of variables $y=F(x,w\zeta)$ in the fiber, whose inverse depends smoothly on $(x,w,y)$ by the inverse function theorem, writes $\Psi_u(x,w)$, in a chart around $x$, as $\int u(y)K(x,w,y)\,\mathrm dy$ with a smooth nonnegative kernel $K$ supported in a ball of radius $C|w|$ about $x$. Differentiation under the integral sign shows that $\Psi_u$ is smooth for $w\neq0$ even when $u$ has poles.

\emph{The joint complex Hessian.} Fix a center, the origin of its normal coordinates $z$, and let $p$ be a smooth plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline{B(0,3r)}$; the general case is treated at the end of the proof. We write $F(z,\xi)$ for $F(x',\zeta)$ when $x'$ has coordinate $z$ and $\zeta=\sum_m\xi_m\partial/\partial z_m$. We use multi-indices $I,J$ with $2\leq|I|,|J|\leq N$, write $1_l$ for the $l$th coordinate multi-index and $e_l$ for the $l$th unit vector of $\mathbb C^n$. A factor $I_ls^{I-1_l}$ is zero when $I_l=0$; the same convention applies to its conjugate and to $J$.

We first separate the holomorphic part of $F$. Let $a(z,\rho)$ be the polynomial formed by the monomials $z^\gamma\xi^I$ of the Taylor expansion of $F$ at $(0,0)$ with $|\gamma|\leq2$ and $|I|\leq N$, with $\xi$ renamed $\rho$. Since $F(z,0)=z$ and $D_\xi F(z,0)$ is the identity, $a(z,0)=z$ and $\partial_\rho a(z,0)=I$. Hence, for small $z$, the holomorphic map $a(z,\cdot)$ has a holomorphic inverse near $0$, and we define
\[
 \rho(z,\xi)=a(z,\cdot)^{-1}\bigl(F(z,\xi)\bigr),\qquad\text{so that}\qquad F(z,\xi)=a\bigl(z,\rho(z,\xi)\bigr).
\]
Call a monomial $z^\gamma\bar z^{\gamma'}\xi^I\bar\xi^{I'}$ discardable if $\gamma'\neq0$, $I'\neq0$, $|\gamma|\geq3$ or $|I|+|I'|\geq N+1$; again the product of a discardable monomial with any monomial is discardable. By construction, the Taylor expansion of $F(z,\xi)-a(z,\xi)$ at $(0,0)$ consists of discardable monomials. Since $\rho(z,\xi)-\xi$ is the integral over $t\in[0,1]$ of the derivative of $a(z,\cdot)^{-1}$ at $a(z,\xi)+t(F(z,\xi)-a(z,\xi))$, applied to $F(z,\xi)-a(z,\xi)$, the same holds for $\rho(z,\xi)-\xi$. Moreover, for each fixed $z$, the Taylor polynomial of degree $N$ of $F(z,\cdot)$ at $0$ is holomorphic, because it is that of $J_z$; since $a(z,\cdot)^{-1}$ is holomorphic, the same holds for $\rho(z,\cdot)$. Together with $\rho(z,0)=0$ and $\partial_\xi\rho(z,0)=I$, Taylor's formula in $\xi$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-fiber-factorization}
 \rho(z,\xi)=\xi+\sum_I\rho_I(z)\xi^I+R(z,\xi),
\end{equation}
with smooth vector-valued coefficients $\rho_I$ and a smooth remainder $R$ whose derivatives of order at most six, with $k$ of them in $\xi$ or $\bar\xi$, are bounded by $C|\xi|^{N+1-k}$. The monomials $z^\gamma\xi^I$ with $|\gamma|\leq2$ are not discardable, so $\rho_I(0)=0$ and $\partial_{z_j}\rho_I(0)=0$. We put
\[
 d_{Ik}=\partial_{\bar z_k}\rho_I(0),\qquad e_{Ijk}=\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\rho_I(0),
\]
vectors with components $d_{Ikm}$ and $e_{Ijkm}$.

For $0<|w|=r<r_0$ put $\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi)=w^{-1}\rho(z,w\xi)$, so that
\[
 \tilde\rho(z,w,\xi)=\xi+\sum_I\rho_I(z)w^{|I|-1}\xi^I+\tilde R(z,w,\xi),\qquad \tilde R(z,w,\xi)=w^{-1}R(z,w\xi).
\]
By the chain rule and the bounds for $R$, for $|\xi|\leq3$ every derivative of $\tilde R$ of order at most two in $(z,\bar z)$, at most three in $(\xi,\bar\xi)$ and $k\leq2$ in $(w,\bar w)$ is bounded by $Cr^{N-k}$. Since also $\rho_I(z)=O(|z|)$, we have $\|\partial_\xi\tilde\rho-I\|+\|\partial_{\bar\xi}\tilde\rho\|\leq\frac14$ for $|\xi|\leq3$ when $|z|$ and $r$ are small. As above, $\xi\mapsto\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi)$ is then a diffeomorphism of $\{|\xi|<3\}$ onto an open set, which contains $\{|s|\leq2\}$ by the contraction mapping principle, applied to $\xi\mapsto s+\xi-\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi)$ on $\{|\xi|\leq8/3\}$ (note that $\tilde\rho(z,w,0)=0$); we denote its inverse by $s\mapsto\xi(z,w,s)$. Since $a(z,w\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi))=F(z,w\xi)$, the substitution $s=\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi)$ gives
\[
 \Psi_u(z,w)=\int u\bigl(a(z,ws)\bigr)W(z,w,s)\,\mathrm ds,\qquad W=\chi(\mathcal Q)\mathcal J,
\]
where $\mathcal Q=|\xi(z,w,s)|^2_{h(z)}$, $\mathcal J$ is $\det h(z)$ times the real Jacobian determinant of $s\mapsto\xi(z,w,s)$, and $W$ is extended by zero outside the image. On the support of $\chi(|\xi|_h^2)$ we have $|\xi|<2$ and hence $|s|<2$ for small $|z|$ and $r$, so $W$ is smooth and vanishes for $|s|\geq2$. The point of this substitution is that the argument $a(z,ws)$ of $u$ is holomorphic in $(z,w,s)$; the nonholomorphic dependence on the base point has been moved into the weight $W$.

\emph{Step 1: the weight at the center.} For functions of $(w,s)$ with $0<|w|=r<r_0$ and $|s|\leq2$, we write $O_N$ for any function whose derivatives of order at most two in $(s,\bar s)$ and $k\leq2$ in $(w,\bar w)$ are bounded by $Cr^{N-k}$. Put
\[
 \beta^k(w,s)=\sum_Id_{Ik}w^{|I|-1}s^I,\qquad \gamma^{jk}(w,s)=\sum_Ie_{Ijk}w^{|I|-1}s^I,
\]
vectors that are holomorphic in $s$; for a holomorphic vector field $b(s)$ write $\operatorname{div}b=\sum_m\partial_{s_m}b_m$, $b\cdot\bar s=\sum_mb_m\bar s_m$, and $b\cdot\bar b'=\sum_mb_m\overline{b'_m}$. We differentiate the identity $\tilde\rho(z,w,\xi(z,w,s))=s$ in $\bar z_k$, in $z_j$, and then in $z_j$ and $\bar z_k$, and evaluate at $z=0$, where $\rho_I=0$ and $\partial_{z_j}\rho_I=0$. In each case the derivative of $\xi$ to be determined appears multiplied by $\partial_\xi\tilde\rho=I+\partial_\xi\tilde R$, and the remaining terms are the explicit terms $\partial_{\bar z_k}\rho_Iw^{|I|-1}\xi^I$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\rho_Iw^{|I|-1}\xi^I$, terms containing a derivative of $\tilde R$, and terms containing the factor $\partial_{z_j}\xi$. Using the bounds for $\tilde R$, and the fact that $\beta^k$ and $\gamma^{jk}$ have bounded derivatives, we find successively that at $z=0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-inverse-jet}
 \xi=s+O_N,\qquad\partial_{z_j}\xi=O_N,\qquad\partial_{\bar z_k}\xi=-\beta^k+O_N,\qquad\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\xi=-\gamma^{jk}+O_N;
\end{equation}
the bounds for one further derivative in $s$ follow in the same way, because $\tilde R$ has bounded derivatives of order three in $\xi$. Differentiating the identity in $\bar s$ instead, we find $\partial_{\bar s}\xi=-(\partial_\xi\tilde\rho)^{-1}\partial_{\bar\xi}\tilde R\;\overline{\partial_s\xi}$, because the coefficients of \eqref{eq:chern-fiber-factorization} do not involve $\bar\xi$; hence $\partial_{\bar s}\xi$ and its derivatives $\partial_{z_j}$, $\partial_{\bar z_k}$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}$ are $O_N$ at $z=0$.

Now $\mathcal Q=\sum_{l,m}h_{l\bar m}(z)\xi_l\bar\xi_m$, where $h(0)=I$, $\mathrm dh(0)=0$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}h_{l\bar m}(0)=-c_{jklm}$. By the product rule, \eqref{eq:chern-inverse-jet} and $\partial_{z_j}\bar\xi=\overline{\partial_{\bar z_j}\xi}$, at $z=0$
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal Q&=|s|^2+O_N,\qquad \partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q=-\beta^k\cdot\bar s+O_N,\\
 \partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q&=-\sum_{l,m}c_{jklm}s_l\bar s_m-\gamma^{jk}\cdot\bar s-\overline{\gamma^{kj}\cdot\bar s}+\beta^k\cdot\overline{\beta^j}+O_N,
\end{align*}
and $\partial_{z_j}\mathcal Q=\overline{\partial_{\bar z_j}\mathcal Q}$, since $\mathcal Q$ is real. For $\mathcal J$, let $M=\partial_s\xi$. The real Jacobian determinant of $s\mapsto\xi$ is the determinant of the matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}M&\partial_{\bar s}\xi\\\overline{\partial_{\bar s}\xi}&\overline M\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, that is, $|\det M|^2$ plus a polynomial in the entries of these blocks each of whose terms contains an entry of $\partial_{\bar s}\xi$ or of its conjugate; by the last part of Step~1, the latter and its relevant derivatives are $O_N$ at $z=0$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:chern-inverse-jet} in $s$, we have at $z=0$: $M=I+O_N$, $\partial_{z_j}M=O_N$, $\partial_{\bar z_k}M=-\partial_s\beta^k+O_N$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}M=-\partial_s\gamma^{jk}+O_N$. Jacobi's formula $\partial\det M=\det M\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}\partial M)$, differentiated once more, gives $\partial_{\bar z_k}\det M=-\operatorname{div}\beta^k+O_N$, $\partial_{z_j}\det M=O_N$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\det M=-\operatorname{div}\gamma^{jk}+O_N$. Writing $|\det M|^2=\det M\cdot\overline{\det M}$ and using $\det h(0)=1$, $\mathrm d\det h(0)=0$ and $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\det h(0)=-\sum_lc_{jkll}$, we obtain at $z=0$
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal J&=1+O_N,\qquad \partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal J=-\operatorname{div}\beta^k+O_N,\\
 \partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal J&=-\sum_lc_{jkll}-\operatorname{div}\gamma^{jk}-\overline{\operatorname{div}\gamma^{kj}}+\operatorname{div}\beta^k\;\overline{\operatorname{div}\beta^j}+O_N.
\end{align*}
Finally we differentiate $W=\chi(\mathcal Q)\mathcal J$. Write $\chi,\chi',\chi''$ for these functions evaluated at $|s|^2$, and recall that $\partial_{s_m}|s|^2=\bar s_m$. Since $\partial_{\bar z_k}W=\chi'(\mathcal Q)\mathcal J\,\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q+\chi(\mathcal Q)\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal J$, we find at $z=0$ that $\partial_{\bar z_k}W=-\chi'\beta^k\cdot\bar s-\chi\operatorname{div}\beta^k+O_N$. For the mixed derivatives, $\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}W$ is the sum of $\chi''\mathcal J\partial_{z_j}\mathcal Q\,\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q$, $\chi'\bigl(\mathcal J\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q+\partial_{z_j}\mathcal Q\,\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal J+\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal Q\,\partial_{z_j}\mathcal J\bigr)$ and $\chi\,\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\mathcal J$, with $\chi$ and its derivatives evaluated at $\mathcal Q$. We insert the formulas above and group the terms. The terms containing $c$ give $-\sum_{l,m}c_{jklm}(\chi's_l\bar s_m+\chi\delta_{lm})$. The terms containing $e$ give $-\chi'\gamma^{jk}\cdot\bar s-\chi\operatorname{div}\gamma^{jk}$ and its counterpart with $\overline{\gamma^{kj}}$. The terms quadratic in $d$ are
\[
 \chi''\bigl(\overline{\beta^j}\cdot s\bigr)\bigl(\beta^k\cdot\bar s\bigr)+\chi'\beta^k\cdot\overline{\beta^j}+\chi'\bigl(\beta^k\cdot\bar s\bigr)\overline{\operatorname{div}\beta^j}+\chi'\bigl(\overline{\beta^j}\cdot s\bigr)\operatorname{div}\beta^k+\chi\operatorname{div}\beta^k\;\overline{\operatorname{div}\beta^j}.
\]
Since $\beta^k$ is holomorphic and $\overline{\beta^j}$ antiholomorphic in $s$, we have $\sum_l\partial_{\bar s_l}(\chi\beta^k_m\overline{\beta^j_l})=\beta^k_m\bigl(\chi'\,\overline{\beta^j}\cdot s+\chi\overline{\operatorname{div}\beta^j}\bigr)$, and applying $\sum_m\partial_{s_m}$ to this produces exactly the five terms above. In the same way the first two groups are divergences. We have proved that at $z=0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-weight-jet}
\begin{aligned}
 W={}&\chi+O_N,\qquad \partial_{\bar z_k}W=-\sum_m\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi\beta^k_m\bigr)+O_N,\\
 \partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}W={}&-\sum_{l,m}c_{jklm}\partial_{s_m}(\chi s_l)-\sum_m\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi\gamma^{jk}_m\bigr)-\sum_m\partial_{\bar s_m}\bigl(\chi\overline{\gamma^{kj}_m}\bigr)\\
 &+\sum_{l,m}\partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_l}\bigl(\chi\beta^k_m\overline{\beta^j_l}\bigr)+O_N,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and $\partial_{z_j}W=\overline{\partial_{\bar z_j}W}$, since $W$ is real. Here $W$ and the explicit terms vanish for $|s|\geq2$, so the remainders do as well.

\emph{Step 2: the weights as divergences.} For a multi-index $I$ with $|I|\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-weight-divergence}
\begin{aligned}
 \chi(|s|^2)\bar s^I&=\sum_m\partial_{s_m}\Bigl(\chi_1(|s|^2)\frac{I_m}{|I|}\bar s^{I-1_m}\Bigr),\\
 \chi(|s|^2)s^I&=\sum_m\partial_{\bar s_m}\Bigl(\chi_1(|s|^2)\frac{I_m}{|I|}s^{I-1_m}\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\partial_{s_m}\chi_1(|s|^2)=\chi(|s|^2)\bar s_m$ because $\chi_1'=\chi$, the monomial $\bar s^{I-1_m}$ is antiholomorphic, and Euler's identity $\sum_mI_m\bar s_m\bar s^{I-1_m}=|I|\bar s^I$ gives the first formula; the second is its complex conjugate. Similarly $\chi(|s|^2)s_l=\partial_{\bar s_l}\chi_1(|s|^2)$. We shall integrate by parts in the fiber variables: if $f$ and $g$ are continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of the support of $g$, and this support is compact, then $\int f\,\partial_{s_m}g\,\mathrm ds=-\int g\,\partial_{s_m}f\,\mathrm ds$, and similarly for $\partial_{\bar s_m}$. Since $\partial_{s_m}=\frac12(\partial_{\Real s_m}-\mathrm i\partial_{\Img s_m})$, this follows from Fubini's theorem and the fundamental theorem of calculus, applied to the compactly supported function $fg$. In all integrations by parts below, one of the two factors is a product of $\chi(|s|^2)$ or $\chi_1(|s|^2)$ with a polynomial, or one of the remainders of Step~1, hence smooth with support in $\{|s|\leq2\}$, so no boundary terms occur.

\emph{Step 3: the derivatives of $p(a(z,ws))$.} Put $G(z,w,s)=p(a(z,ws))$ and $\mathcal L=\sum_jv_j\partial_{z_j}+\eta\,\partial_w$, so that $\mathcal Df[v,\eta]^2=\mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}f$ for every smooth function $f$ of $(z,w)$. At $z=0$ write $D_\rho a=\partial_\rho a(0,ws)$ and
\[
 Y=Da(v,\eta s)=\partial_za(0,ws)v+\eta\,D_\rho a\,s,
\]
both holomorphic in $s$, and let $P$ be the complex Hessian of $p$ at $a(0,ws)$, with $P(y,\bar y')$ and $P[y]^2$ as in the statement of the identity below. Since $a(z,ws)$ is holomorphic in $(z,w,s)$, we have $\mathcal LG=\sum_lp_l(a)\,(\mathcal La)_l$, where $p_l=\partial p/\partial z_l$, the vector $\mathcal La$ is holomorphic and equals $Y$ at $z=0$, and $\partial_{s_m}a(0,ws)=w\,D_\rho a\,e_m$. Hence, at $z=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-integrand-derivatives}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}G=P[Y]^2,\qquad \partial_{\bar s_l}(\mathcal LG)=\bar w\,P\bigl(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr),\\
 \partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_l}G=r^2P\bigl(D_\rho a\,e_m,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: assembling the terms.} The integrand $GW$ is smooth with support in $\{|s|\leq2\}$, so we may differentiate under the integral sign. Since $G$ and $W$ are real, $\bar{\mathcal L}G\,\mathcal LW$ is the conjugate of $\mathcal LG\,\bar{\mathcal L}W$, and at $z=0$
\[
 \mathcal D\Psi_p[v,\eta]^2=\int\Bigl(W\,\mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}G+2\Real\bigl(\mathcal LG\,\bar{\mathcal L}W\bigr)+G\,\mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}W\Bigr)\mathrm ds.
\]
Here, at $z=0$, $\bar{\mathcal L}W=\sum_k\bar v_k\partial_{\bar z_k}W+\bar\eta\partial_{\bar w}W$ and
\[
 \mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}W=\sum_{j,k}v_j\bar v_k\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}W+\bar\eta\sum_jv_j\partial_{\bar w}\partial_{z_j}W+\eta\sum_k\bar v_k\partial_w\partial_{\bar z_k}W+|\eta|^2\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}W,
\]
where the derivatives in $w$ may be taken after putting $z=0$. We insert \eqref{eq:chern-weight-jet}. The explicit terms there do not depend on $w$ except through $\beta^k$ and $\gamma^{jk}$, and $\partial_w\beta^k=\sum_I(|I|-1)d_{Ik}w^{|I|-2}s^I$; each derivative of a remainder in $w$ costs one power of $r$. Thus at $z=0$
\begin{align*}
 W&=\chi+\delta_0,\qquad \bar{\mathcal L}W=-\sum_{k,m}\bar v_k\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi\beta^k_m\bigr)+\delta_1,\\
 \mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}W&=\sum_{j,k}v_j\bar v_k\,[\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}W]^\flat-\eta\sum_{k,m}\bar v_k\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi\partial_w\beta^k_m\bigr)-\bar\eta\sum_{j,m}v_j\partial_{\bar s_m}\bigl(\chi\overline{\partial_w\beta^j_m}\bigr)+\delta_2,
\end{align*}
where $[\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}W]^\flat$ denotes the explicit part of \eqref{eq:chern-weight-jet}, and $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$ vanish for $|s|\geq2$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-weight-remainders}
 |\delta_0|\leq Cr^N,\qquad |\delta_1|+|\nabla_s\delta_1|\leq Cr^{N-1}(|v|+|\eta|),\qquad |\delta_2|\leq Cr^{N-2}(|v|+|\eta|)^2.
\end{equation}
Accordingly $\mathcal D\Psi_p[v,\eta]^2=T_1+T_2+T_3+E$, where $T_1=\int P[Y]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds$, $T_2$ and $T_3$ are the contributions of the explicit parts of $\bar{\mathcal L}W$ and $\mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}W$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-kernel-remainder}
 E=\int\Bigl(P[Y]^2\delta_0+2\Real\bigl(\mathcal LG\,\delta_1\bigr)+G\,\delta_2\Bigr)\mathrm ds.
\end{equation}

\emph{The first-derivative terms.} By \eqref{eq:chern-weight-divergence}, $\chi\beta^k_m=\sum_l\partial_{\bar s_l}(\chi_1\sigma^k_{ml})$ with $\sigma^k_{ml}=\sum_Id_{Ikm}w^{|I|-1}\frac{I_l}{|I|}s^{I-1_l}$, which is holomorphic in $s$. Integrating by parts in $\bar s_l$ and using \eqref{eq:chern-integrand-derivatives} and $\partial_{s_m}\chi_1(|s|^2)=\chi\bar s_m$, we get
\begin{align*}
 -\int\mathcal LG\,\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi\beta^k_m\bigr)\,\mathrm ds
 &=\sum_l\int\partial_{\bar s_l}(\mathcal LG)\,\partial_{s_m}\bigl(\chi_1\sigma^k_{ml}\bigr)\,\mathrm ds\\
 &=\bar w\sum_l\int P\bigl(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr)\bigl(\chi\bar s_m\sigma^k_{ml}+\chi_1\partial_{s_m}\sigma^k_{ml}\bigr)\,\mathrm ds.
\end{align*}
We multiply by $\bar v_k$ and sum over $k$ and $m$. Comparing with the definitions of $\Xi$ and $\Theta$ below, we have $\bar w\sum_{k,m}\bar v_k\bar s_m\sigma^k_{ml}=r^2\,\overline{\Xi^l}$ and $\bar w\sum_{k,m}\bar v_k\partial_{s_m}\sigma^k_{ml}=r^2\,\overline{\Theta^l}$, and $P$ is antilinear in its second argument. Therefore
\[
 T_2=2r^2\Real\int P\bigl(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,\Xi}\bigr)\chi\,\mathrm ds+2r^2\Real\int P\bigl(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,\Theta}\bigr)\chi_1\,\mathrm ds.
\]

\emph{The value terms.} Each explicit term of $\mathcal L\bar{\mathcal L}W$ is a second derivative $\partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_l}$ of a weight, and two integrations by parts move it onto $G$. For the curvature term we use $\partial_{s_m}(\chi s_l)=\partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_l}\chi_1$ and \eqref{eq:chern-integrand-derivatives}:
\[
 -\sum_{j,k,l,m}v_j\bar v_kc_{jklm}\int G\,\partial_{s_m}(\chi s_l)\,\mathrm ds=-r^2\sum_{l,m}\int P\bigl(D_\rho a\,e_m,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr)\chi_1C^v_{ml}\,\mathrm ds.
\]
For the terms containing $\gamma^{jk}$ and $\partial_w\beta^k$, \eqref{eq:chern-weight-divergence} writes $\chi\gamma^{jk}_m=\sum_l\partial_{\bar s_l}(\chi_1\tau^{jk}_{ml})$ and $\chi\partial_w\beta^k_m=\sum_l\partial_{\bar s_l}(\chi_1\partial_w\sigma^k_{ml})$, with $\sigma^k_{ml}$ as above and
\[
 \tau^{jk}_{ml}=\sum_Ie_{Ijkm}w^{|I|-1}\frac{I_l}{|I|}s^{I-1_l},\qquad \partial_w\sigma^k_{ml}=\sum_Id_{Ikm}(|I|-1)w^{|I|-2}\frac{I_l}{|I|}s^{I-1_l}.
\]
Two integrations by parts give the contribution
\[
 -r^2\sum_{l,m}\int P\bigl(D_\rho a\,e_m,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr)\chi_1B_{ml}\,\mathrm ds,\qquad B_{ml}=\sum_{j,k}v_j\bar v_k\tau^{jk}_{ml}+\eta\sum_k\bar v_k\partial_w\sigma^k_{ml}.
\] The terms containing $\overline{\gamma^{kj}}$ and $\overline{\partial_w\beta^j}$ are treated with the conjugate formula in \eqref{eq:chern-weight-divergence}, and after renaming $j\leftrightarrow k$ they give the same expression with the matrix $B$ replaced by its conjugate transpose $B^*$. Finally, with $\beta=\sum_k\bar v_k\beta^k$, two integrations by parts turn the last term of \eqref{eq:chern-weight-jet} into
\[
 \sum_{j,k}v_j\bar v_k\sum_{l,m}\int G\,\partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_l}\bigl(\chi\beta^k_m\overline{\beta^j_l}\bigr)\mathrm ds
 =r^2\int P\bigl[D_\rho a\,\beta\bigr]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds.
\]
For a matrix $M$ in the fiber variables write
\[
 P[Da(M)]=\sum_{l,m}M_{ml}P\bigl(D_\rho a\,e_m,\overline{D_\rho a\,e_l}\bigr),
\]
so that $P[Da(yy'^*)]=P(D_\rho a\,y,\overline{D_\rho a\,y'})$ for vectors $y$ and $y'$. With this notation, we have shown that $T_3$ is equal to $r^2\int P[Da(U)]\chi\,\mathrm ds$, where, on the unit ball, on which $\chi>0$, the matrix $U=-\frac{\chi_1}{\chi}(C^v+B+B^*)+\beta\beta^*$ is the Hermitian part of the matrix displayed below.

\emph{The identity.} By $T_1$, $T_2$ and $T_3$, and by completing the square $P[Y]^2+2r^2\Real P(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,\Xi})=P[Y+r^2D_\rho a\,\Xi]^2-r^4P[D_\rho a\,\Xi]^2$, where $Y+r^2D_\rho a\,\Xi=Da(v,\eta s+r^2\Xi)$, we arrive at the identity \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity} below, valid for every $n\geq1$. Its coefficients are
\begin{align*}
 \Xi^m={}&\sum_{I,j,l}\frac{I_m}{|I|}\overline d_{Ijl}
                 \bar w^{|I|-2}s_l\bar s^{I-1_m}v_j,\\
 \Theta^m={}&\sum_{I,j,l}\frac{I_m}{|I|}(I-1_m)_l\,\overline d_{Ijl}
                 \bar w^{|I|-2}\bar s^{I-1_m-1_l}v_j,\\
 U_{ml}={}&-\frac{\chi_1}{\chi}\left(
      \sum_{j,k}c_{jklm}v_j\bar v_k
      +2\sum_{I,j,k}e_{Ijkm}w^{|I|-1}
                         \frac{I_l}{|I|}s^{I-1_l}v_j\bar v_k\right.\\
 &\hspace{29mm}\left.
      +2\sum_{I,k}d_{Ikm}(|I|-1)w^{|I|-2}
                          \frac{I_l}{|I|}s^{I-1_l}\eta\bar v_k\right)\\
 &+\sum_{I,J,j,k}d_{Ikm}\overline d_{Jjl}
                    w^{|I|-1}\bar w^{|J|-1}s^I\bar s^Jv_j\bar v_k.
\end{align*}
Here the factor $(I-1_m)_l\bar s^{I-1_m-1_l}$ is zero when $(I-1_m)_l=0$, the quotient $\chi_1/\chi$ is evaluated at $|s|^2$, and $U$ stands for its Hermitian part. Let $P=(p_{l\bar m})\geq0$ be the complex Hessian of the local plurisubharmonic potential $p$, evaluated at $a(0,ws)$, and write $P(y,\bar y')=\sum_{l,m}p_{l\bar m}y_l\bar y'_m$, so that $P[y]^2=P(y,\bar y)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D\Psi_p[v,\eta]^2
   ={}&\int P\bigl[Da(v,\eta s+r^2\Xi)\bigr]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds
       +r^2\int P\bigl[Da(U-r^2\Xi\Xi^*)\bigr]\chi\,\mathrm ds\\
   &+2r^2\Real\int P\bigl(Da(0,\Theta),\overline{Da(v,\eta s)}\bigr)\chi_1\,\mathrm ds+E.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In the first integral, $Da$ is the differential of $a$ at $(0,ws)$ applied to the vector $(v,\eta s+r^2\Xi)$; in the second, the Hermitian matrix $U-r^2\Xi\Xi^*$ in the fiber variables is transformed by the fiber differential of $a$ as just defined; in the third, $Da$ is applied to the vectors $(0,\Theta)$ and $(v,\eta s)$, and we used that the real part of $P(Y,\overline{D_\rho a\,\Theta})$ equals that of $P(D_\rho a\,\Theta,\overline Y)$, because $P$ is Hermitian. The remainder $E$ is given by \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-remainder}.

\emph{Bounds for the remainders.} By the Jensen--Lelong formula (\cref{thm:euclidean-jensen-lelong}), the normalized trace mass $D_p(x,t)$ of \eqref{eq:lelong-ball-density} is nondecreasing in $t$, and $\mu_p(B(x,t))$ is a fixed multiple of $t^{d-2}D_p(x,t)$. On a fixed larger ball the trace mass of $p$ is bounded by $C\|p\|_{L^1}$ of a still larger ball: pair $\Delta p\geq0$ with a cutoff function. Here $p=u+q$, and we choose $q$ as a fixed local potential of $\alpha$ on one of finitely many coordinate balls covering $X$ (\cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}); by a Lebesgue number argument, the normal ball of a fixed radius about each center lies in one of them. This choice is harmless, because $\mu_p$, and hence $m_u$, is the trace measure of $\alpha+\mathcal Du$ and does not depend on $q$. Hence
\[
 \mu_p(B(x,t))\leq Ct^{d-2},\qquad
   t^{-d}\int_{B(x,t)}|u|\leq C(1+|\log t|).
\]
For the second estimate, \eqref{eq:euclidean-jensen-lelong} and the bound for $D_p$ give $M_p(x,\varrho)\geq M_p(x,R)-2C\log(R/\varrho)$ for $\varrho<R$, where $M_p$ denotes spherical means. Averaging over $R$ in a fixed interval bounds the right side below by $-C(1+|\log\varrho|)$, since the average of $|p|$ over a fixed annulus is bounded in terms of $\|u\|_{L^1}$. As $|p|=2p^+-p$ and $p^+$ is bounded in terms of $\sup_Xu$, integration over spheres gives $\int_{B(x,t)}|p|\leq Ct^d(1+|\log t|)$, and $|u|\leq|p|+|q|$. In particular $m_u\leq Cr^{-2}$.

To bound $E$, note that $|Y|\leq C(|v|+|\eta|)$ and $P[Y]^2\leq|Y|^2\operatorname{tr}P$ for $|s|\leq2$. Moreover $\mathcal LG=\sum_mb_m\partial_{s_m}G$ with $b=w^{-1}(D_\rho a)^{-1}Y$, by \eqref{eq:chern-integrand-derivatives} and its proof, and $|b|+|\nabla_sb|\leq Cr^{-1}(|v|+|\eta|)$; an integration by parts gives $\int\mathcal LG\,\delta_1\,\mathrm ds=-\int G\sum_m\partial_{s_m}(b_m\delta_1)\,\mathrm ds$. By \eqref{eq:chern-weight-remainders},
\[
 |E|\leq Cr^{N-2}(|v|+|\eta|)^2\int_{|s|\leq2}\bigl(r^2\operatorname{tr}P+|G|\bigr)\mathrm ds.
\]
The map $s\mapsto a(0,ws)$ is injective on $\{|s|\leq2\}$, has real Jacobian determinant at least $r^d/2$, and takes values in $B(0,3r)$. Changing variables and using the two mass bounds, we obtain $|E|\leq Cr^{N-2}(1+|\log r|)(|v|^2+|\eta|^2)\leq Cr^2(|v|^2+|\eta|^2)$, since $N=8$.

\emph{The lower bound.} The first integral of \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity} is nonnegative. For a nonnegative Hermitian matrix $P$ and any matrix $M$ we have $|\sum_{l,m}p_{l\bar m}M_{lm}|\leq\|M\|\operatorname{tr}P$, by diagonalizing $P$; in particular $|P(y,\bar y')|\leq|y||y'|\operatorname{tr}P$. Since $a(0,\rho)$ is the Taylor polynomial of degree $N$ of $F(0,\cdot)$, which is that of $J_0$, we have $D_\rho a=I+O(r^2)$ and $\partial_za(0,ws)=I+O(r)$, and $a(0,ws)=ws+O(r^3)$, for $|s|\leq2$. The formulas for the coefficients give $|\Xi|\leq C|v|$ and $|\Theta|\leq(C_d+Cr)|v|$, where $C_d$ bounds the coefficients $d_{Ikm}$ with $|I|=2$, because the terms of $\Theta$ with $|I|\geq3$ carry a factor $\bar w$. Every term of $U$ except the last carries the factor $-\chi_1/\chi=(1-|s|^2)^2\in[0,1]$; after this factor is removed, the curvature term has norm at most $C_c|v|^2$, where $C_c$ bounds the $c_{jklm}$, the $e$ term at most $Cr|v|^2$ and the mixed $d$ term at most $C|v||\eta|$, while the last term $\beta\beta^*$ of $U$ gives the nonnegative contribution $r^2\int P[D_\rho a\,\beta]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds$. Put
\[
 \lambda_0(x,r)=2r^2\int(-\chi_1(|s|^2))
                           \operatorname{tr}P(x+rs)\,\mathrm ds,
 \qquad m=1+r^{-d}\mu_p(B(x,3r)).
\]
The substitution $s\mapsto w^{-1}a(0,ws)=s+O(r^2)$, whose derivative is $I+O(r^2)$, changes the kernel $-\chi_1(|s|^2)$ by $O(r^2)$ on $\{|s|\leq2\}$, and the unitary substitution $s\mapsto(w/r)s$ does not change it. Hence $r^2\int(-\chi_1)|D_\rho a|^2\operatorname{tr}P\,\mathrm ds\leq\frac12\lambda_0+Cr^4m$, and similarly $r^2\int_{|s|\leq1}\operatorname{tr}P\,\mathrm ds\leq Cr^2m$. Consequently, the second and third integrals of \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity}, together with the square correction, are bounded below by
\[
 -A\lambda_0(x,r)|v|^2
       -C(r^3m+r^2)|v|^2-Cr^2m|v||\eta|,
 \qquad A=\tfrac12C_c+C_d .
\]
Thus $A$ depends only on the fixed geometry, and since also $|E|\leq Cr^2(|v|^2+|\eta|^2)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-potential-joint-bound}
 \mathcal D\Psi_p[v,\eta]^2\geq-\bigl(A\lambda_0+C(r^2+r^3m)\bigr)|v|^2-Cr^2m|v||\eta|-Cr^2|\eta|^2.
\end{equation}
For $v=0$ the coefficients $\Xi$, $\Theta$ and $U$ vanish, and \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity} reads $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}\Psi_p=\int P[D_\rho a\,s]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds+E$.

It remains to subtract the smoothing of the smooth function $q$. Since $q$ is smooth, $\Psi_q(z,w)$ is smooth also at $w=0$, where it equals $q(z)$. By rotation invariance, the terms of degree one and the terms in $w^2$ and $\bar w^2$ of its Taylor expansion in $w$ vanish, so $\Psi_q(z,w)=q(z)+b(z)|w|^2+O(|w|^3)$, with a remainder whose derivatives lose one power of $|w|$ for each derivative in $w$. Hence, with $\mathcal Dq=\alpha$,
\[
 \alpha[v]^2-\mathcal D\Psi_q[v,\eta]^2\geq-Cr^2|v|^2-Cr|v||\eta|-C_q|\eta|^2.
\]
Since $\Psi_u=\Psi_p-\Psi_q$ and $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}(K|w|^2)=K$, we choose $K\geq C_q+1$. Adding these estimates proves \eqref{eq:chern-joint-mass-estimate} with $\lambda_0$ in place of $\lambda_u$ and $m$ in place of $m_u$, for smooth $p$. Moreover, for $v=0$ it gives $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}H_u\geq K-C_q-Cr^2>0$. For a function of $|w|$ we have $\partial_s^2[H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)]=4r^2\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}H_u$ with $r=\mathrm e^s$, because $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}g=\frac1{4r}\partial_r(r\partial_rg)$ for a radial function $g$; so $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)$ is strictly convex in $s$.

\emph{Comparison of $\lambda_0$ with $\lambda_u$.} Let $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_g(r)=\int g(x+rs)\chi(|s|^2)\,\mathrm ds$ denote the Euclidean radial convolution in the normal coordinates. For a smooth function $g$, the radial derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-radial-derivative}
 r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_g(r)=-2r^2\int\chi_1(|s|^2)\sum_mg_{m\bar m}(x+rs)\,\mathrm ds.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $r\partial_r[g(x+rs)]=\sum_m\bigl(s_m\partial_{s_m}+\bar s_m\partial_{\bar s_m}\bigr)[g(x+rs)]$, and by $s_m\chi=\partial_{\bar s_m}\chi_1$ and an integration by parts, $\int s_m\partial_{s_m}[g(x+rs)]\chi\,\mathrm ds=-\int\partial_{s_m}\partial_{\bar s_m}[g(x+rs)]\chi_1\,\mathrm ds=-r^2\int g_{m\bar m}(x+rs)\chi_1\,\mathrm ds$; the terms with $\bar s_m$ are the complex conjugates. In particular $r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p=\lambda_0$, and $|r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_q|\leq Cr^2$.

By \eqref{eq:chern-normal-kernel-map}, the substitution $s\mapsto r^{-1}F(x,rs)=s+O(r^2)$, with derivative $I+O(r^2)$, shows that the kernel of $\Psi_u(x,r)$ differs from that of $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_u(r)$ by at most $Cr^{2-d}$ on $B(x,2r)$, where $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_u=\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p-\Psi^{\mathrm e}_q$. Hence the radial function $\psi(r)=\Psi_u(x,r)-\Psi^{\mathrm e}_u(r)$ satisfies $|\psi(r)|\leq Cr^{2-d}\int_{B(x,2r)}|u|\leq Cr^2(1+|\log r|)$, so $\psi(r)\to0$ as $r\to0$. In the same way, by the case $v=0$ of \eqref{eq:chern-kernel-square-identity}, the matrix-valued kernels against which $P$ is integrated in $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}\Psi_p$ and in $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p=\int P(x+ws)[s]^2\chi\,\mathrm ds$ differ in norm by at most $Cr^{2-d}$ on $B(x,3r)$; since $|P[M]|\leq\|M\|\operatorname{tr}P$, these vertical derivatives differ by at most $Cr^2m+|E|\leq C$. The smooth function $q$ contributes bounded vertical derivatives as well. Thus $|\partial_r(r\partial_r\psi)|=4r|\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}\psi|\leq Cr$ for all small $r$. Integrating from $r_1$ to $r$ shows that $\ell=\lim_{r_1\to0}r_1\psi'(r_1)$ exists and $|r\psi'(r)-\ell|\leq Cr^2$. If $\ell\neq0$, then $\psi'(r)=\ell/r+O(r)$, and $\psi(r)-\psi(r_1)=\ell\log(r/r_1)+O(r^2)$ would be unbounded as $r_1\to0$, contradicting $\psi\to0$. Hence $\ell=0$ and $|r\partial_r\psi|\leq Cr^2$. Since $\lambda_u=r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p-r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_q+r\partial_r\psi+2Kr^2$, we obtain $|\lambda_u-\lambda_0|\leq Cr^2$. Since $m\leq m_u$, this proves \eqref{eq:chern-joint-mass-estimate} for smooth $p$; we record that it holds with $m$ in place of $m_u$.

\emph{Nonsmooth potentials.} Let $p$ be plurisubharmonic near $\overline{B(0,4r)}$, and let $p_\varepsilon=p*\rho_\varepsilon$ with $0<\varepsilon<r$. By \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}, applied to the subharmonic function $p$, we have $p\leq p_\varepsilon\leq\sup_{B(0,4r)}p$, so $|p_\varepsilon|\leq|p|+C$ on $B(0,3r)$. The functions $p_\varepsilon$ are smooth and plurisubharmonic, because for every $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ the function $\sum_{j,k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}p_\varepsilon$ is the mollification of the positive distribution $\sum_{j,k}\xi_j\bar\xi_kp_{j\bar k}$ (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative,prop:distribution-smoothing}). For the same reason the trace measure of $p_\varepsilon$ is the mollification of $\mu_p$, so $\mu_{p_\varepsilon}(B(0,3r))\leq\mu_p(B(0,4r))$, and the monotonicity of the normalized trace mass of $p_\varepsilon$ gives $\mu_{p_\varepsilon}(B(0,t))\leq Ct^{d-2}$ for $t\leq3r$. Thus the mass bounds used above hold for $p_\varepsilon$ on these balls, with constants independent of $\varepsilon$, and \eqref{eq:chern-joint-mass-estimate} holds for $u_\varepsilon=p_\varepsilon-q$ at the center, with $1+r^{-d}\mu_{p_\varepsilon}(B(0,3r))\leq m_u$ in place of $m_u$; so do the inequalities $|\lambda_{u_\varepsilon}-\lambda_0(p_\varepsilon)|\leq Cr^2$ and $\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}H_{u_\varepsilon}\geq K-C_q-Cr^2$. Since $p_\varepsilon\to p$ in $L^1(B(0,3r))$ (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}(2)), and $\Psi$, its derivatives in $(z,w)$ for $w\neq0$ and $\Psi^{\mathrm e}$ are given by integration against smooth kernels, the joint Levi form of $H_{u_\varepsilon}$ at $(0,w)$, $\lambda_{u_\varepsilon}$ and $\lambda_0(p_\varepsilon)=r\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_{p_\varepsilon}$ converge to the corresponding quantities for $u$. Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ proves \eqref{eq:chern-joint-mass-estimate} and the strict convexity of $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Finally, inserting $m_u\leq Cr^{-2}$, or the hypothesis $m_u\leq M(x)/r$, and using $r<1$, we obtain \eqref{eq:chern-initial-joint-bound} and \eqref{eq:chern-improved-joint-bound}.

\emph{Behaviour as $r\to0$.} The kernel of $\Psi_u$ is positive with mass one, so the upper bound for $u$ bounds $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)$ from above as $s\to-\infty$. A convex function of $s$ that is bounded above as $s\to-\infty$ is nondecreasing: if its slope were negative at some $s_0$, the tangent line at $s_0$, which lies below the function, would tend to $+\infty$ as $s\to-\infty$. Hence its slope $\lambda_u$ is nonnegative. By \cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison}(2), applied to the subharmonic function $p$ (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}), the spherical means of $p$ about $x$ decrease to $p(x)$ as the radius decreases to zero; since $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p(r)$ is an average of these spherical means at radii at most $r$, monotone convergence gives $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_p(r)\to p(x)$, and $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_q(r)\to q(x)$. Since $\psi(r)\to0$, we get $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)\to u(x)$ as $s\to-\infty$. If $u$ is bounded near $x$, this limit is finite. The slope of a convex function is nondecreasing, so it has a limit as $s\to-\infty$; a positive limit would force $H_u(x,\mathrm e^s)\to-\infty$. Therefore $\lambda_u(x,r)\to0$.

\emph{The comparison statements.} If $u$ is bounded on $B(x,2r)$, the comparison of the kernels above gives $|\Psi_u(x,r)-\Psi^{\mathrm e}_u(r)|\leq Cr^2\sup_{B(x,2r)}|u|$, which is the asserted comparison with radial convolution. Now let $f$ be a $C^1$ function whose weak complex Hessian is bounded by $M_f$. In normal coordinates its Euclidean Laplacian $4\sum_mf_{m\bar m}$ is bounded by $CM_f$, and so is the Laplacian of its mollifications $f_\varepsilon$. By \eqref{eq:chern-radial-derivative}, $|\partial_r\Psi^{\mathrm e}_{f_\varepsilon}(r)|\leq CM_fr$, and since $\Psi^{\mathrm e}_{f_\varepsilon}(r)\to f_\varepsilon(x)$ as $r\to0$, we get $|\Psi^{\mathrm e}_{f_\varepsilon}(r)-f_\varepsilon(x)|\leq CM_fr^2$. Letting $\varepsilon\to0$, the same bound holds for $f$, by uniform convergence. Finally, by \eqref{eq:chern-normal-kernel-map}, $|f(F(x,r\zeta))-f(x+r\zeta)|\leq Cr^3\|\nabla f\|_\infty$ for $|\zeta|\leq1$, and $h(0)=I$, so $|\Psi_f(x,r)-\Psi^{\mathrm e}_f(r)|\leq Cr^3\|\nabla f\|_\infty$. This proves \eqref{eq:chern-regular-obstacle-error}.

\emph{The generator.} To prove \eqref{eq:chern-smoothing-generator}, we work in a fixed coordinate chart with a smooth frame of $T X$ that is unitary for $h$, obtained by the Gram--Schmidt process, so that $\zeta$ becomes a vector of $\mathbb C^n$ independent of the base point, $\mathrm d\zeta_h=\mathrm d\zeta$, and the frame matrix $e(x)$ satisfies $e\,e^*=(h^{i\bar j})$. In the chart, $F(x,t)=x+e(x)t+Q_x(e(x)t,e(x)t)+O(|t|^3)$ with a quadratic map $Q_x$ that is holomorphic, by the construction of $F$. The first moments of the radial weight vanish, and so do the moments $\int\zeta_i\zeta_j\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta$, by the substitution $\zeta\mapsto\mathrm e^{\mathrm it}\zeta$; by symmetry, $\int\zeta_i\bar\zeta_j\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta=a_\chi\delta_{ij}$. For smooth $g$, the second-order Taylor expansion of $g(F(x,r\zeta))$ in $r\zeta$, integrated against $\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta$, therefore gives
\[
 \Psi_g(x,r)=g(x)+a_\chi r^2\sum_{i,j}h^{i\bar j}g_{i\bar j}(x)+O(r^3),
\]
uniformly on compact subsets of the chart. For the distributional statement let $\varphi$ be a test function in the chart. For fixed $t$ near $0$ the map $\Phi_t(x)=F(x,t)$ is a diffeomorphism near $\Supp\varphi$, with $\Phi_0$ the identity, and its inverse depends smoothly on $(t,y)$, by the inverse function theorem. By Fubini's theorem and the change of variables $y=\Phi_{r\zeta}(x)$, the adjoint kernel is
\begin{gather*}
 \int\Psi_g(x,r)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm dx=\int g(y)\,\Psi_r^*\varphi(y)\,\mathrm dy,\\
 \Psi_r^*\varphi(y)=\int\varphi\bigl(\Phi_{r\zeta}^{-1}(y)\bigr)\bigl|\det D\Phi_{r\zeta}^{-1}(y)\bigr|\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta.
\end{gather*}
The integrand is a smooth function of $(r\zeta,y)$, equal to $\varphi(y)$ at $r=0$, so Taylor's formula in $r\zeta$ gives $\Psi_r^*\varphi=\varphi+r\varphi_1+r^2\varphi_2+O(r^3)$, with continuous functions $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ supported in a fixed compact set and a remainder bounded uniformly in $y$. Pairing this with an arbitrary smooth $g$ and comparing with the expansion of $\Psi_g$, we find $\int g\varphi_1=0$ and $\int g\varphi_2=a_\chi\int\varphi\sum h^{i\bar j}g_{i\bar j}$ for every smooth $g$; hence $\varphi_1=0$ and $\varphi_2=a_\chi\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}(h^{i\bar j}\varphi)$. Pairing the expansion of $\Psi_r^*\varphi$ with a locally integrable $u$ gives
\[
 \int\frac{\Psi_u(\cdot,r)-u}{r^2}\varphi\,\mathrm dx=\int u\,\frac{\Psi_r^*\varphi-\varphi}{r^2}\,\mathrm dx\longrightarrow a_\chi\int u\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}(h^{i\bar j}\varphi)\,\mathrm dx,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:chern-smoothing-generator}; in this way the dependence of the kernel on the metric at the moving base point is taken into account.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A minimum with a varying upper radius}
\label{sec:varying-radius-minimum}
Near the pole set of the potential of a K\"ahler current with analytic singularities the smoothing radius must be taken smaller, so the minimum over radii will be taken over an interval whose upper end depends on the base point. Kiselman's minimum principle (\cref{thm:kiselman-invariant-exhaustion}) concerns functions that are plurisubharmonic on a domain with open fibers. Here the minimum is taken over a closed interval and may be attained at its end point, and the joint Levi form is controlled only where the slope in the radius is small; the curvature of the end point function then enters. The following lemma records this curvature error explicitly.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:varying-log-radius-minimum}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain, let $\widehat\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $D$, and let $b$ be a smooth real function on $D$ with $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial b\leq C_b\widehat\omega$ for a constant $C_b\geq0$. Let $H(z,s)$ be a smooth function, defined for $z\in D$ and $s$ in a neighborhood of $(-\infty,b(z)]$, such that $H_s\geq0$ and $H_{ss}>0$, and such that $H$ is bounded below, locally uniformly in $z$, as $s\to-\infty$. Regard $H$ as a function of $(z,\zeta)\in D\times\mathbb C$ with $s=\Real\zeta$.

Fix $c>0$ and a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\Lambda$ on $D$, and let $\pi_D$ denote the projection to $D$. Suppose that at every point with $s\leq b(z)$ and $H_s\leq c$ the joint Levi form satisfies
\[
 \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial_{z,\zeta}H\geq-\pi_D^*\Lambda.
\]
Then the constrained minimum
\[
 v(z)=\inf_{s\leq b(z)}\bigl(H(z,s)-cs\bigr)
\]
is finite and continuous, and it satisfies, in the sense of distributions,
\[
 \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial v\geq-\Lambda-cC_b\widehat\omega.
\]
In particular, if $b$ is constant, or more generally plurisuperharmonic, we may take $C_b=0$, and the upper end point produces no additional curvature error.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\varepsilon>0$ we minimize instead the penalized function
\[
 H_\varepsilon(z,s)=H(z,s)-cs-\varepsilon\log(b(z)-s),\qquad s<b(z).
\]
For fixed $z$ it tends to $+\infty$ at both ends of the interval: as $s\to-\infty$ the linear term $-cs$ dominates the logarithm, because $H$ is bounded below. Its second derivative in $s$ is $H_{ss}+\varepsilon/(b-s)^2>0$, so it has a unique minimizer $s_\varepsilon(z)$, a nondegenerate critical point. By the implicit function theorem, $s_\varepsilon$ and the minimum value $v_\varepsilon(z)=H_\varepsilon(z,s_\varepsilon(z))$ are smooth. The critical equation is
\[
 H_s-c+\frac\varepsilon{b-s}=0,
\]
so at the minimizer $0\leq H_s\leq c$ and $0\leq\sigma:=\varepsilon/(b-s)\leq c$. Since $\Real\zeta$ is pluriharmonic, the joint Levi form of the barrier $-\varepsilon\log(b(z)-\Real\zeta)$ is $\varepsilon\,\mathrm i\partial\phi\wedge\bar\partial\phi/\phi^2-\sigma\,\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial b$, where $\phi=b(z)-\Real\zeta$; the first term is nonnegative, and the second is at least $-\sigma C_b\widehat\omega\geq-cC_b\widehat\omega$. Hence, at the minimizer, the joint Levi form of $H_\varepsilon$ is bounded below by $-\pi_D^*(\Lambda+cC_b\widehat\omega)$.

We now use the Schur complement computation from the proof of \cref{thm:kiselman-invariant-exhaustion}; it needs the Levi bound only at the minimizer, not elsewhere in the fiber. At the minimizer write the joint complex Hessian of $H_\varepsilon$ in block form $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}P&y/2\\y^*/2&a/4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, where $P=(\partial^2H_\varepsilon/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k)$, $y=(\partial^2H_\varepsilon/\partial z_j\partial s)$ and $a=\partial^2H_\varepsilon/\partial s^2>0$. Differentiating the critical equation shows that the complex Hessian of $v_\varepsilon$ is $P-a^{-1}yy^*$. Adding the horizontal form $\pi_D^*(\Lambda+cC_b\widehat\omega)$ makes the block matrix positive semidefinite, and evaluating it on the vector $(\xi,-2a^{-1}y^*\xi)$ shows that the same lower bound holds for the complex Hessian of $v_\varepsilon$. Thus
\[
 \mathrm i\partial\bar\partial v_\varepsilon
                                  \geq-\Lambda-cC_b\widehat\omega.
\]

It remains to pass to the limit $\varepsilon\to0$. On a compact neighborhood in the base, $b$ is bounded, and evaluating at $s=b-1$ gives a uniform upper bound for $v_\varepsilon$ when $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. Since $H$ is bounded below and $-cs-\log(b-s)$ grows linearly as $s\to-\infty$, the minimizers $s_\varepsilon$ are bounded below uniformly; the minimizers of the unpenalized problem have the same lower bound. Both problems may therefore be restricted to a common compact interval below their varying upper end point. The unpenalized minimum $v$ is finite, and it is continuous: after the substitution $s=b(z)-t$ the upper end point becomes fixed, the range of $t$ is a fixed compact interval, and a minimum of a continuous function over a fixed compact interval depends continuously on the parameter.

On this range the penalty is bounded below by a quantity that tends uniformly to zero, so $v_\varepsilon\geq v-o(1)$. For an upper bound, evaluate $H_\varepsilon$ at an unpenalized minimizer if it lies at least $\sqrt\varepsilon$ below $b$, and otherwise at $s=b-\sqrt\varepsilon$. In the first case the penalty is at most $\varepsilon|\log\sqrt\varepsilon|$; in the second, the unpenalized function changes by $o(1)$, uniformly, by its uniform continuity on the compact range. Consequently $v_\varepsilon\to v$ locally uniformly, and the inequalities for $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial v_\varepsilon$ pass to the distributional limit.
\end{proof}
\index{Kiselman's minimum principle!varying upper end point}

\subsection{Big-class envelopes on the ample locus}
\label{sec:big-envelope-metric-regularity}
We now prove that envelopes in a big class have locally bounded metric Laplacian away from the non-K\"ahler locus. The positivity that $\theta$ lacks is supplied by a K\"ahler current in $\{\theta\}$ with analytic singularities, whose pole set becomes a divisor after a modification. Near that divisor we shrink the smoothing radius of \cref{lem:chern-joint-smoothing}. A first bound for the admissible radius comes from the maximality of the envelope. High powers of the local equations of the divisor then turn the variable coefficients in the joint Hessian estimate into uniform ones, and a second application of maximality gives a quadratic bound for the smoothing error.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-metric-laplacian-rooftop}
Suppose that $\{\theta\}$ is big, let $r\geq1$ be an integer, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be continuous real functions on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Let
\[
 \operatorname{Amp}(\{\theta\})=X\setminus E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\{\theta\})
\]
be the \emph{ample locus}\index{ample locus} of $\{\theta\}$, the complement of its non-K\"ahler locus (\cref{def:minimal-class-multiplicities}). Then
\[
 u=P_\theta\bigl(\min_j f_j\bigr)
\]
has bounded metric Laplacian on every compact subset of $\operatorname{Amp}(\{\theta\})$. On $\operatorname{Amp}(\{\theta\})$ its complex Hessian has locally bounded coefficients, $u\in W^{2,p}_{\loc}$ for every $1\leq p<\infty$, and hence $u\in C^{1,\gamma}_{\loc}$ for every $0<\gamma<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Reduction to one obstacle with bounded complex Hessian.} Choose a K\"ahler form $\kappa\geq\theta$, for instance a large multiple of $\omega$, and put $f=P_\kappa(\min_jf_j)$. By \cref{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}, $f$ is $C^1$ and has bounded complex Hessian. Every $\theta$-psh function below $\min_jf_j$ is $\kappa$-psh, and hence lies below $f$. Conversely $f\leq\min_jf_j$. These two inclusions of competitor families give $P_\theta(f)=u$.

\emph{Passage to a modification.} Put $Z=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\{\theta\})$. By \cref{thm:positivity-loci}, $Z$ is the pole set of a function $\phi_1\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with analytic singularities such that $\theta+\ddc\phi_1\geq2\delta_1\omega$ for some $\delta_1>0$. We apply \cref{lem:logarithmic-cutoff-sum} to $\phi_1$, regarded as an element of $\PSH(X,\theta-2\delta_1\omega)$, with an integer $m=m_0$ so large that the constant $c'$ of that lemma satisfies $c'/m_0\leq\delta_1$, and we let $\phi_0$ be the resulting function $G_{m_0}$. Then $\theta+\ddc\phi_0\geq\delta_1\omega$, and $\phi_0$ has gentle analytic singularities (\cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}) of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, where $c=1/m_0$ and $\mathfrak a=\mathcal I(m_0\phi_1)$. The pole set of $\phi_0$ is the zero set of $\mathfrak a$, and it equals $Z$. Indeed, near a point where $\phi_1$ is finite, $\phi_1$ is bounded, because it has analytic singularities, so $\mathfrak a$ is the unit ideal there; thus the pole set of $\phi_0$ is contained in $Z$. Conversely, $\theta+\ddc\phi_0$ is a K\"ahler current whose potential has analytic singularities, so its pole set contains $Z$ by the definition of the non-K\"ahler locus (\cref{def:minimal-class-multiplicities}). Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$, that is, a principalization of $\mathfrak a$ by finitely many blowups with smooth centers (\cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}), and write $\pi^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_Y(-D_0)$; the support of the effective divisor $D_0$ is $\pi^{-1}(Z)$. By \cref{def:gentle-analytic-singularities}, locally on $Y$ the function $\phi_0\circ\pi$ is $c\log|s_{D_0}|^2$ plus a smooth function. By \cref{thm:marked-order-one-input}, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X\setminus Z$, and all its centers lie over $Z$. Steps~2 and~3 of the proof of \cref{prop:kahler-smooth-center-blowup} show that, for the blowup $\sigma$ of a compact K\"ahler manifold with K\"ahler form $\omega'$ along a submanifold, with exceptional divisor $E$, the form $\sigma^*\omega'+\varepsilon\eta$ is K\"ahler for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, where $\eta$ is the curvature of a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(-E)$. Applying this to the successive blowups of $\pi$, with rational $\varepsilon$, and pulling the forms back to $Y$, we obtain a K\"ahler form
\[
 \omega_Y=\pi^*\omega+\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell\eta_\ell>0,
\]
where $\eta_\ell$ is the curvature of a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(-E_\ell)$ for effective exceptional divisors $E_\ell$, and the coefficients $\varepsilon_\ell>0$ can be taken rational. The pulled-back current $\pi^*(\theta+\ddc\phi_0)$ is at least $\delta_1\pi^*\omega$. Let $s_{E_\ell}$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O(E_\ell)$, with norm $|s_{E_\ell}|$ computed in the dual metric. Choose a rational number $0<\delta_2\leq\delta_1$ and add $\delta_2\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell\log|s_{E_\ell}|^2$ to the pulled-back potential $\phi_0\circ\pi$. Since, by the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula (\cref{thm:poincare-lelong}),
\[
 \ddc\log|s_{E_\ell}|^2=[E_\ell]+\eta_\ell,
\]
the resulting current is at least $\delta_2\omega_Y$. Locally its potential is still of the form $\sum_j\beta_j\log|g_j|^2$ plus a smooth function, where the $g_j$ are local equations of the components of an effective divisor with support $\pi^{-1}(Z)$ (the exceptional divisors lie over $Z$) and the $\beta_j$ are positive rational numbers. Replacing $\omega_Y$ by $\delta_2\omega_Y$, we may assume that the lower bound is $\omega_Y$ itself.

By \cref{prop:smooth-target-psh-modification}, pullback is a bijection from $\PSH(X,\theta)$ onto $\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. Since $f$ is continuous, a potential lies below $f$ if and only if its pullback lies below $f\circ\pi$, so $u\circ\pi=P_{\pi^*\theta}(f\circ\pi)$. The pullback $f\circ\pi$ is $C^1$ and has bounded complex Hessian. Indeed, off $\pi^{-1}(Z)$ the map $\pi$ is a local biholomorphism, and since its differential is bounded, the chain rule bounds the coefficients of $\pi^*(\kappa+\ddc f)$ there. Locally, $f\circ\pi$ plus a smooth local potential of $\pi^*\kappa$ is a bounded plurisubharmonic function. Applying \cref{thm:cln-weighted} to it and to $\log|g|$, where $g$ is a local equation of the hypersurface $\pi^{-1}(Z)$, shows that this logarithm is integrable for the trace measure of its complex Hessian, so the trace measure gives $\pi^{-1}(Z)$ zero mass. Hence the positive current $\pi^*\kappa+\ddc(f\circ\pi)$, whose coefficients are bounded off $\pi^{-1}(Z)$, has bounded coefficients everywhere. We may therefore work on $Y$ with the pulled-back form and obstacle, and return through the isomorphism over $X\setminus Z$ at the end.

From now on we write $X$, $\theta$, $\omega$, $f$ and $u$ for $Y$, $\pi^*\theta$, the K\"ahler form just constructed, $f\circ\pi$ and $u\circ\pi$, and again put $\alpha=2\pi\theta$ and $\widehat\omega=2\pi\omega$. Adding a constant to the obstacle, we may assume that $0\leq f\leq B$ with $B\geq1$. Let $\phi$ be the potential $\phi_0\circ\pi+\delta_2\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell\log|s_{E_\ell}|^2$ constructed above, minus a constant chosen so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-envelope-reference-bounds}
 \alpha+\mathcal D\phi\geq\widehat\omega,\qquad
 \phi\leq-B-1,\qquad \phi\leq u\leq f\leq B;
\end{equation}
here $\phi\leq u$ holds because $\phi$ is a competitor for the envelope. Cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate charts, with smaller charts still covering $X$, on each of which
\[
 \phi=\sum_j \beta_j\log|g_j|^2+\sigma,
 \qquad \beta_j>0,\quad |g_j|<\tfrac12,
\]
where the $g_j$ are local equations of the components of the divisor meeting the chart (there are none if the chart does not meet the divisor) and $\sigma$ is smooth. Write $D$ for the divisor and $U=X\setminus D$. In particular $\mathcal D\phi$ is uniformly bounded on $U$, although $\phi$ tends to $-\infty$ near $D$. All bounds below are uniform on this finite atlas; only the explicitly displayed functions of $\phi$ are allowed to grow.

\emph{A first-order smoothing bound.} Let $H=H_u$ and $\lambda=\lambda_u$ be as in \cref{lem:chern-joint-smoothing}. The initial joint estimate \eqref{eq:chern-initial-joint-bound} improves after adding $K_1r$. Indeed, the vertical Levi form of $K_1|w|$ is $K_1/(4r)$, and Young's inequality absorbs the bounded mixed term at a horizontal cost $Cr$. Hence, for a fixed sufficiently large $K_1$ and small $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-first-corrected-joint}
 \alpha+\mathcal D(H+K_1r)\geq-(A\lambda+Cr)\widehat\omega
\end{equation}
as joint forms; the right side is pulled back from $X$.

Fix a small $\delta_0>0$; it will be decreased finitely many times, each time depending only on the fixed data. For $0<\delta<\delta_0$ and $c>0$ define on $U$
\[
 \psi_{c,\delta}(x)=\inf_{0<t\leq\delta}
 \{H(x,t)+K_1t-K_1\delta-c\log(t/\delta)\}.
\]
The infimum over $t$ is finite and attained, because $H\geq u$, $u$ is locally bounded on $U$, and $-c\log(t/\delta)\to+\infty$ as $t\to0$. At a minimizing radius, including the upper end point, the logarithmic slope $\lambda+K_1t$ of the nondecreasing part $H+K_1t$ is at most $c$, so $\lambda\leq c$. We apply \cref{lem:varying-log-radius-minimum} in the variable $s=\log t$, with the constant upper end point $\log\delta$, to $H(x,\mathrm e^s)+K_1\mathrm e^s$ plus a smooth local potential of $\alpha$; by \eqref{eq:big-first-corrected-joint}, the Levi bound required in the lemma holds with $\Lambda=(Ac+C\delta)\widehat\omega$. Moreover, since $u\leq f$ and the kernel of $\Psi$ is positive, evaluation at $t=\delta$ gives $\psi_{c,\delta}\leq\Psi_u(x,\delta)+K\delta^2\leq\Psi_f(x,\delta)+K\delta^2$, and \eqref{eq:chern-regular-obstacle-error} bounds the right side by $f+C\delta^2$. Thus
\[
 \alpha+\mathcal D\psi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+C\delta)\widehat\omega,
 \qquad \psi_{c,\delta}\leq f+C\delta^2.
\]
Put $e=Ac+C\delta$ and $\tau=e/(1+e)$. Then $(1-\tau)(\psi_{c,\delta}-C\delta^2)+\tau\phi$ is $\theta$-psh on $U$, by \eqref{eq:big-envelope-reference-bounds}, since $-(1-\tau)e+\tau=0$; and it lies below $(1-\tau)f\leq f$, because $\phi\leq0\leq f$. It is bounded above near $D$, so by \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension}, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\alpha$, it extends across $D$; the extension is given by upper limits and therefore still lies below the continuous obstacle. By the maximality of the envelope, this extension lies below $u$. Rearranging, and using $\psi_{c,\delta}\leq B+C\delta^2$ so that no unbounded factor is divided by $1-\tau$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-first-envelope-comparison}
 \psi_{c,\delta}\leq u+C\delta^2
                         +\tau(B-\phi+C\delta^2).
\end{equation}

Now take $c=\delta$, so that $e$ and $\tau$ are $O(\delta)$, and let $t_0$ be a minimizing radius for $\psi_{\delta,\delta}(x)$. The two nonnegative quantities $H(x,t_0)+K_1t_0-u(x)$ and $\delta\log(\delta/t_0)$ add up to $\psi_{\delta,\delta}(x)-u(x)+K_1\delta$, so \eqref{eq:big-first-envelope-comparison} gives, with $R=C(1+B+|\phi|)$ for a suitable uniform $C$,
\[
 t_0\geq\delta\mathrm e^{-R(x)},\qquad
 H(x,t_0)+K_1t_0-u(x)\leq R(x)\delta.
\]
Since $H$ is nondecreasing in the radius, the choice $\delta=r\mathrm e^{R(x)}$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-first-smoothing-error}
 0\leq H(x,r)-u(x)\leq R(x)\mathrm e^{R(x)}r
 \quad\text{if }0<r<\delta_0\mathrm e^{-R(x)}.
\end{equation}
Since $\phi\leq-B-1$, we have $1+B\leq|\phi|$, so we may take $R(x)=B_0|\phi(x)|$ with a fixed large $B_0$.

This estimate also improves the bound for the trace mass. Enlarging $B_0$ if necessary, we may assume $B_0\beta_j\geq1$ for all $j$; since $|g_j|<1/2$, the formula for $\phi$ then gives $\mathrm e^{-R(x)}\leq C|g_j(x)|^2$ for each local equation $g_j$. As the first derivatives of the $g_j$ are bounded, there is a fixed small $S>0$ such that, for $|y-x|\leq S\mathrm e^{-R(x)}$, the ratios $|g_j(y)|/|g_j(x)|$ lie between $1/2$ and $3/2$. Hence $|\phi(y)-\phi(x)|\leq C$ there. On a chart that does not meet the divisor, $\phi$ is smooth, and the same oscillation bound holds after decreasing $S$. By \eqref{eq:big-envelope-reference-bounds}, $|u(y)|\leq C(1+|\phi(x)|)$ on that ball. The comparison with radial convolution in \cref{lem:chern-joint-smoothing} therefore transfers \eqref{eq:big-first-smoothing-error} to the Euclidean radial mean of the local plurisubharmonic potential $p=u+q$, with an additional error $Cr^2(1+|\phi(x)|)$.

We also need the following consequence of the Jensen--Lelong formula, with positive constants $c_0$ and $0<c_1<1$ independent of $x$ and $r$:
\[
 \int p(x+r\zeta)\chi(|\zeta|^2)\,\mathrm d\zeta-p(x)
        \geq c_0 r^{2-d}\mu_p(B(x,c_1r)),
 \qquad d=2n.
\]
Indeed, the spherical means of $p$ about $x$ are nondecreasing in the radius and tend to $p(x)$. Letting $r_1\to0$ in \eqref{eq:euclidean-jensen-lelong} and using that $D_p(x,\cdot)$ is nondecreasing, we see that the spherical mean of radius $\varrho$ exceeds $p(x)$ by at least $2\log(1/c_1)D_p(x,c_1\varrho)$, which by \eqref{eq:lelong-ball-density} is a fixed positive multiple of $\varrho^{2-d}\mu_p(B(x,c_1\varrho))$. It remains to integrate over the radii $\varrho=r\varrho'$ with $\varrho'$ in a fixed subinterval of $(0,1)$ on which $\chi(\varrho'^2)>0$, and to decrease $c_1$. We apply this inequality at a fixed multiple of $r$, decrease $S$, and use \eqref{eq:big-first-smoothing-error}. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-improved-trace-mass}
 m_u(x,r)\leq\frac{M(x)}r\quad(0<r<S\mathrm e^{-R(x)}),
 \qquad 1\leq M(x)\leq C R(x)\mathrm e^{CR(x)}.
\end{equation}
Thus the improved joint estimate \eqref{eq:chern-improved-joint-bound} is available for these radii.

\emph{Uniform joint estimates in divisor coordinates.} On each chart put $G=(\prod_jg_j)^m$, where an empty product equals one. By the formula for $R$ and \eqref{eq:big-improved-trace-mass}, $M$ is bounded by a fixed product of negative powers of the $|g_j|$, and differentiating $G$ loses only one power of one factor. Hence we can choose the integer $m$ so large that $M|G|$ and $M|\mathrm dG|$ are uniformly bounded. Next, by the same formula for $R$, we can choose an integer $k\geq1$ so large that
\[
 \frac{\mathrm e^{-kR(x)}}{|G(x)|}\leq T
\]
with $T$ uniform on the finite cover. Put $\rho_\delta(x)=\delta\mathrm e^{-kR(x)}$, and take $\delta_0<S$, so that these radii lie in the range of \eqref{eq:big-improved-trace-mass}. In the holomorphic coordinate $\xi$ defined by $w=G(x)\xi$, the range $0<|w|\leq\rho_\delta(x)$ corresponds to $|\xi|\leq T\delta$.

The differential of the change of variables $(x,\xi)\mapsto(x,G(x)\xi)$ sends $(v,\eta)$ to $(v,G\eta+\xi\,\mathrm dG(v))$. Substituting this in \eqref{eq:chern-improved-joint-bound} and using the bounds just obtained, $|G|\leq1$ and a uniform bound for $|\mathrm dG|$, we get
\begin{align}\label{eq:big-divisor-transformed-joint}
 (\alpha+\mathcal D H(x,|G\xi|))[v,\eta]^2
 \geq{}&-(A\lambda+C_1|\xi|^2)|v|^2\notag\\
        &-C_1|G||\xi||v||\eta|
                      -C_1|G|^2|\xi||\eta|^2.
\end{align}
For example, the purely horizontal error is bounded by a constant times $M(|G|^2+|G||\mathrm dG|+|G||\mathrm dG|^2)|\xi|^2$, the mixed coefficient by $C|G||\xi|$, and the vertical coefficient by $C|G|^2|\xi|$. These estimates use only the boundedness of $M|G|$ and $M|\mathrm dG|$.

Now add $K_2|G\xi|^2$. Its Levi form is the square $K_2|G\eta+\xi\,\mathrm dG(v)|^2$, and the inequality $|a+b|^2\geq\tfrac12|a|^2-|b|^2$ shows that it is at least $\frac{K_2}2|G|^2|\eta|^2$ minus a horizontal term $K_2|\mathrm dG|^2|\xi|^2|v|^2$. For $\delta_0$ small and $K_2$ fixed and large, this absorbs the last term of \eqref{eq:big-divisor-transformed-joint}, and Young's inequality absorbs its mixed term at a cost $C|\xi|^2|v|^2$. Since $|\xi|\leq T\delta$, we have proved the uniform joint bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-second-corrected-joint}
 \alpha+\mathcal D(H(x,|G\xi|)+K_2|G\xi|^2)
                         \geq-(A\lambda+C_2\delta^2)\widehat\omega.
\end{equation}
All constants here are independent of the chart and of the point of $U$.

\emph{A second-order smoothing bound.} For $c>0$ set
\[
 \psi(x)=\inf_{0<t\leq\rho_\delta(x)}
               \{H(x,t)+K_2t^2-c\log t\}.
\]
In the variable $s=\log|\xi|$, so that $t=|G|\mathrm e^s$, the upper end point of the interval is $b(x)=\log\delta-kR(x)-\log|G(x)|$. The last summand is pluriharmonic on $U$, and $\mathcal D R=-B_0\mathcal D\phi$ is uniformly bounded there. Thus $|\mathcal D b|\leq C_b\widehat\omega$ with a uniform constant $C_b$. The logarithmic slope of the nondecreasing part $H+K_2t^2$ is $\lambda+2K_2t^2$, so at a minimizing radius it is at most $c$. Since $-c\log t=-cs-c\log|G|$ and $\log|G|$ is pluriharmonic on $U$, \cref{lem:varying-log-radius-minimum}, applied to \eqref{eq:big-second-corrected-joint} (again after adding a smooth local potential of $\alpha$), proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-second-minimum-curvature}
 \alpha+\mathcal D\psi\geq-C_0\widehat\omega,
 \qquad C_0=A'c+C_2\delta^2,\quad A'=A+C_b.
\end{equation}

Let $a=ckB_0$ and $C_3=C_f+K+K_2$, with $C_f$ from \eqref{eq:chern-regular-obstacle-error}. Since $-c\log\rho_\delta=-c\log\delta-a\phi$, evaluation at $t=\rho_\delta(x)\leq\delta$, together with $\Psi_u\leq\Psi_f$ and \eqref{eq:chern-regular-obstacle-error}, gives
\[
 Q:=\psi+c\log\delta+a\phi-C_3\delta^2\leq f,
 \qquad (1+a)\alpha+\mathcal DQ\geq-C_0\widehat\omega;
\]
the second inequality follows from \eqref{eq:big-second-minimum-curvature} and $a(\alpha+\mathcal D\phi)\geq0$, a consequence of \eqref{eq:big-envelope-reference-bounds}. Put
\[
 \nu=\frac{C_0}{1+a+C_0},\qquad
 W=(1-\nu)Q+\nu(1+a)\phi.
\]
Since $\nu(1+a)=(1-\nu)C_0$, we have $(1+a)\alpha+\mathcal DW\geq0$, that is, $W$ is $(1+a)\theta$-psh. It lies below $f$, because $Q\leq f$, $\phi\leq0$ and $f\geq0$. As before, $W$ extends across $D$ by \cref{thm:psh-bounded-zero-extension}, preserving this upper bound. Consequently $W/(1+a)$, which lies below $f/(1+a)\leq f$, is a competitor for $u$, and $(1+a)u\geq W$. Since $u\leq B$ and $Q\leq B$, we can rearrange this without losing an unbounded positive term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-second-envelope-comparison}
 u\geq Q+\nu(1+a)\phi-(\nu+a)B.
\end{equation}

Now take $c=\delta^2$, so that $a$ and $\nu$ are $O(\delta^2)$, and let $t_0$ be a minimizing radius for $\psi(x)$. Writing $\psi(x)$ as the value at $t_0$ of the minimized expression and substituting it into \eqref{eq:big-second-envelope-comparison}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-second-radius-and-value}
 t_0\geq\delta\mathrm e^{-R_2(x)},\qquad
 0\leq H(x,t_0)+K_2t_0^2-u(x)\leq R_2(x)\delta^2,
 \qquad R_2=C(1+B+|\phi|).
\end{equation}
Indeed, after rearrangement the left side consists of the two nonnegative terms $H(x,t_0)+K_2t_0^2-u(x)$ and $-\delta^2\log(t_0/\delta)$ (recall $H\geq u$ and $t_0\leq\delta$), while every term on the right is bounded by $C\delta^2(1+B+|\phi|)$.

Fix a compact set $F\Subset U$ and a number $M_2\geq\sup_FR_2$. For every sufficiently small $r$, choose $\delta=r\mathrm e^{M_2}$. Then $r\leq t_0$ at every $x\in F$, and since $H$ is nondecreasing in the radius we obtain the uniform quadratic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-quadratic-smoothing-error}
 0\leq H(x,r)-u(x)\leq M_2\mathrm e^{2M_2}r^2,
 \qquad x\in F.
\end{equation}

\emph{Regularity.} Divide \eqref{eq:big-quadratic-smoothing-error} by $r^2$: on $F$ we have $0\leq(\Psi_u(x,r)-u(x))/r^2+K\leq M_2\mathrm e^{2M_2}$. Pairing with nonnegative test functions supported in the interior of $F$ and letting $r\to0$, \eqref{eq:chern-smoothing-generator} shows that, in the sense of distributions on the interior of $F$, $a_\chi D_hu+K$ lies between zero and the constant $M_2\mathrm e^{2M_2}$. In particular $|\langle a_\chi D_hu,\varphi\rangle|\leq(K+M_2\mathrm e^{2M_2})\int\varphi\,\mathrm dx$ for these test functions $\varphi\geq0$. Since every point of $U$ lies in the interior of such a set $F$, \cref{cor:distribution-bounded-density}(2), applied in the charts with Lebesgue measure, shows that $D_hu$ is an $L^\infty_{\loc}$ function on $U$. The positive current $\alpha+\mathcal Du$ therefore has locally bounded $h$-trace, so in a fixed chart each of its diagonal coefficient measures is bounded by a constant times Lebesgue measure. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for positive Hermitian matrices bounds the off-diagonal coefficient measures as well. Thus all complex coefficients of $\alpha+\mathcal Du$, and hence those of $\mathcal Du$, are locally bounded on $U$.

It remains to obtain the real Sobolev regularity; note that no weak first derivatives of $u$ are known in advance. Let $V\Subset U$ be a coordinate ball. The complex second derivatives $u_{i\bar j}$ are bounded on $V$, so the Euclidean Laplacian $\Delta u=4\sum_iu_{i\bar i}$ is a bounded function there. Let $V'$ be a smaller concentric ball with $\overline{V'}\subset V$, choose $\vartheta\in C^\infty_c(V)$ equal to one near $\overline{V'}$, and let $N=E_{2n}*g$ be the fundamental potential of the bounded compactly supported function $g=\vartheta\Delta u$, where $E_{2n}$ is the fundamental solution of \cref{prop:laplacian-fundamental-solution}. By \cref{prop:fundamental-potential}, applied to the positive and negative parts of $g\,\mathrm dx$, we have $\Delta N=g$ in the sense of distributions. Hence $u-N$ is distributionally harmonic near $\overline{V'}$, and by Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}) it is smooth there. It therefore suffices to show that $N\in W^{2,p}(V')$ for $1<p<\infty$.

The Hessian estimate of \cref{thm:hessian-multiplier-lp} concerns compactly supported smooth functions, so we mollify. Let $g_\varepsilon$ be the mollifications of $g$, and put $N_\varepsilon=E_{2n}*g_\varepsilon$. For small $\varepsilon$ the $g_\varepsilon$ are smooth and supported in a fixed compact set, and $g_\varepsilon\to g$ in $L^s$ for every $1\leq s<\infty$, by \cref{lem:mollifier-density}. By \cref{prop:laplacian-fundamental-solution}, $N_\varepsilon$ is smooth and $\Delta N_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon$. Moreover $\partial_a N_\varepsilon=E_{2n}*\partial_ag_\varepsilon=(\partial_aE_{2n})*g_\varepsilon$, where the second identity follows by integration by parts outside a small ball about the singularity of $E_{2n}$, whose boundary term tends to zero. Fix $q$ with $1<q<2n/(2n-1)$, and let $q'=q/(q-1)$. The functions $E_{2n}$ and $\partial_aE_{2n}$, the latter bounded by $C|y|^{1-2n}$, belong to $L^q$ on bounded sets, so H\"older's inequality gives
\[
 \sup_V|N_\varepsilon-N|+\sup_V\bigl|\partial_aN_\varepsilon-(\partial_aE_{2n})*g\bigr|
   \leq C\|g_\varepsilon-g\|_{L^{q'}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $N_\varepsilon\to N$ and $\partial_aN_\varepsilon\to(\partial_aE_{2n})*g$ uniformly on $V$. Passing to the limit in the sense of distributions, we see that $N$ is bounded on $V$ and that its first distributional derivatives there are the bounded functions $(\partial_aE_{2n})*g$. Choose $\vartheta_1\in C^\infty_c(V)$ equal to one on $V'$. For small $\varepsilon,\varepsilon'$, the function $\vartheta_1(N_\varepsilon-N_{\varepsilon'})$ is smooth with compact support, and \eqref{eq:euclidean-hessian-lp} gives
\[
 \bigl\|D^2\bigl(\vartheta_1(N_\varepsilon-N_{\varepsilon'})\bigr)\bigr\|_p
 \leq C_{2n,p}\bigl\|\vartheta_1(g_\varepsilon-g_{\varepsilon'})
   +2\nabla\vartheta_1\cdot\nabla(N_\varepsilon-N_{\varepsilon'})
   +(N_\varepsilon-N_{\varepsilon'})\Delta\vartheta_1\bigr\|_p.
\]
By the convergences just proved, the right side tends to zero as $\varepsilon,\varepsilon'\to0$. Hence, along a sequence $\varepsilon_k\to0$, the second derivatives $\partial_a\partial_b(\vartheta_1N_{\varepsilon_k})$ form a Cauchy sequence in $L^p(\mathbb R^{2n})$ and converge in $L^p$ to functions $G_{ab}$. These functions are supported in the fixed compact set $\Supp\vartheta_1$, so by H\"older's inequality the convergence also holds in the sense of distributions. On the other hand $\vartheta_1N_{\varepsilon_k}\to\vartheta_1N$ uniformly, so $\partial_a\partial_b(\vartheta_1N_{\varepsilon_k})\to\partial_a\partial_b(\vartheta_1N)$ in the sense of distributions. Therefore $\partial_a\partial_b(\vartheta_1N)=G_{ab}\in L^p$, and since $\vartheta_1=1$ on $V'$, the second distributional derivatives of $N$ belong to $L^p(V')$. Hence $N\in W^{2,p}(V')$, and therefore $u\in W^{2,p}_{\loc}(U)$ for every $1<p<\infty$; the case $p=1$ follows on relatively compact sets because their volume is finite. Morrey's inequality (\cref{lem:morrey-sobolev-representative}), with $p>2n/(1-\gamma)$, gives a representative in $C^{1,\gamma}_{\loc}$ for every $\gamma<1$. This continuous representative coincides with $u$: locally $u+q$ is plurisubharmonic, hence is the limit of its averages over small balls, and these averages converge to the continuous representative. Transferring these statements through the biholomorphism $Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(Z)\to X\setminus Z$, under which all smooth metrics are locally equivalent and local Sobolev spaces are preserved (\cref{lem:sobolev-leibniz-chain}(2)), proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
