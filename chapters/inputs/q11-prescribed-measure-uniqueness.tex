\subsection{Uniqueness by concentration of mass}
\label{sec:relative-prescribed-measure-uniqueness}
We now show that the solution given by \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-relative-prescribed-measure-existence} is unique up to an additive constant. The proof uses existence once more. If two solutions $u,v$ with $\mu_u=\mu_v=\mu$ do not differ by a constant $\mu$-almost everywhere, then, after a shift of $v$, the set $U=\{u<v\}$ carries a proper fraction of the mass of $\mu$. We solve the equation whose right-hand side is $\mu$ restricted to $U$ and rescaled to total mass $m$. Mixing this auxiliary solution into $v$ raises the density of the Monge--Amp\`ere measure on $U$ (\cref{cor:nonpluripolar-common-measure-convex}), in contradiction with the comparison principle. The domination principle then turns equality $\mu$-almost everywhere into equality everywhere.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-prescribed-measure-uniqueness}
Let $u,v\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ satisfy $\mu_u=\mu_v$. Then there is a constant $C\in\mathbb R$ such that $u=v+C$ everywhere on $X$. Consequently, every non-pluripolar measure of mass $m$ is the Monge--Amp\`ere measure $\mu_u$ of exactly one $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ with $\sup_Xu=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\mu=\mu_u=\mu_v$. Since $u\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, this is a finite measure of mass $\int_X\langle\theta_u^n\rangle=\int_X\langle\theta_\phi^n\rangle=m$, and it is non-pluripolar, because non-pluripolar products give no mass to pluripolar sets (\cref{lem:nonpluripolar-truncation-limit}). Hence the pole sets of $u$ and $v$ are $\mu$-null, and $u-v$ is defined and finite $\mu$-almost everywhere. We first show that $u-v$ is constant $\mu$-almost everywhere.

Suppose not. Let $\lambda$ be the image of $\mu$ under $u-v$, a finite Borel measure on $\mathbb R$ of total mass $m>0$, and put
\[
 c_-=\sup\{c\in\mathbb R:\lambda((-\infty,c))=0\},\qquad
 c_+=\inf\{c\in\mathbb R:\lambda((c,\infty))=0\},
\]
with $\sup\varnothing=-\infty$ and $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. The sets $(-\infty,c)$ increase to $\mathbb R$ as $c\to+\infty$, so $\lambda((-\infty,c))\to m>0$; hence $c_-<+\infty$, and similarly $c_+>-\infty$. If $c_->-\infty$, the $\lambda$-null sets $(-\infty,c)$ with $c<c_-$ increase to $(-\infty,c_-)$, so $\lambda((-\infty,c_-))=0$; similarly $\lambda((c_+,\infty))=0$ if $c_+<+\infty$. Hence $c_+<c_-$ is impossible, since $\mathbb R=(-\infty,c_-)\cup(c_+,\infty)$ would then be $\lambda$-null. If $c_-=c_+$, then $\lambda$ is carried by this point, that is, $u-v$ is constant $\mu$-almost everywhere, contrary to our assumption. Thus $c_-<c_+$, and every $c\in(c_-,c_+)$ satisfies $\lambda((-\infty,c))>0$ and $\lambda((c,\infty))>0$, that is,
\[
 \mu(\{u-v<c\})>0,\qquad\mu(\{u-v>c\})>0.
\]
The singletons $\{c\}$ with $\lambda(\{c\})>0$ are pairwise disjoint sets of positive measure, so there are only countably many of them by \cref{lem:disjoint-positive-countable}. Since the interval $(c_-,c_+)$ is uncountable, we may choose $c$ in it with $\mu(\{u-v=c\})=0$. Replacing $v$ by $v+c$ changes neither the class $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ nor the measure $\mu_v$, so we may assume $c=0$. Then $\mu(\{u<v\})$ and $\mu(\{u>v\})$ are positive, and the set $U=\{u<v\}$ satisfies
\[
 0<\mu(U)<m,\qquad\mu(\{u=v\})=0.
\]
Set $q=(m/\mu(U))^{1/n}>1$. The measure $q^n\mathbf1_U\mu$ is non-pluripolar and has mass $m$, so \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-relative-prescribed-measure-existence} gives $h\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$ with
\[
 \mu_h=q^n\mathbf1_U\mu.
\]

For $0<t<1$ put $w_t=(1-t)v+th$. By \cref{cor:relative-energy-classes-convex}, $w_t\in\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$. Since $\theta_{w_t}=(1-t)\theta_v+t\theta_h$, \cref{cor:nonpluripolar-common-measure-convex}, applied to the currents $\theta_v,\theta_h$, the common measure $\mu$ and the densities $1$ and $q^n\mathbf1_U$, gives
\[
 \mu_{w_t}\geq\big((1-t)+tq\mathbf1_U\big)^n\mu.
\]
Let $U_t=\{u<w_t\}$, and put
\[
 d_t=(1-t)^n,\qquad b_t=(1-t+tq)^n.
\]
The comparison principle for relative full mass, \cref{prop:relative-mixed-comparison}, applied to $u$ and $w_t$, and the preceding lower bound give
\[
 \mu(U_t)=\mu_u(U_t)\geq\mu_{w_t}(U_t)
       \geq d_t\mu(U_t)+(b_t-d_t)\mu(U_t\cap U).
\]
Since $1-d_t>0$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-uniqueness-concentration}
 \frac{b_t-d_t}{1-d_t}\,\mu(U_t\cap U)\leq\mu(U_t).
\end{equation}

We let $t\downarrow0$. At every point where $u,v,h$ are finite and $u\neq v$, we have $w_t=v+t(h-v)$, so $\mathbf1_{U_t}\to\mathbf1_U$ there. These points have full $\mu$-measure, because the three pole sets and the level set $\{u=v\}$ are $\mu$-null. By dominated convergence,
\[
 \mu(U_t\cap U)\longrightarrow\mu(U),\qquad\mu(U_t)\longrightarrow\mu(U).
\]
Moreover $b_t-d_t=ntq+O(t^2)$ and $1-d_t=nt+O(t^2)$, so $(b_t-d_t)/(1-d_t)\to q$. Passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:relative-uniqueness-concentration} gives $q\mu(U)\leq\mu(U)$, which contradicts $q>1$ and $\mu(U)>0$.

Hence, for the original pair, there is a finite constant $C$ such that $u=v+C$ $\mu$-almost everywhere. Both potentials $u$ and $v+C$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta,\phi)$, and their measures $\mu_u=\mu_{v+C}=\mu$ give zero mass to the two strict inequality sets $\{u<v+C\}$ and $\{v+C<u\}$. The domination principle, \cref{thm:relative-full-mass-domination}, applied in each direction, gives $u\geq v+C$ and $v+C\geq u$ everywhere, including at the common poles. This proves the first assertion.

For the second, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-relative-prescribed-measure-existence} provides a solution with supremum zero. If $u$ and $v$ are two such solutions, then $u=v+C$, and comparing suprema gives $C=0$.
\end{proof}
