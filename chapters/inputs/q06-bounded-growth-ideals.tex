\section{Bounded growth ideals}
\label{sec:analytic-bounded-growth-ideals}
A germ $f$ belongs to the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(v)$ when $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-v}$ is integrable, a condition that allows $f$ to vanish to a slightly lower order than the growth of $\mathrm e^v$ suggests. In this section we study the stricter condition $|f|^2\leq C\mathrm e^v$. For a weight with analytic singularities we prove that the corresponding ideal sheaf is coherent (\cref{thm:analytic-bounded-growth-coherence}) and that it contains $\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon)v)$ for all large $k$ (\cref{cor:analytic-integrability-bounded-growth}). In the proof of coherence, the fractional power $|g|^{p/q}$ of a tuple of holomorphic functions is replaced by a holomorphic object: on the normalization of a root cover of a cone, it becomes the modulus of a function that is homogeneous of weight $p$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:analytic-bounded-growth-ideal}
Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on a complex manifold $X$. The \emph{bounded growth ideal} $\mathcal I_\infty(v)$ is the sheaf of holomorphic germs $f$ satisfying
\[
 \log|f|^2\leq v+O(1),
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad |f|^2\leq C\exp(v),
\]
on a neighborhood of their base point, for some finite constant $C$.
\end{definition}
\index{ideal!bounded growth}\index{$\mathcal I_\infty$}

The zero germ belongs to $\mathcal I_\infty(v)$, and $\mathcal I_\infty(v)$ does not change if $v$ is changed by a bounded function. It is a sheaf of ideals, because $|f+g|^2\leq2|f|^2+2|g|^2$ and holomorphic coefficients are locally bounded. It is contained in $\mathcal I(v)$, since $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-v}$ is locally bounded for $f\in\mathcal I_\infty(v)$.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic-bounded-growth-coherence}
Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a complex manifold $X$. Then $\mathcal I_\infty(v)$ is a coherent ideal sheaf. More precisely, let $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$, with $r\geq1$, be a tuple of holomorphic functions on a coordinate neighborhood $U$, not all identically zero, put $|g|=(\sum_j|g_j|^2)^{1/2}$, and let $p,q$ be positive integers. Then the ideal sheaf
\[
 \mathcal J_{p/q}(g)=\{f\in\mathcal O_U:
               |f|\leq C|g|^{p/q}\text{ near the base point of $f$, for some }C\}
\]
is coherent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Coherence is a local property. Fix $x\in U$ and shrink $U$ to a connected neighborhood of $x$. Choose an index $i$ with $g_i$ not identically zero, and put $U_i=\{g_i\ne0\}$. By \cref{lem:bounded-holomorphic-removal}, $U_i$ is a connected dense open subset of $U$.

\emph{Step 1: a cone over the blowup.} Let $B\to U$ be the blowup of the ideal $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$, constructed in \cref{prop:analytic-blowup-universal}. Thus $B\subset U\times\mathbb P^{r-1}$, and a point $(z,[\xi])$ of $B$ determines the line $\mathbb C\xi\subset\mathbb C^r$. Let $\Lambda=\{(z,[\xi],a)\in B\times\mathbb C^r:a\in\mathbb C\xi\}$ be the total space of this line bundle, and consider the map $\Lambda\to U\times\mathbb C^r$, $(z,[\xi],a)\mapsto(z,a)$. It is proper: the inverse image of a compact set is closed and contained in the product of a compact subset of $B$, namely the inverse image of its projection to $U$, with a bounded set of vectors. By \cref{thm:proper-analytic-image}, its image $C\subset U\times\mathbb C^r$ is analytic, and we regard it as a reduced complex space. It is invariant under the scalar multiplications $a\mapsto\lambda a$. Over $U_i$ the blowup is the graph of $z\mapsto[g(z)]$, so the part of $C$ over $U_i$ is the smooth family of lines
\[
 a_j=\tau g_j(z),\qquad z\in U_i,\quad\tau\in\mathbb C.
\]
This family is dense in $C$. Indeed, the graph over $U_i$ is dense in $B$, since $U_i$ is dense in $U\setminus V(g)$ and the graph over $U\setminus V(g)$ is dense in $B$; hence its part of $\Lambda$ is dense in $\Lambda$, and the image of a dense set under the continuous surjection $\Lambda\to C$ is dense. Since this family is a connected complex manifold, $C$ is irreducible.

\emph{Step 2: a root cover and its normalization.} In $C\times\mathbb C_b$ consider the analytic set defined by
\[
 b^q=a_ig_i^{q-1},
\]
and let $C'$ be the closure of its part over $U_i$. The set $C'$ is analytic, being the union of the local irreducible branches of that set not contained in $\{g_i=0\}$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:analytic-blowup-universal}. The projection $C'\to C$ is finite: the monic equation bounds $b$ over compact subsets of $C$ and allows at most $q$ values of $b$. Over $U_i$ we have $a=\tau g(z)$ and $b^q=\tau g_i(z)^q$, so $b=tg_i(z)$ with $t^q=\tau$. Hence the part of $C'$ over $U_i$ is the image of the injective immersion
\[
 U_i\times\mathbb C\to C',\qquad (z,t)\longmapsto
       (z,\ a=t^qg(z),\ b=tg_i(z)),
\]
whose inverse is $t=b/g_i(z)$; it is a dense open connected submanifold of $C'$, so $C'$ is irreducible. Let $Y\to C'$ be the normalization (\cref{thm:analytic-normalization}), and let $\nu\colon Y\to C$ be its composition with the projection; $\nu$ is finite. Over $U_i$ the normalization is an isomorphism, and we identify the corresponding open subset of $Y$ with $U_i\times\mathbb C_t$; we call it the \emph{dense open family}. Its complement in $Y$ is a nowhere dense analytic subset.

\emph{Step 3: the direct image $\mathcal F=\nu_*\mathcal O_Y$ is a coherent $\mathcal O_C$-module.} We deduce this from the finite direct-image argument in the proof of \cref{lem:strong-analytic-noetherian}. First, the local coordinate functions of $Y$ are integral over $\mathcal O_C$. Indeed, $C'$ is a closed subspace of the monic cover $b^q=a_ig_i^{q-1}$ of $C$, whose algebra is free over $\mathcal O_C$ with basis $1,b,\ldots,b^{q-1}$. Near each of the finitely many points of a fiber of $Y\to C'$, the construction of the normalization in \cref{thm:analytic-normalization} embeds $Y$ by finitely many coordinates which are integral over the local rings of $C'$. They are also integral over $\mathcal O_C$: the algebra generated over $\mathcal O_C$ by $b$ and any finite list of these coordinates is a finite $\mathcal O_C$-module, since powers of $b$ and of each coordinate can be reduced by their monic equations, and the determinant argument applied to multiplication by a coordinate on this finite module gives a monic equation for it with coefficients in $\mathcal O_C$. We apply this at all points of a fiber of $\nu$ and shrink to neighborhoods on which these monic equations hold.

Now fix a point of $C$. Lift the coefficients of the monic equations to holomorphic functions on an ambient neighborhood $A$ of this point in $U\times\mathbb C^r$. Near each point of the fiber of $\nu$, $Y$ is then a closed analytic subspace of an iterated monic cover of $A$, whose ideal is coherent by \cref{thm:oka-coherence,lem:coherent-sheaf-algebra}. The argument in \cref{lem:strong-analytic-noetherian} — separate the clusters of roots of one monic polynomial by contour integrals, divide by the corresponding monic factors, and represent multiplication by the fiber coordinate by a companion matrix — shows that the direct image, under the projection forgetting that coordinate, of a coherent module annihilated by a monic polynomial is coherent, and that this direct image is exact. Applying this once for each coordinate, we find that the direct image of $\mathcal O_Y$ to $A$ is a coherent $\mathcal O_A$-module. It is annihilated by the ideal of $C$, since functions vanishing on $C$ pull back to zero on the reduced space $Y$; hence it is a coherent $\mathcal O_C$-module. Finally, properness of $\nu$ confines the inverse image of a small neighborhood of the chosen point to the chosen neighborhoods of the finitely many points of its fiber, so $\mathcal F$ is the finite direct sum of these direct images there.

\emph{Step 4: the circle action.} The group $\mathbb C^*$ acts holomorphically on $C'$ by
\[
 \lambda\cdot(z,a,b)=(z,\lambda^q a,\lambda b),
\]
which preserves the equation $b^q=a_ig_i^{q-1}$ and the part over $U_i$; in the coordinates of the dense open family it is $(z,t)\mapsto(z,\lambda t)$. The action lifts uniquely to a holomorphic action on $Y$. To see this, note first that the product of a normal space with a disc is normal. Indeed, a bounded holomorphic function on the complement of a nowhere dense analytic subset of the product first extends across this subset on the regular part of the product, by \cref{lem:bounded-holomorphic-removal}; the coefficients of its Cauchy expansion in the disc variable are then bounded holomorphic functions on the regular part of the normal factor and extend by \cref{lem:normal-locus-open}, and the locally uniformly convergent Cauchy series gives the extension on the product. Hence $\mathbb C^*\times Y$ is normal. We apply \cref{cor:finite-bimeromorphic-normal-target} to the map $\mathbb C^*\times Y\to C'$, $(\lambda,y)\mapsto\lambda\cdot y$, where $y$ is mapped to $C'$ by the normalization; since the singular locus of $C'$ is invariant under the action, its inverse image is $\mathbb C^*$ times its inverse image in $Y$, which is nowhere dense. This gives a unique holomorphic lift $\mathbb C^*\times Y\to Y$, which is an action by uniqueness and is $t\mapsto\lambda t$ on the dense open family. The map $\nu$ intertwines it with the action $a\mapsto\lambda^qa$ on $C$.

\emph{Step 5: homogeneous generators.} The point $(x,0)$ of $C$ is fixed by the action, and it has a basis of neighborhoods invariant under the circle $S^1\subset\mathbb C^*$. Let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{C,(x,0)}$. The finite-dimensional vector space $\mathcal F_{(x,0)}/\mathfrak m\mathcal F_{(x,0)}$ carries the circle action $h\mapsto h(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}\cdot{})$, which preserves $\mathfrak m\mathcal F_{(x,0)}$ because $z$ is invariant and $a$ is multiplied by $\mathrm e^{\mathrm iq\theta}$. Averaging any Hermitian inner product over the circle makes the action unitary, and commuting unitary operators have a common eigenbasis. Each continuous one-dimensional character of the circle has the form $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^d$ with $d\in\mathbb Z$, as one sees by lifting its argument on a small interval and using the additive equation for this lift. For a section $h$ of $\mathcal F$ over an invariant neighborhood and an integer $d$, we define its component of weight $d$ by
\[
 (P_dh)(y)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
         \mathrm e^{-\mathrm id\theta}
         h(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}\cdot y)\,\mathrm d\theta.
\]
On the regular locus of $Y$, differentiation under this integral over a compact interval shows that $P_dh$ is holomorphic. It is locally bounded, so it extends holomorphically across the singular locus by \cref{lem:normal-locus-open}. It is \emph{homogeneous of weight $d$}, that is, $(P_dh)(\lambda\cdot y)=\lambda^d(P_dh)(y)$ for $|\lambda|=1$. Lift the eigenbasis to sections $s_1,\ldots,s_N$ of $\mathcal F$ over an invariant neighborhood, where $s_j$ lifts an eigenvector of weight $d_j$, and put $h_j=P_{d_j}s_j$; the class of $h_j$ in $\mathcal F_{(x,0)}/\mathfrak m\mathcal F_{(x,0)}$ is the chosen eigenvector. By the determinant argument for finite modules in the proof of \cref{lem:normal-locus-open}, these finitely many homogeneous sections $h_1,\ldots,h_N$, of weights $d_1,\ldots,d_N$, generate $\mathcal F_{(x,0)}$, hence $\mathcal F$ near $(x,0)$ by coherence. After shrinking the base neighborhood of $x$, they generate $\mathcal F$ on an invariant neighborhood of the zero section $\{(z,0)\}$. Every $d_j$ is nonnegative. Indeed, on the dense open family a function $h$ with $h(z,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}t)=\mathrm e^{\mathrm id\theta}h(z,t)$ is holomorphic in $t$ near $t=0$, and comparing coefficients of its power series in $t$ shows that $h=f(z)t^d$; holomorphy at $t=0$ then excludes $d<0$ unless $h=0$.

\emph{Step 6: sections of weight $p$.} Let $h$ be a section of $\mathcal F$ near $(x,0)$ of weight $p$, that is, $h(\lambda\cdot y)=\lambda^ph(y)$ for $|\lambda|=1$. Write $h=\sum_jk_jh_j$ locally, with $k_j$ holomorphic on $C$, and apply $P_p$ to this equation; $P_p$ fixes $h$. Since $h_j$ has weight $d_j$, it replaces each coefficient $k_j$ by its component of weight $p-d_j$, defined by the same averaging for the action $a\mapsto\lambda^qa$ on $C$. A holomorphic function on $C$ is the restriction of an ambient holomorphic function $\sum_\alpha k_\alpha(z)a^\alpha$ near $(x,0)$; projecting this convergent power series under $a\mapsto\lambda^qa$ shows that its component of weight $p-d_j$ is the homogeneous polynomial $\sum_{q|\alpha|=p-d_j}k_\alpha(z)a^\alpha$ in $a$, with holomorphic coefficients in $z$, and is zero unless $(p-d_j)/q$ is a nonnegative integer. Consequently the sections of weight $p$ are generated over $\mathcal O_U$ by the finite list
\[
 a^\alpha h_j,\qquad q|\alpha|+d_j=p.
\]
The same argument applies near each point of the zero section over a neighborhood of $x$, and after shrinking we may assume that all these generators are defined on a common invariant neighborhood.

\emph{Step 7: identification with $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$.} On the dense open family every section of weight $p$ has the form $f(z)t^p$, with $f$ holomorphic on $U_i$. Choose a smaller neighborhood of $x$ and a sufficiently small fixed $\delta>0$. For $z$ in this neighborhood with $g_i(z)\neq0$, evaluate the section at a point $t$ with
\[
 |t|^q|g(z)|=\delta.
\]
The images $(z,a)$, with $|a|=\delta$, stay in a fixed compact subset of the invariant neighborhood of the zero section in $C$, and their inverse images under the finite proper map $\nu$ are compact. Thus the section is bounded there, and
\[
 |f(z)|\leq C\delta^{-p/q}|g(z)|^{p/q}.
\]
In particular $f$ is bounded near $\{g_i=0\}$, so it extends holomorphically across this set by \cref{lem:bounded-holomorphic-removal}, and the inequality persists on $\{g_i=0\}$, since both sides are continuous and $U_i$ is dense. Applied to the finitely many generators $a^\alpha h_j$ of Step 6, this gives finitely many members of $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$, their coefficient functions.

Conversely, let $f$ be a germ of $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ at a point near $x$. On the dense open family the function $f(z)t^p$ satisfies
\[
 |f(z)t^p|\leq C|t^qg(z)|^{p/q}=C|a|^{p/q}.
\]
It is therefore locally bounded on $Y$, and it extends holomorphically to $Y$ by normality (\cref{lem:normal-locus-open}). The extension still has weight $p$, by density. By Step 6 it is a combination of the generators $a^\alpha h_j$ with coefficients holomorphic in $z$, and comparing the coefficients of $t^p$ on the dense open family writes $f$ as a holomorphic combination of the finitely many coefficient functions found above. Thus these functions generate $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ near $x$, and $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ is coherent by \cref{thm:oka-coherence,lem:coherent-sheaf-algebra}.

Finally, if $v=(p/q)\log\sum_j|g_j|^2+O(1)$ is a local presentation of $v$, then $\mathcal I_\infty(v)=\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ on its domain. These local identifications are compatible, so $\mathcal I_\infty(v)$ is coherent. (If $g(x)\neq0$, both ideals are the unit ideal near $x$; in dimension zero every finite weight gives the unit ideal.)
\end{proof}
\index{normalization!bounded growth ideals}\index{coherence!bounded growth ideals}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:analytic-integrability-bounded-growth}
Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a compact complex manifold $X$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $k_0>0$ such that for every real $k\geq k_0$
\[
 \mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon)v)\subseteq\mathcal I_\infty(kv).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:analytic-weighted-integrability-growth} with $\eta=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$ and $t=k(1+\varepsilon)$, so that $t(1-\eta)=k$. The lemma provides one threshold $t_0$ for all germs on $X$; put $k_0=t_0/(1+\varepsilon)$. For $k\geq k_0$, every germ $f\in\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon)v)$ satisfies $\log|f|^2\leq kv+O(1)$ near its base point, that is, $f\in\mathcal I_\infty(kv)$. In dimension zero all these ideals are the unit ideal.
\end{proof}
