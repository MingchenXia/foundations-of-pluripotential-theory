\section{Compact convex envelopes and full-mass limits}
\label{sec:compact-convex-interfaces}
We add two complements to the preceding sections. The first concerns finitely many closed convex functions whose effective domains lie in a common compact set, such as the conjugates of finitely many functions in $\mathcal P(N_{\mathbb R},P)$: their convex envelope is then closed, and the infimum defining it is attained. The second shows that vague convergence of real Monge--Amp\`ere measures improves to narrow convergence when the total masses converge, which is the case for functions of full mass.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact-domain-finite-convex-envelope}
Let $G_1,\ldots,G_r$, with $r\geq1$, be closed proper convex functions on a finite-dimensional real vector space, whose effective domains lie in a common compact set $K$, and let $H$ be the largest convex function below $\min_iG_i$, given by the formula of \cref{prop:convex-envelope-algebra}(2). Then $H$ is closed and proper, and at every point $x$ with $H(x)<+\infty$ the infimum defining $H(x)$ is attained. Consequently $H=(\max_iG_i^*)^*$. In particular, if $P\subset M_{\mathbb R}$ is a convex body and $F_1,\ldots,F_r\in\mathcal P(N_{\mathbb R},P)$, then the convex envelope of $F_1^*,\ldots,F_r^*$ is closed and equals $(\max_iF_i)^*$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $G_i$ is bounded below: the increasing open sets $\{G_i>-k\}$, $k\in\mathbb N$, cover the compact set $K$, because $G_i$ is lower semicontinuous and does not take the value $-\infty$, so one of them contains $K$; and $G_i=+\infty$ outside $K$. Let $B$ be a common lower bound. In a combination $\sum_j\lambda_jG_{i_j}(x_j)$ as in \cref{prop:convex-envelope-algebra}(2), the terms with the same function $G_i$ can be merged: if $\Lambda_i$ is the sum of their weights, the convexity of $G_i$ gives $\sum_{j:i_j=i}\lambda_jG_i(x_j)\geq\Lambda_iG_i(y_i)$ with $y_i=\Lambda_i^{-1}\sum_{j:i_j=i}\lambda_jx_j$, and the barycenter is unchanged. Hence it suffices to consider combinations $\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_iG_i(x_i)$ with one point $x_i\in\operatorname{dom}G_i$ for each $i$ and weights $\lambda_i\geq0$ summing to one; for an index $i$ with $\lambda_i=0$ we let $x_i$ be a fixed point of $\operatorname{dom}G_i$, and the corresponding term is omitted from the sum.

Let $x_\nu\to x$ with $\varliminf_\nu H(x_\nu)=L<+\infty$. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that $H(x_\nu)\to L$, and we choose combinations for $x_\nu$, with points $x_{i,\nu}$ and weights $\lambda_{i,\nu}$, whose values tend to $L$. Since $K$ and the simplex of weights are compact, a further subsequence satisfies $x_{i,\nu}\to x_i\in K$ and $\lambda_{i,\nu}\to\lambda_i$ for every $i$. The limiting weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and since the $x_{i,\nu}$ are bounded, the terms with $\lambda_i=0$ disappear in the limit of the barycenters, so $\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_ix_i=x$. The nonnegative numbers $\lambda_{i,\nu}(G_i(x_{i,\nu})-B)$ have sum tending to $L-B$. For $\lambda_i>0$, lower semicontinuity gives $\varliminf_\nu\lambda_{i,\nu}(G_i(x_{i,\nu})-B)\geq\lambda_i(G_i(x_i)-B)$, and therefore
\[
 \sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i(G_i(x_i)-B)
 \leq \varliminf_\nu\sum_i
       \lambda_{i,\nu}(G_i(x_{i,\nu})-B)=L-B.
\]
In particular, all the values $G_i(x_i)$ with $\lambda_i>0$ are finite, and the combination with points $x_i$ and weights $\lambda_i>0$ is admissible for $x$, with value at most $L$. Thus $H(x)\leq L$, which proves that $H$ is lower semicontinuous. Moreover $B\leq H\leq G_1$, so $H$ is proper. Applying the same argument to the constant sequence $x_\nu=x$ and admissible combinations whose values tend to $H(x)<+\infty$, we see that the infimum defining $H(x)$ is attained. By \cref{prop:convex-envelope-algebra}(2), $H^*=\max_iG_i^*$, and since $H$ is closed and proper, \cref{thm:finite-dimensional-fenchel-duality} gives $H=H^{**}=(\max_iG_i^*)^*$.

For the last assertion, the conjugates $F_i^*$ are closed proper convex functions whose effective domains lie in the compact set $P$, by \cref{thm:finite-dimensional-fenchel-duality,prop:convex-support-growth-characterization}. Since $F_i^{**}=F_i$, the first part gives the formula $(\max_iF_i)^*$ for their convex envelope.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:full-mass-real-measure-bounded-testing}
Let $\mu_j$ and $\mu$ be finite positive Radon measures on a finite-dimensional real vector space $W$ such that $\mu_j\to\mu$ vaguely and $\mu_j(W)\to\mu(W)$. Then $\int h\,\mathrm d\mu_j\to\int h\,\mathrm d\mu$ for every bounded continuous function $h$ on $W$, that is, $\mu_j\to\mu$ narrowly (\cref{sec:weak-measures}). In particular, if $P\subset M_{\mathbb R}$ is a convex body, $F_j,F\in\mathcal E(N_{\mathbb R},P)$ and $F_j\to F$ locally uniformly, then $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F_j)\to\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F)$ narrowly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. By inner regularity there is a compact set $S$ with $\mu(W\setminus S)<\varepsilon$; let $\eta$ be a continuous function with compact support, $0\leq\eta\leq1$ and $\eta=1$ on $S$. Vague convergence of $\int\eta\,\mathrm d\mu_j$ and the convergence of the total masses give
\[
 \lim_j\int(1-\eta)\,\mathrm d\mu_j
       =\int(1-\eta)\,\mathrm d\mu<\varepsilon,
\]
so that $\mu_j(W\setminus\Supp\eta)\leq\int(1-\eta)\,\mathrm d\mu_j<\varepsilon$ for all large $j$. Thus the sequence $(\mu_j)$ is eventually uniformly tight, and \cref{prop:tight-vague-narrow} gives narrow convergence. (If $W$ is a point, every function on $W$ has compact support, and vague and narrow convergence coincide.) For the real Monge--Amp\`ere measures, \cref{thm:real-monge-ampere-vague-continuity} gives vague convergence and the common total mass $n!\operatorname{vol}(P)$.
\end{proof}
