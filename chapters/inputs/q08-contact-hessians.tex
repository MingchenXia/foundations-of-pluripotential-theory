\section{Bounded complex Hessians and contact sets}
\label{sec:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}

Monge--Amp\`ere measures of envelopes are computed on contact sets: if a potential $u$ lies below an obstacle $f$ and $u=f$ on a set $E$, one expects the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $u$ on $E$ to be determined by the Hessian of $f$. Contact sets usually have empty interior, so the locality of the products on open sets (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}) says nothing about them. In this section we show that weak derivatives are local on arbitrary measurable sets: two functions whose weak derivatives up to order two are locally square-integrable have the same such derivatives almost everywhere on the set where they agree. For potentials with bounded complex Hessians this yields a formula for the Bedford--Taylor product restricted to a contact set (\cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density}), which is used for envelopes in \cref{chap:q10}.

For an open set $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ and an integer $k\ge0$, let $W^{k,2}_{\loc}(D)$ be the space of functions in $L^2_{\loc}(D)$ whose distributional derivatives of order at most $k$ belong to $L^2_{\loc}(D)$. If $g\in W^{k,2}_{\loc}(D)$, its mollifications converge to $g$ in this space on every relatively compact open subset of $D$: differentiation commutes with convolution, and each of the finitely many derivatives of order at most $k$ converges in $L^2$ there by \cref{lem:mollifier-density}.
\index{Sobolev space!local square-integrable derivatives}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weak-derivative-zero-set}
Let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be open and let $g\in W^{1,2}_{\loc}(D)$ be real. Then $\nabla g=0$ almost everywhere on $\{g=0\}$. If moreover $g\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(D)$, then all first and second weak derivatives of $g$ vanish almost everywhere on $\{g=0\}$. Here $\{g=0\}$ may be formed with any measurable representative of $g$, since changing $g$ on a null set does not affect these assertions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the chain rule
\[
 \partial_a\Phi(g)=\Phi'(g)\,\partial_a g\qquad(1\le a\le d)
\]
for a $C^1$ function $\Phi\colon\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with bounded derivative. On a relatively compact open subset of $D$, the mollifications $g_\varepsilon$ of $g$ converge to $g$ together with their first derivatives in $L^2$; passing to a sequence $\varepsilon_k\to0$, we may also assume that $g_{\varepsilon_k}\to g$ almost everywhere. Since $\Phi$ is Lipschitz, $\Phi(g_{\varepsilon_k})\to\Phi(g)$ in $L^2$. Moreover,
\[
 \Phi'(g_{\varepsilon_k})\partial_ag_{\varepsilon_k}-\Phi'(g)\partial_ag
 =\Phi'(g_{\varepsilon_k})(\partial_ag_{\varepsilon_k}-\partial_ag)
  +\bigl(\Phi'(g_{\varepsilon_k})-\Phi'(g)\bigr)\partial_ag
\]
tends to zero in $L^2$: the first term is bounded by $\|\Phi'\|_\infty|\partial_ag_{\varepsilon_k}-\partial_ag|$, and the second tends to zero by dominated convergence, since $\Phi'$ is bounded and continuous. Passing to the limit in the classical chain rule for $g_{\varepsilon_k}$, tested against compactly supported smooth functions, proves the formula.

Choose a smooth function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$ supported in $(-2,2)$, with $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi=1$ on $[-1,1]$, and put $\Phi_\varepsilon(t)=\int_0^t\chi(s/\varepsilon)\,\mathrm ds$. Then $|\Phi_\varepsilon|\le2\varepsilon$, and by the chain rule
\[
 \nabla\Phi_\varepsilon(g)=\chi(g/\varepsilon)\nabla g
       \longrightarrow\mathbf1_{\{g=0\}}\nabla g
       \quad\hbox{in }L^2_{\loc}(D)
\]
as $\varepsilon\to0$, by dominated convergence, since $\chi(g/\varepsilon)\to\mathbf1_{\{g=0\}}$ pointwise. On the other hand, $\Phi_\varepsilon(g)\to0$ uniformly, so $\nabla\Phi_\varepsilon(g)\to0$ in the sense of distributions. Hence $\mathbf1_{\{g=0\}}\nabla g=0$ almost everywhere, which is the first assertion.

If $g\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(D)$, then each $\partial_ag$ belongs to $W^{1,2}_{\loc}(D)$ and, by the first assertion, vanishes almost everywhere on $\{g=0\}$. Applying the first assertion to $\partial_ag$ shows that $\nabla\partial_ag=0$ almost everywhere on $\{\partial_ag=0\}$, a set which contains $\{g=0\}$ up to a null set. Since there are finitely many derivatives, the union of the exceptional null sets is null.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}
Let $f$ be a real continuous function on an open set $D\subset\mathbb R^d$. If the distributional Laplacian $\Delta f$ is a locally bounded measurable function, then $f\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(D)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $f_\varepsilon$ be the mollifications of $f$ on a neighborhood of a compact set. They converge to $f$ locally uniformly, and $\Delta f_\varepsilon=(\Delta f)*\rho_\varepsilon\to\Delta f$ in $L^2$ on compact sets. We first show that the gradients converge. For a real smooth function $h$ and a real cutoff $\chi\in C^\infty_c$, integration by parts and the inequality $2xy\le x^2/2+2y^2$ give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int\chi^2|\nabla h|^2
 &=-\int\chi^2h\Delta h-2\int\chi h\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla h\\
 &\le \tfrac12\int\chi^2|\nabla h|^2
       +\int\chi^2|h\Delta h|+2\int h^2|\nabla\chi|^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Apply this to $h=f_\varepsilon-f_\delta$. Since $f_\varepsilon-f_\delta\to0$ uniformly on the support of $\chi$ and $\Delta f_\varepsilon-\Delta f_\delta$ is bounded in $L^2$ there, the right side tends to zero as $\varepsilon,\delta\to0$. Hence the gradients $\nabla f_\varepsilon$ form a Cauchy family in $L^2$ on every compact set where $\chi=1$, and their limits are the distributional first derivatives of $f$.

For the second derivatives, choose a cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near a given compact set, and put $v_\varepsilon=\chi f_\varepsilon$, extended by zero. These are smooth compactly supported functions, and
\[
 \Delta v_\varepsilon
 =\chi\Delta f_\varepsilon+2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla f_\varepsilon
                                      +f_\varepsilon\Delta\chi
\]
is a Cauchy family in $L^2(\mathbb R^d)$, by the first part. For every real $v\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R^d)$, integrating by parts twice gives
\[
 \sum_{a,b=1}^d\|\partial_a\partial_b v\|_2^2
 =\sum_{a,b=1}^d\int(\partial_a^2v)(\partial_b^2v)
 =\|\Delta v\|_2^2.
\]
Applying this identity to $v_\varepsilon-v_\delta$, we see that all second derivatives of $v_\varepsilon$ form Cauchy families in $L^2$. Testing against compactly supported smooth functions identifies their limits with the distributional second derivatives of $\chi f$, which agree with those of $f$ where $\chi=1$. Exhausting $D$ by compact sets proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open. We say that a continuous function $f$ on $\Omega$ has a \emph{bounded complex Hessian} if its distributional derivatives $f_{j\bar k}=\partial^2 f/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k$ are locally bounded measurable functions. Since the Laplacian of $\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ is $4\sum_j\partial^2/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_j$, such a function belongs to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}(\Omega)$ by \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}.
\index{Hessian!bounded complex coefficients}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}
Let $f,g\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(\Omega)$ be real and continuous, and put $E=\{f=g\}$. Then the real weak first and second derivatives of $f$ and $g$ agree almost everywhere on $E$. In particular,
\[
 \mathbf1_E f_{j\bar k}=\mathbf1_E g_{j\bar k}
 \quad\hbox{almost everywhere, for all }j,k.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:weak-derivative-zero-set} to $f-g$. The complex derivatives $f_{j\bar k}$ and $g_{j\bar k}$ are fixed linear combinations of the real second derivatives, which gives the second assertion.
\end{proof}
By \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}, the proposition applies in particular to continuous functions with bounded Laplacians, and hence to continuous functions with bounded complex Hessians.

Recall from \cref{sec:positive-currents} the mixed discriminant of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices: $D(A_1,\dots,A_n)$ is $1/n!$ times the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_n$ in $\det(\sum_jt_jA_j)$, so that $D(A,\dots,A)=\det A$. In dimension zero we put $D()=1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-density}
Let $u_1,\dots,u_n$ be continuous plurisubharmonic functions with bounded complex Hessians on $\Omega$, and let $A_j=((u_j)_{a\bar b})$ be their complex Hessian matrices. Then
\[
 \ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n
       =\frac{n!}{\pi^n}D(A_1,\dots,A_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
\]
If $v_1,\dots,v_n\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(\Omega)$ are real and continuous, with weak complex Hessian matrices $B_j=((v_j)_{a\bar b})$, and $E=\bigcap_j\{u_j=v_j\}$, then
\[
 \mathbf1_E\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n
       =\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\mathbf1_E D(B_1,\dots,B_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
\]
The density on the right is locally bounded even if the $v_j$ are not plurisubharmonic; no integrability of $D(B_1,\dots,B_n)$ off $E$ is required. In dimension zero the empty product is counting measure, and both identities hold with this interpretation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For smooth $u_j$, polarize \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}: in the identity
\[
 \Bigl(\ddc\sum_jt_ju_j\Bigr)^n=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\det\Bigl(\sum_jt_jA_j\Bigr)\,\mathrm dV_{2n},
\]
we compare the coefficients of $t_1\cdots t_n$. On the left this coefficient is $n!$ times the mixed product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$, and on the right it is $(n!)^2\pi^{-n}D(A_1,\dots,A_n)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$. This gives the first formula.

For continuous $u_j$, take their local radial mollifications. They converge to $u_j$ uniformly on compact sets, so their products converge weakly by \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}. The complex Hessian coefficients of the mollifications are the mollifications of the bounded functions $A_j$. By \cref{thm:lebesgue-differentiation}, they converge to $A_j$ at all common Lebesgue points, hence almost everywhere, and they are uniformly bounded near the support of a given test function. Since $D$ is a polynomial, dominated convergence shows that the mixed discriminants of the mollified Hessian matrices, which are the densities of the smooth products by the first part, converge to $D(A_1,\dots,A_n)$ in $L^1$ there. This identifies the limit measure and proves the first formula.

By \cref{prop:bounded-complex-hessian-contact}, which applies to $u_j$ by \cref{lem:bounded-laplacian-weak-hessian}, $A_j=B_j$ almost everywhere on $\{u_j=v_j\}$, hence on $E$. Substituting into the first formula proves the second, and also shows that $\mathbf1_ED(B_1,\dots,B_n)=\mathbf1_ED(A_1,\dots,A_n)$ is locally bounded. In dimension zero there are no Hessian coefficients, and both identities reduce to the stated convention.
\end{proof}

On a complex manifold, the second identity is applied in holomorphic charts to functions $u_j$ which become plurisubharmonic after the addition of a smooth local potential $g_j$ of a closed real $(1,1)$-form. If $g_j+u_j$ is plurisubharmonic with bounded complex Hessian, one applies the proposition to $g_j+u_j$ and $g_j+v_j$: adding the same smooth function to both members of a contact pair does not change the contact set, and $g_j+v_j$ belongs to $W^{2,2}_{\loc}$ whenever $v_j$ does.
