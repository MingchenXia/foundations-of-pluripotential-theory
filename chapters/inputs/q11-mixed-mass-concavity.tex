\subsection{Mixed masses and concavity}
\label{sec:ordinary-mixed-mass-concavity}
We now compare the mixed non-pluripolar mass of $n$ closed positive currents, which may lie in different classes, with their individual masses. The idea is to replace each current by a solution of a Monge--Amp\`ere equation with one and the same smooth right-hand side. Since singularity envelopes preserve mixed masses (\cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus}), such a replacement does not change the mixed mass, and for the solutions the pointwise mixed lower bound of \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-multifactor-common-measure} integrates to the desired inequality. The equations can be solved because, by \cref{thm:positive-nonpluripolar-mass-bigness}, every current of positive mass lies in a big class.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean}
Let $X$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean}
 \int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_n\rangle
       \geq\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\int_X\langle T_i^n\rangle\right)^{1/n}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the functions
\[
 T\longmapsto\left(\int_X\langle T^n\rangle\right)^{1/n},
 \qquad T\longmapsto\log\left(\int_X\langle T^n\rangle\right)
\]
are concave on the convex cone of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, with the convention $\log0=-\infty$ for the second function.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All the masses involved are finite by \cref{thm:compact-nonpluripolar-finiteness}. If one of the masses $\int_X\langle T_i^n\rangle$ vanishes, the right side of \eqref{eq:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean} is zero and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise put $m_i=\int_X\langle T_i^n\rangle>0$. Both sides of \eqref{eq:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean} are positively homogeneous of degree one in each $T_i$, by the multilinearity of non-pluripolar products (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}), so after replacing $T_i$ by $m_i^{-1/n}T_i$ we may assume $m_i=1$ for all $i$.

By \cref{thm:positive-nonpluripolar-mass-bigness}, the class of each $T_i$ is big. Let $\theta_i$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form in the class of $T_i$ (\cref{def:real-one-one-cohomology}). By \cref{cor:current-theta-psh-potential} we can write $T_i=\theta_i+\ddc u_i$ with $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$; subtracting constants, we may assume $u_i\leq0$. Set $\phi_i=P_{\theta_i}[u_i]$. By the mass preservation in \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus} and by \cref{thm:positive-mass-model-characterization}, $\phi_i$ is a model potential of mass one.

Let $\nu=\omega^n/\int_X\omega^n$. This smooth probability measure is non-pluripolar, as noted after \cref{def:nonpluripolar-radon-measure}. In each big class $\{\theta_i\}$, \cref{thm:nonpluripolar-relative-prescribed-measure-existence} gives
\[
 w_i\in\mathcal E(X,\theta_i,\phi_i),\qquad
 S_i=\theta_i+\ddc w_i\geq0,\qquad
                 \langle S_i^n\rangle=\nu.
\]
By \cref{prop:relative-envelope-full-mass-characterization}, $P_{\theta_i}[w_i]=P_{\theta_i}[\phi_i]=\phi_i$. Applying the mixed mass preservation of \cref{prop:p-envelope-calculus} first to the tuple $(w_1,\ldots,w_n)$ and then to the tuple $(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$, we obtain
\[
 \int_X\langle S_1\wedge\cdots\wedge S_n\rangle
 =\int_X\left\langle\bigwedge_{i=1}^n
               (\theta_i+\ddc\phi_i)\right\rangle
 =\int_X\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_n\rangle.
\]
On the other hand, \cref{prop:nonpluripolar-multifactor-common-measure}, with all densities equal to one, gives $\langle S_1\wedge\cdots\wedge S_n\rangle\geq\nu$. Hence the common value of these mixed masses is at least $\nu(X)=1$, which is the normalized inequality. Undoing the scalings proves \eqref{eq:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean}.

For the concavity, let $T,S$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents and $0<t<1$; for $t=0,1$ there is nothing to prove. Put $a=\int_X\langle T^n\rangle$ and $b=\int_X\langle S^n\rangle$. By multilinearity,
\[
 \int_X\langle((1-t)T+tS)^n\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk(1-t)^kt^{n-k}
                           \int_X\langle T^k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle.
\]
For $1\leq k\leq n-1$, \eqref{eq:ordinary-mixed-mass-geometric-mean} with $k$ factors $T$ and $n-k$ factors $S$ gives $\int_X\langle T^k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle\geq a^{k/n}b^{(n-k)/n}$; the terms with $k=n$ and $k=0$ are $a$ and $b$, also when one of them vanishes. The binomial formula therefore yields
\[
 \int_X\langle((1-t)T+tS)^n\rangle\geq
              \big((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big)^n.
\]
Taking $n$th roots proves that the function $T\mapsto(\int_X\langle T^n\rangle)^{1/n}$ is concave. If $a$ and $b$ are both positive, then, since the logarithm is increasing and concave,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\Big(\int_X\langle((1-t)T+tS)^n\rangle\Big)
               &\geq n\log\big((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big)\\
               &\geq(1-t)\log a+t\log b.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If one of them is zero, the right side is $-\infty$ for $0<t<1$, so there is nothing to prove.
\end{proof}
\index{non-pluripolar mass!geometric-mean inequality}
\index{non-pluripolar mass!concavity}

The connectedness of $X$ cannot be omitted when $n\geq2$. Let $X$ be the disjoint union of two compact K\"ahler manifolds $X',X''$ of dimension $n\geq2$, with K\"ahler forms $\omega'$ and $\omega''$. Let $T_1$ equal $\omega'$ on $X'$ and zero on $X''$, let $T_2$ equal zero on $X'$ and $\omega''$ on $X''$, and let $T_3,\ldots,T_n$ equal $\omega'$ on $X'$ and $\omega''$ on $X''$. Every mass $\int_X\langle T_i^n\rangle$ is positive, but $\langle T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_n\rangle=0$. The theorem applies separately on each connected component.
