\subsection{Penalized equations and metric-Laplacian envelopes}
\label{sec:penalized-kahler-envelopes}
We now show that, for a K\"ahler background, the envelope of finitely many obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian again has bounded metric Laplacian (\cref{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}). Throughout this subsection $\kappa$ is a fixed K\"ahler form on $X$. It is the background of the envelopes, while $\omega$ remains the metric with respect to which the Laplacians of obstacles are measured. Constants are $\kappa$-psh, so for a continuous obstacle $f$ the envelope $P_\kappa(f)$ is a bounded $\kappa$-psh function with $\min_Xf\leq P_\kappa(f)\leq f$ everywhere (\cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}). For smooth $f$ we approximate $P_\kappa(f)$ by the smooth solutions $a_\beta$ of the Monge--Amp\`ere equation with exponential penalty
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n,
\]
where $\beta>0$ is a large parameter. On the set where $a>f$ the right side is very large, which forces the solution to lie essentially below $f$; on the set where $a<f$ it is very small, just as the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the envelope vanishes off the contact set. As $\beta\to\infty$ the solutions converge uniformly to $P_\kappa(f)$, with an explicit rate (\cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}).

The main estimate is a bound for the Laplacian of $a_\beta$ that is uniform in $\beta$ and depends on the obstacle only through its supremum norm and an \emph{upper} bound for its Laplacian (\cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}). This one-sided dependence is what makes finite minima accessible: the second derivatives of $\min_jf_j$ have in general a singular negative part where two of the $f_j$ cross, but the smooth approximations $-\delta\log\sum_j\mathrm e^{-f_j/\delta}$ of the minimum have Laplacians that are uniformly bounded above. The existence of smooth solutions is proved by the continuity method (\cref{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}); besides the trace bound, its a priori estimates consist of a third-order estimate for the difference of the connections of two K\"ahler metrics and elliptic bootstrapping.

In local holomorphic coordinates we sum over repeated indices and write
\[
 \omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}h_{i\bar j}\,
                 \mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}k_{i\bar j}\,
                 \mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j,
\]
so that $h=(h_{i\bar j})$ and $k=(k_{i\bar j})$ are the coefficient matrices of $2\pi\omega$ and $2\pi\kappa$. Let $(h^{i\bar j})$ be the inverse matrix of $h$, normalized by $h^{i\bar j}h_{l\bar j}=\delta_{il}$, and for a Hermitian matrix $m=(m_{i\bar j})$ put $\operatorname{tr}_hm=h^{i\bar j}m_{i\bar j}$. For a real function $a$ write $a_{i\bar j}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}a$ and
\[
 D_ha=h^{i\bar j}a_{i\bar j}=\Lambda\ddc a=-\frac{La}{4\pi},
\]
where the last two equalities come from \eqref{eq:kahler-trace-star}. Thus $D_h$ is the metric Laplacian up to the factor $-1/(4\pi)$, and a bound for $D_hf$ is a bound for $Lf$. Since $X$ is compact, the norms defined by $h$ and by the metric of $\omega$ differ by fixed factors, and there is $\gamma>0$ with $k\geq\gamma h$. For a potential $a$ put $g_{i\bar j}=k_{i\bar j}+a_{i\bar j}$, so that $\kappa+\ddc a=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}g_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$. When $g>0$ we define $D_g=g^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}$ and $\operatorname{tr}_gm=g^{i\bar j}m_{i\bar j}$ in the same way, write $|\xi|_g^2=g^{i\bar j}\xi_i\bar\xi_j$ for a $(1,0)$-form $\xi=\xi_i\,\mathrm dz_i$, and set
\[
 H=\operatorname{tr}_hg=\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_ha.
\]
All these quantities are independent of the holomorphic coordinates. Finally $\operatorname{osc}_Xf=\max_Xf-\min_Xf$ denotes the oscillation of a continuous function $f$. Note that $\int_XD_hf\,\mathrm dV=-(4\pi)^{-1}\int_XLf\,\mathrm dV=0$ for smooth $f$ by Stokes' formula, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}
 \sup_XD_hf\geq0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:penalized-equation-trace}
Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$ and $\beta>0$, and let $a$ be a smooth real function satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-kahler-equation}
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n,
 \qquad \kappa+\ddc a>0.
\end{equation}
Then $\min_Xf\leq a\leq\max_Xf$, and the metrics $g$ and $h$ are uniformly equivalent: $C^{-1}h\leq g\leq Ch$ on $X$, with $C$ depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$, $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$. Moreover, there is $\beta_0>0$, depending only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$, such that for $\beta>\beta_0$
\[
 H\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{A\operatorname{osc}_Xf}\quad\text{on }X,
 \qquad B=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hf,
\]
where $A>0$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. In particular, for $\beta>\beta_0$ the trace $H$ is bounded by a constant depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the value bounds.} Since $(\kappa+\ddc a)^n/\kappa^n=\det g/\det k$, the equation says that $\det g=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\det k$. At a maximum point of $a$ the complex Hessian $(a_{i\bar j})$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<g\leq k$ and $\det g\leq\det k$ there. Hence $a\leq f\leq\max_Xf$ at that point, and therefore everywhere. At a minimum point of $a$ the inequalities are reversed, and $a\geq\min_Xf$.

\emph{Step 2: a differential inequality for $\log H$.} Fix a point of $X$. Since $\omega$ is K\"ahler, there are holomorphic coordinates centred at this point in which $h_{i\bar j}=\delta_{ij}$ and all first derivatives of $h$ vanish at the point (see the proof of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-star}); after a unitary change of coordinates we may also assume that $g$ is diagonal at the point, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n>0$. On the compact manifold $X$ these coordinates can be chosen with derivatives bounded independently of the point. At the point $H=\sum_i\lambda_i$ and $\operatorname{tr}_gh=\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1}$, so the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
 H\operatorname{tr}_gh\geq n^2.
\]
Locally $\kappa$ and $\kappa+\ddc a$ have K\"ahler potentials, so the derivatives $\partial_pg_{i\bar q}$ are symmetric in $p$ and $i$, and the second derivatives $\partial_p\partial_{\bar l}g_{i\bar q}$ are symmetric in $p,i$ and in $\bar l,\bar q$. All the following computations are at the chosen point. Differentiating $H=h^{i\bar q}g_{i\bar q}$ twice, and using that the first derivatives of $h$ vanish, we get
\[
 D_gH=\sum_p\lambda_p^{-1}\sum_i\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}+E,
 \qquad E=\sum_p\lambda_p^{-1}\bigl(\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}h^{i\bar q}\bigr)g_{i\bar q}.
\]
Since $g$ is diagonal with positive eigenvalues, $|E|\leq CH\operatorname{tr}_gh$, with $C$ depending only on the second derivatives of $h^{-1}$. On the other hand, differentiating the identity $\partial_p\log\det g=g^{i\bar q}\partial_pg_{i\bar q}$ and using $\partial(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}(\partial g)g^{-1}$ gives
\[
 \partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\log\det g
 =\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1}\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}
  -\sum_{i,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_q}.
\]
By the symmetry of the second derivatives, $\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar i}g_{p\bar p}$. Summing over $p$ and comparing with the formula for $D_gH$, we obtain
\[
 D_gH=\sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\log\det g
       +\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_q}+E.
\]
Moreover $\partial_pH=\sum_i\partial_pg_{i\bar i}=\sum_i\partial_ig_{p\bar i}$ by the symmetry of the first derivatives. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality with weights $\lambda_i$ therefore gives
\[
 |\partial H|_g^2
 =\sum_p\frac1{\lambda_p}\Bigl|\sum_i\partial_ig_{p\bar i}\Bigr|^2
 \leq H\sum_{p,i}\frac{|\partial_ig_{p\bar i}|^2}
                           {\lambda_p\lambda_i}
 \leq H\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}
                           {\lambda_i\lambda_q},
\]
the last sum containing the previous one. Since $D_g\log H=H^{-1}D_gH-H^{-2}|\partial H|_g^2$, the quadratic term in $D_gH$ absorbs the negative term, and
\[
 D_g\log H\geq\frac1H\Bigl(\sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\log\det g+E\Bigr).
\]
Now we use the equation in the form $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-f)$. At the point, $\sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}=D_h$ and $D_ha=H-\operatorname{tr}_hk$, so
\[
 \sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\log\det g
 =D_h\log\det k+\beta\bigl(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk-D_hf\bigr).
\]
The term $D_h\log\det k$ is bounded by a constant depending only on $\omega$ and $\kappa$, because of the uniform choice of coordinates. Dividing by $H$ and using $H^{-1}\leq\operatorname{tr}_gh/n^2$ and $|E|/H\leq C\operatorname{tr}_gh$, we arrive at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-log-trace}
 D_g\log H\geq\beta\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{tr}_h k+D_hf}{H}\right)
                                  -C_0\operatorname{tr}_g h,
\end{equation}
where $C_0$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. Both sides are independent of the coordinates, so \eqref{eq:penalized-log-trace} holds everywhere on $X$.

\emph{Step 3: the maximum principle.} By \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, $B>0$. For a constant $A>0$, to be chosen, let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $Q=\log H-Aa$. Since
\[
 D_ga=g^{i\bar j}(g_{i\bar j}-k_{i\bar j})=n-\operatorname{tr}_gk
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{tr}_gk\geq\gamma\operatorname{tr}_gh,
\]
inequality \eqref{eq:penalized-log-trace} and $\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_hf\leq B$ give at $x_0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-trace-maximum}
 0\geq D_gQ\geq\beta\left(1-\frac B H\right)
             +(A\gamma-C_0)\operatorname{tr}_g h-An.
\end{equation}

First let $\beta$ be fixed, and take $A=(C_0+\beta B/n^2+1)/\gamma$. Since $1/H\leq\operatorname{tr}_gh/n^2$, the right side of \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-maximum} is at least $\beta+\operatorname{tr}_gh-An$, so $\operatorname{tr}_gh\leq An$ at $x_0$. Thus every eigenvalue of $g$ relative to $h$ is at least $(An)^{-1}$ at $x_0$. By Step 1, $|a-f|\leq\operatorname{osc}_Xf\leq2\|f\|_\infty$, and therefore, by the equation, the product of the eigenvalues satisfies
\[
 \Bigl(\inf_X\frac{\det k}{\det h}\Bigr)\mathrm e^{-2\beta\|f\|_\infty}
 \leq\frac{\det g}{\det h}=\frac{\det k}{\det h}\,\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}
 \leq\Bigl(\sup_X\frac{\det k}{\det h}\Bigr)\mathrm e^{2\beta\|f\|_\infty}
\]
everywhere on $X$. Together with the lower bound for the eigenvalues at $x_0$, the upper bound for their product bounds each eigenvalue above at $x_0$, and hence bounds $H(x_0)$. For every $x\in X$, since $Q(x)\leq Q(x_0)$ and $\min_Xf\leq a\leq\max_Xf$,
\[
 \log H(x)\leq\log H(x_0)+A\bigl(a(x)-a(x_0)\bigr)
 \leq\log H(x_0)+A\operatorname{osc}_Xf.
\]
Thus $H$ is bounded on $X$, so every eigenvalue of $g$ relative to $h$ is bounded above on $X$, and the lower bound for their product then bounds every eigenvalue below. All the constants depend only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$, $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$, which proves the uniform equivalence of $g$ and $h$.

Now let $A=(C_0+1)/\gamma$ and $\beta_0=2An$; both depend only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. Suppose that $\beta>\beta_0$. If $H(x_0)>2B$, then $1-B/H>1/2$ at $x_0$, and the right side of \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-maximum} is at least $\beta/2+\operatorname{tr}_gh-An>0$, a contradiction. Hence $H(x_0)\leq2B$, and the same argument as above gives
\[
 H\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{A\operatorname{osc}_X f}\quad\text{on }X.
\]
Since $\operatorname{osc}_Xf\leq2\|f\|_\infty$, this proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere equation!trace estimate for an exponential penalty}

The existence of solutions of the penalized equation is proved by the continuity method. Besides the trace bound, the a priori estimates require control of the third derivatives of the potential. We obtain it from a maximum principle for the squared norm of the difference of the Chern connections of $g$ and $h$, and then bootstrap with the elliptic $L^p$ estimates of \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}
For every smooth real function $f$ on $X$ and every $\beta>0$, equation \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation} has a unique smooth real solution $a_\beta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $0\leq t\leq1$ consider the equation
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-tf)}\kappa^n,
 \qquad \kappa+\ddc a>0,
\]
and let $T\subset[0,1]$ be the set of $t$ for which it has a smooth real solution. For $t=0$ the solution is $a=0$, so $T\neq\varnothing$. We show that $T$ is open and closed in $[0,1]$; then $T=[0,1]$, and the case $t=1$ gives existence. Uniqueness is proved at the end.

\emph{Step 1: the second-order estimate.} Let $a$ be a smooth solution for some $t\in[0,1]$. Since $\|tf\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty$ and, by \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, $\sup_XD_h(tf)=t\sup_XD_hf\leq\sup_XD_hf$, \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace} gives $C^{-1}h\leq g\leq Ch$ with $C$ independent of $t$; indeed, the constants in its proof increase with $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$. In Steps 1--3, constants may depend on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$ and finitely many derivatives of $f$, but not on $t$ or on the solution.

\emph{Step 2: the third-order estimate.} Let $\nabla$ and $\widehat\nabla$ be the Chern connections of the K\"ahler metrics $g$ and $h$ on the holomorphic tangent bundle. In holomorphic coordinates their coefficients are
\[
 \Gamma^m_{ij}=g^{m\bar l}\partial_ig_{j\bar l},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Gamma^m_{ij}=h^{m\bar l}\partial_ih_{j\bar l},
\]
both symmetric in $i$ and $j$. On a tensor with holomorphic and antiholomorphic indices, $\nabla_p$ acts by $\partial_p$, plus a term $\Gamma^m_{pl}$ contracted with each upper holomorphic index, minus a term $\Gamma^l_{pi}$ contracted with each lower holomorphic index; antiholomorphic indices receive no connection term from $\nabla_p$. The operator $\nabla_{\bar q}$ is given by the conjugate rule. With this rule and the formula for the derivative of an inverse matrix one checks that $\nabla g=0$. Put
\[
 \Psi^m_{ij}=\Gamma^m_{ij}-\widehat\Gamma^m_{ij}
             =g^{m\bar l}\widehat\nabla_i g_{j\bar l},
 \qquad S=|\Psi|_g^2=g^{i\bar p}g^{j\bar q}g_{m\bar l}\,\Psi^m_{ij}\overline{\Psi^l_{pq}};
\]
the second expression for $\Psi$ follows from $\widehat\nabla_ig_{j\bar l}=\partial_ig_{j\bar l}-\widehat\Gamma^p_{ij}g_{p\bar l}$. The difference $\Psi$ of the two connections is a tensor, since the inhomogeneous terms in the transformation rules of $\Gamma$ and $\widehat\Gamma$ cancel; hence $S$ is a smooth function on $X$.

We need some curvature identities. Put $R^m{}_{i\bar qj}=-\partial_{\bar q}\Gamma^m_{ij}$, define $\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}$ in the same way from $h$, and let
\[
 R_{i\bar j}=R^m{}_{i\bar jm}=-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det g,
 \qquad R_j{}^m=g^{m\bar l}R_{j\bar l},
\]
be the Ricci tensor and the Ricci endomorphism of $g$; the formula for $R_{i\bar j}$ follows from $\Gamma^m_{im}=\partial_i\log\det g$. Since $\nabla_{\bar q}$ acts on holomorphic indices as $\partial_{\bar q}$,
\[
 \nabla_{\bar q}\Psi^m_{ij}=\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}-R^m{}_{i\bar qj}.
\]
The K\"ahler condition also gives the contracted Bianchi identity $g^{p\bar q}\nabla_pR^m{}_{i\bar qj}=\nabla_iR_j{}^m$. To check it at a point, use holomorphic coordinates in which the first derivatives of $g$ vanish at that point. There all connection coefficients vanish, so covariant derivatives at the point are coordinate derivatives. Since $R^m{}_{i\bar qj}=-g^{m\bar l}\partial_i\partial_{\bar q}g_{j\bar l}$ and $R_{j\bar l}=-g^{p\bar q}\partial_j\partial_{\bar l}g_{p\bar q}$ up to terms quadratic in the first derivatives of $g$, both sides of the identity are equal at the point to $-g^{m\bar l}g^{p\bar q}\partial_i\partial_p\partial_{\bar q}g_{j\bar l}$, because the derivatives of a local K\"ahler potential commute. Applying $g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p$ to the formula for $\nabla_{\bar q}\Psi$ and using this identity, we obtain for the rough Laplacian $\Delta=g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p\nabla_{\bar q}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-calabi-tensor}
 \Delta\Psi^m_{ij}
  =g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}
                                      -\nabla_iR_j{}^m.
\end{equation}
Let $\overline\Delta=g^{p\bar q}\nabla_{\bar q}\nabla_p$ be the Laplacian with the derivatives in the opposite order. The commutator $[\nabla_p,\nabla_{\bar q}]$ acts on an upper holomorphic index through $R^m{}_{p\bar ql}$ and on a lower one through $-R^l{}_{p\bar qi}$. Contracting with $g^{p\bar q}$ and using the K\"ahler symmetry $g^{p\bar q}R^m{}_{p\bar ql}=R_l{}^m$, we find $\overline\Delta\Psi=\Delta\Psi+\mathcal R\Psi$, where
\[
 (\mathcal R\Psi)^m_{ij}
   =R_i{}^l\Psi^m_{lj}+R_j{}^l\Psi^m_{il}
                                  -R_l{}^m\Psi^l_{ij}.
\]
Write $\nabla\Psi=(\nabla_p\Psi^m_{ij})$ and $\overline\nabla\Psi=(\nabla_{\bar q}\Psi^m_{ij})$. Since $\nabla g=0$, the product rule gives $D_gS=|\nabla\Psi|_g^2+|\overline\nabla\Psi|_g^2+\langle\Delta\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g+\langle\Psi,\overline\Delta\Psi\rangle_g$. The Ricci endomorphism is Hermitian with respect to $g$, so $\langle\Psi,\mathcal R\Psi\rangle_g=\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g$ is real, and we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-calabi-norm}
 D_gS=|\nabla\Psi|_g^2+|\overline\nabla\Psi|_g^2
       +2\operatorname{Re}\langle\Delta\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g
       +\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g.
\end{equation}

The equation determines the Ricci tensor: applying $-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}$ to $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-tf)$ gives
\[
 R_{i\bar j}=-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det k
       -\beta\bigl(g_{i\bar j}-k_{i\bar j}-tf_{i\bar j}\bigr).
\]
Here $-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det k$ is the Ricci tensor of $\kappa$. Thus the Ricci tensor of $g$ is a combination of $g$ and of the fixed smooth tensors $k$, $\operatorname{Ric}(k)$ and $(f_{i\bar j})$, and by Step 1 it is bounded in $g$-norm. For a fixed smooth tensor $T$, the difference $\nabla T-\widehat\nabla T$ is a contraction of $\Psi$ with $T$, while $\widehat\nabla T$ is bounded in $h$-norm and hence in $g$-norm; therefore $|\nabla T|_g\leq C(1+|\Psi|_g)$. Since moreover $\nabla g=0$, we obtain $|\nabla R_j{}^m|_g\leq C(1+S^{1/2})$, and the same bound holds for $\nabla\widehat R$. By \eqref{eq:elliptic-calabi-tensor}, $|\Delta\Psi|_g\leq C(1+S^{1/2})$, and $|\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g|\leq CS$ because the Ricci endomorphism is bounded. Discarding the two nonnegative gradient terms in \eqref{eq:elliptic-calabi-norm}, we conclude that
\[
 D_gS\geq-2C(1+S^{1/2})S^{1/2}-CS\geq-C_1(S+1).
\]

To compensate this term we use the trace $H$. Step 2 of the proof of \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}, with $f$ replaced by $tf$ and with the quadratic term retained, gives at the centre of the coordinates used there
\[
 D_gH=D_h\log\det g+\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_q}+E,
\]
where $|E|\leq CH\operatorname{tr}_gh\leq C$ by Step 1, and $D_h\log\det g=D_h\log\det k+\beta(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk-tD_hf)$ is bounded, again by Step 1. At that point $\partial_pg_{i\bar q}=\widehat\nabla_pg_{i\bar q}$ and $g$ is diagonal, so
\[
 S=\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\widehat\nabla_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_p\lambda_i\lambda_q},
\]
and the quadratic sum is at least $(\min_p\lambda_p)S$. By Step 1 the eigenvalues are bounded below, so $D_gH\geq cS-C_2$ with $c>0$. Choose $K$ with $Kc\geq C_1+1$. At a maximum point of $S+KH$,
\[
 0\geq D_g(S+KH)\geq(Kc-C_1)S-C_1-KC_2\geq S-C_1-KC_2,
\]
so $S$ is bounded at that point. Since $H$ is bounded on $X$, the function $S+KH$, and hence $S$, is bounded on $X$.

\emph{Step 3: the higher-order estimates.} The bound for $S$ controls all first derivatives of $g$: the holomorphic ones by $\widehat\nabla_ig_{j\bar l}=g_{m\bar l}\Psi^m_{ij}$ and the metric equivalence, and the antiholomorphic ones as their complex conjugates. In particular the first derivatives of $H$ are bounded, and $g^{-1}$ is uniformly Lipschitz. Since $La=-4\pi D_ha=-4\pi(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk)$ is bounded, \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} bounds $a$ in $W^{2,p}(X)$ for every finite $p>1$. To bound the third derivatives, let $\chi$ be a cutoff function supported in a chart, let $\partial_\mu$ be a real coordinate derivative, and apply \eqref{eq:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} to $v=\chi\partial_\mu a$, extended by zero. Since
\[
 Lv=\chi\partial_\mu(La)+[L,\chi\partial_\mu]a,
\]
where $\partial_\mu(La)=-4\pi\partial_\mu(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk)$ is bounded and the commutator is a second-order operator with smooth coefficients, $Lv$ is bounded in $L^p$ by the $W^{2,p}$ bound for $a$. Hence $a$ is bounded in $W^{3,p}(X)$ for every finite $p$, and Morrey's inequality (\cref{lem:morrey-sobolev-representative}), applied to the first derivatives of $a$, gives a uniform $C^{2,\alpha}$ bound when $p>2n/(1-\alpha)$.

For the fourth derivatives we differentiate the equation itself. Differentiating $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-tf)$ in the direction of a real coordinate $x_\mu$, we find that $v=\partial_\mu a$ satisfies
\[
 g^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}v
   =\beta(v-t\partial_\mu f)+\partial_\mu\log\det k
                           -g^{i\bar j}\partial_\mu k_{i\bar j}.
\]
In real coordinates, $D_g=g^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}$ is a real elliptic operator without lower-order terms whose coefficients satisfy uniform ellipticity bounds, by Step 1, and a uniform Lipschitz bound, by Step 2. Therefore Step 1 of the proof of \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} applies to $-D_g$ in place of $L$, with balls of a fixed radius, and gives $\|D^2w\|_p\leq C(\|D_gw\|_p+\|\nabla w\|_p)$ for smooth $w$ supported in such a ball. Differentiating the last equation in the direction of $x_\nu$, we see that $D_g(\partial_\nu v)$ equals $-(\partial_\nu g^{i\bar j})\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}v$ plus the $x_\nu$-derivative of the right side. The latter is bounded, and the coefficient derivative $\partial_\nu g^{i\bar j}$ is bounded and multiplies a third derivative of $a$, which is already bounded in $L^p$. Applying the estimate to $w=\chi\partial_\nu v$, with $\chi$ a cutoff function supported in one of these balls, and noting that the cutoff commutators involve only third derivatives of $a$, we obtain bounds for $a$ in $W^{4,p}$ for every finite $p$.

Now assume inductively that $a$ is bounded in $W^{\ell,p}$ for every finite $p$, where $\ell\geq4$. By Morrey's inequality the derivatives of $a$ of order at most $\ell-1$ are bounded. Differentiate the equation $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-tf)$ $\ell-1$ times in real coordinate directions, and keep on the left its single highest-order term, which is $D_g$ applied to an $(\ell-1)$st derivative of $a$. Every other term is either bounded, being a product of entries of $g^{-1}$ with derivatives of $\log\det k$, of $k$, of $f$, or of $a$ of order at most $\ell-1$, or it is a product of entries of $g^{-1}$ with at least two derivatives of $g$ whose positive orders add up to at most $\ell-1$. In such a product each factor has order at most $\ell-2$, corresponding to a derivative of $a$ of order at most $\ell$, and at most one factor has this order, since $2(\ell-2)>\ell-1$; all other factors involve derivatives of $a$ of order at most $\ell-1$ and are bounded. Hence all these terms are bounded in $L^p$. The estimate for $D_g$ just described, whose balls have a fixed radius by the uniform Lipschitz bound for $g^{-1}$, now gives bounds in $W^{\ell+1,p}$; the cutoff commutators involve only derivatives of order at most $\ell$, which are already bounded. If a solution is a priori only known to lie in $W^{\ell,p}$, we replace the equation differentiated $\ell-1$ times by difference quotients of the equation differentiated $\ell-2$ times. The same estimate then bounds the difference quotients of the derivatives of order $\ell-2$ in $W^{2,p}$, uniformly in the increment, so that the derivatives of order $\ell-1$ lie in $W^{2,p}$ with the same bound. By induction and Morrey's inequality, every $C^\ell$ norm of $a$ is bounded independently of $t$.

\emph{Step 4: openness.} We use the Sobolev spaces $H^s$ of the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations} and fix an integer $s>n+6$. These spaces satisfy the multiplication estimate
\[
 \|vw\|_{H^s}\leq C_s\|v\|_{H^s}\|w\|_{H^s}.
\]
After a finite partition of unity it suffices to prove this for functions with compact support in one chart, written as functions on $\mathbb R^{2n}$. The Fourier transform of $vw$ is a constant multiple of the convolution $\hat v*\hat w$. With $\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^2)^{1/2}$ we have $\langle\xi\rangle\leq\langle\eta\rangle+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle$, hence $\langle\xi\rangle^s\leq2^s(\langle\eta\rangle^s+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^s)$, and therefore
\[
 \langle\xi\rangle^s|\hat v*\hat w|(\xi)
 \leq2^s\Bigl(\bigl(\langle\cdot\rangle^s|\hat v|\bigr)*|\hat w|
        +|\hat v|*\bigl(\langle\cdot\rangle^s|\hat w|\bigr)\Bigr)(\xi).
\]
By Young's inequality $\|F*G\|_2\leq\|F\|_2\|G\|_1$, it remains to bound $\|\hat w\|_1$ and $\|\hat v\|_1$ by the $H^s$ norms. This follows from the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $\|\hat w\|_1\leq\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-s}\|_2\|\langle\cdot\rangle^s\hat w\|_2$, since $\langle\xi\rangle^{-s}$ is square integrable on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ for $s>n$. Young's inequality in this form is proved by the weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $|F*G(x)|^2\leq\|G\|_1\int|F(x-y)|^2|G(y)|\,\mathrm dy$, followed by integration in $x$ and Fubini's theorem.

Let $t_0\in T$, with solution $a$, and let $g$ be the metric of $a$. For a real function $b\in H^{s+2}$ let $Y$ be the matrix with entries $Y^m{}_j=g^{m\bar l}b_{j\bar l}$, so that
\[
 \det\bigl(k_{i\bar j}+a_{i\bar j}+b_{i\bar j}\bigr)=\det g\,\det(I+Y),
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}Y=D_gb.
\]
Since $H^{s+2}\subset C^2$, the matrix $k+(a_{i\bar j}+b_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite when $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}$ is small. Then, by the multiplication estimate, the power series of $\log(I+Y)$ converges in $H^s$, and $\operatorname{tr}\log(I+Y)=\log\det(I+Y)$. For such $b$ we put
\[
 \mathcal N_t(a+b)=\log\frac{\det(k_{i\bar j}+a_{i\bar j}+b_{i\bar j})}{\det k}
                                                 -\beta(a+b-tf)\in H^s,
\]
so that $a+b$ solves the equation for $t$ exactly when $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$. Since $\mathcal N_{t_0}(a)=0$, we obtain
\[
 \mathcal N_t(a+b)=\beta(t-t_0)f+\mathcal Db+\mathcal Q(b),
 \qquad \mathcal D=D_g-\beta,
\]
where $\mathcal Q(b)=\operatorname{tr}(\log(I+Y)-Y)$ satisfies $\|\mathcal Q(b)\|_{H^s}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}^2$ and $\|\mathcal Q(b)-\mathcal Q(b')\|_{H^s}\leq C(\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|b'\|_{H^{s+2}})\|b-b'\|_{H^{s+2}}$ for small $b$, $b'$. The operator $\mathcal D\colon H^{s+2}\to H^s$ is invertible. Indeed, integrating by parts with respect to the volume form $(\kappa+\ddc a)^n$ of $g$, the quadratic form of $-\mathcal D$ is the sum of the integral of $|\partial b|_g^2$ and $\beta\int|b|^2$, and it is equivalent to the square of the $H^1$ norm because $\beta>0$. Riesz representation and the difference-quotient argument in the proof of \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}, applied to this divergence-form operator with smooth coefficients, give the inverse and its bound from $H^s$ to $H^{s+2}$.

The solutions of $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$ are the fixed points of
\[
 \Phi_t(b)=b-\mathcal D^{-1}\mathcal N_t(a+b)
 =-\mathcal D^{-1}\bigl(\beta(t-t_0)f+\mathcal Q(b)\bigr).
\]
By the bounds for $\mathcal Q$, there is $r>0$, small enough for the above expansion to be valid on the closed ball of radius $r$ in $H^{s+2}$, such that, whenever $|t-t_0|$ is sufficiently small, $\Phi_t$ maps this ball into itself and has Lipschitz constant at most $1/2$ there. The iterates $b_0=0$, $b_{\ell+1}=\Phi_t(b_\ell)$ then form a Cauchy sequence, since their successive differences decrease geometrically, and their limit $b$ is a fixed point of $\Phi_t$, that is, $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$. By the Sobolev embedding, $a+b$ has at least six continuous derivatives, and $\kappa+\ddc(a+b)>0$ if $r$ is small. The regularity argument of Step 3, in its difference-quotient form, shows that $a+b$ is smooth. Hence $T$ contains a neighbourhood of $t_0$ in $[0,1]$, and $T$ is open.

\emph{Step 5: closedness.} Let $t_k\in T$ converge to $t_*\in[0,1]$, with solutions $a_k$. By Steps 1--3, every $C^\ell$ norm of $a_k$ is bounded independently of $k$. By the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem and a diagonal argument, a subsequence converges in every $C^\ell$ norm to a smooth function $a_*$. The uniform lower bound for the eigenvalues of $g$ relative to $h$ gives $\kappa+\ddc a_*>0$, and $a_*$ solves the equation for $t_*$. Thus $T$ is closed, and $T=[0,1]$ since $[0,1]$ is connected.

\emph{Step 6: uniqueness.} Let $a$ and $a'$ be smooth solutions of \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation}. At a maximum point of $a-a'$ the complex Hessian of $a-a'$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<k+(a_{i\bar j})\leq k+(a'_{i\bar j})$ and the equations give $\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\leq\mathrm e^{\beta(a'-f)}$ there. Hence $a-a'\leq0$ at that point, and therefore everywhere. Interchanging $a$ and $a'$ proves that $a=a'$.
\end{proof}
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere equation!smooth exponential penalty}

As $\beta\to\infty$, the solutions $a_\beta$ converge to the envelope of $f$. The upper bound follows from the maximum principle, the lower bound from the comparison principle applied to a slightly lowered convex combination of the envelope and a constant.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}
Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, and for $\beta>0$ let $a_\beta$ be the solution of \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation} given by \cref{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}. Put
\[
 B=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hf,\qquad
 d_0=\inf_X\frac{\det k}{\det h},\qquad
 M=\frac{B^n}{n^nd_0}.
\]
Then $M\geq1$, and for every $\beta\geq1$
\[
 P_\kappa(f)-\frac{\operatorname{osc}_Xf+n\log\beta}{\beta}
  \leq a_\beta
  \leq P_\kappa(f)+\frac{\log M}{\beta}.
\]
In particular $a_\beta\to P_\kappa(f)$ uniformly on $X$ as $\beta\to\infty$. Moreover $P_\kappa(f)$ has bounded metric Laplacian, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each point, the eigenvalues of $k$ relative to $h$ are positive, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives $\operatorname{tr}_hk\geq n(\det k/\det h)^{1/n}\geq nd_0^{1/n}$. Since $\sup_XD_hf\geq0$ by \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, it follows that $B\geq nd_0^{1/n}$, that is, $M\geq1$.

\emph{The upper bound.} Write $a=a_\beta$ and $g=k+(a_{i\bar j})$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $a-f$. There the complex Hessian of $a-f$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<g\leq k+(f_{i\bar j})$. In particular $k+(f_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite at $x_0$, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for its eigenvalues relative to $h$ gives
\[
 \mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}=\frac{\det g}{\det k}
 \leq\frac{\det(k+(f_{i\bar j}))}{\det h}\cdot\frac{\det h}{\det k}
 \leq\Bigl(\frac{\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_hf}{n}\Bigr)^n\frac1{d_0}
 \leq M
\]
at $x_0$. Hence $a-f\leq\log M/\beta$ on $X$. The function $a-\log M/\beta$ is therefore a smooth $\kappa$-psh function below $f$, that is, a competitor for $P_\kappa(f)$, and so $a\leq P_\kappa(f)+\log M/\beta$.

\emph{The lower bound.} Put $v=P_\kappa(f)$; as noted at the beginning of this subsection, $\min_Xf\leq v\leq f$. For $0<t\leq1$ put
\[
 w=(1-t)v+t\min_Xf-\frac{n\log(1/t)}{\beta}.
\]
This is a bounded $\kappa$-psh function, and $\kappa+\ddc w=(1-t)(\kappa+\ddc v)+t\kappa$. Expanding the $n$th power by multilinearity of the Monge--Amp\`ere operator on bounded potentials and discarding the positive mixed terms, we get $(\kappa+\ddc w)^n\geq t^n\kappa^n$. On the other hand $w\leq f-n\log(1/t)/\beta$, so $\mathrm e^{\beta(w-f)}\leq t^n$. We claim that $w\leq a$. Let $E=\{a<w\}$. Since $a$ and $w$ are bounded, \cref{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison} applies without the correction terms, and together with the two inequalities just proved and the equation for $a$ it gives
\[
 \int_E\mathrm e^{\beta(w-f)}\kappa^n
 \leq\int_E(\kappa+\ddc w)^n
 \leq\int_E(\kappa+\ddc a)^n
 =\int_E\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n.
\]
On $E$ the first integrand is strictly larger than the last one, so $E$ has volume zero. Thus $w\leq a$ almost everywhere. Adding a smooth local potential of $\kappa$ to both functions, we obtain plurisubharmonic functions, and the ball-average formula \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} shows that $w\leq a$ everywhere. Now take $t=1/\beta$, which is allowed since $\beta\geq1$. Since $\min_Xf-v\geq\min_Xf-\max_Xf=-\operatorname{osc}_Xf$, we obtain
\[
 a\geq w=v+\frac{\min_Xf-v}{\beta}-\frac{n\log\beta}{\beta}
 \geq v-\frac{\operatorname{osc}_Xf+n\log\beta}{\beta}.
\]

\emph{The Laplacian bound.} Let $A$ and $\beta_0$ be as in \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}. For $\beta>\beta_0$ that lemma and $D_ha_\beta=H-\operatorname{tr}_hk$ with $H>0$ give
\[
 -\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\leq D_ha_\beta\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{2A\|f\|_\infty}.
\]
Since $a_\beta\to v$ uniformly, $D_ha_\beta\to D_hv$ in the sense of distributions, and the same two inequalities hold for the distribution $D_hv$. In a chart, $D_hv+\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk$ is therefore a positive distribution, hence a positive measure, dominated by a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure; by \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density} it is a bounded function. Hence $Lv=-4\pi D_hv$ is a bounded function, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$. Finally $v$ is continuous, as a uniform limit of continuous functions, so by \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} it belongs to $C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$.
\end{proof}
\index{envelope!penalized approximation}

We can now remove the smoothness of the obstacle and pass to finite minima.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}
Let $r\geq1$ be an integer, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be continuous real functions on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Then the envelope
\[
 u=P_\kappa\bigl(\min_{1\leq j\leq r}f_j\bigr)
\]
has bounded metric Laplacian, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, and on bounds for $\|f_j\|_\infty$ and $\|Lf_j\|_{L^\infty}$, $1\leq j\leq r$. In particular $u$ belongs to $C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$, and the positive current $\kappa+\ddc u$ has bounded coefficients. (For $r=1$ this is the corresponding statement for a single obstacle.)
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: smoothing the obstacles.} By \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp}, each $f_j$ belongs to $W^{2,p}(X)$ for every finite $p$; fix $p>2n$. Choose a finite smooth partition of unity $(\chi_\nu)$ with each $\chi_\nu$ supported in the interior of a coordinate chart. For small $\varepsilon>0$, let $f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$ be the convolution of $f_j$, written in the chart of $\chi_\nu$, with the mollifier $\rho_\varepsilon$; it is defined on a neighbourhood of the support of $\chi_\nu$. Put $f_{j,\varepsilon}=\sum_\nu\chi_\nu f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$. These are smooth functions with $\|f_{j,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\leq\|f_j\|_\infty$, and $f_{j,\varepsilon}\to f_j$ in $C^1$, because $f_j$ is of class $C^1$ by \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} and convolutions of a $C^1$ function converge to it in $C^1$ on compact sets.

We bound the Laplacians of the approximations. In the real coordinates underlying a holomorphic chart, $D_h=\sum_{\mu,\sigma}c^{\mu\sigma}(x)\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma$ with smooth real coefficients; there are no first-order terms. The commutator of $D_h$ with convolution is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &D_h(f_j*\rho_\varepsilon)(x)-\bigl((D_hf_j)*\rho_\varepsilon\bigr)(x)\\
 &\qquad=\int\sum_{\mu,\sigma}\bigl(c^{\mu\sigma}(x)-c^{\mu\sigma}(y)\bigr)
     \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma f_j(y)\,\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,\mathrm dy.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since $|c^{\mu\sigma}(x)-c^{\mu\sigma}(y)|\leq C\varepsilon$ on the support of $\rho_\varepsilon(x-\cdot)$ and $\|\rho_\varepsilon\|_{L^{p'}}=\varepsilon^{-2n/p}\|\rho\|_{L^{p'}}$ with $p'=p/(p-1)$, H\"older's inequality bounds this commutator by $C\varepsilon^{1-2n/p}\|f_j\|_{W^{2,p}}$, which tends to zero because $p>2n$. As $(D_hf_j)*\rho_\varepsilon$ lies between the essential infimum and the essential supremum of $D_hf_j$, we obtain on a neighbourhood of the support of $\chi_\nu$
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,inf}_XD_hf_j-o(1)\leq D_hf_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}
            \leq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_XD_hf_j+o(1)\qquad(\varepsilon\to0).
\]
Next, $D_hf_{j,\varepsilon}=\sum_\nu\chi_\nu D_hf_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}+\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu]f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$, where each commutator $[D_h,\chi_\nu]$ is a first-order operator. Since $\sum_\nu\chi_\nu=1$, we have $\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu]f_j=0$, so the second sum equals $\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu](f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}-f_j)$, which involves only $f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}-f_j$ and its first derivatives and tends to zero uniformly. Hence, for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, the smooth functions $f_{j,\varepsilon}$ satisfy $|D_hf_{j,\varepsilon}|\leq\|D_hf_j\|_{L^\infty}+1$ and $\|f_{j,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\leq\|f_j\|_\infty$.

\emph{Step 2: smoothing the minimum.} For $\delta>0$ set
\[
 F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
       =-\delta\log\sum_{j=1}^r
                    \mathrm e^{-f_{j,\varepsilon}/\delta},
 \qquad
 p_j=\frac{\mathrm e^{-f_{j,\varepsilon}/\delta}}
                   {\sum_l\mathrm e^{-f_{l,\varepsilon}/\delta}}.
\]
The weights $p_j$ are positive with $\sum_jp_j=1$, and $\min_jf_{j,\varepsilon}-\delta\log r\leq F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq\min_jf_{j,\varepsilon}$. A direct computation with the chain rule gives
\[
 D_hF_{\varepsilon,\delta}
 =\sum_jp_jD_hf_{j,\varepsilon}
 -\frac1\delta\left(\sum_jp_j|\partial f_{j,\varepsilon}|_h^2
                    -\left|\sum_jp_j\partial f_{j,\varepsilon}\right|_h^2\right)
 \leq\max_j\sup_XD_hf_{j,\varepsilon},
\]
where $|\xi|_h^2=h^{i\bar j}\xi_i\bar\xi_j$. The term in parentheses is nonnegative by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, as the variance of the vectors $\partial f_{j,\varepsilon}$ with respect to the probability weights $p_j$. Choose sequences $\varepsilon_k,\delta_k\to0$ with $\varepsilon_k$ small as in Step 1, and put $F_k=F_{\varepsilon_k,\delta_k}$. Then $F_k\to\min_jf_j$ uniformly on $X$, while $\|F_k\|_\infty\leq\max_j\|f_j\|_\infty+\delta_k\log r$ and $\sup_XD_hF_k\leq\max_j\|D_hf_j\|_{L^\infty}+1$ remain bounded.

\emph{Step 3: passage to the limit.} The proof of \cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit} shows that $-\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\leq D_hP_\kappa(F_k)\leq2B_k\mathrm e^{2A\|F_k\|_\infty}$ in the sense of distributions, where $A$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, and $B_k=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hF_k$. By Step 2 these bounds are uniform in $k$ and depend only on the asserted data. By the stability of envelopes in \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}, $\|P_\kappa(F_k)-u\|_\infty\leq\|F_k-\min_jf_j\|_\infty\to0$. Therefore $D_hP_\kappa(F_k)\to D_hu$ in the sense of distributions, and the same two-sided bound holds for $D_hu$. As in the proof of \cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}, \cref{lem:dominated-measure-density} shows that $D_hu$, and hence $Lu=-4\pi D_hu$, is a bounded function with the asserted bound. The function $u$ is continuous, as a uniform limit of the continuous functions $P_\kappa(F_k)$, so \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} shows that $u\in C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$. Finally, $\kappa+\ddc u$ is a positive current whose trace $\Lambda(\kappa+\ddc u)=\Lambda\kappa+D_hu$ is bounded, and, as in the proof of \cref{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}, its coefficients are bounded.
\end{proof}
