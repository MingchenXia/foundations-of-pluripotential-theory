\subsection{Penalized equations and metric-Laplacian envelopes}
\label{sec:penalized-kahler-envelopes}
We now show that, for a K\"ahler background, the envelope of the minimum of finitely many obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian again has bounded metric Laplacian (\cref{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}). Throughout this subsection $\kappa$ is a fixed K\"ahler form on $X$. It is the background of the envelopes, while $\omega$ remains the metric with respect to which the Laplacians of obstacles are measured. Constants are $\kappa$-psh, so for a continuous obstacle $f$ the envelope $P_\kappa(f)$ is a bounded $\kappa$-psh function with $\min_Xf\leq P_\kappa(f)\leq f$ everywhere, by \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}(1),(2). For smooth $f$ we approximate $P_\kappa(f)$ by the smooth solutions $a_\beta$ of the Monge--Amp\`ere equation with exponential penalty
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n,
\]
where $\beta>0$ is a large parameter. On the set where $a>f$ the right side is very large, which forces the solution to lie essentially below $f$; on the set where $a<f$ it is very small, just as the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the envelope vanishes off the contact set. As $\beta\to\infty$ the solutions converge uniformly to $P_\kappa(f)$, with an explicit rate (\cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}).

The main estimate is a bound for the Laplacian of $a_\beta$ that is uniform in $\beta$ and depends on the obstacle $f$ only through its supremum norm and an \emph{upper} bound for the trace $\Lambda\ddc f=-Lf/(4\pi)$ of its complex Hessian, that is, a \emph{lower} bound for its metric Laplacian (\cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}). This one-sided dependence is what makes finite minima accessible. The complex Hessian of $\min_jf_j$ can have a negative singular part on the set where two of the $f_j$ cross. For instance, in a chart, with $x_1=\Real z_1$ and $y_1=\Img z_1$, the function $\min\{x_1,-x_1\}=-|x_1|$ satisfies $\partial_{z_1}\partial_{\bar z_1}(-|x_1|)=-\frac12\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is Lebesgue measure on the hyperplane $\{x_1=0\}$: indeed $\partial_{z_1}\partial_{\bar z_1}=\frac14(\partial_{x_1}^2+\partial_{y_1}^2)$, integration by parts on the two half-lines gives $\int_{\mathbb R}|s|\chi''(s)\,\mathrm ds=2\chi(0)$ for every test function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$, and $\int_{\mathbb R}\partial_{y_1}^2\chi\,\mathrm dy_1=0$. But the smooth approximations $-\delta\log\sum_j\mathrm e^{-f_j/\delta}$ of the minimum have complex Hessians whose traces are uniformly bounded above (\cref{lem:soft-minimum-trace}).

We solve the penalized equation by the continuity method (\cref{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}). Besides the trace bound, the a priori estimates require control of the third derivatives of the solutions. We obtain it from Calabi's maximum principle for the squared norm of the difference of two Chern connections (\cref{lem:penalized-third-order}). The higher derivatives, and the smoothness of the solutions produced by perturbation, are then controlled by the elliptic $L^p$ estimates of \cref{sec:metric-laplacian-obstacles} (\cref{lem:penalized-local-regularity}).

In local holomorphic coordinates we put $\partial_i=\partial_{z_i}$ and $\partial_{\bar j}=\partial_{\bar z_j}$, sum over repeated indices, and write
\[
 \omega=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}h_{i\bar j}\,
                 \mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}k_{i\bar j}\,
                 \mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j,
\]
so that $h=(h_{i\bar j})$ and $k=(k_{i\bar j})$ are the coefficient matrices of $2\pi\omega$ and $2\pi\kappa$. As in \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}, let $(h^{i\bar j})$ be the inverse matrix of $h$, normalized by $h^{i\bar j}h_{l\bar j}=\delta_{il}$ (then also $h^{j\bar i}h_{j\bar l}=\delta_{il}$), and for a Hermitian matrix $m=(m_{i\bar j})$ put $\operatorname{tr}_hm=h^{i\bar j}m_{i\bar j}$. For a real function $a$ write $a_{i\bar j}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}a$. The operator $D_h=h^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}$ is the operator $D_g$ of \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace} for the K\"ahler form $\omega'=\omega$, so that
\[
 D_ha=h^{i\bar j}a_{i\bar j}=\Lambda\ddc a=-\frac{La}{4\pi}.
\]
Hence $D_h$ is the metric Laplacian up to the factor $-1/(4\pi)$; in particular, an upper bound for $D_hf$ is a lower bound for $Lf$. Since $X$ is compact, the norms defined by $h$ and by the metric of $\omega$ differ by fixed factors, and there is $\gamma>0$ with $k\geq\gamma h$. For a smooth real function $a$ put $g_{i\bar j}=k_{i\bar j}+a_{i\bar j}$, so that $\kappa+\ddc a=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}g_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$. When $g>0$, that is, when $\kappa+\ddc a$ is a K\"ahler form, we define $D_g=g^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}$ and $\operatorname{tr}_gm=g^{i\bar j}m_{i\bar j}$ in the same way; by \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}, $D_g=-L_{\kappa+\ddc a}/(4\pi)$. We write $|\xi|_g^2=g^{i\bar j}\xi_i\bar\xi_j$ for a $(1,0)$-form $\xi=\xi_i\,\mathrm dz_i$, and set
\[
 H=\operatorname{tr}_hg=\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_ha.
\]
The \emph{eigenvalues of $g$ relative to $h$} are the eigenvalues of the matrix $h^{-1}g$; they are positive, their sum is $H$, the sum of their inverses is $\operatorname{tr}_gh$, and their product is $\det g/\det h$. Since $\omega$ is an arbitrary K\"ahler form on $X$, everything in this subsection holds with $\omega$ replaced by any K\"ahler form, for instance by $\kappa$.

Under a holomorphic change of coordinates $z=z(w)$ with Jacobian matrix $J=(\partial z_a/\partial w_b)$, the coefficient matrices change by $k\mapsto J^{\mathrm T}k\bar J$, so $\det k$ is multiplied by $|\det J|^2$, and $\log\det k$ changes by the function $\log|\det J|^2$. This function is pluriharmonic: for a nonvanishing holomorphic function $F$, $\partial_{\bar j}\log|F|^2=\overline{\partial_jF}/\bar F$ is antiholomorphic, so $\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log|F|^2=0$. Consequently the ratios $\det k/\det h$ and $\det g/\det k$ are smooth positive functions on $X$, the functions $D_h\log\det k$ and $D_h\log\det g$ do not depend on the coordinates, and the functions $\operatorname{Ric}(k)_{i\bar j}=-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det k$ are the coefficients of a well-defined smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\operatorname{Ric}(k)_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ on $X$. Recall from \cref{sec:positive-currents} that $\omega_A^n=\det A\,\omega_I^n$ for every Hermitian $n\times n$ matrix $A$, where $\omega_A=\mathrm i\sum_{i,j}A_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$; since $\kappa+\ddc a=\omega_{g/2\pi}$ and $\kappa=\omega_{k/2\pi}$, it follows that $(\kappa+\ddc a)^n/\kappa^n=\det g/\det k$. We use Jacobi's formula $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\tau}\log\det M(\tau)=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}M')$ for a differentiable family of matrices with positive determinant, which follows from $\det(M+\epsilon N)=\det M\det(I+\epsilon M^{-1}N)$ and $\det(I+\epsilon N')=1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}N'+O(\epsilon^2)$; with our normalization of the inverse matrix it reads $\partial\log\det g=g^{i\bar j}\partial g_{i\bar j}$ for every first-order derivative $\partial$. Moreover, if a smooth real function $b$ has a local maximum at a point $x$, then its complex Hessian is negative semidefinite at $x$: for $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ the function $s\mapsto b(x+s\xi)$ of one complex variable has a local maximum at $s=0$, so its Laplacian $4b_{i\bar j}\xi_i\bar\xi_j$ is nonpositive there. Consequently $D_gb\leq0$ at $x$, since the trace of the product of a positive definite and a negative semidefinite Hermitian matrix is nonpositive.

Finally $\operatorname{osc}_Xf=\max_Xf-\min_Xf$ denotes the oscillation of a continuous function $f$. For smooth $f$, Stokes' formula gives $\int_XD_hf\,\mathrm dV=-(4\pi)^{-1}\int_XLf\,\mathrm dV=-(4\pi)^{-1}\int_X\langle\mathrm df,\mathrm d1\rangle\,\mathrm dV=0$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}
 \sup_XD_hf\geq0.
\end{equation}

The maximum principle arguments below are carried out at a point, in holomorphic coordinates centered there in which the coefficient matrix of a K\"ahler form equals the identity matrix at the point and has vanishing first derivatives there. Such coordinates exist at every point by \cref{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates}; that lemma writes the form as $\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_{k,l}\tilde g_{k\bar l}\,\mathrm dw_k\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_l$, and replacing the coordinates $w$ by $\sqrt\pi\,w$ converts this into the normalization $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}$ of this subsection. Our estimates must, however, be uniform in the point, so we use the following explicit choice, for which all derivatives of the coefficients at the center are bounded uniformly.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates-uniform}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open, and let $e=(e_{i\bar j})$ be a smooth function on $U$ with values in the positive definite Hermitian matrices such that the form $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}e_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ is closed. For every $z_0\in U$ there is a polynomial map $\Phi_{z_0}\colon\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^n$ of degree at most two, with $\Phi_{z_0}(0)=z_0$, which is a biholomorphism from a neighborhood of $0$ onto a neighborhood of $z_0$, such that the coefficient matrix $\tilde e$ of the form in the coordinates $w$ given by $z=\Phi_{z_0}(w)$ satisfies $\tilde e(0)=I$ and $\mathrm d\tilde e(0)=0$. For every compact set $K\subset U$ and every integer $m\geq0$, the derivatives of order at most $m$ of $\tilde e$ at $0$ are bounded independently of $z_0\in K$. These properties persist if $\Phi_{z_0}$ is composed with a unitary linear map.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The form is closed if and only if its $(2,1)$-part $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial_le_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_l\wedge\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ and the conjugate $(1,2)$-part vanish, that is, if and only if $\partial_le_{i\bar j}=\partial_ie_{l\bar j}$; this holds in all holomorphic coordinates. If $z=\Phi(w)$ with $\Phi$ holomorphic and $J=D\Phi$ its complex Jacobian matrix, then $\mathrm dz_i=J_{ia}\,\mathrm dw_a$, so the new coefficients are $\tilde e(w)=J(w)^{\mathrm T}e(\Phi(w))\overline{J(w)}$.

Let $A=\overline{e(z_0)^{-1/2}}$, where $e(z_0)^{-1/2}$ is the positive square root of the inverse matrix; its operator norm is $m(z_0)^{-1/2}$, where $m(z)=\min_{|\xi|=1}\sum_{i,j}e_{i\bar j}(z)\xi_i\bar\xi_j$ is the smallest eigenvalue of $e(z)$, and $A^{\mathrm T}e(z_0)\bar A=e(z_0)^{-1/2}e(z_0)e(z_0)^{-1/2}=I$. In the coordinates $v$ with $z=z_0+Av$ the coefficient matrix $e'$ satisfies $e'(0)=I$. Put $c^m_{ij}=\partial_{v_i}e'_{j\bar m}(0)$, which is symmetric in $i$ and $j$ by the closedness, and define $v=w-\frac12c(w,w)$, that is, $v_m=w_m-\frac12c^m_{ij}w_iw_j$. Then $J_{ia}=\partial v_i/\partial w_a=\delta_{ia}-c^i_{aj}w_j$, so $J(0)=I$ and $\partial_{w_p}J_{ia}(0)=-c^i_{ap}$. Differentiating $\tilde e_{a\bar b}=J_{ia}e'_{i\bar j}(v(w))\overline{J_{jb}}$ at $w=0$, and using that $\overline{J_{jb}}$ and $\bar v$ are antiholomorphic in $w$, we find
\[
 \partial_{w_p}\tilde e_{a\bar b}(0)=-c^b_{ap}+\partial_{v_p}e'_{a\bar b}(0)=-\partial_{v_a}e'_{p\bar b}(0)+\partial_{v_p}e'_{a\bar b}(0)=0,
\]
by the closedness, and $\partial_{\bar w_p}\tilde e_{a\bar b}(0)=\overline{\partial_{w_p}\tilde e_{b\bar a}(0)}=0$. Put $\Phi_{z_0}(w)=z_0+A(w-\frac12c(w,w))$. Its derivative at $0$ is the invertible matrix $A$, so it is a biholomorphism near $0$ by the holomorphic inverse function theorem (\cref{prop:holomorphic-inverse-function}(1)).

By the chain rule, the derivatives of $\tilde e$ at $0$ are polynomials in the derivatives of $e$ at $z_0$ and in the entries of $A$, $\bar A$ and $c$, and the entries of $c$ are polynomials in the first derivatives of $e$ at $z_0$ and in the entries of $A$ and $\bar A$. The derivatives of $e$ are bounded on compact sets, and so are the entries of $A$, because the function $m$ is continuous and positive, as a minimum over the unit sphere of a function that is continuous in $(z,\xi)$; hence these polynomials are bounded for $z_0\in K$. Finally, if $Q$ is unitary, replacing $\Phi_{z_0}(w)$ by $\Phi_{z_0}(Qw)$ replaces $\tilde e(w)$ by $Q^{\mathrm T}\tilde e(Qw)\bar Q$, which equals $Q^{\mathrm T}\bar Q=\overline{Q^*Q}=I$ at $0$, still has vanishing differential at $0$, and has derivatives at $0$ bounded by a constant depending only on $n$ times those of $\tilde e$.
\end{proof}
\index{normal coordinates of a K\"ahler form}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kahler-trace-laplacian}
There is a constant $C_E$, depending only on $X$ and $\omega$, with the following property. Let $a$ be a smooth real function on $X$ with $\kappa+\ddc a>0$, let $g=(k_{i\bar j}+a_{i\bar j})$ be the coefficient matrix of $2\pi(\kappa+\ddc a)$, and put $H=\operatorname{tr}_hg$. Then $H\operatorname{tr}_gh\geq n^2$ on $X$. Moreover, every $x\in X$ is the center of holomorphic coordinates in which $h_{i\bar j}=\delta_{ij}$ and all first derivatives of $h$ vanish at $x$, and $g$ is diagonal at $x$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n>0$, such that at $x$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-trace-laplacian}
 D_gH=D_h\log\det g
       +\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_q}+E,
 \qquad |E|\leq C_EH\operatorname{tr}_gh,
\end{equation}
and
\[
 D_g\log H\geq\frac1H\bigl(D_h\log\det g+E\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At every point the eigenvalues of $g$ relative to $h$ are positive, their sum is $H$ and the sum of their inverses is $\operatorname{tr}_gh$, so the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $H\operatorname{tr}_gh\geq n^2$.

Fix finitely many charts and compact subsets of their domains whose union is $X$, and let $x\in X$. By \cref{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates-uniform}, applied to $h$ in one of these charts, followed by a unitary change of coordinates that diagonalizes $g$ at $x$, there are holomorphic coordinates centered at $x$ in which $h_{i\bar j}=\delta_{ij}$ and all first derivatives of $h$ vanish at $x$, $g$ is diagonal at $x$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n>0$, and the derivatives of order at most two of $h$ at $x$ are bounded by a constant depending only on $X$ and $\omega$. All the following computations are at $x$, in these coordinates. Locally $\kappa$ and $\kappa+\ddc a$ have K\"ahler potentials (\cref{lem:smooth-potential-on-ball}): $g_{i\bar j}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\varphi$ with $\varphi$ smooth. Hence the derivatives $\partial_pg_{i\bar q}$ are symmetric in $p$ and $i$, and the second derivatives $\partial_p\partial_{\bar l}g_{i\bar q}$ are symmetric in $p,i$ and in $\bar l,\bar q$. Differentiating $H=h^{i\bar q}g_{i\bar q}$ twice, and using that the first derivatives of $h$, hence of $h^{-1}$, vanish at $x$, we get
\[
 D_gH=\sum_p\lambda_p^{-1}\sum_i\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}+E,
 \qquad E=\sum_p\lambda_p^{-1}\bigl(\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}h^{i\bar q}\bigr)g_{i\bar q}.
\]
Since $g$ is diagonal with positive eigenvalues, $|E|\leq C_EH\operatorname{tr}_gh$, where $C_E$ bounds the second derivatives of $h^{-1}$ at $x$. As $h=I$ and $\mathrm dh=0$ at $x$, the formula $\partial(h^{-1})=-h^{-1}(\partial h)h^{-1}$ shows that these are the second derivatives of $-h$, up to transposition; so $C_E$ depends only on $X$ and $\omega$. On the other hand, differentiating the identity $\partial_p\log\det g=g^{i\bar q}\partial_pg_{i\bar q}$ and using $\partial(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}(\partial g)g^{-1}$ gives
\[
 \partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\log\det g
 =\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1}\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}
  -\sum_{i,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_q}.
\]
By the symmetry of the second derivatives, $\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar i}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar i}g_{p\bar p}$. Summing over $p$, comparing with the formula for $D_gH$, and noting that $\sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}=D_h$ at $x$, we obtain the identity \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-laplacian}, with the bound for $E$ just proved. Moreover $\partial_pH=\sum_i\partial_pg_{i\bar i}=\sum_i\partial_ig_{p\bar i}$ by the symmetry of the first derivatives. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality with weights $\lambda_i$ therefore gives
\[
 |\partial H|_g^2
 =\sum_p\frac1{\lambda_p}\Bigl|\sum_i\partial_ig_{p\bar i}\Bigr|^2
 \leq H\sum_{p,i}\frac{|\partial_ig_{p\bar i}|^2}
                           {\lambda_p\lambda_i}
 \leq H\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}
                           {\lambda_i\lambda_q},
\]
the last sum containing the previous one (take $p=q$ in the term of index $(i,p,q)$ and rename). Since $D_g\log H=H^{-1}D_gH-H^{-2}|\partial H|_g^2$, the quadratic term in $D_gH$ absorbs the negative term, and
\[
 D_g\log H\geq\frac1H\bigl(D_h\log\det g+E\bigr).
\]
\end{proof}
\index{K\"ahler form!Laplacian of a trace}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:penalized-equation-trace}
Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$ and $\beta>0$, and let $a$ be a smooth real function satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-kahler-equation}
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n,
 \qquad \kappa+\ddc a>0.
\end{equation}
Then $\min_Xf\leq a\leq\max_Xf$, and the metrics $g$ and $h$ are uniformly equivalent: $C^{-1}h\leq g\leq Ch$ on $X$, with $C$ depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$ and upper bounds for $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$. Moreover, there is $\beta_0>0$, depending only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$, such that for $\beta>\beta_0$
\[
 H\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{A\operatorname{osc}_Xf}\quad\text{on }X,
 \qquad B=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hf,
\]
where $A>0$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. In particular, for $\beta>\beta_0$ the trace $H$ is bounded by a constant depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$ and upper bounds for $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the value bounds.} Since $(\kappa+\ddc a)^n/\kappa^n=\det g/\det k$, the equation says that $\det g=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\det k$. At a maximum point of $a$ the complex Hessian $(a_{i\bar j})$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<g\leq k$ and therefore $\det g\leq\det k$ there, the eigenvalues of $k^{-1/2}gk^{-1/2}$ lying in $(0,1]$. Hence $a\leq f\leq\max_Xf$ at that point, and therefore everywhere. At a minimum point of $a$ the inequalities are reversed, and $a\geq\min_Xf$.

\emph{Step 2: a differential inequality for $\log H$.} Let $x\in X$, and take the coordinates of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-laplacian} at $x$. By that lemma, at $x$
\[
 D_g\log H\geq\frac1H\bigl(D_h\log\det g+E\bigr),\qquad |E|\leq C_EH\operatorname{tr}_gh,
\]
and $H\operatorname{tr}_gh\geq n^2$. Now we use the equation in the form $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-f)$, valid in every chart. Since $D_ha=H-\operatorname{tr}_hk$,
\[
 D_h\log\det g
 =D_h\log\det k+\beta\bigl(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk-D_hf\bigr).
\]
The function $D_h\log\det k$ is a smooth function on $X$, so it is bounded by a constant depending only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. Dividing by $H$ and using $H^{-1}\leq\operatorname{tr}_gh/n^2$ and $|E|/H\leq C_E\operatorname{tr}_gh$, we arrive at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-log-trace}
 D_g\log H\geq\beta\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{tr}_h k+D_hf}{H}\right)
                                  -C_0\operatorname{tr}_g h,
\end{equation}
where $C_0$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. Both sides are independent of the coordinates, so \eqref{eq:penalized-log-trace} holds everywhere on $X$.

\emph{Step 3: the maximum principle.} Let $B'$ be a constant with $B'\geq B$. By \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, $B>0$, and $\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_hf\leq B'$ on $X$. For a constant $A>0$, to be chosen, let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $Q=\log H-Aa$. Since
\[
 D_ga=g^{i\bar j}(g_{i\bar j}-k_{i\bar j})=n-\operatorname{tr}_gk
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{tr}_gk\geq\gamma\operatorname{tr}_gh,
\]
inequality \eqref{eq:penalized-log-trace} gives at $x_0$, where the complex Hessian of $Q$ is negative semidefinite,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-trace-maximum}
 0\geq D_gQ\geq\beta\left(1-\frac{B'}H\right)
             +(A\gamma-C_0)\operatorname{tr}_g h-An.
\end{equation}

First let $\beta$ be fixed, let $M\geq\|f\|_\infty$ and $N\geq\sup_XD_hf$ be given, take $B'=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+N$, and $A=(C_0+\beta B'/n^2+1)/\gamma$. Since $1/H\leq\operatorname{tr}_gh/n^2$, the right side of \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-maximum} is at least $\beta+\operatorname{tr}_gh-An$, so $\operatorname{tr}_gh\leq An$ at $x_0$. Thus every eigenvalue of $g$ relative to $h$ is at least $(An)^{-1}$ at $x_0$. By Step 1, $|a-f|\leq\operatorname{osc}_Xf\leq2M$, and therefore, by the equation, the product of the eigenvalues satisfies
\[
 \Bigl(\inf_X\frac{\det k}{\det h}\Bigr)\mathrm e^{-2\beta M}
 \leq\frac{\det g}{\det h}=\frac{\det k}{\det h}\,\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}
 \leq\Bigl(\sup_X\frac{\det k}{\det h}\Bigr)\mathrm e^{2\beta M}
\]
everywhere on $X$. Together with the lower bound for the eigenvalues at $x_0$, the upper bound for their product bounds each eigenvalue above at $x_0$, and hence bounds $H(x_0)$. For every $x\in X$, since $Q(x)\leq Q(x_0)$ and $\min_Xf\leq a\leq\max_Xf$,
\[
 \log H(x)\leq\log H(x_0)+A\bigl(a(x)-a(x_0)\bigr)
 \leq\log H(x_0)+A\operatorname{osc}_Xf.
\]
Thus $H$ is bounded on $X$, so every eigenvalue of $g$ relative to $h$ is bounded above on $X$, and the lower bound for their product then bounds every eigenvalue below. All the constants depend only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$, $M$ and $N$, which proves the uniform equivalence of $g$ and $h$.

Now take $B'=B$, $A=(C_0+1)/\gamma$ and $\beta_0=2An$; both $A$ and $\beta_0$ depend only on $X$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$. Suppose that $\beta>\beta_0$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $Q=\log H-Aa$ for this $A$. If $H(x_0)>2B$, then $1-B/H>1/2$ at $x_0$, and the right side of \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-maximum} is at least $\beta/2+\operatorname{tr}_gh-An>0$, a contradiction. Hence $H(x_0)\leq2B$, and the same argument as above gives
\[
 H\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{A\operatorname{osc}_X f}\quad\text{on }X.
\]
Since $\operatorname{osc}_Xf\leq2\|f\|_\infty$ and $B\leq\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+N$, this proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere equation!trace estimate for an exponential penalty}

The third-order estimate uses a little tensor calculus, which we now set up. Let $z=z(w)$ be a holomorphic change of coordinates on an open subset of $X$, with Jacobian matrix $J=(J_{ab})$, $J_{ab}=\partial z_a/\partial w_b$. A \emph{tensor} is given, in every holomorphic chart, by functions $T^{m_1\cdots m_r\bar n_1\cdots\bar n_{r'}}_{i_1\cdots i_s\bar j_1\cdots\bar j_{s'}}$ of upper and lower, holomorphic and antiholomorphic indices in $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, whose components in the coordinates $w$ are obtained from those in $z$ by contracting each upper holomorphic index $m$ with $(J^{-1})_{m'm}$, each lower holomorphic index $i$ with $J_{ii'}$, and each upper or lower antiholomorphic index with the complex conjugate of the corresponding factor; for instance $T'^{m'}_{i'\bar j'}=(J^{-1})_{m'm}J_{ii'}\overline{J_{jj'}}\,T^m_{i\bar j}$. Examples are the first derivatives $\partial_iu$ of a function, the matrices $h_{i\bar j}$, $k_{i\bar j}$, $g_{i\bar j}$, the complex Hessian $u_{i\bar j}$ of a function and $\operatorname{Ric}(k)_{i\bar j}$ (all with lower indices, since $\mathrm dz_i=J_{ii'}\,\mathrm dw_{i'}$), the inverse matrices $h^{i\bar j}$, $g^{i\bar j}$ (with upper indices), and the identity $\delta^i_j$. The complex conjugate of a tensor is a tensor, with the roles of holomorphic and antiholomorphic indices exchanged; products of tensors are tensors, and so are contractions of an upper with a lower index of the same kind. For a Hermitian metric $g$ and a tensor $T$, the pointwise norm $|T|_g$ is defined by $|T|_g^2=$ the complete contraction of $T$ with $\overline T$ in which the lower indices are paired by $g^{\cdot\bar\cdot}$ and the upper ones by $g_{\cdot\bar\cdot}$; for instance $|T|_g^2=g^{i\bar a}g_{m\bar c}T^m_i\overline{T^c_a}$. At a point, in coordinates in which $g_{i\bar j}=\delta_{ij}$ there, $|T|_g^2$ is the sum of the squared moduli of the components. Hence the contraction of one index of $T$ with one index of $U$ satisfies $|T\cdot U|_g\leq|T|_g|U|_g$, contraction of two indices of $T$ with $g^{p\bar q}$ or $g_{p\bar q}$ changes the norm at most by the factor $\sqrt n$, and, diagonalizing $g$ and $h$ simultaneously, $C^{-1}h\leq g\leq Ch$ implies $|T|_g\leq C^{N/2}|T|_h$ for a tensor with $N$ indices.

Let $g$ be the coefficient matrix of $2\pi\eta$, where $\eta$ is a K\"ahler form on an open subset of $X$. Its \emph{Chern connection}\index{Chern connection} has the coefficients
\[
 \Gamma^m_{ij}=g^{m\bar l}\partial_ig_{j\bar l},
\]
which are symmetric in $i$ and $j$ because $\partial_ig_{j\bar l}=\partial_jg_{i\bar l}$. For a tensor $T$, the covariant derivative $\nabla_pT$ is obtained from $\partial_pT$ by adding, for each upper holomorphic index $m$, the term $\Gamma^m_{pa}$ contracted with $T$ in that index, and subtracting, for each lower holomorphic index $i$, the term $\Gamma^a_{pi}$ contracted with $T$ in that index; antiholomorphic indices receive no term. For instance $\nabla_pT^m_{i\bar j}=\partial_pT^m_{i\bar j}+\Gamma^m_{pa}T^a_{i\bar j}-\Gamma^a_{pi}T^m_{a\bar j}$. We put $\nabla_{\bar q}T=\overline{\nabla_q\overline T}$; thus $\nabla_{\bar q}$ acts by $\partial_{\bar q}$ and conjugate terms for the antiholomorphic indices only. We write $\widehat\Gamma$, $\widehat\nabla$ for the Chern connection of $h$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:chern-connection-identities}
Let $g$ be as above, on an open set $O\subset X$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T$ is a tensor, then $\nabla_pT$ and $\nabla_{\bar q}T$ are tensors, with one more lower index $p$, respectively $\bar q$. The covariant derivatives satisfy the Leibniz rule for products, they commute with contractions, and $\nabla g=0$, $\nabla g^{-1}=0$. Consequently, if $S$ is a complete contraction of a product of tensors, then $\partial_pS$ is the sum of the contractions in which one factor is replaced by its covariant derivative $\nabla_p$, and similarly for $\partial_{\bar q}$.
\item The functions $\Psi^m_{ij}=\Gamma^m_{ij}-\widehat\Gamma^m_{ij}$ form a tensor, and $\Psi^m_{ij}=g^{m\bar l}\widehat\nabla_ig_{j\bar l}$.
\item The functions $R^m{}_{i\bar qj}=-\partial_{\bar q}\Gamma^m_{ij}$ form a tensor, symmetric in $i$ and $j$. The \emph{Ricci tensor}\index{Ricci tensor} $R_{i\bar j}=R^m{}_{i\bar jm}$ equals $-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det g$, and with $R_i{}^m=g^{m\bar l}R_{i\bar l}$ we have
\[
 g^{p\bar q}R^m{}_{i\bar qp}=R_i{}^m,
 \qquad
 g^{p\bar q}\nabla_pR^m{}_{i\bar qj}=\nabla_iR_j{}^m.
\]
\item If $T$ has only holomorphic indices, then $\nabla_{\bar q}T=\partial_{\bar q}T$, and, for instance for $T=(T^m_{ij})$,
\[
 (\nabla_{\bar q}\nabla_p-\nabla_p\nabla_{\bar q})T^m_{ij}
 =-R^m{}_{a\bar qp}T^a_{ij}+R^a{}_{i\bar qp}T^m_{aj}+R^a{}_{j\bar qp}T^m_{ia}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) In the coordinates $w$ the metric and its inverse have the components
\[
 g'_{a\bar b}=J_{ia}g_{i\bar j}\overline{J_{jb}},\qquad g'^{d\bar b}=(J^{-1})_{dm}\overline{(J^{-1})_{bl}}g^{m\bar l}.
\]
By these rules and the chain rule $\partial_{w_c}(F\circ z)=J_{kc}\,(\partial_{z_k}F)\circ z$, which holds because $\partial_{w_c}\bar z_k=0$, we have $\partial_{w_c}g'_{a\bar b}=(\partial_{w_c}J_{ia})g_{i\bar j}\overline{J_{jb}}+J_{ia}J_{kc}\,\partial_kg_{i\bar j}\,\overline{J_{jb}}$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-symbol-change}
 \Gamma'^d_{ca}=(J^{-1})_{dm}J_{kc}J_{ia}\Gamma^m_{ki}+(J^{-1})_{dm}\partial_{w_c}J_{ma}.
\end{equation}
Let $T$ be a tensor, and differentiate its transformation rule by $\partial_{w_c}$. By the product rule, this gives the transformed array $J_{kc}\partial_kT$, plus one term for each holomorphic index, in which $\partial_{w_c}$ falls on the Jacobian factor of that index; the factors of the antiholomorphic indices are antiholomorphic and are not differentiated. For an upper holomorphic index, the term contains $\partial_{w_c}(J^{-1})_{dm}=-(J^{-1})_{dm'}(\partial_{w_c}J_{m'b})(J^{-1})_{bm}$, and it cancels against the contribution of the second term of \eqref{eq:chern-symbol-change} to $\Gamma'^d_{cb}T'^{\cdots b\cdots}$; for a lower holomorphic index $a$, the term contains $\partial_{w_c}J_{ia}$, and it cancels against the contribution of the second term of \eqref{eq:chern-symbol-change} to $-\Gamma'^b_{ca}T'_{\cdots b\cdots}$. The contributions of the first term of \eqref{eq:chern-symbol-change} are the transforms of the connection terms of $T$. Hence $\nabla_cT'$ is the transform of $\nabla_kT$, contracted with $J_{kc}$, so $\nabla_pT$ is a tensor; by conjugation, so is $\nabla_{\bar q}T$. The Leibniz rule follows from the product rule for $\partial_p$ and the additivity of the connection terms over the indices. If an upper and a lower holomorphic index of $T$ are contracted, their connection terms $\Gamma^m_{pa}T^{a\cdots}{}_{\cdots m}$ and $-\Gamma^a_{pm}T^{m\cdots}{}_{\cdots a}$ cancel, so $\nabla$ commutes with contractions. Next,
\[
 \nabla_pg_{i\bar j}=\partial_pg_{i\bar j}-\Gamma^a_{pi}g_{a\bar j}=\partial_pg_{i\bar j}-g^{a\bar l}\partial_pg_{i\bar l}\,g_{a\bar j}=0,
\]
because $g^{a\bar l}g_{a\bar j}=\delta_{lj}$, and $\nabla_{\bar q}g_{i\bar j}=\overline{\nabla_qg_{j\bar i}}=0$, since $\overline{g_{i\bar j}}=g_{j\bar i}$. The connection terms of the identity tensor cancel, so $\nabla\delta=0$, and the Leibniz rule applied to $g^{i\bar j}g_{l\bar j}=\delta^i_l$ gives $(\nabla g^{i\bar j})g_{l\bar j}=0$, hence $\nabla g^{-1}=0$. For a complete contraction $S$, a function, $\nabla_pS=\partial_pS$, and the last assertion follows from the Leibniz rule and the commutation with contractions.

(2) By \eqref{eq:chern-symbol-change} for $g$ and for $h$, the inhomogeneous terms of $\Gamma'$ and $\widehat\Gamma'$ coincide, so the difference transforms as a tensor. Moreover $\widehat\nabla_ig_{j\bar l}=\partial_ig_{j\bar l}-\widehat\Gamma^p_{ij}g_{p\bar l}$, and contracting with $g^{m\bar l}$ gives $\Gamma^m_{ij}-\widehat\Gamma^m_{ij}$.

(3) The second term of \eqref{eq:chern-symbol-change} is holomorphic in $w$, so it is annihilated by $\partial_{\bar w_e}=\overline{J_{le}}\,\partial_{\bar z_l}$, and $R$ transforms as a tensor; it is symmetric in $i$ and $j$ because $\Gamma$ is. Since $\Gamma^m_{im}=g^{m\bar l}\partial_ig_{m\bar l}=\partial_i\log\det g$, we get $R_{i\bar j}=-\partial_{\bar j}\partial_i\log\det g$. Both sides of each of the two identities are tensors, so it suffices to prove them at a point $x$ in coordinates in which $g_{i\bar j}(x)=\delta_{ij}$ and $\mathrm dg(x)=0$, which exist by \cref{lem:kahler-normal-coordinates-uniform}. There $\Gamma=0$, so covariant derivatives at $x$ are ordinary derivatives, and $\partial g^{-1}=-g^{-1}(\partial g)g^{-1}$ and $\bar\partial g^{-1}$ vanish at $x$. Using a local K\"ahler potential $\varphi$, $g_{i\bar j}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\varphi$, whose derivatives commute, we find at $x$
\[
 g^{p\bar q}R^m{}_{i\bar qp}=-\sum_p\partial_i\partial_{\bar p}g_{p\bar m}=-\sum_p\partial_i\partial_{\bar m}g_{p\bar p}=R_i{}^m,
\]
and
\[
 g^{p\bar q}\nabla_pR^m{}_{i\bar qj}=-\sum_p\partial_p\partial_{\bar p}\partial_ig_{j\bar m}
 =-\sum_p\partial_i\partial_j\partial_{\bar m}g_{p\bar p}=\nabla_iR_j{}^m;
\]
in each case all terms containing a first derivative of $g$ or of $g^{-1}$ vanish at $x$.

(4) A tensor with only holomorphic indices receives no connection term from $\nabla_{\bar q}$, so $\nabla_{\bar q}T=\partial_{\bar q}T$. The tensor $\nabla_pT$ again has only holomorphic indices, so $\nabla_{\bar q}\nabla_pT^m_{ij}$ is the $\partial_{\bar q}$-derivative of $\partial_pT^m_{ij}+\Gamma^m_{pa}T^a_{ij}-\Gamma^a_{pi}T^m_{aj}-\Gamma^a_{pj}T^m_{ia}$. On the other hand, $\nabla_{\bar q}T=\partial_{\bar q}T$ has the additional antiholomorphic index $\bar q$, which receives no term from $\nabla_p$, so $\nabla_p\nabla_{\bar q}T^m_{ij}=\partial_p\partial_{\bar q}T^m_{ij}+\Gamma^m_{pa}\partial_{\bar q}T^a_{ij}-\Gamma^a_{pi}\partial_{\bar q}T^m_{aj}-\Gamma^a_{pj}\partial_{\bar q}T^m_{ia}$. The difference consists of the terms in which $\partial_{\bar q}$ falls on $\Gamma$; since $\partial_{\bar q}\Gamma^m_{pa}=-R^m{}_{a\bar qp}$ by the symmetry of $R$, this is the asserted formula.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern connection!curvature identities}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:penalized-third-order}
Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, let $\beta\in\mathbb R$ and $0\leq t\leq1$, and let $a$ be a smooth real function with $\kappa+\ddc a>0$ and
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-tf)}\kappa^n.
\]
Suppose that $C_0^{-1}h\leq g\leq C_0h$ on $X$ for a constant $C_0\geq1$, and let $\Psi$ be the tensor of \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(2) for $g$. Then
\[
 S=|\Psi|_g^2=g^{i\bar p}g^{j\bar q}g_{m\bar l}\,\Psi^m_{ij}\overline{\Psi^l_{pq}}\leq C,
\]
where $C$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$, $C_0$ and $f$, but not on $t$ or $a$. Consequently, in each chart of a fixed finite atlas, the first derivatives of the functions $g_{i\bar j}$ are bounded on every compact subset by a constant of the same kind.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In this proof $C,C_1,C_2,\ldots$ denote constants depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$, $C_0$ and $f$. By \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(1),(2), $S$ is a smooth function on $X$. The fixed smooth tensors $\widehat R$ (the tensor of \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(3) for $h$), $\widehat\nabla\widehat R$, $\tau=\operatorname{Ric}(k)+\beta k+\beta t(f_{i\bar j})$ and $\widehat\nabla\tau$ have $h$-norms bounded on $X$ independently of $t\in[0,1]$, being continuous functions on the compact manifold $X$ that depend affinely on $t$; by the assumption on $g$, their $g$-norms are bounded as well.

\emph{The Laplacian of $\Psi$.} Let $R$ be the tensor of \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(3) for $g$. Since $\Psi$ has only holomorphic indices,
\[
 \nabla_{\bar q}\Psi^m_{ij}=\partial_{\bar q}\Psi^m_{ij}=\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}-R^m{}_{i\bar qj}.
\]
Put $D_gT=g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p\nabla_{\bar q}T$ and $\overline D_gT=g^{p\bar q}\nabla_{\bar q}\nabla_pT$ for tensors $T$. On a function $u$ both operators reduce to $D_gu=g^{p\bar q}\partial_p\partial_{\bar q}u$, since $\nabla_p$ adds no term to the antiholomorphic index of $\partial_{\bar q}u$, and $\nabla_{\bar q}$ none to the holomorphic index of $\partial_pu$. Applying $g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p$ to the last identity and using $\nabla g^{-1}=0$ and \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(3), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-calabi-tensor}
 D_g\Psi^m_{ij}
  =g^{p\bar q}\nabla_p\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}
                                      -\nabla_iR_j{}^m.
\end{equation}
By \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(4) and the first identity of (3), $\overline D_g\Psi=D_g\Psi+\mathcal R\Psi$, where
\[
 (\mathcal R\Psi)^m_{ij}
   =-R_a{}^m\Psi^a_{ij}+R_i{}^a\Psi^m_{aj}+R_j{}^a\Psi^m_{ia}.
\]

\emph{The Laplacian of $S$.} By \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(1) and $\nabla_r\overline{\Psi^l_{pq}}=\overline{\nabla_{\bar r}\Psi^l_{pq}}$, which holds because $\nabla_r$ adds no term to antiholomorphic indices,
\[
 \partial_rS=g^{i\bar p}g^{j\bar q}g_{m\bar l}\bigl(\nabla_r\Psi^m_{ij}\,\overline{\Psi^l_{pq}}+\Psi^m_{ij}\,\overline{\nabla_{\bar r}\Psi^l_{pq}}\bigr).
\]
The right side is a tensor with one lower holomorphic index $r$, on which $\nabla_{\bar s}$ acts as $\partial_{\bar s}$. Applying \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(1) once more and contracting with $g^{r\bar s}$, we obtain, with the inner products and norms defined by $g$,
\[
 D_gS=|\nabla\Psi|_g^2+|\overline\nabla\Psi|_g^2+\langle\overline D_g\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g+\langle\Psi,D_g\Psi\rangle_g,
\]
where $\nabla\Psi=(\nabla_r\Psi^m_{ij})$ and $\overline\nabla\Psi=(\nabla_{\bar s}\Psi^m_{ij})$; for the last term we used $\overline{g^{r\bar s}}=g^{s\bar r}$. Inserting $\overline D_g\Psi=D_g\Psi+\mathcal R\Psi$, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-calabi-norm}
 D_gS=|\nabla\Psi|_g^2+|\overline\nabla\Psi|_g^2
       +2\Real\langle D_g\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g
       +\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g;
\end{equation}
in particular $\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g$ is real.

\emph{Bounds from the equation.} In every chart, $\log\det g=\log\det k+\beta(a-tf)$, so
\[
 R_{i\bar j}=-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det g=\operatorname{Ric}(k)_{i\bar j}-\beta a_{i\bar j}+\beta tf_{i\bar j}=\tau_{i\bar j}-\beta g_{i\bar j}.
\]
Hence $R_i{}^m=g^{m\bar l}\tau_{i\bar l}-\beta\delta^m_i$ is bounded in $g$-norm, and so $|\langle\mathcal R\Psi,\Psi\rangle_g|\leq CS$. Moreover $\nabla_iR_j{}^m=g^{m\bar l}\nabla_i\tau_{j\bar l}$, because $\nabla g^{-1}=0$ and $\nabla\delta=0$. For a tensor the difference $\nabla_p-\widehat\nabla_p$ consists of the connection terms with $\Gamma-\widehat\Gamma=\Psi$ in place of $\Gamma$; thus $\nabla_i\tau_{j\bar l}=\widehat\nabla_i\tau_{j\bar l}-\Psi^a_{ij}\tau_{a\bar l}$ and
\[
 \nabla_p\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}=\widehat\nabla_p\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qj}+\Psi^m_{pa}\widehat R^a{}_{i\bar qj}-\Psi^a_{pi}\widehat R^m{}_{a\bar qj}-\Psi^a_{pj}\widehat R^m{}_{i\bar qa}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:elliptic-calabi-tensor} it follows that $|D_g\Psi|_g\leq C(1+S^{1/2})$. Discarding the two nonnegative gradient terms in \eqref{eq:elliptic-calabi-norm}, we conclude that
\[
 D_gS\geq-2C(1+S^{1/2})S^{1/2}-CS\geq-C_1(S+1).
\]

\emph{A compensating term.} We use the trace $H=\operatorname{tr}_hg$. Let $x\in X$, and take the coordinates of \cref{lem:kahler-trace-laplacian} at $x$. By that lemma, identity \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-laplacian} holds at $x$, with $|E|\leq C_EH\operatorname{tr}_gh\leq C$ by the assumption on $g$, and $D_h\log\det g=D_h\log\det k+\beta(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk-tD_hf)$ is bounded by $C$. At $x$ the coefficients $\widehat\Gamma$ vanish, so $\Psi^m_{ij}=\lambda_m^{-1}\partial_ig_{j\bar m}$ and
\[
 S=\sum_{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial_pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda_p\lambda_i\lambda_q}.
\]
Hence the quadratic sum in \eqref{eq:penalized-trace-laplacian} is at least $(\min_p\lambda_p)S\geq C_0^{-1}S$, and $D_gH\geq C_0^{-1}S-C_2$ at $x$. Both sides are functions on $X$, so this inequality holds everywhere.

\emph{The maximum principle.} Choose $K$ with $KC_0^{-1}\geq C_1+1$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $S+KH$. There the complex Hessian of $S+KH$ is negative semidefinite, so
\[
 0\geq D_g(S+KH)\geq(KC_0^{-1}-C_1)S-C_1-KC_2\geq S-C_1-KC_2.
\]
Thus $S\leq C_1+KC_2$ at $x_0$. Since $H\leq nC_0$ on $X$, the function $S+KH$, and hence $S$, is bounded on $X$.\index{Monge--Amp\`ere equation!third-order estimate}

\emph{First derivatives of $g$.} In a chart of the fixed atlas, $\partial_pg_{i\bar q}=\widehat\nabla_pg_{i\bar q}+\widehat\Gamma^l_{pi}g_{l\bar q}=g_{m\bar q}\Psi^m_{pi}+\widehat\Gamma^l_{pi}g_{l\bar q}$ by \cref{lem:chern-connection-identities}(2). On a compact subset of the chart, $h$ lies between two positive multiples of the identity matrix and $\widehat\Gamma$ is bounded, so both terms are bounded, by the bounds for $S$ and $g$. Finally $\partial_{\bar p}g_{i\bar q}=\overline{\partial_pg_{q\bar i}}$, so all first derivatives of the $g_{i\bar j}$ in the underlying real coordinates are bounded.
\end{proof}

The perturbation argument uses Sobolev spaces that are algebras.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sobolev-multiplication}
Let $d\geq1$, let $s>d/2$ be an integer, and for $v\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d)$ put
\[
 \|v\|_{\langle s\rangle}=\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb R^d}(1+|\xi|^2)^s|\widehat v(\xi)|^2\,\mathrm d\xi\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
Then $\|vw\|_{\langle s\rangle}\leq C_s\|v\|_{\langle s\rangle}\|w\|_{\langle s\rangle}$ for $v,w\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d)$, with $C_s$ depending only on $d$ and $s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the inversion formula of \cref{thm:plancherel} and Fubini's theorem, $\widehat{vw}=\widehat v*\widehat w$. With $\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^2)^{1/2}$ we have $\langle\xi\rangle\leq\langle\eta\rangle+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle$, since $(1,\xi)=(1,\eta)+(0,\xi-\eta)$ in $\mathbb R^{1+d}$; hence $\langle\xi\rangle^s\leq2^s(\langle\eta\rangle^s+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^s)$, and therefore
\[
 \langle\xi\rangle^s|\widehat v*\widehat w|(\xi)
 \leq2^s\Bigl(\bigl(\langle\cdot\rangle^s|\widehat v|\bigr)*|\widehat w|
        +|\widehat v|*\bigl(\langle\cdot\rangle^s|\widehat w|\bigr)\Bigr)(\xi).
\]
Young's inequality $\|F*G\|_2\leq\|F\|_2\|G\|_1$ follows from the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $|F*G(x)|^2\leq\|G\|_1\int|F(x-y)|^2|G(y)|\,\mathrm dy$ by integration in $x$ and Fubini's theorem. It therefore remains to bound $\|\widehat w\|_1$ and $\|\widehat v\|_1$ by the norms $\|\cdot\|_{\langle s\rangle}$. This follows from the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $\|\widehat w\|_1\leq\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-s}\|_2\|\langle\cdot\rangle^s\widehat w\|_2$, since $\langle\xi\rangle^{-2s}$ is integrable on $\mathbb R^d$ for $2s>d$.
\end{proof}
\index{Sobolev space!multiplication estimate}\index{Young's inequality!for convolutions}

The next lemma gives the regularity of solutions of the penalized equation, and the estimates for their higher derivatives, in a chart. For a function $G=(G_{i\bar j})$ with values in the positive definite Hermitian matrices we write $(g^{i\bar j})$ for its inverse in the normalization above and $D_Gw=g^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}w$; by Jacobi's formula, $\partial\log\det G=g^{i\bar j}\partial G_{i\bar j}=\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial G)$ for every first-order derivative $\partial$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:penalized-local-regularity}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open, let $k=(k_{i\bar j})$ be a smooth function on $U$ with values in the Hermitian matrices, let $\phi_0$ be a smooth real function on $U$, and let $\beta\in\mathbb R$. Let $u\in C^2(U)$ be real, suppose that $G=(k_{i\bar j}+u_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite on $U$, and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-local-equation}
 \log\det G=\phi_0+\beta u\qquad\text{on }U.
\end{equation}
Then $u$ is smooth. Moreover, let $V\Subset U$ be open, let $\ell\geq3$ be an integer and $2\leq p<\infty$, and suppose that $\lambda I\leq G\leq\Lambda I$ on $U$ for constants $0<\lambda\leq\Lambda$, that $|G(x)-G(y)|\leq\varpi(|x-y|)$ for $x,y\in U$, where $\varpi(r)\to0$ as $r\to0$, and that $\|u\|_{C^1(U)}+\|D^2u\|_{L^q(U)}\leq M$, where $q=2^\ell(p+2n)$. Then $\|u\|_{W^{\ell,p}(V)}\leq C$, where $C$ depends only on $n$, $\ell$, $p$, $V$, $U$, $\lambda$, $\Lambda$, $\varpi$, $M$, $|\beta|$ and bounds on $U$ for the derivatives of order at most $\ell$ of $k$ and $\phi_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the quantitative assertion, $C$ denotes constants depending only on the quantities listed there; $O'\Subset O\Subset O_1\Subset U$ denote open sets, and the constants may also depend on them. The qualitative assertion follows from the quantitative one: if $V\Subset U'\Subset U$ with $U'$ open, then on $U'$ the matrix $G$ is bounded above and below and uniformly continuous, and $u$ has finite $C^1$ norm and bounded second derivatives, so the quantitative assertion, applied on $U'$, gives $u\in W^{\ell,p}(V)$ for all $\ell$ and $p$; by Morrey's inequality (\cref{lem:morrey-sobolev-representative}), applied to the derivatives of $u$, all derivatives of $u$ are continuous on $V$.

\emph{Step 0: an interior estimate.} Let $(a^{i\bar j})$ be a function on $O$ with $\overline{a^{i\bar j}}=a^{j\bar i}$, $\Lambda^{-1}|\zeta|^2\leq a^{i\bar j}\zeta_i\bar\zeta_j\leq\lambda^{-1}|\zeta|^2$ and $|a(x)-a(y)|\leq\varpi'(|x-y|)$, where $\varpi'(r)\to0$ as $r\to0$. We claim that every real $w\in W^{2,p}_{\loc}(O)$ with $w\in W^{1,p}(O)$ satisfies
\[
 \|D^2w\|_{L^p(O')}\leq C\bigl(\|a^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}w\|_{L^p(O)}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}(O)}\bigr),
\]
with $C$ depending also on $\varpi'$. Indeed, by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-real-form}, $a^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}w=\alpha:D^2w$ with a real symmetric $\alpha\geq(4\Lambda)^{-1}I$, $|\alpha|\leq C\lambda^{-1}$ and $|\alpha(x)-\alpha(y)|\leq\varpi'(|x-y|)$. Let $\delta_0$ and $C_A$ be the constants of \cref{lem:frozen-coefficient-lp} for $d=2n$, $p$ and $(4\Lambda)^{-1}$, and choose $r>0$ with $\varpi'(\varrho)\leq\delta_0$ for $0<\varrho<r$ and $r<\operatorname{dist}(O',\mathbb R^{2n}\setminus O)$. Cover $\overline{O'}$ by finitely many balls $B(x_k,r/2)$ with $x_k\in\overline{O'}$, and let $\chi_k=\chi(\cdot-x_k)$, where $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B(0,r))$ equals $1$ on $B(0,r/2)$. By \cref{lem:sobolev-leibniz-chain}(1) and \cref{lem:frozen-coefficient-lp},
\[
 \|D^2(\chi_kw)\|_p\leq C_A\|\alpha:D^2(\chi_kw)\|_{L^p(B(x_k,r))},
\]
and $\alpha:D^2(\chi_kw)=\chi_k\,\alpha:D^2w+2\sum_{\mu,\sigma}\alpha^{\mu\sigma}\partial_\mu\chi_k\,\partial_\sigma w+w\,\alpha:D^2\chi_k$. Summing over $k$ proves the claim.

\emph{Step 1: $u\in W^{3,p}_{\loc}$.} Let $e$ be a real coordinate unit vector, and let $0<|h|<\operatorname{dist}(O,\mathbb R^{2n}\setminus O_1)$, with the notation $\tau_h$, $\delta_h$ of \cref{lem:difference-quotients}. For $x\in O$ and $0\leq\tau\leq1$ the matrix $G_\tau(x)=(1-\tau)G(x)+\tau G(x+he)$ satisfies $\lambda I\leq G_\tau\leq\Lambda I$; let $(g_\tau^{i\bar j})$ be its inverse and $a_h^{i\bar j}=\int_0^1g_\tau^{i\bar j}\,\mathrm d\tau$. By Jacobi's formula, $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\tau}\log\det G_\tau=g_\tau^{i\bar j}(G_{i\bar j}(x+he)-G_{i\bar j}(x))$, so integration over $0\leq\tau\leq1$ gives $\delta_h\log\det G=a_h^{i\bar j}\delta_hG_{i\bar j}$. Since $\delta_hG_{i\bar j}=\delta_hk_{i\bar j}+\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\delta_hu$, equation \eqref{eq:penalized-local-equation} gives
\[
 a_h^{i\bar j}\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}(\delta_hu)=\delta_h\phi_0+\beta\,\delta_hu-a_h^{i\bar j}\delta_hk_{i\bar j}\qquad\text{on }O.
\]
The right side is bounded by $\sup_U|\nabla\phi_0|+|\beta|\sup_U|\nabla u|+C\lambda^{-1}\sup_U|\nabla k|$. The matrices $a_h$ satisfy the hypotheses of Step 0 with $\varpi'=2\lambda^{-2}\varpi$, because they are averages of inverses of matrices between $\lambda I$ and $\Lambda I$, and $|M^{-1}-N^{-1}|=|M^{-1}(N-M)N^{-1}|\leq\lambda^{-2}|M-N|$ for such matrices. By \cref{lem:difference-quotients}(1), $\|\delta_hu\|_{W^{1,p}(O)}\leq\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(O_1)}$. Step 0 therefore gives $\|D^2\delta_hu\|_{L^p(O')}\leq C$, uniformly in $h$. Since $\delta_h\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma u=\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\delta_hu$, \cref{lem:difference-quotients}(2), applied to $Z=\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma u$, shows that $\partial_e\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma u\in L^p(O')$, with norm at most $C$. Thus $\|u\|_{W^{3,p}(O')}\leq C$, where $C$ depends on $M$ through $\|u\|_{C^1(U)}$ and $\|D^2u\|_{L^p(O_1)}$.

\emph{Step 2: from $W^{\ell}$ to $W^{\ell+1}$.} Let $\ell\geq3$ and suppose that $u\in W^{\ell,q'}(O_1)$ for some $q'\geq2p+2n$. By Morrey's inequality (\cref{lem:morrey-sobolev-representative}), applied to the derivatives of $u$ of order at most $\ell-2$, which lie in $W^{2,q'}(O_1)$, the function $u$ is of class $C^{\ell-1}$ on $O_1$, and on a slightly smaller set, which we again call $O_1$, its derivatives of order at most $\ell-1$ are bounded by $C\|u\|_{W^{\ell,q'}}$. Thus $G\in C^{\ell-3}(O_1)$, its derivatives of order at most $\ell-3$ lie in $W^{1,2p}(O_1)$, and those of order $\ell-2$ lie in $L^{2p}(O_1)$.

We first record a formula for the derivatives of $\log\det G$. For a smooth function $G$ with values in the positive definite Hermitian matrices and a multi-index $\gamma$ with $|\gamma|\geq1$,
\[
 \partial^\gamma\log\det G=\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)+P_\gamma(G),
\]
where $P_\gamma(G)$ is a linear combination, with coefficients depending only on $\gamma$, of functions $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^{\beta_1}G\,G^{-1}\partial^{\beta_2}G\cdots G^{-1}\partial^{\beta_r}G)$ with $r\geq2$, $|\beta_i|\geq1$ and $\beta_1+\cdots+\beta_r=\gamma$; in particular $|\beta_i|\leq|\gamma|-1$. For $|\gamma|=1$ this is Jacobi's formula, with $P_\gamma=0$. If it holds for $\gamma$, differentiate once more: by the Leibniz rule and $\partial_\mu(G^{-1})=-G^{-1}(\partial_\mu G)G^{-1}$, the derivative of each term $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^{\beta_1}G\cdots)$ is a sum of terms of the same form, with $r$ or $r+1$ factors and total multi-index $\gamma+e_\mu$, and the derivative of $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)$ is $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^{\gamma+e_\mu}G)-\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial_\mu G\,G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)$. Note that $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)=g^{i\bar j}\partial^\gamma G_{i\bar j}$.

Now fix $\gamma$ with $|\gamma|=\ell-2\geq1$. We claim that, almost everywhere on $O_1$,
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)+P_\gamma(G)=\partial^\gamma\phi_0+\beta\,\partial^\gamma u,
\]
where $\partial^\gamma G$ is formed with weak derivatives. Let $K\subset O_1$ be compact, and let $u_\varepsilon=u*\rho_\varepsilon$ and $G_\varepsilon=k+(\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}u_\varepsilon)$. By \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}(1) and \cref{lem:mollifier-density}(1),(2), as $\varepsilon\to0$ the derivatives of $u_\varepsilon$ of order at most $\ell-1$ converge uniformly on $K$ to those of $u$, and those of order $\ell$ converge in $L^{2p}(K)$. Hence $G_\varepsilon$ is positive definite on $K$ for small $\varepsilon$, its derivatives of order at most $\ell-3$ converge uniformly on $K$ to those of $G$, and $\partial^\gamma G_\varepsilon\to\partial^\gamma G$ in $L^{2p}(K)$. The formula above holds for the smooth functions $G_\varepsilon$ on the interior of $K$. Its left side converges to $\partial^\gamma\log\det G=\partial^\gamma\phi_0+\beta\partial^\gamma u$ in the sense of distributions, since $\log\det G_\varepsilon\to\log\det G$ uniformly on $K$ and $\log\det G=\phi_0+\beta u$, and its right side converges in $L^1(K)$, since every factor of $P_\gamma(G_\varepsilon)$ involves derivatives of $G_\varepsilon$ of order at most $\ell-3$. Hence the identity holds in the sense of distributions on the interior of $K$, and therefore almost everywhere (\cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}(4)). This proves the claim.

Put $v=\partial^\gamma u\in W^{2,2p}(O_1)$. Since $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}\partial^\gamma G)=D_Gv+g^{i\bar j}\partial^\gamma k_{i\bar j}$, the claim says that
\[
 D_Gv=R:=\partial^\gamma\phi_0+\beta v-g^{i\bar j}\partial^\gamma k_{i\bar j}-P_\gamma(G)\qquad\text{almost everywhere on }O_1.
\]
For $x\in O$ and small $h$, as in Step 1, the discrete product rule $\delta_h(FF')=(\tau_hF)\delta_hF'+(\delta_hF)F'$ gives
\[
 (\tau_hg^{i\bar j})\,\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}(\delta_hv)=\delta_hR-(\delta_hg^{i\bar j})\,\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}v\qquad\text{almost everywhere on }O.
\]
We bound the right side in $L^p(O)$ uniformly in $h$, using \cref{lem:difference-quotients}(1) on $O\Subset O_1$. First, $\delta_h(G^{-1})=-(\tau_hG)^{-1}(\delta_hG)G^{-1}$, so $\|\delta_hG^{-1}\|_{L^{2p}(O)}\leq\lambda^{-2}\|\partial_eG\|_{L^{2p}(O_1)}\leq C$, as $\partial_eG$ involves derivatives of $u$ of order three. Hence the last term is bounded by $\|\delta_hG^{-1}\|_{L^{2p}}\|\partial\bar\partial v\|_{L^{2p}}\leq C$. Next, $\|\delta_h\partial^\gamma\phi_0\|_\infty\leq C$ and $\|\delta_hv\|_{L^p(O)}\leq\|\partial_ev\|_{L^p(O_1)}\leq C$. Finally, $g^{i\bar j}\partial^\gamma k_{i\bar j}$ and $P_\gamma(G)$ are sums of products of bounded continuous factors, namely entries of $G^{-1}$, derivatives of $k$, and derivatives of $G$ of order at most $\ell-3$, each of which has difference quotients bounded in $L^p(O)$; by the discrete product rule their difference quotients are bounded in $L^p(O)$. Thus the right side is bounded in $L^p(O)$, and $\|\delta_hv\|_{W^{1,p}(O)}\leq\|\partial_ev\|_{W^{1,p}(O_1)}\leq C$. The matrices $\tau_hG^{-1}$ satisfy the hypotheses of Step 0 with $\varpi'=\lambda^{-2}\varpi$. By Step 0, $\|D^2\delta_hv\|_{L^p(O')}\leq C$ uniformly in $h$, and \cref{lem:difference-quotients}(2), applied to the second derivatives of $v$, shows that the third derivatives of $v$ lie in $L^p(O')$, with norm at most $C$. Every multi-index of length $\ell+1$ is the sum of a multi-index of length $\ell-2$ and of three coordinate unit vectors, so $\|u\|_{W^{\ell+1,p}(O')}\leq C$, where $C$ depends on $u$ only through $\|u\|_{W^{\ell,q'}(O_1)}$.

\emph{Step 3: conclusion.} Choose open sets $V=O_{3\ell}\Subset O_{3\ell-1}\Subset\cdots\Subset O_0=U$, depending only on $V$, $U$ and $\ell$, and put $e_m=2^{\ell-m}(p+2n)-2n$ for $3\leq m\leq\ell$. Then $e_\ell=p\geq2$, $e_m=2e_{m+1}+2n$, and $e_3\leq q$. Step 1, applied with the exponent $e_3$ in place of $p$ and with the sets $O_1$, $O_2$, $O_3$ in the roles of $O_1$, $O$, $O'$, bounds $\|u\|_{W^{3,e_3}(O_3)}$, since $\|D^2u\|_{L^{e_3}(O_1)}\leq|O_1|^{1/e_3-1/q}\|D^2u\|_{L^q(U)}$. For $m=3,\ldots,\ell-1$ we then apply Step 2 with $\ell$ replaced by $m$, with the exponent $e_{m+1}$ in place of $p$ and $q'=e_m$, and with the sets $O_{3m}$ (shrunk to $O_{3m+1}$), $O_{3m+2}$, $O_{3m+3}$ in the roles of $O_1$, $O$, $O'$; this bounds $\|u\|_{W^{m+1,e_{m+1}}(O_{3m+3})}$ in terms of $\|u\|_{W^{m,e_m}(O_{3m})}$. For $m=\ell-1$ we obtain the bound for $\|u\|_{W^{\ell,p}(V)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}
For every smooth real function $f$ on $X$ and every $\beta>0$, equation \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation} has a unique smooth real solution $a_\beta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $0\leq t\leq1$ consider the equation
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc a)^n=\mathrm e^{\beta(a-tf)}\kappa^n,
 \qquad \kappa+\ddc a>0,
\]
and let $T\subset[0,1]$ be the set of $t$ for which it has a smooth real solution. For $t=0$ the solution is $a=0$, so $T\neq\varnothing$. We show that $T$ is open and closed in $[0,1]$; then $T=[0,1]$, since $[0,1]$ is connected, and the case $t=1$ gives existence. Uniqueness is proved at the end.

\emph{Step 1: a priori estimates.} Let $a$ be a smooth solution for some $t\in[0,1]$, with metric $g$. In this step, constants depend only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, $\beta$ and $f$, but not on $t$ or on the solution. Since $\|tf\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty$ and, by \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, $\sup_XD_h(tf)=t\sup_XD_hf\leq\sup_XD_hf$, \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}, applied to $tf$, gives $|a|\leq\|f\|_\infty$ and $C_0^{-1}h\leq g\leq C_0h$ with $C_0$ independent of $t$. Hence $La=-4\pi D_ha=-4\pi(H-\operatorname{tr}_hk)$ is bounded, and \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} bounds $a$ in $W^{2,p}(X)$ for every finite $p$; by \eqref{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction} and Morrey's inequality, this bounds $\|a\|_{C^1}$ and $\|D^2a\|_{L^p}$ on compact subsets of charts. By \cref{lem:penalized-third-order}, the first derivatives of the $g_{i\bar j}$ are bounded on compact subsets of the charts of a fixed finite atlas.

Fix finitely many coordinate balls $U_\nu$ whose closures lie in chart domains, and concentric balls $V'_\nu\Subset V_\nu\Subset U_\nu$ such that the $V'_\nu$ cover $X$. In the coordinates of $U_\nu$, the function $a$ solves \eqref{eq:penalized-local-equation} with $\phi_0=\log\det k-\beta tf$, whose derivatives are bounded independently of $t$. By the preceding paragraph, on $U_\nu$ the matrix $G=g$ lies between two fixed positive multiples of the identity, it is Lipschitz with a fixed constant, so that we may take $\varpi(r)=Cr$, and $\|a\|_{C^1(U_\nu)}+\|D^2a\|_{L^q(U_\nu)}$ is bounded for every finite $q$. By \cref{lem:penalized-local-regularity}, $\|a\|_{W^{\ell,p}(V_\nu)}$ is bounded for all $\ell\geq3$ and $p<\infty$, and by Morrey's inequality every derivative of $a$ is bounded on $V'_\nu$. Thus every $C^\ell$ norm of $a$, computed in these coordinates on the sets $V'_\nu$, is bounded independently of $t$.

\emph{Step 2: openness.} Let $t_0\in T$, with smooth solution $a$ and metric $g$; thus $\kappa+\ddc a$ is a K\"ahler form. Fix an integer $s>n$. We use the spaces $H^r$ of \cref{sec:global-trace-equation}, defined with a finite atlas $(U_\nu)$ and a partition of unity $(\chi_\nu)$; for a smooth function $v$ with compact support in $U_\nu$, written in the coordinates of $U_\nu$, let $\|v\|_{\langle r\rangle}$ be defined by the formula of \cref{lem:sobolev-multiplication} with $d=2n$, so that $\|u\|_{H^r}^2=\sum_\nu\|\chi_\nu u\|_{\langle r\rangle}^2$ for smooth $u$. Choose smooth functions $\tilde\chi_\nu$, $\check\chi_\nu$ on $X$ with values in $[0,1]$ and compact supports in $U_\nu$ such that $\tilde\chi_\nu=1$ near $\Supp\chi_\nu$ and $\check\chi_\nu=1$ near $\Supp\tilde\chi_\nu$. We use three facts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Since $s>n$, \cref{lem:compact-sobolev-spaces}(3) gives continuous inclusions $H^{s+2}\subset C^2(X)$ and $H^s\subset C^0(X)$.
\item[(ii)] For a smooth function $\psi$ with compact support in $U_\nu$, multiplication by $\psi$ is bounded for $\|\cdot\|_{\langle s\rangle}$, by \cref{lem:sobolev-multiplication}; and $\|\partial_\mu v\|_{\langle r\rangle}\leq2\pi\|v\|_{\langle r+1\rangle}$ for real coordinate derivatives, since $\widehat{\partial_\mu v}=2\pi\mathrm i\xi_\mu\widehat v$.
\item[(iii)] $\|\check\chi_\nu b\|_{\langle s+2\rangle}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}$ for smooth $b$. Indeed, $\Sigma=\sum_\mu\check\chi_\mu\geq1$, because $\check\chi_\mu=1$ wherever $\chi_\mu>0$; so the functions $\check\chi_\mu/\Sigma$ form a partition of unity subordinate to the same charts, and by \cref{lem:compact-sobolev-spaces}(1) the norm of $H^{s+2}$ computed with them is equivalent to the given one. Moreover $\check\chi_\nu b=\eta_\nu\cdot(\check\chi_\nu/\Sigma)b$, where $\eta_\nu$ is a smooth function with compact support in $U_\nu$ equal to $\Sigma$ near $\Supp\check\chi_\nu$, and multiplication by $\eta_\nu$ is bounded for $\|\cdot\|_{\langle s+2\rangle}$ by \cref{lem:sobolev-multiplication}.
\end{enumerate}

For a real function $b\in C^2(X)$, let $Y$ be the tensor $Y^m{}_j=g^{m\bar l}b_{j\bar l}$; its powers $(Y^k)^m{}_j=Y^m{}_{a_1}Y^{a_1}{}_{a_2}\cdots Y^{a_{k-1}}{}_j$ are tensors, so the traces $\operatorname{tr}(Y^k)$ are functions on $X$. In a chart, with the matrices $G=(g_{i\bar j})$ and $B=(b_{i\bar j})$, the traces of the powers of $Y$ are those of $BG^{-1}$, which is similar to the Hermitian matrix $G^{-1/2}BG^{-1/2}$; let $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n$ be their real eigenvalues. Then $\det(G+B)=\det G\prod_i(1+\mu_i)$ and $\operatorname{tr}Y=\sum_i\mu_i=g^{i\bar j}b_{i\bar j}=D_gb$. Since $|\mu_i|\leq\|G^{-1}\|\,|B|$, and $G$ is bounded below on compact subsets of the charts of a finite atlas, (i) gives $r_0>0$ such that $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}\leq r_0$ implies $|\mu_i|\leq1/2$; then $G+B>0$, that is, $\kappa+\ddc(a+b)>0$, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal Q(b)&:=\log\det(G+B)-\log\det G-D_gb\\
 &=\sum_i\bigl(\log(1+\mu_i)-\mu_i\bigr)=\sum_{k\geq2}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}k\operatorname{tr}(Y^k).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Put $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=\log(\det(G+B)/\det k)-\beta(a+b-tf)$, so that $a+b$ solves the equation for $t$ exactly when $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$. Since $\log(\det G/\det k)=\beta(a-t_0f)$,
\[
 \mathcal N_t(a+b)=\beta(t-t_0)f+\mathcal Db+\mathcal Q(b),
 \qquad \mathcal D=D_g-\beta.
\]

We estimate $\mathcal Q$ in $H^s$. Let $b$ be smooth and real with $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}\leq r_0$. In the chart $U_\nu$ let $Y_\nu=\tilde\chi_\nu Y$, a matrix of smooth functions with compact support, and $\|Y_\nu\|_{\langle s\rangle}=\max_{m,j}\|\tilde\chi_\nu Y^m{}_j\|_{\langle s\rangle}$. Since $\tilde\chi_\nu Y^m{}_j=(\tilde\chi_\nu g^{m\bar l})\,\partial_j\partial_{\bar l}(\check\chi_\nu b)$, facts (ii) and (iii) give $\|Y_\nu\|_{\langle s\rangle}\leq C_Y\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}$. For matrices $M$, $N$ of smooth compactly supported functions, \cref{lem:sobolev-multiplication} gives $\|(MN)_{mj}\|_{\langle s\rangle}\leq nC_s\max\|M_{\cdot\cdot}\|_{\langle s\rangle}\max\|N_{\cdot\cdot}\|_{\langle s\rangle}$, and therefore
\[
 \|\chi_\nu\operatorname{tr}(Y_\nu^k)\|_{\langle s\rangle}\leq C_\chi n(nC_s)^{k-1}\|Y_\nu\|_{\langle s\rangle}^k,
\]
where $C_\chi$ bounds multiplication by $\chi_\nu$. Since $Y_\nu=Y$ on $\Supp\chi_\nu$, the function $\chi_\nu\mathcal Q(b)$ is the sum of the series $\sum_{k\geq2}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}k\chi_\nu\operatorname{tr}(Y_\nu^k)$, which converges uniformly, with supports in $\Supp\chi_\nu$. Decreasing $r_0$, we may assume that $\rho=nC_sC_Yr_0\leq1/2$. Then the series converges absolutely for $\|\cdot\|_{\langle s\rangle}$; since its partial sums converge in $L^2$, their Fourier transforms converge in $L^2$ (\cref{thm:plancherel}), a subsequence converges almost everywhere, and Fatou's lemma gives
\[
 \|\chi_\nu\mathcal Q(b)\|_{\langle s\rangle}\leq\sum_{k\geq2}\frac1kC_\chi n(nC_s)^{k-1}\|Y_\nu\|_{\langle s\rangle}^k\leq2C_\chi n^2C_s\|Y_\nu\|_{\langle s\rangle}^2.
\]
Summing over $\nu$, we obtain $\|\mathcal Q(b)\|_{H^s}\leq C_Q\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}^2$. In the same way, using $Y^k-Y'^k=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}Y^i(Y-Y')Y'^{k-1-i}$, we obtain
\[
 \|\mathcal Q(b)-\mathcal Q(b')\|_{H^s}\leq C_Q\bigl(\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|b'\|_{H^{s+2}}\bigr)\|b-b'\|_{H^{s+2}}
\]
for two such functions $b$, $b'$. Let $H^{s+2}_{\mathbb R}$ be the closed subspace of real elements of $H^{s+2}$. Smooth real functions are dense in it, since taking real parts does not increase the norms: $|\widehat{\chi\Real v}(\xi)|^2\leq\frac12(|\widehat{\chi v}(\xi)|^2+|\widehat{\chi v}(-\xi)|^2)$ for real $\chi$. If $b\in H^{s+2}_{\mathbb R}$ with $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}<r_0$, and smooth real $b_j$ converge to $b$ in $H^{s+2}$, with norms less than $r_0$, then the $\mathcal Q(b_j)$ form a Cauchy sequence in $H^s$; by (i), $b_j\to b$ in $C^2$, so $\mathcal Q(b_j)\to\mathcal Q(b)$ uniformly, and, again by (i), the limit in $H^s$ is $\mathcal Q(b)$. Hence the two estimates for $\mathcal Q$ hold on the ball $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}<r_0$ of $H^{s+2}_{\mathbb R}$.

The operator $\mathcal D\colon H^{s+2}\to H^s$ is invertible, with bounded inverse. Indeed, $\kappa+\ddc a=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}g_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dz_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$ is a K\"ahler form, and by \eqref{eq:complex-laplacian-trace}, applied with $\omega'=\kappa+\ddc a$, we have $D_g-\beta=-(4\pi)^{-1}(L_{\omega'}+4\pi\beta)$. Since $\omega$ is an arbitrary K\"ahler form on $X$, \cref{lem:compact-elliptic-foundations}(3) holds for $\omega'$; applied to functions, on which the Hodge Laplacian of $\omega'$ is $L_{\omega'}$, with $c=4\pi\beta>0$, it shows that $D_g-\beta$ maps $H^{s+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^s$, because these spaces do not depend on the K\"ahler form (\cref{lem:compact-sobolev-spaces}(4)). Let $M_{\mathcal D}$ be the norm of its inverse. Since $\mathcal D$ is a real operator, its inverse maps real elements to real elements. For $b\in H^{s+2}$, the element $\mathcal Db$ of $H^s$ is the continuous function $D_gb-\beta b$, by approximation with smooth functions and (i).

The solutions $b\in H^{s+2}_{\mathbb R}$ of $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$ with $\|b\|_{H^{s+2}}<r_0$ are the fixed points of
\[
 \Phi_t(b)=-\mathcal D^{-1}\bigl(\beta(t-t_0)f+\mathcal Q(b)\bigr).
\]
Choose $0<r<r_0$ with $4M_{\mathcal D}C_Qr\leq1$, and suppose that $|t-t_0|\leq\delta$, where $2M_{\mathcal D}\beta\delta\|f\|_{H^s}\leq r$. On the closed ball $\overline B_r$ of radius $r$ in $H^{s+2}_{\mathbb R}$ we then have $\|\Phi_t(b)\|_{H^{s+2}}\leq M_{\mathcal D}(\beta\delta\|f\|_{H^s}+C_Qr^2)\leq r/2+r/4$ and $\|\Phi_t(b)-\Phi_t(b')\|_{H^{s+2}}\leq2M_{\mathcal D}C_Qr\|b-b'\|_{H^{s+2}}\leq\frac12\|b-b'\|_{H^{s+2}}$. The iterates $b_0=0$, $b_{\ell+1}=\Phi_t(b_\ell)$ therefore stay in $\overline B_r$, their successive differences decrease geometrically, so they form a Cauchy sequence, and their limit $b$ is a fixed point of $\Phi_t$, by the Lipschitz bound. Thus $\mathcal Db=-\beta(t-t_0)f-\mathcal Q(b)$ in $H^s$, hence pointwise by (i), that is, $\mathcal N_t(a+b)=0$. The function $u=a+b$ is of class $C^2$, satisfies $\kappa+\ddc u>0$, and in every chart it solves \eqref{eq:penalized-local-equation} with $\phi_0=\log\det k-\beta tf$. By \cref{lem:penalized-local-regularity}, $u$ is smooth. Hence $T$ contains $[t_0-\delta,t_0+\delta]\cap[0,1]$, and $T$ is open in $[0,1]$.

\emph{Step 3: closedness.} Let $t_k\in T$ converge to $t_*\in[0,1]$, with solutions $a_k$. By Step 1, every $C^\ell$ norm of $a_k$ on the compact sets $\overline{V'_\nu}$ is bounded independently of $k$. Hence, for every $\ell$, the derivatives of order at most $\ell$ of the $a_k$ are uniformly bounded on each $\overline{V'_\nu}$, and they are equicontinuous there by the mean value theorem, since the derivatives of order $\ell+1$ are bounded and the balls $V'_\nu$ are convex. By the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem (\cref{prop:arzela-ascoli}) and a diagonal argument over $\ell$ and $\nu$, a subsequence converges, together with all its derivatives, uniformly on each $\overline{V'_\nu}$. The limit $a_*$ is smooth, and its derivatives are the limits of the derivatives of the $a_k$, because uniform convergence of functions and of their first derivatives on a ball implies that the limit is continuously differentiable with the limit derivatives. The uniform lower bound $g_k\geq C_0^{-1}h$ of Step 1 gives $\kappa+\ddc a_*>0$, and $a_*$ solves the equation for $t_*$. Thus $t_*\in T$, and $T$ is closed.

\emph{Step 4: uniqueness.} Let $a$ and $a'$ be smooth solutions of \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation}. At a maximum point of $a-a'$ the complex Hessian of $a-a'$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<k+(a_{i\bar j})\leq k+(a'_{i\bar j})$, and the equations give $\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\leq\mathrm e^{\beta(a'-f)}$ there. Hence $a-a'\leq0$ at that point, and therefore everywhere. Interchanging $a$ and $a'$ proves that $a=a'$.
\end{proof}

As $\beta\to\infty$, the solutions $a_\beta$ converge to the envelope of $f$. The upper bound follows from the maximum principle, the lower bound from the comparison principle applied to a slightly lowered convex combination of the envelope and a constant.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}
Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, and for $\beta>0$ let $a_\beta$ be the solution of \eqref{eq:penalized-kahler-equation} given by \cref{thm:smooth-penalized-kahler-equation}. Put
\[
 B=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hf,\qquad
 d_0=\inf_X\frac{\det k}{\det h},\qquad
 K_0=\frac{B^n}{n^nd_0}.
\]
Then $K_0\geq1$, $a_\beta\leq P_\kappa(f)+\beta^{-1}\log K_0$ for every $\beta>0$, and for every $\beta\geq1$
\[
 P_\kappa(f)-\frac{\operatorname{osc}_Xf+n\log\beta}{\beta}
  \leq a_\beta
  \leq P_\kappa(f)+\frac{\log K_0}{\beta}.
\]
In particular $a_\beta\to P_\kappa(f)$ uniformly on $X$ as $\beta\to\infty$. Moreover $P_\kappa(f)$ has bounded metric Laplacian: with the constant $A$ of \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-envelope-laplacian-bound}
 -\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\leq D_hP_\kappa(f)\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{2A\|f\|_\infty}
\end{equation}
in the sense of distributions, that is, $\langle D_hP_\kappa(f),\chi\rangle$ lies between $-\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\int_X\chi\,\mathrm dV$ and $2B\mathrm e^{2A\|f\|_\infty}\int_X\chi\,\mathrm dV$ for every smooth function $\chi\geq0$. Finally, $P_\kappa(f)\in C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each point, the eigenvalues of $k$ relative to $h$ are positive, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives $\operatorname{tr}_hk\geq n(\det k/\det h)^{1/n}\geq nd_0^{1/n}$. Since $\sup_XD_hf\geq0$ by \eqref{eq:penalized-upper-laplacian-nonnegative}, it follows that $B\geq nd_0^{1/n}$, that is, $K_0\geq1$.

\emph{The upper bound.} Let $\beta>0$, write $a=a_\beta$ and $g=k+(a_{i\bar j})$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $a-f$. There the complex Hessian of $a-f$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<g\leq k+(f_{i\bar j})$. In particular $k+(f_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite at $x_0$, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for its eigenvalues relative to $h$ gives
\[
 \mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}=\frac{\det g}{\det k}
 \leq\frac{\det(k+(f_{i\bar j}))}{\det h}\cdot\frac{\det h}{\det k}
 \leq\Bigl(\frac{\operatorname{tr}_hk+D_hf}{n}\Bigr)^n\frac1{d_0}
 \leq K_0
\]
at $x_0$. Hence $a-f\leq\log K_0/\beta$ on $X$. The function $a-\log K_0/\beta$ is therefore a smooth $\kappa$-psh function below $f$, that is, a competitor for $P_\kappa(f)$, and so $a\leq P_\kappa(f)+\log K_0/\beta$.

\emph{The lower bound.} Let $\beta\geq1$ and put $v=P_\kappa(f)$; as noted at the beginning of this subsection, $\min_Xf\leq v\leq f$. For $0<t\leq1$ put
\[
 w=(1-t)v+t\min_Xf-\frac{n\log(1/t)}{\beta}.
\]
This is a bounded $\kappa$-psh function. For a bounded $\kappa$-psh function $b$ write $\mu_b=\langle(\kappa+\ddc b)^n\rangle$, the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure, which is the Bedford--Taylor measure since $b$ is bounded (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-bounded-factor-compatibility}, applied with $q=p=n$ to the sums of $b$ with smooth local potentials of $\kappa$), and equals the smooth form $(\kappa+\ddc b)^n$ if $b$ is smooth (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(1)). Locally $\kappa=\ddc\rho$ with $\rho$ smooth, and $\kappa+\ddc w=\ddc\bigl((1-t)(\rho+v)+t\rho\bigr)$. Expanding the $n$th power by multilinearity and positive homogeneity (\cref{prop:nonpluripolar-local-calculus}), and discarding the mixed terms, which are positive measures, we get $\mu_w\geq t^n\kappa^n$. On the other hand $w\leq f-n\log(1/t)/\beta$, so $\mathrm e^{\beta(w-f)}\leq t^n$. We claim that $w\leq a$. Let $E=\{a<w\}$, a Borel set. Since $a$ and $w$ are bounded, $a\geq w-O(1)$, so \cref{prop:global-nonpluripolar-comparison} applies without the correction terms; together with the two inequalities just proved and the equation for $a$ it gives
\[
 \int_E\mathrm e^{\beta(w-f)}\kappa^n
 \leq\int_E\mu_w
 \leq\int_E\mu_a
 =\int_E\mathrm e^{\beta(a-f)}\kappa^n.
\]
On $E$ the first integrand is strictly larger than the last one, so $E$ has volume zero. Thus $w\leq a$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere: both functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic, so by \cref{lem:quasi-psh-basic}(1) each of them is, at every point, the limit of its averages over small coordinate balls centered at that point. Now take $t=1/\beta$, which is allowed since $\beta\geq1$. Since $\min_Xf-v\geq\min_Xf-\max_Xf=-\operatorname{osc}_Xf$, we obtain
\[
 a\geq w=v+\frac{\min_Xf-v}{\beta}-\frac{n\log\beta}{\beta}
 \geq v-\frac{\operatorname{osc}_Xf+n\log\beta}{\beta}.
\]

\emph{The Laplacian bound.} Let $\beta_0$ be the constant of \cref{lem:penalized-equation-trace}. For $\beta>\beta_0$ that lemma, together with $D_ha_\beta=H-\operatorname{tr}_hk$, $H>0$ and $\operatorname{osc}_Xf\leq2\|f\|_\infty$, gives
\[
 -\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\leq D_ha_\beta\leq2B\,\mathrm e^{2A\|f\|_\infty}.
\]
Since $D_h=-L/(4\pi)$ is formally self-adjoint and $a_\beta\to v$ uniformly, $\langle D_hv,\chi\rangle=\int_Xv\,D_h\chi\,\mathrm dV=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\int_X(D_ha_\beta)\chi\,\mathrm dV$ for smooth $\chi$, which proves \eqref{eq:penalized-envelope-laplacian-bound}. In a chart, where $\mathrm dV=\varrho\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ with $\varrho$ smooth and positive, it follows that the real distribution $D_hv$, paired with test functions through Lebesgue measure, satisfies $|\langle D_hv,\chi\rangle|\leq c\int\chi\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ for nonnegative test functions $\chi$, where $c=\max\{\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk,2B\mathrm e^{2A\|f\|_\infty}\}$. By \cref{cor:distribution-bounded-density}(1), $D_hv$ is a Borel function with $|D_hv|\leq c$. Hence $Lv=-4\pi D_hv$ is a bounded function, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$ and upper bounds for $\|f\|_\infty$ and $\sup_XD_hf$. Finally $v$ is continuous, as a uniform limit of continuous functions, so by \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} it belongs to $C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$.
\end{proof}

We can now remove the smoothness of the obstacle and pass to finite minima. A finite minimum of smooth functions is approximated by smooth functions whose complex Hessians have traces bounded above by those of the given functions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:soft-minimum-trace}
Let $r\geq1$ be an integer, let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be smooth real functions on $X$, let $\delta>0$, and put
\[
 F=-\delta\log\sum_{j=1}^r\mathrm e^{-f_j/\delta}.
\]
Then $F$ is a smooth real function with $\min_jf_j-\delta\log r\leq F\leq\min_jf_j$, and $D_hF\leq\max_j\sup_XD_hf_j$ on $X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 p_j=\frac{\mathrm e^{-f_j/\delta}}
                   {\sum_l\mathrm e^{-f_l/\delta}}.
\]
The weights $p_j$ are positive with $\sum_jp_j=1$, and $\min_jf_j-\delta\log r\leq F\leq\min_jf_j$, because the sum lies between $\mathrm e^{-\min_jf_j/\delta}$ and $r$ times this number. In local holomorphic coordinates, the chain rule gives $\partial_iF=\sum_jp_j\partial_if_j$ and $\partial_{\bar k}p_j=-\delta^{-1}p_j\bigl(\partial_{\bar k}f_j-\sum_lp_l\partial_{\bar k}f_l\bigr)$; differentiating once more and contracting with $h^{i\bar k}$, we get
\[
 D_hF
 =\sum_jp_jD_hf_j
 -\frac1\delta\left(\sum_jp_j|\partial f_j|_h^2
                    -\left|\sum_jp_j\partial f_j\right|_h^2\right),
\]
where $|\xi|_h^2=h^{i\bar j}\xi_i\bar\xi_j$. The term in parentheses equals $\sum_jp_j|\partial f_j-\sum_lp_l\partial f_l|_h^2\geq0$, the variance of the vectors $\partial f_j$ with respect to the probability weights $p_j$. Hence $D_hF\leq\sum_jp_jD_hf_j\leq\max_j\sup_XD_hf_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:kahler-metric-laplacian-rooftop}
Let $r\geq1$ be an integer, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be continuous real functions on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Then the envelope
\[
 u=P_\kappa\bigl(\min_{1\leq j\leq r}f_j\bigr)
\]
has bounded metric Laplacian, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, and on bounds for $\|f_j\|_\infty$ and $\|Lf_j\|_{L^\infty}$, $1\leq j\leq r$. In particular $u$ belongs to $C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$, and the positive current $\kappa+\ddc u$ has bounded coefficients.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: smoothing the obstacles.} By \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp}, each $f_j$ belongs to $W^{2,p}(X)$ for every finite $p$, and it is of class $C^1$; fix $p>2n$. Choose a finite smooth partition of unity $(\chi_\nu)$ with each $\chi_\nu$ supported in the interior of a coordinate chart. For small $\varepsilon>0$, let $f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$ be the convolution of $f_j$, written in the chart of $\chi_\nu$, with the mollifier $\rho_\varepsilon$ of \cref{sec:convolution}; it is defined on a neighborhood of the support of $\chi_\nu$. Put $f_{j,\varepsilon}=\sum_\nu\chi_\nu f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$. These are smooth functions with $\|f_{j,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\leq\|f_j\|_\infty$, and $f_{j,\varepsilon}\to f_j$ in $C^1$, because the convolutions of the $C^1$ function $f_j$ and of its first derivatives converge to them uniformly on compact sets (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}(1)), and $\partial_\mu(f_j*\rho_\varepsilon)=(\partial_\mu f_j)*\rho_\varepsilon$.

We bound the Laplacians of the approximations. By \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-real-form}, in the real coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_{2n}$ underlying a holomorphic chart, with $\partial_\mu=\partial/\partial x_\mu$, we have $D_h=\sum_{\mu,\sigma}c^{\mu\sigma}(x)\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma$ on real functions, with smooth real coefficients; there are no first-order terms. Since $f_j\in W^{2,p}_{\loc}$ in the chart, by \eqref{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction}, and $\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma(f_j*\rho_\varepsilon)=(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma f_j)*\rho_\varepsilon$, the commutator of $D_h$ with convolution is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &D_h(f_j*\rho_\varepsilon)(x)-\bigl((D_hf_j)*\rho_\varepsilon\bigr)(x)\\
 &\qquad=\int\sum_{\mu,\sigma}\bigl(c^{\mu\sigma}(x)-c^{\mu\sigma}(y)\bigr)
     \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma f_j(y)\,\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,\mathrm dy,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where $D_hf_j=-Lf_j/(4\pi)$ is a bounded function. Since $|c^{\mu\sigma}(x)-c^{\mu\sigma}(y)|\leq C\varepsilon$ on the support of $\rho_\varepsilon(x-\cdot)$ and $\|\rho_\varepsilon\|_{L^{p'}}=\varepsilon^{-2n/p}\|\rho\|_{L^{p'}}$ with $p'=p/(p-1)$, H\"older's inequality and \eqref{eq:sobolev-chart-restriction} bound this commutator by $C\varepsilon^{1-2n/p}\|f_j\|_{W^{2,p}(X)}$, which tends to zero because $p>2n$. As $(D_hf_j)*\rho_\varepsilon$ lies between the essential infimum and the essential supremum of $D_hf_j$, since $\rho_\varepsilon\geq0$ has integral one, we obtain on a neighborhood of the support of $\chi_\nu$
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,inf}_XD_hf_j-o(1)\leq D_hf_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}
            \leq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_XD_hf_j+o(1)\qquad(\varepsilon\to0).
\]
Next, $D_hf_{j,\varepsilon}=\sum_\nu\chi_\nu D_hf_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}+\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu]f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}$, where each commutator $[D_h,\chi_\nu]$ is a first-order operator with coefficients supported in $\Supp\chi_\nu$. Since $\sum_\nu\chi_\nu=1$, we have $\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu]f_j=D_hf_j-D_hf_j=0$, so the second sum equals $\sum_\nu[D_h,\chi_\nu](f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}-f_j)$, which involves only $f_{j,\nu,\varepsilon}-f_j$ and its first derivatives and tends to zero uniformly. The first sum is a convex combination. Hence $|D_hf_{j,\varepsilon}|\leq\|D_hf_j\|_{L^\infty}+1$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

\emph{Step 2: smoothing the minimum.} For $\delta>0$ set
\[
 F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
       =-\delta\log\sum_{j=1}^r
                    \mathrm e^{-f_{j,\varepsilon}/\delta}.
\]
By \cref{lem:soft-minimum-trace}, $\min_jf_{j,\varepsilon}-\delta\log r\leq F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq\min_jf_{j,\varepsilon}$ and $\sup_XD_hF_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq\max_j\sup_XD_hf_{j,\varepsilon}$. Choose sequences $\varepsilon_k,\delta_k\to0$ with $\varepsilon_k$ small as in Step 1, and put $F_k=F_{\varepsilon_k,\delta_k}$. Then $F_k\to\min_jf_j$ uniformly on $X$, since $|\min_jf_{j,\varepsilon_k}-\min_jf_j|\leq\max_j\|f_{j,\varepsilon_k}-f_j\|_\infty$, while $\|F_k\|_\infty\leq\max_j\|f_j\|_\infty+\delta_k\log r$ and $\sup_XD_hF_k\leq\max_j\|D_hf_j\|_{L^\infty}+1$ remain bounded.

\emph{Step 3: passage to the limit.} By \eqref{eq:penalized-envelope-laplacian-bound}, applied to the smooth obstacles $F_k$, we have $-\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk\leq D_hP_\kappa(F_k)\leq2B_k\mathrm e^{2A\|F_k\|_\infty}$ in the sense of distributions, where $A$ depends only on $X$, $\omega$, $\kappa$, and $B_k=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk+\sup_XD_hF_k$. By Step 2 these bounds are uniform in $k$ and depend only on the asserted data. By the stability of envelopes in \cref{prop:obstacle-envelope-maximal-member}(4), $\|P_\kappa(F_k)-u\|_\infty\leq\|F_k-\min_jf_j\|_\infty\to0$. Therefore $D_hP_\kappa(F_k)\to D_hu$ in the sense of distributions, and the same two-sided bound holds for $D_hu$: for every smooth function $\chi\geq0$, $\langle D_hu,\chi\rangle$ lies between $-c_1\int_X\chi\,\mathrm dV$ and $c_2\int_X\chi\,\mathrm dV$, where $c_1=\sup_X\operatorname{tr}_hk$ and $c_2=\sup_k2B_k\mathrm e^{2A\|F_k\|_\infty}$. In a chart, where $\mathrm dV=\varrho\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ with $\varrho$ smooth and positive, it follows that the real distribution $D_hu$, paired with test functions through Lebesgue measure, satisfies $|\langle D_hu,\chi\rangle|\leq c\int\chi\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ for nonnegative test functions $\chi$, where $c=\max\{c_1,c_2\}$. By \cref{cor:distribution-bounded-density}(1), $D_hu$, and hence $Lu=-4\pi D_hu$, is a bounded function with the asserted bound. The function $u$ is continuous, as a uniform limit of the functions $P_\kappa(F_k)$, which are continuous by \cref{prop:penalized-envelope-uniform-limit}; so \cref{prop:compact-metric-laplacian-lp} shows that $u\in C^{1,\alpha}(X)$ for every $0<\alpha<1$. Finally, $u$ is a continuous $\kappa$-psh function with bounded metric Laplacian, so by \cref{prop:metric-laplacian-regular-contact-density}, applied with $U=X$ and $\theta_j=\kappa$, $u_j=f_j=u$ for all $j$, the coefficients of $\kappa+\ddc u$ are bounded on every coordinate ball $W\Subset X$, hence on $X$.
\end{proof}
