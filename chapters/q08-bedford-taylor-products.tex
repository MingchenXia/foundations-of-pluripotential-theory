\chapter{Bedford--Taylor products and local capacity}
\label{chap:q08}

Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^n$. By \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, $\ddc u$ is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current, but in general its coefficients are only measures, and products of measures have no general meaning; so the exterior powers of $\ddc u$ cannot be formed by multiplying coefficients. For $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ the top power is the Monge--Amp\`ere measure $(\ddc u)^n=n!\,\pi^{-n}\det(u_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}$, a fully nonlinear expression in the second derivatives of $u$, which plays in pluripotential theory the role that the Laplacian plays in classical potential theory. Bedford and Taylor showed that for locally bounded $u$ these powers, and more generally the mixed products $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ with a closed positive current $T$, can be defined by induction: since $T$ has measure coefficients and $u$ is a locally bounded Borel function, the product $uT$ makes sense, and one puts $\ddc u\wedge T=\ddc(uT)$. This chapter develops the local theory of these products. Their factors are always locally bounded; products of unbounded potentials are treated in \cref{chap:q09}.

The first two sections establish the basic properties of the products: positivity, the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg mass bounds, continuity along decreasing sequences and symmetry (\cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}), and the comparison principle. The products then define the Monge--Amp\`ere capacity, a complex analogue of the relative capacity of \cref{chap:p04}. Plurisubharmonic functions are quasi-continuous with respect to it, and the products are continuous under convergence in capacity and along increasing sequences. Solving the homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere equation on a ball, we show that a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function is maximal if and only if its Monge--Amp\`ere measure vanishes. These are the tools with which \cref{chap:q03} characterizes pluripolar sets.

The remaining sections, needed for non-pluripolar products and envelopes on compact manifolds in \cref{chap:q09,chap:q10}, solve the equation $(\ddc u)^n=\mu$ on a ball for increasingly singular measures $\mu$: continuous densities, bounded densities, and finally compactly supported measures dominated by the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded plurisubharmonic function. The main application is a lower bound for mixed products: if $(\ddc u_i)^n\geq f_i\mu$ for $1\le i\le n$, then $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n\geq(f_1\cdots f_n)^{1/n}\mu$ (\cref{thm:bounded-multifactor-common-measure}). The chapter closes with the locality of complex Hessians on contact sets and a chain rule for gradients against positive currents.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in \cref{sec:history-bounded-products}.

\section{Bounded potentials and recursive products}
\label{sec:bt-construction}

Throughout this section and the next, $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ is open, $n\ge1$, and $T$ is a closed positive current on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, where $0\le r\le n$ is an integer; thus $T$ has bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$. Let $\beta=(\mathrm{i}/2)\sum_j\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j$ be the form of \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}. For a positive current $S$ of bidimension $(s,s)$ on $\Omega$ and a compact set $K\subset\Omega$ we write
\[
 \|S\|_K=\int_KS\wedge\frac{\beta^s}{s!}
\]
for the mass of $S$ on $K$; it is $1/s!$ times the value on $K$ of the trace measure of $S$ (\cref{def:positive-current}). By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, $S$ is a current of order zero, and the total variations of its coefficient measures are bounded by a constant multiple, depending only on $n$ and $s$, of its trace measure. Since plurisubharmonic functions are upper semicontinuous, they are bounded above on compact sets; hence a plurisubharmonic function is locally bounded if and only if it is bounded below on compact sets.

\begin{definition}\label{def:bedford-taylor-product}
Suppose that $r\ge1$, and let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. Let $uT$ be the current of order zero obtained by multiplying the coefficient measures of $T$ by the locally bounded Borel function $u$ (\cref{sec:positive-currents}), and define
\[
 \ddc u\wedge T=\ddc(uT).
\]
For an integer $1\le q\le r$ and locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$, define recursively
\[
 \ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 =\ddc\bigl(u_1\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\bigr).
\]
Here $\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ is a closed positive current, hence of order zero, by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion} below applied to $q-1$ factors; so its product with $u_1$ is defined as before. The product with no factors is $T$. When $T=1$ (so that $r=n$), $q=n$ and $u_1=\cdots=u_n=u$, the resulting positive current of bidegree $(n,n)$ is a positive measure, the \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere measure} $(\ddc u)^n$ of $u$.
\end{definition}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product}\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!locally bounded potentials}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-positive-recursion}
Every product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product} is a closed positive current, and it has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are smooth, it coincides with the exterior product of the smooth forms $\ddc u_j$ with $T$.
\item The construction is local: if $u_j=v_j$ for every $j$ and $T=T'$ on an open set $U\subset\Omega$, then the two products agree on $U$.
\item It is additive and positively homogeneous in each $u_j$ and in $T$, whenever the summands are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, respectively closed positive currents of bidimension $(r,r)$, and it does not change if a real constant is added to some $u_j$.
\item If $\gamma$ is a smooth closed form of bidegree $(s,s)$, $q\le r-s$, such that $T\wedge\gamma$ is positive (for instance, $\gamma$ strongly positive), then
\[
 \ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge(T\wedge\gamma)=(\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T)\wedge\gamma.
\]
\item It is invariant under biholomorphic maps: if $F\colon\Omega'\to\Omega$ is biholomorphic and $F^*$ denotes the pullback of currents of \cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback}, then
\[
 F^*\bigl(\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\bigr)=\ddc(u_1\circ F)\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc(u_q\circ F)\wedge F^*T.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first record two consequences of the Leibniz rule of \cref{sec:positive-currents}. Let $R$ be a current of even degree on an open subset of $\mathbb C^n$ such that $\partial R=\bar\partial R=0$; this holds if $R$ is closed and of pure bidegree. If $f$ is a smooth function, then $\mathrm d(fR)=\mathrm df\wedge R$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(fR)=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge R$, and applying $\mathrm d$ to the latter gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz}
 \ddc(fR)=\ddc f\wedge R.
\end{equation}
If $\gamma$ is a smooth closed form of pure bidegree, then $\partial\gamma$ and $\bar\partial\gamma$ are the components of $\mathrm d\gamma=0$ of two different bidegrees, so they vanish, and the Leibniz rule gives $\ddc(R'\wedge\gamma)=\ddc R'\wedge\gamma$ for every current $R'$.

We now treat a single factor $u$. Its regularizations $u_\varepsilon=u*\rho_\varepsilon$ by the radial mollifiers of \cref{sec:convolution} are smooth on $\Omega_\varepsilon$, and their complex Hessians are the regularizations of the positive current $\ddc u$ (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}), which are positive by the discussion of regularization following \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}. Hence $(u_\varepsilon)_{j\bar k}$ is positive semidefinite at every point, and $u_\varepsilon$ is plurisubharmonic by the criterion for smooth functions stated after \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. By \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative} and \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}, $u_\varepsilon$ decreases to $u$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. On each compact set the $u_\varepsilon$ are bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$, because $u\le u_\varepsilon\le u_{\varepsilon_0}$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ and $u$ is locally bounded. Dominated convergence with respect to the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ therefore gives $u_\varepsilon T\to uT$ weakly. By \eqref{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz}, $\ddc(u_\varepsilon T)=\ddc u_\varepsilon\wedge T$. The smooth $(1,1)$-form $\ddc u_\varepsilon$ has a positive semidefinite coefficient matrix, so it is strongly positive, since weak and strong positivity agree in bidegree $(1,1)$ (\cref{sec:positive-currents}); hence the product of the positive current $T$ with it is positive; it is also closed. As $\ddc$ is continuous for weak convergence, $\ddc u_\varepsilon\wedge T\to\ddc(uT)$ weakly, and positivity and closedness pass to the limit. Thus $\ddc u\wedge T$ is positive and closed. Applying this with $T$ replaced by the closed positive current $\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$, we obtain the first assertion by induction on $q$.

(1) If the $u_j$ are smooth, \eqref{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz} shows at each step of the recursion that the product is the exterior product of smooth forms with $T$.

(2), (3) The definition uses only multiplication of coefficient measures by functions and the operator $\ddc$; both operations are local and linear, and no product of two measures occurs. This gives locality, and additivity and positive homogeneity in $u_1$ and in $T$; for the other factors they follow by induction on $q$, since multiplication by $u_1$ and $\ddc$ are linear. If $c$ is a constant and $R=\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$, then $\ddc((u_1+c)R)=\ddc(u_1R)+c\,\ddc R=\ddc(u_1R)$, since $R$ is closed; for the other factors the invariance under constants follows again by induction.

(4) The product $T\wedge\gamma$ is closed, and multiplication of coefficient measures by $u_1$ commutes with the exterior product by $\gamma$. By the identity $\ddc(R'\wedge\gamma)=\ddc R'\wedge\gamma$ and induction on $q$, every step of the recursion commutes with $\wedge\gamma$.

(5) The pullback $F^*T$ is closed and positive and commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, hence with $\ddc$ (\cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback}), and the functions $u_j\circ F$ are locally bounded and plurisubharmonic (\cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}). We claim that $F^*(uT)=(u\circ F)F^*T$ for a locally bounded plurisubharmonic $u$. For a smooth function $f$ we have $F^*(fT)=(f\circ F)F^*T$: both sides are weakly continuous in $T$, they agree when $T$ is smooth, and on every relatively compact open subset of $\Omega$ the current $T$ is the weak limit of its smooth regularizations (discussion following \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}). Applying this to the regularizations $u_\varepsilon$ on relatively compact open subsets, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, we obtain the claim: $u_\varepsilon T\to uT$ as shown above, so $F^*(u_\varepsilon T)\to F^*(uT)$, while $(u_\varepsilon\circ F)F^*T\to(u\circ F)F^*T$ by dominated convergence with respect to the coefficient measures of the positive current $F^*T$. Consequently every step of the recursion commutes with $F^*$, which proves~(5). In particular, by~(2) and~(5), products of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions with closed positive currents on a complex manifold can be computed in holomorphic charts: the products defined in two charts agree on their overlap.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!positivity and locality}

Let $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ be real, and write $a_{jk}=u_{j\bar k}$, so that $\ddc u=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}. The $2$-forms $\alpha_{jk}=\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ commute with each other, and $\alpha_{j_1k_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{j_nk_n}=0$ unless $(j_1,\ldots,j_n)$ and $(k_1,\ldots,k_n)$ are permutations of $(1,\ldots,n)$. If $j_i=\sigma(i)$ and $k_i=\tau(i)$ for permutations $\sigma,\tau$, we may reorder the commuting factors so that the first indices increase; with $\pi=\tau\circ\sigma^{-1}$ we get $\mathrm dz_1\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_{\pi(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_{\pi(n)}$, and permuting the $1$-forms $\mathrm d\bar z_{\pi(i)}$ among themselves gives the sign $\operatorname{sgn}\pi$. Hence
\[
 \Bigl(\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}\alpha_{jk}\Bigr)^n
 =\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\prod_{i=1}^na_{\sigma(i)\tau(i)}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\tau\sigma^{-1})\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^n\alpha_{jj}
 =n!\det(a_{jk})\bigwedge_{j=1}^n\alpha_{jj},
\]
because for each of the $n!$ permutations $\sigma$ the sum over $\tau$ is $\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}\pi\prod_ja_{j\pi(j)}=\det(a_{jk})$. Since $\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j=2\,\mathrm{d}x_j\wedge\mathrm{d}y_j$, we have $\bigwedge_j(\mathrm i\alpha_{jj})=2^n\mathrm dV_{2n}$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}
 (\ddc u)^n=\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\Bigr)^nn!\det(u_{j\bar k})\bigwedge_{j=1}^n\alpha_{jj}=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\det(u_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}.
\end{equation}
Here $(\ddc u)^n$ is the exterior power of the continuous form $\ddc u$; when $u$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic, it is the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $u$, by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(1), and the same holds for plurisubharmonic $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ (see the remark after \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}). In particular $\ddc|z|^2=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_j\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j=\beta/\pi$ and $(\ddc|z|^2)^n=n!\pi^{-n}\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$. (The formula also holds for $n=0$: the empty product is the constant $1$, and both sides are the unit mass $\mathrm{d}V_0$ on the point $\mathbb C^0$.)
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!determinant formula}

\begin{theorem}[Chern--Levine--Nirenberg]\label{thm:cln-bounded}
Let $K,L\subset\Omega$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $0\le q\le r$ be an integer. There is a constant $C_{K,L,q}$, depending only on $K$, $L$, $n$ and $q$, such that
\[
 \|\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\|_K
 \le C_{K,L,q}\|T\|_L\prod_{j=1}^q\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}
\]
for every closed positive current $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ on $\Omega$ and all locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$. Here $\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}=\sup_L|u_j|$ is the pointwise supremum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $q=0$ the estimate holds with constant $1$, since $K\subset L$. Let $q=1$ and write $u=u_1$. By \cref{lem:mollifier-density} there is a smooth function $0\le\chi\le1$ with compact support in $\Int L$ which equals $1$ on a neighborhood of $K$; it depends only on $K$ and $L$. Since $\ddc u\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ is a positive measure, $\beta$ is closed, and $(\ddc S)(\chi)=S(\ddc\chi)$ for currents $S$ of bidimension $(1,1)$, we have
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_K\ddc u\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}
 &\le\int\chi\,\ddc(uT)\wedge\beta^{r-1}
 =\bigl(\ddc(uT\wedge\beta^{r-1})\bigr)(\chi)\\
 &=\int uT\wedge\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}
 \le C\|u\|_{L^\infty(L)}\|T\|_L.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The form $\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ is smooth with support in $L$, though in general not positive; its coefficients are bounded by a constant depending only on $\chi$ and $n$. The last inequality therefore holds because the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ on $L$ are bounded by a constant multiple of $\|T\|_L$, the multiple depending only on $n$ and $r$ (\cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}). Dividing by $(r-1)!$ and taking the largest of the constants obtained for $r=1,\ldots,n$, we obtain the case $q=1$ with a constant depending only on $K$, $L$ and $n$.

For $q>1$, let $\delta>0$ be the distance from $K$ to $\mathbb C^n\setminus\Int L$ (if $K=\varnothing$ there is nothing to prove), and put $L_j=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,K)\le j\delta/(q+1)\}$ for $0\le j<q$ and $L_q=L$. These are compact sets, depending only on $K$, $L$ and $q$, with $K=L_0$ and $L_{j-1}\subset\Int L_j$ for $1\le j\le q$. By \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}, the currents $S_j=\ddc u_{j+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$, $0\le j\le q$, are positive and closed, and $S_{j-1}=\ddc u_j\wedge S_j$. The case $q=1$, applied to $S_j$ and the pair $L_{j-1}\subset\Int L_j$, gives $\|S_{j-1}\|_{L_{j-1}}\le C_j\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}\|S_j\|_{L_j}$ for $j=1,\ldots,q$, since $\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L_j)}\le\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}$. Combining these $q$ inequalities, we obtain the estimate with $C_{K,L,q}=C_1\cdots C_q$.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality}

For continuous potentials, uniform convergence is enough to pass to the limit in the products.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}
Let $T_k\to T$ weakly, where $T_k$ and $T$ are closed positive currents on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, and let $0\le q\le r$. For $1\le j\le q$, let $(u_{j,k})_{k\ge1}$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ converging locally uniformly, as $k\to\infty$, to a continuous function $u_j$, and let $(u_{0,k})_{k\ge1}$ be locally bounded Borel functions on $\Omega$ converging locally uniformly to a continuous function $u_0$. Then $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are plurisubharmonic, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T,\\
 u_{0,k}\ddc u_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly as $k\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first show that $u_j$ is plurisubharmonic for $1\le j\le q$. Since plurisubharmonicity is a local property, it suffices to prove this on every ball $W\Subset\Omega$. Choose $k_1<k_2<\cdots$ such that $|u_{j,k_m}-u_j|\le2^{-m}$ on $W$, and put $w_m=u_{j,k_m}+3\cdot2^{-m}$. These functions are plurisubharmonic on $W$, and they decrease, because
\[
 w_{m+1}\le u_j+2^{-m-1}+3\cdot2^{-m-1}=u_j+2^{1-m}\le u_{j,k_m}+2^{-m}+2^{1-m}=w_m.
\]
Their limit $u_j$ is finite, so it is plurisubharmonic on $W$ by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}.

Since the $u_{j,k}$ converge locally uniformly to continuous functions, they are bounded on each compact set uniformly in $k$. Since $T_k\to T$ weakly, the masses $\|T_k\|_L$ are bounded for each compact set $L$: if $\chi\in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$ satisfies $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi=1$ on $L$, then $\|T_k\|_L\le T_k(\chi\beta^r)/r!$, and the right-hand side converges. By \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, the masses of all the products considered below are therefore bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and so are the total variations of their coefficient measures (\cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}).

We claim: if $S_k\to S$ weakly, where $S_k$ and $S$ are closed positive currents whose masses are bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and if locally bounded Borel functions $v_k$ converge locally uniformly to a continuous function $v$, then $v_kS_k\to vS$ weakly. Applying this to $S_k=\ddc u_{2,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k$ and $v_k=u_{1,k}$, and then $\ddc$, which is weakly continuous, proves the first assertion by induction on $q$; the case $q=0$ is the hypothesis. The second assertion is the claim with $v_k=u_{0,k}$.

To prove the claim, fix a test form $\varphi$ with compact support $K$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{lem:mollifier-density}(1), applied to $v$ on a relatively compact open neighborhood of $K$, there is a smooth function $v_\varepsilon$ on a neighborhood of $K$ with $|v-v_\varepsilon|\le\varepsilon$ on $K$. Write
\[
 v_kS_k-vS=(v_k-v)S_k+(v-v_\varepsilon)S_k
             +v_\varepsilon(S_k-S)+(v_\varepsilon-v)S.
\]
Evaluated on $\varphi$, a term $fS_k$ with a bounded Borel function $f$ is at most $C_\varphi\sup_K|f|$ times the total variation on $K$ of the coefficient measures of $S_k$, where $C_\varphi$ bounds the coefficients of $\varphi$; the same holds for $S$. Hence the first term tends to zero, since $v_k\to v$ uniformly on $K$; the second and fourth terms are bounded by a constant times $\varepsilon$, uniformly in $k$; and the third tends to zero as $k\to\infty$, since $v_\varepsilon\varphi$ is a test form. Letting first $k\to\infty$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!uniformly convergent potentials}

If $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ is plurisubharmonic, its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is given by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}. Indeed, the regularizations $u_\varepsilon$ are smooth and plurisubharmonic and converge to $u$ locally uniformly, and their second derivatives are the regularizations of the continuous second derivatives of $u$, so they also converge locally uniformly (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}(1)). Hence $\det((u_\varepsilon)_{j\bar k})\to\det(u_{j\bar k})$ locally uniformly, and \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} for $u_\varepsilon$, together with \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, gives the formula for $u$.

\section{Decreasing continuity and symmetry}
\label{sec:bt-monotone}

A bounded plurisubharmonic function need not be continuous (an example is given after \cref{prop:bt-maximum-locality}), so in general it cannot be approximated uniformly by smooth plurisubharmonic functions, since a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous; it is, however, the limit of the decreasing sequence of its regularizations. The main result of this section, \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, states that the products are continuous along decreasing sequences and symmetric in their factors. Upper semicontinuity bounds every weak limit of weighted products from above (\cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}). To prove equality, we compare total masses by an integration by parts, after modifying all functions so that they coincide with one fixed smooth function near the boundary of a ball (\cref{lem:bt-common-collar}).

We use the real operator $\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}$ of \eqref{eq:dc-operator}, for which $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}=\ddc$. If $a$ and $b$ are smooth real functions, the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}b$ is $\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}(\partial a\wedge\bar\partial b+\partial b\wedge\bar\partial a)$, which is symmetric in $a$ and $b$. Only this part survives multiplication by a current $R$ of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, so
\[
 \mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R=\mathrm{d}b\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}a\wedge R.
\]
Moreover, the $(2,0)$- and $(0,2)$-parts of $\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}a$ are multiples of $\partial a\wedge\partial a=0$ and $\bar\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a=0$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt-gradient-form}
 \mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}a=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial a\wedge\bar\partial a;
\end{equation}
this is a positive $(1,1)$-form, because $\bar\partial a=\overline{\partial a}$ for real $a$.

If $B$ is a ball, a \emph{collar} of $\partial B$ is an open neighborhood of the sphere $\partial B$.
\index{collar of a sphere}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bt-common-collar}
Let $B$ be a ball and $R$ a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Let $a$ be a smooth function and $b$ a bounded plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and suppose that $a=b$ on a collar of $\partial B$. Then
\[
 \int_B a\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int_B b\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
In particular one may take $R=S\wedge\gamma$, where $S$ is a closed positive current, $\gamma$ is a smooth closed form of pure bidegree, and $S\wedge\gamma$ is positive of bidimension $(1,1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both integrals are finite, since $a$ and $b$ are bounded on $B$ and $\ddc a\wedge R$ and $\ddc b\wedge R$ are measures of finite total variation near $\overline B$. The function $h=b-a$ is bounded and Borel, and it vanishes on a collar $V$ of $\partial B$; hence its restriction to $B$ vanishes outside the compact set $K_0=\overline B\setminus V\subset B$. On $B$ we have $bR=aR+hR$, and since $R$ is closed, $\ddc(aR)=\ddc a\wedge R$ by \eqref{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz}; thus
\[
 \ddc b\wedge R=\ddc a\wedge R+\ddc(hR)\quad\text{on }B,
\]
where $\ddc(hR)$ is a signed measure with support in $K_0$. Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of $K_0$. By the definition of $\ddc$ on currents,
\[
 \int_Ba\,\ddc(hR)=\int\chi a\,\ddc(hR)=\int h\,\ddc(\chi a)\wedge R=\int_Bh\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int_Ba\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int_Ba\,\ddc a\wedge R+\int_Bh\,\ddc a\wedge R=\int_Bb\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
The last assertion holds because $S\wedge\gamma$ is then closed, by the Leibniz rule, and positive of bidimension $(1,1)$; moreover $\ddc b\wedge(S\wedge\gamma)=(\ddc b\wedge S)\wedge\gamma$ by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(4).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}
Let $\mu_j\to\mu$ weakly, where $\mu_j$ and $\mu$ are positive Radon measures on $\Omega$, and let $f_j$ be real-valued upper semicontinuous functions on $\Omega$ which decrease to a locally bounded function $f$. If $f_{j_k}\mu_{j_k}\to\nu$ weakly along a subsequence, then $\nu\le f\mu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\chi\ge0$ be a continuous function with compact support, let $N$ be a compact neighborhood of $\Supp\chi$, and put $W=\Int N$. The function $f_1$ is upper semicontinuous, hence bounded above on $N$, and $f$ is bounded below on $N$; since $f\le f_j\le f_1$, there is $c\ge0$ with $|f_j|\le c$ on $N$ for all $j$. The positive Radon measures $(f_{j_k}+c)\mu_{j_k}$ on $W$ converge weakly to $\nu+c\mu$. By \cref{prop:positive-distribution-convergence}, $\nu+c\mu$ is a positive Radon measure on $W$, and the convergence is vague. Since also $\mu_j\to\mu$ vaguely, we obtain $\int\psi f_{j_k}\,\mathrm d\mu_{j_k}\to\int\psi\,\mathrm d\nu$ for every continuous function $\psi$ with compact support in $W$.

Fix $l$. By \cref{lem:usc-majorants}, there are bounded continuous functions $g_m$ on $N$ decreasing to $f_l$. For $j_k\ge l$ we have $f_{j_k}\le f_l\le g_m$ on $N$, so
\[
 \int\chi f_{j_k}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j_k}\le\int\chi g_m\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j_k}.
\]
Letting $k\to\infty$ gives $\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le\int\chi g_m\,\mathrm{d}\mu$. Since $-c\le f_l\le g_m\le g_1$ on $N$ and $\mu(N)<\infty$, bounded convergence as $m\to\infty$ gives $\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le\int\chi f_l\,\mathrm{d}\mu$, and bounded convergence as $l\to\infty$ gives $\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$. Since $\chi\ge0$ was arbitrary, $\nu\le f\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-maximum}
There are smooth convex functions $M_\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$, $\varepsilon>0$, increasing in each variable, such that $M_\varepsilon\downarrow\max$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $\max\le M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$, and $M_\varepsilon(s,t)=\max\{s,t\}$ when $|s-t|>2\varepsilon$. If $a$ and $b$ are smooth plurisubharmonic functions, so is $M_\varepsilon(a,b)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be the mollifier of \cref{sec:convolution} on $\mathbb{R}^2$; it is a nonnegative even radial probability density supported in the disc of radius $\varepsilon$. Put $M_\varepsilon=\max*\rho_\varepsilon$, that is, $M_\varepsilon(x)=\int\max(x-y)\rho_\varepsilon(y)\,\mathrm dy$, where $\max(s,t)=\max\{s,t\}$; it is smooth, since $\rho_\varepsilon$ is (\cref{sec:convolution}). Since $\rho_\varepsilon\ge0$ has integral $1$, and $x\mapsto\max(x-y)$ is convex and increasing in each variable for each $y$, the function $M_\varepsilon$ is convex and increasing in each variable. Since $\rho_\varepsilon$ is even, $\int y_i\rho_\varepsilon(y)\,\mathrm dy=0$ for $i=1,2$; integrating $\max(s-y_1,t-y_2)\ge s-y_1$ and $\max(s-y_1,t-y_2)\ge t-y_2$ against $\rho_\varepsilon$ therefore gives $M_\varepsilon(s,t)\ge s$ and $M_\varepsilon(s,t)\ge t$, that is, $M_\varepsilon\ge\max$. Since $|\max(x-y)-\max(x)|\le|y|\le\varepsilon$ on the support of $\rho_\varepsilon$, we have $M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$. If $s-t>2\varepsilon$ and $|y|\le\varepsilon$, then $(s-y_1)-(t-y_2)\ge s-t-2\varepsilon>0$, so $M_\varepsilon(s,t)=\int(s-y_1)\rho_\varepsilon(y)\,\mathrm dy=s$; similarly $M_\varepsilon(s,t)=t$ if $t-s>2\varepsilon$. For fixed $x\in\mathbb R^2$, the function $g(\varepsilon)=\int\max(x-\varepsilon y)\rho_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}y$, defined for all real $\varepsilon$, equals $M_\varepsilon(x)$ for $\varepsilon>0$ and $\max(x)$ for $\varepsilon=0$; it is convex, since $\varepsilon\mapsto\max(x-\varepsilon y)$ is convex, and even, since $\rho_1$ is even. For $0\le\varepsilon_1<\varepsilon_2$, writing $\varepsilon_1=\lambda(-\varepsilon_2)+(1-\lambda)\varepsilon_2$ with $\lambda\in(0,1]$, we get $g(\varepsilon_1)\le\lambda g(-\varepsilon_2)+(1-\lambda)g(\varepsilon_2)=g(\varepsilon_2)$. Hence $M_\varepsilon$ increases with $\varepsilon$, and together with $\max\le M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$ this gives $M_\varepsilon\downarrow\max$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Finally, let $a$ and $b$ be smooth and plurisubharmonic, and write $(a_1,a_2)=(a,b)$. Denoting by $\partial_kM_\varepsilon$ and $\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon$ the partial derivatives evaluated at $(a,b)$, the chain rule gives
\[
 \mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial M_\varepsilon(a,b)
 =\sum_{k=1}^2\partial_kM_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial a_k
 +\mathrm{i}\sum_{k,l=1}^2\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon\,\partial a_k\wedge\bar\partial a_l.
\]
The first sum is positive because $\partial_kM_\varepsilon\ge0$, $M_\varepsilon$ being increasing in each variable, and the forms $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial a_k$ are positive. For the second, note that the real Hessian $H=(\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon)$ is positive semidefinite: for $v\in\mathbb R^2$, the function $t\mapsto M_\varepsilon(x+tv)$ is convex, so its second derivative $v^tHv$ is nonnegative. Writing $H=\sum_m\lambda_mv_mv_m^t$ with $\lambda_m\ge0$ and real orthonormal vectors $v_m$, and putting $\theta_m=\sum_k(v_m)_k\partial a_k$, we get $\mathrm i\sum_{k,l}\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon\,\partial a_k\wedge\bar\partial a_l=\sum_m\lambda_m\mathrm i\theta_m\wedge\bar\theta_m$, because $\bar\partial a_l=\overline{\partial a_l}$ for real $a_l$; this is a positive $(1,1)$-form. Hence the complex Hessian of $M_\varepsilon(a,b)$ is positive semidefinite, and $M_\varepsilon(a,b)$ is plurisubharmonic by the criterion for smooth functions stated after \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bedford--Taylor]\label{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}
Let $T$ be a closed positive current on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, and let $0\le q\le r$ be an integer. For $0\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a decreasing sequence of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ whose limit $u_l$ is locally bounded (and therefore plurisubharmonic, by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}). Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T,\\
 u_{0,j}\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly as $j\to\infty$. Moreover, the product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions is symmetric in $u_1,\ldots,u_q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the induction.} We prove the second (weighted) assertion by induction on $q$, simultaneously for all open sets $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, all $r\ge q$, all closed positive currents $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ on $\Omega$ and all decreasing sequences as in the statement. For $q=0$ it states that $u_{0,j}T\to u_0T$; this follows from dominated convergence with respect to the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$, since $u_0\le u_{0,j}\le u_{0,1}$. Let $q\ge1$ and assume the weighted assertion for fewer than $q$ factors. Applying it to the $q-1$ factors $u_{2,j},\ldots,u_{q,j}$ with the weight $u_{1,j}$, and then applying the weakly continuous operator $\ddc$, we obtain the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. Next, let $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ be locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, and apply the unweighted assertion on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ to their regularizations $u_l*\rho_{\varepsilon_k}$, where $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ and $\varepsilon_k<\varepsilon$; these are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ decreasing to $u_l$ (see the proof of \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}). It shows that on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ the product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ is the weak limit of exterior products of smooth forms with $T$ (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(1)), which are symmetric in the factors because $2$-forms commute. Since the sets $\Omega_\varepsilon$ exhaust $\Omega$, products with $q$ factors are symmetric. It remains to prove the weighted assertion for $q$ factors, and in doing so we may use the unweighted assertion and the symmetry for $q$ factors, as well as the weighted assertion for fewer factors.

\emph{Step 2: reduction to a common collar.} Weak convergence is a local property: if currents converge weakly on each member of an open cover of $\Omega$, they converge weakly on $\Omega$, because a partition of unity subordinate to the cover (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}) writes every test form as a finite sum of test forms supported in members of the cover. Fix a ball $B=B(a,R)$, a larger ball $B_1$ with $\overline B\subset B_1\Subset\Omega$, and a compact set $K\subset B$; we prove the convergence on a neighborhood of $K$. Since $u_l\le u_{l,j}\le u_{l,1}$, all the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ are bounded on $B_1$ uniformly in $j$. Subtracting a constant from $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ changes neither the Hessians nor, since the unweighted assertion is already known, the validity of the weighted assertion. We may therefore assume that $-N\le u_{l,j},u_l\le-1$ on $B_1$ for all $l$ and $j$, where $N\ge1$. Put $\psi=|z-a|^2-R^2$, choose $\delta>0$ with $K\subset\{\psi<-\delta\}$, and set $A=N/\delta$. Replace every function $v$ among the $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ by $\max\{v,A\psi\}$, which is plurisubharmonic on $B_1$ by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, as a maximum of two plurisubharmonic functions. Since $A\psi<-N\le v$ on $\{\psi<-\delta\}$, the functions are unchanged there, and by locality so are their products. Since $v\le-1<A\psi$ on $V=\{z\in B_1:\psi(z)>-1/A\}$, which is a collar of $\partial B$, all modified functions coincide with $A\psi$ on $V$. The modified sequences still decrease to the modified limits, and on $B$ they take values in $[-N,0)$. Hence we may assume, in addition, that all the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ coincide with $A\psi$ on $V$ and are negative on $B$.

\emph{Step 3: smooth approximation with the same collar.} Fix $\eta>0$ such that $\{\psi\le\eta\}\subset B_1$, and let $V'=\{z:-1/(2A)<\psi(z)<\eta\}$; it is a collar of $\partial B$ contained in $V$. Fix $l$ and $j$, and let $\tilde u$ be the function $u_{l,j}$ before the modification of Step 2, so that $\tilde u\le-1$ on $B_1$ and $u_{l,j}=\max\{\tilde u,A\psi\}$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ smaller than the distance from $\{\psi\le\eta\}$ to $\partial B_1$, put
\[
 u^\varepsilon_{l,j}=M_\varepsilon(\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon,A\psi),
\]
with $M_\varepsilon$ as in \cref{lem:smooth-maximum}. These are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on $\{\psi<\eta\}$. Since $\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon\le-1$, on $V'$ we have $A\psi-\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon>1/2>2\varepsilon$, and therefore $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}=A\psi$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon$ decreases to $\tilde u$ and $M_\varepsilon$ decreases to $\max$; since $M_\varepsilon$ is increasing in each variable and $\max\le M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$, it follows that $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}$ decreases to $\max\{\tilde u,A\psi\}=u_{l,j}$. In particular $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}\ge u_{l,j}\ge u_l$, and all the functions $u_{l,j}$, $u_l$ and $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}$ coincide with $A\psi$ on $V'$.

\emph{Step 4: an upper bound for weak limits.} Put
\[
 S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T,\qquad
 S=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T.
\]
By Step 1, $S_j\to S$ weakly. On $B$ the weights $u_{0,j}$ take values in $[-N,0)$, so the currents $-u_{0,j}S_j$ are positive there, and by \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, since $|u_{l,j}|\le N$ on $B_1$, their masses on compact subsets of $B$ are bounded uniformly in $j$. By the compactness part of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly on $B$. Let $\Theta$ be the limit of such a subsequence. Let $\varphi$ be a strongly positive test form on $B$ of complementary bidegree. The measures $\mu_j=S_j\wedge\varphi$ are positive and converge weakly to $S\wedge\varphi$, and along the chosen subsequence $u_{0,j}\mu_j\to\Theta\wedge\varphi$. Hence \cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}, applied on $B$ with the real-valued upper semicontinuous functions $f_j=u_{0,j}$, gives $\Theta\wedge\varphi\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$. Evaluating this inequality of measures on a nonnegative test function equal to $1$ on $\Supp\varphi$, we get $(u_0S-\Theta)(\varphi)\ge0$; thus $u_0S-\Theta$ is a positive current on $B$. By locality (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}), all the currents $u_{0,j}S_j$, and also $u_0S$, coincide on $V'$ with the fixed current $A\psi\,(A\ddc\psi)^q\wedge T$; hence so does $\Theta$, and $u_0S-\Theta$ has compact support in $B$.

\emph{Step 5: comparison of masses.} Set $\gamma=\beta^{r-q}$ and
\[
 I=\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma,
 \qquad I_j=\int_Bu_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
We claim that $I\le I_j$ for every $j$. Fix $j$ and parameters $\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_q$, and abbreviate $w_l=u^{\varepsilon_l}_{l,j}$. For $0\le k\le q$ put
\[
 J_k=\int_Bu_k\,\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma,
\]
so that $J_0=I$. The measure in $J_k$ is positive and $u_k\le w_k$, so $J_k$ does not decrease if $u_k$ is replaced by $w_k$. If $k<q$, symmetry of products with $q$ factors and \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(4) let us write that measure as $\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge R_k$, where
\[
 R_k=\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge\ddc u_{k+2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma
\]
is a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on $\{\psi<\eta\}$; then \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, with $a=w_k$ and $b=u_{k+1}$, which both equal $A\psi$ on the collar $V'$, gives
\[
 J_k\le\int_Bw_k\,\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge R_k=\int_Bu_{k+1}\,\ddc w_k\wedge R_k=J_{k+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
 I=J_0\le J_1\le\cdots\le J_q\le\int_Bw_q\,\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{q-1}\wedge T\wedge\gamma.
\]

Now let $\varepsilon_q\downarrow0$, then $\varepsilon_{q-1}\downarrow0$, and so on down to $\varepsilon_1\downarrow0$, keeping $\varepsilon_0$ fixed. We claim that the right-hand side of the last inequality then tends to
\[
 \int_Bu_{q,j}\,\ddc w_0\wedge\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T\wedge\gamma.
\]
When $\varepsilon_q\downarrow0$, the measure is fixed and the bounded weights $w_q$ decrease to $u_{q,j}$, so dominated convergence applies. When $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ for some $1\le k\le q-1$, the integrand is, after moving the smooth factors next to $T$ by symmetry,
\[
 u_{q,j}\,\ddc u^{\varepsilon_k}_{k,j}\wedge\ddc u_{k+1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T_k\wedge\gamma,
\]
where $T_k=\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge T$ is a fixed closed positive current on $\{\psi<\eta\}$ of bidimension $(r-k,r-k)$. There are $q-k\le q-1$ Hessian factors and the fixed weight $u_{q,j}$. Along any sequence $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$, the functions $u^{\varepsilon_k}_{k,j}$ decrease to $u_{k,j}$ (Step 3), so the induction hypothesis, applied on $\{\psi<\eta\}$ with the base current $T_k$, constant sequences for the other factors and the constant weight $u_{q,j}$, shows that these currents converge weakly; so do their exterior products with $\gamma$. In each case the convergence holds for the integrals over $B$, and not only against compactly supported test forms, because all the currents involved coincide on the collar $V'$ with the same fixed current: if $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equals $1$ on $B\setminus V'$, the integrals against $\chi$ converge, and the integrals against $1-\chi$ do not depend on the parameters.

By \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, with $a=w_0$ and $b=u_{q,j}$, and by symmetry of products with $q$ factors, the limit integral equals
\[
 \int_Bw_0\,\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T\wedge\gamma
 =\int_Bu^{\varepsilon_0}_{0,j}\,S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
Hence $I\le\int_Bu^{\varepsilon_0}_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma$ for every $\varepsilon_0$, and dominated convergence with respect to the finite measure $S_j\wedge\gamma$ on $B$ gives $I\le I_j$ as $\varepsilon_0\downarrow0$.

\emph{Step 6: conclusion.} Along the subsequence defining $\Theta$, splitting the integrals with a cutoff as in Step 5 shows that $I_j\to\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma$. Therefore
\[
 \int_B(u_0S-\Theta)\wedge\gamma=I-\lim_jI_j\le0.
\]
Since $u_0S-\Theta$ is positive with compact support in $B$, its trace measure $(u_0S-\Theta)\wedge\gamma$ is a positive measure of total mass at most zero, hence zero; by the coefficient bounds of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, $u_0S-\Theta=0$ on $B$. Thus every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly to $u_0S$ on $B$. Hence the whole sequence converges to $u_0S$ on $B$: otherwise there would be a test form $\varphi$ on $B$ and a subsequence along which $|(u_{0,j}S_j-u_0S)(\varphi)|$ stays bounded away from zero, and no further subsequence of it could converge to $u_0S$. The replacement by maxima in Step 2 changes neither the functions nor, by locality, the weighted products on the neighborhood $\{\psi<-\delta\}$ of $K$, and the subtraction of constants does not affect the convergence; hence the original weighted products converge on this neighborhood. As $K$ and $B$ were arbitrary, this proves the weighted assertion for $q$ factors on $\Omega$ and completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bt-polynomial-weights}
Let $P$ be a real polynomial in $m$ variables and, for $1\le i\le m$, let $(v_{i,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a decreasing sequence of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ with locally bounded limit $v_i$. Then in \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} the weight $u_{0,j}$ may be replaced by $P(v_{1,j},\ldots,v_{m,j})$, and the limit is then
\[
 P(v_1,\ldots,v_m)\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T.
\]
Moreover, the identity of \cref{lem:bt-common-collar} also holds when $a$ and $b$ are both bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ which coincide on a collar of $\partial B$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since weak convergence is a local property (Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}), it suffices to prove the convergence on every relatively compact open set $W\Subset\Omega$. On $W$ all the functions $v_{i,j}$ and $v_i$ are bounded, because $v_i\le v_{i,j}\le v_{i,1}$. Adding a constant $c_i$ to each $v_{i,j}$ and to $v_i$ replaces $P$ by the polynomial $P(x_1-c_1,\ldots,x_m-c_m)$; so we may assume that all these functions are nonnegative on $W$. Since the weighted products depend linearly on the weight, it suffices to treat a monomial. A constant weight is covered by the unweighted assertion of the theorem. A monomial of degree $N\ge1$ is a product $a_1\cdots a_N$, where each $a_l$ is one of the $v_i$, repetitions being allowed; we write $a_{l,j}$ for the corresponding function $v_{i,j}$. For real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ we have the polarization identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt-polarization-identity}
 N!\,x_1\cdots x_N=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}}(-1)^{N-|I|}\Bigl(\sum_{l\in I}x_l\Bigr)^N.
\end{equation}
To prove it, expand $(\sum_{l\in I}x_l)^N=\sum_\alpha\frac{N!}{\alpha!}x^\alpha$, the sum running over the multi-indices $\alpha\in\mathbb N^N$ with $|\alpha|=N$ and $\alpha_l=0$ for $l\notin I$. Fix such an $\alpha$, and let $J=\{l:\alpha_l>0\}$ have $k$ elements. The monomial $x^\alpha$ occurs exactly in the terms with $I\supset J$, so its total coefficient on the right-hand side is
\[
 \frac{N!}{\alpha!}\sum_{I\supset J}(-1)^{N-|I|}=\frac{N!}{\alpha!}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}\binom{N-k}{i}(-1)^{N-k-i}=\frac{N!}{\alpha!}(1-1)^{N-k}.
\]
This vanishes unless $k=N$, in which case $\alpha=(1,\ldots,1)$ and the coefficient is $N!$.

Each sum $s=\sum_{l\in I}a_l$ is a bounded nonnegative plurisubharmonic function on $W$, and so is $s^N$. For smooth $s$ this follows from
\[
 \ddc(s^N)=Ns^{N-1}\ddc s+N(N-1)s^{N-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}s,
\]
in which both terms are positive by \eqref{eq:bt-gradient-form}, and from the criterion for smooth functions stated after \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}. In general, the regularizations $s*\rho_\varepsilon$ are smooth, plurisubharmonic and nonnegative, and they decrease to $s$ (see the proof of \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}); since $t\mapsto t^N$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$, the plurisubharmonic functions $(s*\rho_\varepsilon)^N$ decrease to $s^N$, which is therefore plurisubharmonic by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}. Likewise the sums $s_j=\sum_{l\in I}a_{l,j}$ are nonnegative and decrease to $s$, so the $s_j^N$ are bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $W$ decreasing to $s^N$. The weighted assertion of the theorem therefore applies with the weights $s_j^N$, and by \eqref{eq:bt-polarization-identity}, with $x_l=a_{l,j}$, it applies to the monomial weights $a_{1,j}\cdots a_{N,j}$.

For the second assertion, let $a$ and $b$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood $U$ of $\overline B$ which are equal on a collar $V$ of $\partial B$. Let $d>0$ be smaller than the distances from $\partial B$ to $\mathbb C^n\setminus V$ and from $\overline B$ to $\mathbb C^n\setminus U$, and put $V'=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial B)<d/2\}$. For $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon_k<d/2$, the regularizations $a_\varepsilon=a*\rho_\varepsilon$ and $b_\varepsilon=b*\rho_\varepsilon$ are smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and decrease to $a$ and $b$; moreover $a_\varepsilon=b_\varepsilon$ on the collar $V'$, because $a=b$ on the ball $B(z,\varepsilon)\subset V$ for $z\in V'$. By \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, applied to the smooth function $a_\varepsilon$ and the bounded plurisubharmonic function $b_\varepsilon$,
\[
 \int_Ba_\varepsilon\,\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R=\int_Bb_\varepsilon\,\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R.
\]
Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on $B\setminus V'$. On $V'$ the measures $a_\varepsilon\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R$ and $b_\varepsilon\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R$ coincide, by locality, so the identity is equivalent to $\int\chi a_\varepsilon\,\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R=\int\chi b_\varepsilon\,\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R$. By the weighted assertion of the theorem with one factor and the fixed base current $R$, this passes to the limit: $\int\chi a\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int\chi b\,\ddc a\wedge R$. Since $a=b$ on $V$, the measures $a\,\ddc b\wedge R$ and $b\,\ddc a\wedge R$ also coincide on $V'$, and adding the integrals against $1-\chi$ over $B$ gives the identity for $a$ and $b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-maximum-locality}
Let $T$ be a closed positive current on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, let $u$ and $v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$, and let $0\le p\le r$ be an integer. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}(\ddc\max\{u,v\})^p\wedge T
 =\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}(\ddc u)^p\wedge T.
\]
Here the indicator function multiplies the coefficient measures of these currents of order zero, as in \cref{sec:positive-currents}; the set $\{u>v\}$ is Borel, but in general not open (see the example following the proof).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The assertion is local. Let $u_j$ be smooth plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to $u$, for instance radial regularizations. Since $v$ is upper semicontinuous, the set $\{u_j>v\}$ is open, and on it $w_j=\max\{u_j,v\}$ equals $u_j$; by locality, $(\ddc w_j)^p\wedge T=(\ddc u_j)^p\wedge T$ there. The nonnegative Borel function $f_j=w_j-v$ vanishes outside $\{u_j>v\}$, so
\[
 f_j(\ddc w_j)^p\wedge T=f_j(\ddc u_j)^p\wedge T.
\]
The functions $w_j$ decrease to $w=\max\{u,v\}$. Applying \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} to both sides, once with the weights $w_j$ and once with the constant weight $v$, we obtain $f(\ddc w)^p\wedge T=f(\ddc u)^p\wedge T$, where $f=w-v\ge0$. Multiply the coefficient measures of both sides by $1/(f+\varepsilon)$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Since $f/(f+\varepsilon)$ increases to the indicator of $\{f>0\}=\{u>v\}$, dominated convergence with respect to the locally finite total variations of the coefficient measures gives the assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!maximum locality}
To see that $\{u>v\}$ need not be open, let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C^n:|z_1|<1/2\}$, put $a_j=\mathrm e^{-j}$ for $j\ge1$, and let $w=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}\log|z_1-a_j|$ on $D$. Each term is a positive multiple of $\log|z_1-a_j|^2$, which is plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}, and it is negative on $D$, because $|z_1-a_j|<1/2+\mathrm e^{-1}<1$. Hence the partial sums form a decreasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions (\cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}), and since $w(0)=-\sum_jj2^{-j}=-2$ is finite, $w$ is plurisubharmonic on the domain $D$ by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}. The function $u=\max\{w,-4\}$ is plurisubharmonic with values in $[-4,0]$, and for the constant $v=-3$ the set $\{u>v\}=\{w>-3\}$ contains the origin but none of the points $(a_j,0,\ldots,0)$, at which $w=-\infty$; these points converge to the origin. Since $u(0)=-2$ while $u=-4$ at these points, $u$ is also an example of a bounded plurisubharmonic function which is not continuous.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-comparison}
Let $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ be a bounded open set, and let $u$ and $v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ such that $\liminf_{D\ni z\to x}(u(z)-v(z))\ge0$ for every $x\in\partial D$. Then
\[
 \int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc v)^n\le\int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc u)^n,
\]
where both sides may be infinite. If $(\ddc u)^n=0$, then $u\ge v$ on $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Every $x\in\partial D$ has an open neighborhood $U_x$ with $u>v-\varepsilon$ on $U_x\cap D$, and finitely many of them cover the compact set $\partial D$; let $U$ be their union. Then $F=D\setminus U=\overline D\setminus U$ is a compact subset of $D$, it contains $\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}$, and $w=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}$ equals $u$ on $D\setminus F$. For $0\le l\le n-1$ put $R_l=(\ddc w)^l\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-1-l}$. By additivity and symmetry of the products (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion,thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}),
\[
 \ddc\bigl((w-u)R_l\bigr)=\ddc w\wedge R_l-\ddc u\wedge R_l=(\ddc w)^{l+1}\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-1-l}-(\ddc w)^l\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-l},
\]
and summing over $l$, the right-hand sides telescope:
\[
 (\ddc w)^n-(\ddc u)^n
 =\ddc\Bigl((w-u)\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}R_l\Bigr).
\]
The current in parentheses vanishes outside $F$, since $w-u$ does. Hence the signed measure $(\ddc w)^n-(\ddc u)^n$ is supported in $F$, and if $\chi\in C^\infty_c(D)$ equals $1$ on a neighborhood of $F$, then its total mass is
\[
 \int\chi\,\mathrm d\bigl((\ddc w)^n-(\ddc u)^n\bigr)=\Bigl((w-u)\sum_lR_l\Bigr)(\ddc\chi)=0,
\]
because $\ddc\chi=0$ on a neighborhood of $F$. Choose a relatively compact open set $D'\subset D$ containing $F$. Both measures are finite on $D'$, and $\int_{D'}(\ddc w)^n=\int_{D'}(\ddc u)^n$. By \cref{prop:bt-maximum-locality}, $(\ddc w)^n$ coincides with $(\ddc v)^n$ on $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ and with $(\ddc u)^n$ on $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$; here we use that adding the constant $-\varepsilon$ does not change $(\ddc v)^n$. Since $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ and $D'\cap\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$ are disjoint Borel subsets of $D'$, and $\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}\subset F\subset D'$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{\{u<v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc v)^n
 &\le\int_{D'}(\ddc w)^n
       -\int_{D'\cap\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc u)^n\\
 &=\int_{\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc u)^n
 \le\int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc u)^n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the sets $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ increase to $\{u<v\}$, and monotone convergence proves the comparison inequality.

For the last assertion choose $R$ with $D\subset B(0,R)$, and for $\eta>0$ put $v_\eta=v+\eta(|z|^2-R^2)$. Since $v_\eta\le v$, the boundary condition still holds for $u$ and $v_\eta$. By additivity, positive homogeneity and symmetry,
\[
 (\ddc v_\eta)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\eta^k(\ddc v)^{n-k}\wedge(\ddc|z|^2)^k\ge\eta^n(\ddc|z|^2)^n=\eta^n\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\,\mathrm dV_{2n},
\]
since every term of the sum is a positive measure, and by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}. The comparison inequality gives
\[
 \eta^n\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\bigl|\{u<v_\eta\}\bigr|\le\int_{\{u<v_\eta\}}(\ddc v_\eta)^n\le\int_{\{u<v_\eta\}}(\ddc u)^n=0,
\]
so $\{u<v_\eta\}$ is a Lebesgue null set. Hence $\max\{u,v_\eta\}=u$ almost everywhere. Both functions are plurisubharmonic, so the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives in \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, applied on each connected component of $D$, gives $\max\{u,v_\eta\}=u$ everywhere, that is, $u\ge v_\eta$. Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives $u\ge v$.
\end{proof}
\index{comparison principle!bounded plurisubharmonic functions}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bt-local-domination}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded open set, $n\geq1$, and let $u$ and $v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$. Suppose that
\[
 \liminf_{D\ni z\to x}(v(z)-u(z))\geq0
       \quad(x\in\partial D),\qquad
 u\leq v\quad\text{almost everywhere for }(\ddc v)^n.
\]
Then $u\leq v$ everywhere on $D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $R$ with $D\subset B(0,R)$, and for $\varepsilon,\delta>0$ put $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=u-\varepsilon+\delta(|z|^2-R^2)$, a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function. Since $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\le u-\varepsilon$ on $D$, we have $\liminf_{D\ni z\to x}(v(z)-u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(z))\ge\varepsilon>0$ for $x\in\partial D$, and the set $A=\{v<u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\}$ is contained in $\{v<u\}$. The comparison inequality of \cref{thm:bt-comparison}, applied to $v$ and $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}$, and the expansion of $(\ddc u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^n$ used in the proof of that theorem give
\[
 \delta^n\int_A(\ddc|z|^2)^n
 \leq\int_A(\ddc u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^n
 \leq\int_A(\ddc v)^n=0,
\]
where the last equality holds because $A\subset\{v<u\}$ and $u\le v$ almost everywhere for $(\ddc v)^n$. By \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}, $A$ is a Lebesgue null set. Thus $\max\{u_{\varepsilon,\delta},v\}=v$ almost everywhere, and the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}), applied on each connected component of $D$, gives $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq v$ everywhere. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $u\le v$.
\end{proof}
\index{domination principle!locally bounded potentials}

\section{Weighted estimates and Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
\label{sec:bt-capacity}

We now use the products to define a capacity adapted to plurisubharmonic functions. The Monge--Amp\`ere capacity of a set $E$ is the supremum of the masses that the measures $(\ddc v)^n$ give to $E$ when $v$ ranges over the plurisubharmonic functions with values in $[0,1]$. To control exceptional sets with it, we need integral estimates that are uniform over this whole class. The first one is a version of the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality with an unbounded plurisubharmonic weight.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cln-weighted}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open, $n\ge1$, let $K,L\subset\Omega$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. There is a constant $C_{K,L,q}$, depending only on $K$, $L$, $n$ and $q$, such that for every $V\in\PSH(\Omega)$ and all locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$,
\[
 \int_K |V|\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}
 \le C_{K,L,q}\|V\|_{L^1(L)}
            \prod_{l=1}^q\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)}.
\]
In particular, the function $V$, with its values $-\infty$ on the pole set $\{V=-\infty\}$, is locally integrable with respect to the trace measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: reductions.} By \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, $V$ is locally integrable, so $\|V\|_{L^1(L)}<\infty$. For $q=0$ the left-hand side equals $n!\int_K|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}\le n!\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$, by \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}. Let $q\ge1$. If $\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)}=0$ for some $l$, then $u_l=0$ on $\Int L$, so the product vanishes on $\Int L\supset K$ by locality. Otherwise we replace each $u_l$ by $u_l/(2\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)})-3/2$, which takes values in $[-2,-1]$ on $L$; by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(3) this multiplies the left-hand side by $\prod_l(2\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)})^{-1}$. For each $a\in K$ choose $R_a>0$ with $\overline{B(a,R_a)}\subset\Int L$; finitely many of the balls $B(a,R_a/2)$ cover $K$, and this choice depends only on $K$ and $L$. Hence it suffices to prove the following: for concentric balls $B'=B(a,R')$ and $B=B(a,R)$ with $R'<R$ and $\overline B\subset\Int L$, there is a constant $C$, depending only on $B'$, $B$, $L$, $n$ and $q$, such that
\[
 \int_{B'}|V|\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}\le C\|V\|_{L^1(L)}
\]
whenever $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ with values in $[-2,-1]$ on $L$.

\emph{Step 2: a common collar.} Put $\psi=|z-a|^2-R^2$ and $b=\ddc\psi=\beta/\pi$, choose $\delta>0$ with $\overline{B'}\subset\{\psi<-\delta\}$, and set $A=2/\delta$. The open set $V_0=\{z\in\Int L:\psi(z)>-\delta/2\}$ is a collar of $\partial B$, since $\overline B\subset\Int L$. Since $u_l\in[-2,-1]$ on $L$, we have $A\psi<-2\le u_l$ on $\{\psi<-\delta\}\cap L$ and $A\psi>-1\ge u_l$ on $V_0$. Hence replacing each $u_l$ by $\max\{u_l,A\psi\}$, which is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, leaves it unchanged on $\{\psi<-\delta\}$, which lies in $B\subset L$, and makes it equal to $A\psi$ on $V_0$; on $B$ the modified functions take values in $[-2,0)$, since $\psi<0$ there. Let $S=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ be the product of the modified functions. Put $\tau=\delta/4$ and $B_\tau=\{\psi<-\tau\}$. Then $\overline{B'}\subset B_\tau$, and on the neighborhood $V_0$ of $\overline B\setminus B_\tau$ we have $S=(A\,\ddc\psi)^q=A^qb^q$ by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(1),(2). By \cref{lem:mollifier-density}(4) there is $\theta\in C^\infty_c(B)$ with $0\le\theta\le1$ and $\theta=1$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_\tau}$; the function $\chi=-\psi\theta$ is smooth, nonnegative, has compact support in $B$, and equals $-\psi$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_\tau}$. All these choices depend only on $B'$, $B$ and $L$.

\emph{Step 3: smooth $V$ and $q\le n-1$.} Suppose that $q\le n-1$ and that $V$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ with $V\le0$ on $B_\tau$. The signed measure $S\wedge b^{n-q-1}\wedge\ddc\chi$ has compact support in $B$. Splitting its integral against $V$ over $B_\tau$, where $\ddc\chi=-b$, and over $B\setminus B_\tau\subset V_0$, where $S=A^qb^q$, we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{B_\tau}(-V)S\wedge b^{n-q}
 &=\int_B V S\wedge b^{n-q-1}\wedge\ddc\chi
       -A^q\int_{B\setminus B_\tau}V b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi\\
 &=\int_B\chi\,\ddc V\wedge S\wedge b^{n-q-1}
       -A^q\int_{B\setminus B_\tau}V b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality holds because the first integral is $(VS\wedge b^{n-q-1})(\ddc\chi)=\bigl(\ddc(VS\wedge b^{n-q-1})\bigr)(\chi)$, and $\ddc(VS\wedge b^{n-q-1})=\ddc V\wedge S\wedge b^{n-q-1}$ by \eqref{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz}, the current $S\wedge b^{n-q-1}$ being closed. The smooth form $\ddc V$ is closed and strongly positive, so by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(4), with $T=1$ and $\gamma=\ddc V$, the current $\ddc V\wedge S$ is the product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\ddc V$ with the closed positive base current $\ddc V$ of bidimension $(n-1,n-1)$. Choose a compact set $L'\subset B$ whose interior contains $\Supp\chi$. Since $0\le\chi\le\sup\chi$ and the modified $u_l$ take values in $[-2,0)$ on $B$, \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, applied to $\Supp\chi\subset\Int L'$, shows that the first term is at most $C\|\ddc V\|_{L'}$, with $C$ depending only on $\chi$, $L'$, $n$ and $q$. Let $\chi'\in C^\infty_c(B)$ satisfy $0\le\chi'\le1$ and $\chi'=1$ on $L'$. Since $\ddc V\wedge\beta^{n-1}\ge0$,
\[
 \|\ddc V\|_{L'}\le\int\chi'\,\ddc V\wedge\frac{\beta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\int V\,\ddc\chi'\wedge\frac{\beta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\le C\int_B|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n},
\]
where $C$ bounds the coefficient of the smooth $(n,n)$-form $\ddc\chi'\wedge\beta^{n-1}/(n-1)!$. The collar term is bounded by the same quantity, because $b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi$ has bounded smooth coefficients. Hence
\[
 \int_{B_\tau}(-V)S\wedge b^{n-q}\le C\int_B|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}.
\]

\emph{Step 4: general $V$ and $q\le n-1$.} Let $V\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and choose $r_0>0$ with $2r_0<\operatorname{dist}(\overline B,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Int L)$. The function $V$ is subharmonic (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}). If $x$ lies within distance $r_0$ of $\overline B$, then $\overline{B(x,r_0)}\subset\Int L$, and the ball submean inequality of \cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison}(2) gives
\[
 V(x)\le\frac1{|B(x,r_0)|}\int_{B(x,r_0)}V\,\mathrm dV_{2n}\le c_0\|V\|_{L^1(L)},\qquad c_0=|B(0,r_0)|^{-1};
\]
put $c=c_0\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$. For $0<\varepsilon<r_0$ the regularization $V_\varepsilon=V*\rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic on the neighborhood $\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,\overline B)<r_0\}$ of $\overline B$ (see the proof of \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}). It satisfies $V_\varepsilon\le c$ on $\overline B$, since $V_\varepsilon(x)$ is an average of values of $V$ at points within distance $\varepsilon$ of $x$, and $\int_B|V_\varepsilon|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}\le\int\rho_\varepsilon(y)\int_B|V(x-y)|\,\mathrm dV_{2n}(x)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}(y)\le\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$ by Tonelli's theorem, since $B-y\subset L$ for $|y|\le\varepsilon$. Applying Step 3 to $V_\varepsilon-c$ gives
\[
 \int_{B_\tau}(c-V_\varepsilon)\,S\wedge b^{n-q}\le C\bigl(\|V\|_{L^1(L)}+c|B|\bigr)\le C'\|V\|_{L^1(L)}.
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the nonnegative functions $c-V_\varepsilon$ increase to $c-V$, by \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}, so monotone convergence gives the same bound for $\int_{B_\tau}(c-V)\,S\wedge b^{n-q}$. Since $V\le c$ on $B_\tau$, we have $|V|\le(c-V)+c$ there, and the mass of $S\wedge b^{n-q}$ on $B_\tau$ is bounded by a constant depending only on $B'$, $B$, $n$ and $q$, by \cref{thm:cln-bounded} applied to $\overline{B_\tau}\subset B=\Int\overline B$, since the modified $u_l$ take values in $[-2,0]$ on $\overline B$. Since $S$ coincides with the original product on $B'\subset B_\tau$, by locality, this proves the estimate of Step 1 for $q\le n-1$. By the finite cover and the normalizations of Step 1, the theorem holds for $q\le n-1$, in every dimension.

\emph{Step 5: the case $q=n$.} Consider $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ with coordinates $(z,w)$, the unit disc $\Delta\subset\mathbb C$, and the projection $p(z,w)=z$; let $\beta_z$, $\beta_w$ be the forms \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form} in the variables $z$ and $w$, so that $\beta_z+\beta_w$ is the form $\beta$ of $\mathbb C^{n+1}$, and $\beta_w=\mathrm dV_2(w)$. By \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, the functions $u_l\circ p$ are locally bounded and plurisubharmonic on $\Omega\times\Delta$, and so is $V\circ p$, which is not identically $-\infty$ on any connected component $\Omega_0\times\Delta$ of $\Omega\times\Delta$. Let $\mu=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$, a positive measure on $\Omega$. We claim that
\[
 \ddc(u_1\circ p)\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc(u_n\circ p)\wedge(\beta_z+\beta_w)=\mu\otimes\mathrm{d}V_2.
\]
If the $u_l$ are smooth, then $\ddc(u_l\circ p)=p^*\ddc u_l$, so the product on the left is the pullback $p^*\mu$ of the $(n,n)$-form $\mu=g\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ (\cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(1)). Its exterior product with $\beta_z$ is $p^*(\mu\wedge\beta)=0$, since $\mu\wedge\beta$ has bidegree $(n+1,n+1)$ on $\mathbb C^n$, and its exterior product with $\beta_w$ is $g(z)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}(z)\wedge\mathrm dV_2(w)$, the density of $\mu\otimes\mathrm dV_2$. In general, let $u_{l,\varepsilon}=u_l*\rho_\varepsilon$, which are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega_{\varepsilon_0}$ for $\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, decreasing to $u_l$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. By \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, applied on $\Omega_{\varepsilon_0}\times\Delta$ to the functions $u_{l,\varepsilon}\circ p$, which decrease to $u_l\circ p$, the left-hand sides for $u_{l,\varepsilon}$ converge weakly to the left-hand side for $u_l$. By the same theorem on $\Omega_{\varepsilon_0}$, the measures $\mu_\varepsilon=\ddc u_{1,\varepsilon}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{n,\varepsilon}$ converge weakly to $\mu$; hence $\mu_\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm dV_2\to\mu\otimes\mathrm dV_2$, because for a test function $g$ on $\Omega_{\varepsilon_0}\times\Delta$ we have $\int g\,\mathrm d(\mu_\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm dV_2)=\int G\,\mathrm d\mu_\varepsilon$ with $G(z)=\int_\Delta g(z,w)\,\mathrm dV_2(w)$, a test function on $\Omega_{\varepsilon_0}$ (differentiate under the integral sign). Since $\varepsilon_0$ is arbitrary, the claim follows from the smooth case.

Now apply the case already proved in dimension $n+1$, with $q=n\le(n+1)-1$, to the compact sets $\widetilde K=K\times\{|w|\le1/2\}$ and $\widetilde L=L\times\{|w|\le3/4\}$ in $\Omega\times\Delta$; note that $\widetilde K\subset\Int\widetilde L$. By the claim and Fubini's theorem, the left-hand side of the estimate for $V\circ p$ and the $u_l\circ p$ is $\frac\pi4\int_K|V|\,\mathrm d\mu$, while $\|V\circ p\|_{L^1(\widetilde L)}=\frac{9\pi}{16}\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$ and $\|u_l\circ p\|_{L^\infty(\widetilde L)}=\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)}$. Hence the estimate holds in $\mathbb C^n$ with the constant $\frac94C_{\widetilde K,\widetilde L,n}$, which depends only on $K$, $L$ and $n$. This proves the case $q=n$.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality!weighted}
The base current in \cref{thm:cln-weighted} is $T=1$, and for a general closed positive current $T$ no such estimate holds, already for $q=0$. Let $n\ge1$, let $H=\{z_1=0\}\subset\mathbb C^n$, and let $T=[H]$ be the current of integration of \cref{ex:submanifold-current}, a closed positive current of bidimension $(n-1,n-1)$. In the coordinates $z_2,\ldots,z_n$ of $H$, the restriction of $\beta$ to $H$ is the form \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form} of $\mathbb C^{n-1}$, so $T\wedge\beta^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is Lebesgue measure on $H$. The function $V=\log|z_1|$ is plurisubharmonic (\cref{prop:holomorphic-tuple-logarithm}), hence $\|V\|_{L^1(L)}<\infty$ for every compact $L$, but $V=-\infty$ on $H$. Therefore $\int_K|V|\,T\wedge\beta^{n-1}=\infty$ for the closed unit ball $K$, which meets $H$ in a set of positive Lebesgue measure on $H$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:monge-ampere-capacity}
Let $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$, $n\ge1$, be a bounded open set. The \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere capacity} of a Borel set $E\subset D$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D(E)=\sup\left\{\int_E(\ddc v)^n:
            v\in\PSH(D),\ 0\le v\le1\right\}.
\]
The functions $v$ in this supremum are called \emph{competitors} for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. The \emph{outer capacity} of an arbitrary set $A\subset D$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(A)=\inf\{\operatorname{Cap}_D(G):A\subset G\subset D,
                                                  \ G\text{ open}\}.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{capacity!Monge--Amp\`ere}\index{capacity!outer Monge--Amp\`ere}\index{competitor!Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
On a complex manifold we use these capacities in coordinate charts, taking for $D$ a bounded open set, usually a ball, which is relatively compact in the chart.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-capacity-basic}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded open set. The set functions $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ and $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D$ are increasing with respect to inclusion and countably subadditive. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{Cap}_D(K)<\infty$ for every compact $K\subset D$, and $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)=\sup\{\operatorname{Cap}_D(K):K\subset E\text{ compact}\}$ for every Borel set $E\subset D$;
\item if $E_j\subset D$ are Borel sets increasing to $E$, then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E_j)\to\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$;
\item there is a constant $C_D$ such that $|E|\le C_D\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset D$, where $|E|$ is Lebesgue measure;
\item if $B\Subset D$ is a ball and $K\subset B$ is compact, there is a constant $C$ such that, for every Borel set $E\subset K$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D(E)\le\operatorname{Cap}_B(E)
                              \le C\operatorname{Cap}_D(E),
\]
and the same inequalities hold for the outer capacities of arbitrary subsets of $K$;
\item both capacities are invariant under biholomorphisms between bounded open sets: if $F\colon D\to D'$ is biholomorphic, then $\operatorname{Cap}_{D'}(F(E))=\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$ for Borel sets $E\subset D$ and $\operatorname{Cap}^*_{D'}(F(A))=\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(A)$ for arbitrary sets $A\subset D$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each competitor $v$, the measure $(\ddc v)^n$ is increasing and countably subadditive on Borel sets: $\int_{\bigcup_jE_j}(\ddc v)^n\le\sum_j\int_{E_j}(\ddc v)^n\le\sum_j\operatorname{Cap}_D(E_j)$. Taking the supremum over $v$ gives the same properties for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. Outer capacity is increasing by definition. For countable subadditivity, given sets $A_j$ with $\sum_j\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(A_j)<\infty$ and $\varepsilon>0$, choose open sets $G_j\supset A_j$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_j)<\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(A_j)+\varepsilon2^{-j}$; then $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(\bigcup_jA_j)\le\operatorname{Cap}_D(\bigcup_jG_j)\le\sum_j\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(A_j)+\varepsilon$. Finiteness on a compact set $K$ follows from \cref{thm:cln-bounded} with $T=1$, applied to $K$ and a compact neighborhood $L$ of $K$ in $D$, since $\|v\|_{L^\infty(L)}\le1$ for every competitor. Inner regularity of each Radon measure $(\ddc v)^n$ gives $\int_E(\ddc v)^n=\sup_K\int_K(\ddc v)^n\le\sup_K\operatorname{Cap}_D(K)$ over compact $K\subset E$, and taking the supremum over $v$ gives~(1). For~(2), $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E_j)\le\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$ by monotonicity, and for every competitor $v$ monotone convergence gives $\int_E(\ddc v)^n=\lim_j\int_{E_j}(\ddc v)^n\le\lim_j\operatorname{Cap}_D(E_j)$; taking the supremum over $v$ gives~(2). For~(3), choose $a\in D$ and $d>0$ with $D\subset B(a,d)$; the function $|z-a|^2/d^2$ is a competitor, and by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} and \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(3) its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is $n!\pi^{-n}d^{-2n}\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$. Hence~(3) holds with $C_D=\pi^nd^{2n}/n!$.

For~(4), the first inequality holds because the restriction to $B$ of a competitor for $D$ is a competitor for $B$, with the same Monge--Amp\`ere measure on $B$. For the second, write $B=B(a,r)$ and choose $A>0$ such that $\rho=A(|z-a|^2-r^2)<-1$ on a neighborhood of $K$. Given a competitor $v$ for $B$, let $w$ be the function equal to $\max\{v-1,\rho\}$ on $B$ and to $\rho$ on $D\setminus B$. Since $v-1\le0$ on $B$ and $\rho=0$ on $\partial B$, \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} shows that $w$ is plurisubharmonic on $D$. It equals $v-1$ near $K$, satisfies $w\ge-1$ on $D$, and is bounded above by $\Lambda=\max\{0,\sup_D\rho\}$, which does not depend on $v$. Thus $(w+1)/(\Lambda+1)$ is a competitor for $D$, and by locality its Monge--Amp\`ere measure near $K$ is $(\Lambda+1)^{-n}(\ddc v)^n$. This gives the second inequality with $C=(\Lambda+1)^n$; the first inequality holds for all Borel subsets of $B$. For outer capacities, let $K'$ be a compact neighborhood of $K$ in $B$; the constant obtained for $K'$ also serves for $K$, and we use it. If $A\subset K$ and $G$ is an open subset of $D$ or of $B$ containing $A$, then $G\cap\Int K'$ is open in both $B$ and $D$, contains $A$, lies in $K'$, and has no larger capacity than $G$. Applying the inequalities to these sets and taking infima over $G$ gives them for the outer capacities of $A$.

For~(5), let $F\colon D\to D'$ be biholomorphic. By \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, applied to $F$ and to $F^{-1}$, the map $v\mapsto v\circ F$ is a bijection from the competitors for $D'$ onto the competitors for $D$, and by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}(5), $(\ddc(v\circ F))^n=F^*\bigl((\ddc v)^n\bigr)$. We claim that for every positive Radon measure $\mu$ on $D'$, regarded as a current of bidegree $(n,n)$, the pullback $F^*\mu$ is the image of $\mu$ under $F^{-1}$, that is, $\int\psi\,\mathrm d(F^*\mu)=\int\psi\circ F^{-1}\,\mathrm d\mu$ for every test function $\psi$ on $D$. If $\mu=g\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$ with $g$ smooth, then $F^*\mu=(g\circ F)\,|\det F'|^2\,\mathrm dV_{2n}$, because $F^*(\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n)=\det F'\,\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_n$ and similarly for the conjugate forms; since $|\det F'|^2$ is the Jacobian determinant of $F$ as a map of $\mathbb R^{2n}$, the change of variables formula gives the claim. Both sides of the claimed identity are weakly continuous in $\mu$ (\cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback}), and on relatively compact open subsets of $D'$ every $\mu$ is the weak limit of its smooth regularizations, so the claim holds in general. Consequently $(F^*\mu)(F^{-1}(E'))=\mu(E')$ for Borel sets $E'\subset D'$, since two Radon measures that agree on test functions agree on Borel sets. Applying this to $\mu=(\ddc v)^n$ and $E'=F(E)$ and taking the supremum over $v$ gives $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)=\operatorname{Cap}_{D'}(F(E))$. Since $F$ is a homeomorphism, it maps the open sets containing a set $A$ bijectively onto the open sets containing $F(A)$, so the outer capacities agree as well.
\end{proof}
\index{capacity!comparison of Monge--Amp\`ere capacities}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded open set, let $K,L\subset D$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $V\in\PSH(D)$. There is a constant $C_{K,L}$, depending only on $K$, $L$ and $n$, such that
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{V<-s\})
                 \le\frac{C_{K,L}}{s}\|V\|_{L^1(L)},\qquad s>0.
\]
Consequently, let $E\subset D$ be a set locally contained in pole sets of plurisubharmonic functions, that is, every point of $D$ has an open neighborhood $W\subset D$ and a plurisubharmonic function $V_W$ on $W$ with $E\cap W\subset\{V_W=-\infty\}$. (Such sets are called pluripolar in \cref{chap:q03}.) Then $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K)=0$ for every compact set $K\subset D$, and $E$ is contained in a Borel set which has measure zero for every measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$ with locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ on $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose an open neighborhood $U$ of $K$ with compact closure in $\Int L$. For every competitor $v$, \cref{thm:cln-weighted}, applied to $\overline U\subset\Int L$ with $q=n$ and $u_1=\cdots=u_n=v$, gives
\[
 \int_{U\cap\{V<-s\}}(\ddc v)^n\le s^{-1}\int_{\overline U}|V|\,(\ddc v)^n
\le C s^{-1}\|V\|_{L^1(L)}.
\]
Since $V$ is upper semicontinuous, $U\cap\{V<-s\}$ is an open set containing $K\cap\{V<-s\}$; taking the supremum over $v$ proves the estimate. Since $V$ is locally integrable (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}), $\|V\|_{L^1(L)}<\infty$, and letting $s\to\infty$ gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{V=-\infty\})=0$.

Now let $E$ be as in the statement. Let $x\in D$, let $W$ and $V_W$ be as in the hypothesis, and choose $r>0$ with $\overline{B(x,3r)}\subset W$; put $B_x=B(x,3r)$, $K_x=\overline{B(x,r)}$ and $L_x=\overline{B(x,2r)}$. Then $E\cap B_x\subset\{V_W=-\infty\}$, and the restriction of $V_W$ to $B_x$ is plurisubharmonic. The estimate just proved, applied in $B_x$ to this restriction and to $K_x\subset\Int L_x$, gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_{B_x}(E\cap K_x)=0$; since every open set in $B_x$ is open in $D$, the first inequality of \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4) gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K_x)=0$. A compact set $K\subset D$ is covered by finitely many interiors $\Int K_x$, and subadditivity gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K)=0$. Finally, by \cref{thm:cln-weighted}, applied on $B_x$ to $K_x\subset\Int L_x$ with $q=n$, the function $V_W$ is integrable on $K_x$ for every measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$ as in the statement, so the Borel set $K_x\cap\{V_W=-\infty\}$ has measure zero for all these measures. Since $D$ has a countable basis, countably many sets $\Int K_x$ cover $D$, and the union of the corresponding sets $K_x\cap\{V_W=-\infty\}$ is a Borel set with the required property.
\end{proof}
\index{pole set!zero Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}

The next estimate controls, uniformly over all competitors, the set where two bounded plurisubharmonic functions with the same boundary values differ; it is the main tool of the next section.

\begin{proposition}[B\l{}ocki]\label{prop:blocki-difference-estimate}
Let $B$ be a ball, and let $u$ and $h$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ with $u\le h$ on $B$ and $u=h$ on a collar of $\partial B$. Let $v$ be a bounded plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and put $M=\sup_Bv-\inf_Bv$. Then
\[
 \int_B(h-u)^{n+1}(\ddc v)^n
       \le(n+1)!M^n\int_B(h-u)(\ddc u)^n.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that $u$, $h$ and $v$ are smooth. Subtracting $\sup_Bv$ from $v$, we may assume $-M\le v\le0$ on $B$. Put $w=h-u\ge0$; it vanishes on a collar of $\partial B$. Let $p\ge2$, and let $R$ be a smooth closed strongly positive form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$; the exterior product of $R$ with a positive $(1,1)$-form is then a nonnegative multiple of the volume form, by \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, since positive $(1,1)$-forms are strongly positive. Since $\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}w$ is a positive $(1,1)$-form by \eqref{eq:bt-gradient-form} and $\ddc h\wedge R\ge0$, the chain rule $\ddc(w^p)=pw^{p-1}\ddc w+p(p-1)w^{p-2}\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc(w^p)\wedge R&=\bigl(p w^{p-1}(\ddc h-\ddc u)
               +p(p-1)w^{p-2}\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}w\bigr)\wedge R\\
 &\ge-pw^{p-1}\ddc u\wedge R.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since $w^p$ has compact support in $B$ and $R$ is closed, $\int_Bw^p\ddc v\wedge R=(\ddc(vR))(w^p)=(vR)(\ddc(w^p))$ by \eqref{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz}; as $v\le0$ and $-v\le M$, the preceding inequality yields
\[
 \int_Bw^p\ddc v\wedge R
   =\int_Bv\,\ddc(w^p)\wedge R
   \le pM\int_Bw^{p-1}\ddc u\wedge R.
\]
For $k=1,\ldots,n$, apply this with $p=n+2-k$ and $R=(\ddc v)^{n-k}\wedge(\ddc u)^{k-1}$, a product of positive $(1,1)$-forms, hence strongly positive (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}) and closed. This gives
\[
 \int_Bw^{n+2-k}(\ddc v)^{n+1-k}\wedge(\ddc u)^{k-1}\le(n+2-k)M\int_Bw^{n+1-k}(\ddc v)^{n-k}\wedge(\ddc u)^k,
\]
and chaining these $n$ inequalities from $k=1$ to $k=n$ gives the asserted inequality, since $(n+1)n\cdots2=(n+1)!$.

In general, write $B=B(a,r)$ and let $V$ be a collar of $\partial B$ on which $u=h$. Since $\partial B$ is compact, there is $r''<r$ with $\{z:r''\le|z-a|\le r\}\subset V$; choose $r'\in(r'',r)$ and put $B'=B(a,r')$. Then $h-u$ vanishes on $B\setminus\overline{B(a,r'')}$. Convolve $u$, $h$ and $v$ with the same radial kernel $\rho_\varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon<\min\{(r'-r'')/2,r-r'\}$ so small that the regularizations are defined, smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline{B'}$ (see the proof of \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}). At points of $\overline{B'}$ they are averages over balls of radius $\varepsilon$ contained in $B$, where $u\le h$; hence $u_\varepsilon\le h_\varepsilon$ on $\overline{B'}$. Moreover $h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$ vanishes on the collar $\{z:r''+\varepsilon<|z-a|<r-\varepsilon\}$ of $\partial B'$ and outside $\overline{B(a,r''+\varepsilon)}$ in $B'$, hence outside the compact set $\overline{B(a,(r'+r'')/2)}\subset B'$. The values of $v_\varepsilon$ on $B'$ are averages of values of $v$ on $B$, so the oscillation of $v_\varepsilon$ on $B'$ is at most $M$. Apply the smooth case on $B'$ and let $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$. The weights $(h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon)^{n+1}$ and $h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$ are polynomials in decreasing sequences of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and they, as well as their limits $(h-u)^{n+1}$ and $h-u$, vanish in $B'$ outside the fixed compact set $\overline{B(a,(r'+r'')/2)}$. Testing against a function $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B')$ equal to $1$ on this set, \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} lets both sides pass to the limit. Since $h-u$ vanishes on $B\setminus B'$, the limiting inequality on $B'$ is the asserted inequality on $B$.
\end{proof}
\index{B\l{}ocki's estimate}

\section{Quasi-continuity and convergence in capacity}
\label{sec:bt-quasicontinuity}

Throughout this section and the next, $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$, $n\ge1$, is a bounded open set. By \cref{thm:subharmonic-quasicontinuity}, locally integrable subharmonic functions are quasi-continuous in the sense of \cref{part:potential}: they are continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small relative capacity. We now define quasi-continuity with respect to the Monge--Amp\`ere capacity instead, which is the meaning of the term in the rest of the book, prove that plurisubharmonic functions are quasi-continuous in this sense, and then show that the products are continuous under convergence in capacity.

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-continuity}
A Borel function $f\colon D\to[-\infty,\infty]$ is \emph{quasi-continuous} (for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$) if for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ such that the restriction of $f$ to $K\setminus G$ is continuous. Here $[-\infty,\infty]$ carries its order topology.
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-continuity!Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
The same definition applies to a function defined on an open subset $W\subset D$, with compact sets $K\subset W$ and still with $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. A difference of two real quasi-continuous functions $f_1,f_2$ is quasi-continuous: given $K$ and $\varepsilon$, choose open sets $G_i$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_i)<\varepsilon/2$ such that $f_i$ is continuous on $K\setminus G_i$; then $G=G_1\cup G_2$ satisfies $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ by subadditivity, and $f_1-f_2$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}
Let $u\in\PSH(D)$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ which contains the pole set $\{u=-\infty\}$ and such that the restriction of $u$ to $D\setminus G$ is finite and continuous. In particular $u$ is quasi-continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: locally bounded $u$.} Let $u$ be locally bounded, $K\subset D$ compact and $\varepsilon>0$; we find an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ such that $u$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$. For each $x\in K$ choose $r_x>0$ with $\overline{B(x,3r_x)}\subset D$; finitely many balls $B(x_i,r_{x_i})$, $1\le i\le m$, cover $K$. If for each $i$ we find an open set $G_i$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_i)<\varepsilon/m$ such that $u$ is continuous on $\overline{B(x_i,r_{x_i})}\setminus G_i$, then $G=\bigcup_iG_i$ satisfies $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and $u$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$: the finitely many sets $(K\setminus G)\cap\overline{B(x_i,r_{x_i})}$ are closed in $K\setminus G$ and cover it, and $u$ is continuous on each of them. We may therefore assume that $K=\overline{B'}$, where $B'=B(x,r)$, $B=B(x,2r)$ and $B_1=B(x,3r)$ satisfy $\overline{B_1}\subset D$. Subtracting a constant, we may assume that $-N\le u\le-1$ on $B_1$. Put $\psi=|z-x|^2-4r^2$, choose $\delta>0$ with $\overline{B'}\subset\{\psi<-\delta\}$, set $A=N/\delta$ as in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, and put
\[
 g=\max\{u,A\psi\},\qquad g_j=\max\{u*\rho_{1/j},A\psi\},
\]
for $j>1/r$. The functions $g_j$ are continuous and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and decrease to $g$, since $u*\rho_{1/j}$ decreases to $u$ (see the proof of \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}). Moreover $g=u$ on $\{\psi<-\delta\}$, since $A\psi<-N\le u$ there, and $g_j=g=A\psi$ on the common collar $\{z\in B_1:\psi(z)>-1/A\}$ of $\partial B$, since $u*\rho_{1/j}\le-1$ and $u\le-1$ near $\overline B$. For $\delta'>0$ put $G_{j,\delta'}=\{z\in B:g_j(z)-g(z)>\delta'\}$; it is open, because $g_j$ is continuous and $g$ is upper semicontinuous. Every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ has oscillation at most $1$ on $B$, so \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate}, applied with $h=g_j$ and $u=g$, and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \int_{G_{j,\delta'}}(\ddc v)^n\le\frac{1}{\delta'^{n+1}}\int_B(g_j-g)^{n+1}(\ddc v)^n
 \le\frac{(n+1)!}{\delta'^{n+1}}\int_B(g_j-g)(\ddc g)^n.
\]
Taking the supremum over $v$, we obtain the same bound for $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_{j,\delta'})$. The right-hand side tends to zero as $j\to\infty$ by dominated convergence, because $g$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic near $\overline B$, so $(\ddc g)^n$ has finite mass on $B$, and $0\le g_j-g\le g_{j_0}-g$ decreases to zero. Choose $j_1<j_2<\cdots$ such that $G_l=G_{j_l,2^{-l}}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_l)<\varepsilon2^{-l}$, and let $G=\bigcup_lG_l$. Then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and on $B\setminus G$ we have $0\le g_{j_l}-g\le2^{-l}$ for every $l$. Thus $g$ is a uniform limit of continuous functions on $B\setminus G$, hence continuous there; in particular $u=g$ is continuous on $\overline{B'}\setminus G$.

\emph{Step 2: arbitrary $u$.} Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity} there is $s>0$ with $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{u<-s\})<\varepsilon/2$, and hence an open set $G_1\supset K\cap\{u<-s\}$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_1)<\varepsilon/2$. By Step 1, applied to the locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\max\{u,-s\}$, there is an open set $G_2$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_2)<\varepsilon/2$ such that $\max\{u,-s\}$ is continuous on $K\setminus G_2$. On $K\setminus(G_1\cup G_2)$ we have $u\ge-s$, hence $u=\max\{u,-s\}$. Thus $G=G_1\cup G_2$ contains $K\cap\{u=-\infty\}$, $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and $u$ is finite and continuous on $K\setminus G$.

\emph{Step 3: a single exceptional set.} Choose compact sets $K_l$ with $K_l\subset\Int K_{l+1}$ and $\bigcup_lK_l=D$. By Step 2 there are open sets $G^{(l)}\supset K_l\cap\{u=-\infty\}$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G^{(l)})<\varepsilon2^{-l}$ such that $u$ is finite and continuous on $K_l\setminus G^{(l)}$. Put $G=\bigcup_lG^{(l)}$. Then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and $G$ contains the pole set of $u$. Every $x\in D\setminus G$ lies in some $\Int K_l$, and on the neighborhood $\Int K_l\setminus G$ of $x$ in $D\setminus G$ the function $u$ is a restriction of the continuous function $u|_{K_l\setminus G^{(l)}}$. Hence $u|_{D\setminus G}$ is finite and continuous.
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!quasi-continuity}

In the proofs below we extend continuous functions from compact sets, using the following elementary case of Tietze's extension theorem\index{Tietze extension theorem}: if $F\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ is compact and $f\colon F\to\mathbb R$ is continuous with $|f|\le a$, then $f$ has a continuous extension $g$ to $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|g|\le a$. For completeness we give a direct construction. If $F=\varnothing$ or $a=0$, take $g=0$. Otherwise, for $L>0$ put
\[
 f_L(x)=\max\Bigl\{-a,\min\bigl\{a,\inf_{y\in F}(f(y)+L|x-y|)\bigr\}\Bigr\}.
\]
This is a Lipschitz function on $\mathbb{R}^d$, and $f_L\to f$ uniformly on $F$ as $L\to\infty$. Indeed, $f_L\le f$ on $F$. Given $\eta>0$, uniform continuity gives $r>0$ with $|f(y)-f(x)|<\eta$ for $x,y\in F$ with $|x-y|<r$; if $Lr>2a$, then points $y$ with $|x-y|\ge r$ contribute values larger than $a\ge f(x)$, while the other points contribute values larger than $f(x)-\eta$, so $f_L(x)\ge f(x)-\eta$ for $x\in F$. Hence there is a continuous $g_1$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|g_1|\le a$ and $|f-g_1|\le a/2$ on $F$. Applying the same construction to $f-g_1$ on $F$, we find $g_2$ with $|g_2|\le a/2$ and $|f-g_1-g_2|\le a/4$ on $F$, and so on. The series $\sum_kg_k$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}^d$; its sum is continuous and equals $f$ on $F$, and clamping it to $[-a,a]$ does not change its values on $F$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity}
Let $K,L\subset D$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $f$ be a real, bounded Borel function which is defined and quasi-continuous on a neighborhood of $L$. Then, for every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f|>\delta\})
       \le\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\eta>0$. Choose an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\eta$ such that $f$ is continuous on $L\setminus G$, and let $g$ be a continuous extension of $f|_{L\setminus G}$ to $\mathbb{C}^n$, as constructed above. The open set $G\cup(\Int L\cap\{|g|>\delta/2\})$ contains $K\cap\{|f|>\delta\}$, since $g=f$ on $K\setminus G$. Its part outside $G$ is contained in $L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}$, because $g=f$ on $L\setminus G$. By subadditivity,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f|>\delta\})\le\eta+\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}).
\]
Let $\eta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:local-capacity-convergence}
A net $(f_a)$ of Borel functions $f_a\colon D\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ \emph{converges locally in capacity} to a Borel function $f\colon D\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ if, for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{f_a>f+\delta\})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{f_a<f-\delta\})\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here $\pm\infty+\delta=\pm\infty$ and $\pm\infty-\delta=\pm\infty$.
\end{definition}
\index{convergence!Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
For finite functions the two conditions together are equivalent to $\operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f_a-f|>\delta\})\to0$, by monotonicity and subadditivity of outer capacity. By the comparison of capacities in \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), convergence locally in capacity is a local property: it holds on $D$ if and only if, for every ball $B\Subset D$, it holds on $B$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ in place of $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. In particular it does not depend on the bounded open set containing the compact sets under consideration.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-decreasing-capacity}
Let $u_j$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ decreasing to a locally bounded function $u$. Then $u_j\to u$ locally in capacity. Moreover, a net of real Borel functions on $D$ which is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$ and converges locally in capacity to a locally bounded real Borel function also converges in $L^1_{\loc}(D)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $\delta>0$. Since $u_j\ge u$, the sets $\{u_j<u-\delta\}$ are empty, and we must show that $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{u_j-u>\delta\})\to0$. Covering $K$ by finitely many balls, we may assume that $K\subset\Int L$, where $L\subset B'$ is compact and $B'\Subset B\Subset D$ are concentric balls. Subtracting a constant $c$ and arguing as in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, we obtain bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\hat u_j$ and $\hat u$ near $\overline B$, with $\hat u_j\downarrow\hat u$, which coincide with $u_j-c$ and $u-c$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B'}$ and with a common function $A\psi$ on a collar of $\partial B$. Applying \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} and Chebyshev's inequality to every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$, as in the proof of \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}, we get
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{\hat u_j-\hat u>\delta/2\}\bigr)
 \le(n+1)!\,(2/\delta)^{n+1}\int_B(\hat u_j-\hat u)(\ddc\hat u)^n,
\]
and the right-hand side tends to zero by dominated convergence. The function $w_j=u_j-u$ is bounded on a neighborhood of $L$ and quasi-continuous there, by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}. Hence \cref{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{w_j>\delta\})\le\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{w_j>\delta/2\})\le\operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{\hat u_j-\hat u>\delta/2\}\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]

For the second assertion, let $f_a\to f$ be as in the statement, let $K\subset D$ be compact, and let $M$ bound $|f_a|$ and $|f|$ on $K$. Splitting $K$ into the set where $|f_a-f|>\delta$ and its complement, and using \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(3), we get
\[
 \int_K|f_a-f|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}
 \le 2MC_D\operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f_a-f|>\delta\})
          +\delta|K|.
\]
Taking the limit along the net and then letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves $L^1$ convergence on $K$.
\end{proof}
\index{convergence!capacity for decreasing sequences}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a ball, and let $\mathcal U$ be a family of plurisubharmonic functions on $B$ which is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $B$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every compact set $K\subset B$ there is a constant $C$ such that, for all integers $0\le q\le n$, all $u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathcal U$ and all Borel sets $E\subset K$, the trace measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}$, and the total variation of every coefficient measure of $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$, give $E$ mass at most $C\operatorname{Cap}_B(E)$.
\item Let $S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ with $u_{l,j}\in\mathcal U$, and suppose that $S_j\to S$ weakly. Let $f_j$ be real Borel functions on $B$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $B$, which converge locally in capacity (for $\operatorname{Cap}_B$) to a real Borel function $f$ that is locally bounded and quasi-continuous. Then $f_jS_j\to fS$ weakly, and the same holds for the trace measures of $S_j$ and $S$. This applies in particular to a fixed weight $f_j=f$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Let $B=B(a,R)$, fix $K$, and choose $R'<R$ with $K\subset B'=B(a,R')$. Let $M\ge1$ be a common bound for $|u|$ on $B'$, $u\in\mathcal U$. For $u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathcal U$ put $v_l=(u_l+M)/(2M)$ and $\phi=|z-a|^2/R'^2$; these functions take values in $[0,1]$ on $B'$, and so does
\[
 F=\frac1n\bigl(v_1+\cdots+v_q+(n-q)\phi\bigr),
\]
which is therefore a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}$. Expand $(\ddc F)^n$ by additivity and symmetry. All terms are positive, and those in which each $\ddc v_l$ occurs once and $\ddc\phi$ occurs $n-q$ times add up to a positive multiple, depending only on $n$ and $q$, of $\ddc v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc v_q\wedge(\ddc\phi)^{n-q}$. Since $\ddc\phi=\beta/(\pi R'^2)$ and $\ddc u_l=2M\ddc v_l$, it follows that $\int_E\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}\le C\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}(E)$ for Borel $E\subset B'$. The bound for the coefficient measures follows from \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, and \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), applied to $B'\Subset B$ and $K$, replaces $\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}$ by $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ on subsets of $K$.

(2) Let $\sigma_j$ be either the trace measure or a coefficient measure of $S_j$, and $\sigma$ the corresponding measure of $S$; then $\sigma_j\to\sigma$ weakly. Fix a continuous function $\chi$ with compact support in $B$ and a compact set $K\subset B$ with $\Supp\chi\subset\Int K$, and let $C$ be the constant of~(1) for $K$. A similar bound holds for $\sigma$ on open subsets $U\subset\Int K$: for the trace measure, \cref{prop:vague-set-inequalities} gives $\sigma(U)\le\liminf_j\sigma_j(U)\le C\operatorname{Cap}_B(U)$, and the total variations of the coefficient measures of the positive current $S$ are bounded by a constant multiple of its trace measure.

Consider first the fixed weight $f$, and let $\eta>0$. Since $f$ is quasi-continuous, there is an open set $G\subset B$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_B(G)<\eta$ such that $f$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$; let $g$ be a continuous extension of $f|_{K\setminus G}$ with $\sup|g|\le\sup_K|f|$, as constructed above. Then
\[
 \Bigl|\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j-\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\Bigr|\le2\sup_K|f|\sup|\chi|\,|\sigma_j|(G\cap\Supp\chi)\le C'\eta,
\]
and similarly for $\sigma$, using the open set $G\cap\Int K$. Since $\chi g$ is continuous with compact support and the total variations of the $\sigma_j$ are bounded on compact sets, $\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\to\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma$. Hence $\limsup_j|\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j-\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma|\le2C'\eta$ for every $\eta>0$, which proves $f\sigma_j\to f\sigma$.

For varying weights, let $M_K$ bound $|f_j|$ and $|f|$ on $K$, and let $\delta>0$. By~(1),
\[
 \Bigl|\int\chi(f_j-f)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\Bigr|
 \le\sup|\chi|\Bigl(\delta\,C\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)+2M_KC\operatorname{Cap}^*_B\bigl(K\cap\{|f_j-f|>\delta\}\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
As $j\to\infty$, the second term tends to zero by convergence in capacity; then we let $\delta\downarrow0$. Together with the fixed-weight case, this proves $f_j\sigma_j\to f\sigma$, and hence $f_jS_j\to fS$.
\end{proof}
\index{capacity!domination of mixed Monge--Amp\`ere measures}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}
Let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. For $1\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a sequence of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converges locally in capacity to a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u_l$. Then
\[
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
             \longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q
\]
weakly. More generally, if $f_j$ are real Borel functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converge locally in capacity to a locally bounded, quasi-continuous real Borel function $f$, then $f_j\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\to f\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ weakly.
\end{theorem}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!continuity under convergence in capacity}
\begin{proof}
Weak convergence is local. By \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), the hypotheses hold with $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ in place of $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ on every ball $B\Subset D$, and the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$, restricted to $B$, form a family which is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $B$. We may therefore work on such a ball and use \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}. We argue by induction on $q$. For $q=0$ the product is $1$, and the weighted assertion is the second part of the lemma with $S_j=1$. Let $q\ge1$, and assume that $S_j=\ddc u_{2,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ converges weakly to $S=\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$. The functions $u_{1,j}$ converge in capacity to $u_1$, which is quasi-continuous by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}; hence the second part of the lemma gives $u_{1,j}S_j\to u_1S$, and applying $\ddc$ proves the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. The weighted assertion then follows from the second part of the lemma once more.
\end{proof}
Unlike \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, \cref{thm:bt-capacity-continuity} concerns only products with the base current $T=1$.

\section{Increasing limits and exceptional envelopes}
\label{sec:bt-increasing}

The supremum of an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions need not be upper semicontinuous, and it may differ from its upper semicontinuous regularization on an exceptional set. We show that the products are nevertheless continuous along increasing sequences. The proof follows the pattern of the decreasing case: the inequality $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ bounds weak limits from above, while the quasi-continuity of the fixed weights, through \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}, supplies the lower bound for the masses. As a consequence, the exceptional set of an upper envelope has outer capacity zero.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}
Let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. For $0\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converges almost everywhere to a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u_l$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q,\\
 u_{0,j}\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly. Moreover $u_{l,j}\to u_l$ locally in capacity for every $l$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the weighted assertion by induction on $q$, for all sequences as in the statement. For $q=0$ it follows from dominated convergence with respect to Lebesgue measure. Let $q\ge1$ and assume the weighted assertion for $q-1$ factors; applying $\ddc$ to it, with the weight $u_{1,j}$, gives the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. Put $S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ and $S=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$, so that $S_j\to S$ weakly.

As in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, we work on a ball $B\Subset D$ and may assume that all functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ coincide with a fixed function $A\psi$ on a collar $V$ of $\partial B$ and are negative on $B$, without changing them near a given compact subset of $B$; the maximum with $A\psi$ preserves increasing sequences and their limits almost everywhere. On $V$, all the currents considered below coincide with fixed currents, so integrals over $B$ may be split by a cutoff $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on $B\setminus V$, as in Step 5 of that proof; we use this without further comment. By \cref{thm:cln-bounded} and the compactness part of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly on $B$; let $\Theta$ be its limit.

Since the sequence $(u_{0,j})$ increases to $u_0$ almost everywhere, we have $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ almost everywhere, and the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives, applied to $\max\{u_{0,j},u_0\}$ and $u_0$, gives $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ everywhere. Hence $u_{0,j}S_j\le u_0S_j$. For a smooth strongly positive test form $\varphi$ of complementary bidegree, a further subsequence of $u_0S_j\wedge\varphi$ converges weakly to some $\nu$, and \cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}, applied to the constant sequence $f_j=u_0$, gives $\nu\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$. Therefore $\Theta\wedge\varphi\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$, so $u_0S-\Theta$ is a positive current; it vanishes on $V$. Write $\gamma=\beta^{n-q}$.

For fixed $k$ and all $j\ge k$, positivity of $S_j\wedge\gamma$ and $u_{0,j}\ge u_{0,k}$ give
\[
 \int_Bu_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma\ge\int_Bu_{0,k}S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
Let $j\to\infty$ along the subsequence defining $\Theta$. The left-hand side tends to $\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma$. The fixed weight $u_{0,k}$ is quasi-continuous by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}, so by \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination} the right-hand side tends to $\int_Bu_{0,k}S\wedge\gamma$. By the second assertion of \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} and symmetry,
\[
 \int_Bu_{0,k}S\wedge\gamma=\int_Bu_1\,\ddc u_{0,k}\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\gamma.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors, already established, gives $\ddc u_{0,k}\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\to\ddc u_0\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ weakly, and \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}, with the fixed quasi-continuous weight $u_1$, passes the integral to the limit. A second application of \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} identifies the limit with $\int_Bu_0\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\gamma=\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma$. We have shown that $\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma\ge\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma$. Since $u_0S-\Theta$ is positive with compact support in $B$, its trace measure has total mass $\int_B(u_0S-\Theta)\wedge\gamma\le0$ and therefore vanishes, and $\Theta=u_0S$ by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}. As in the decreasing case, it follows that the whole sequence $u_{0,j}S_j$ converges to $u_0S$, which completes the induction.

Now that both assertions are known for every $q\le n$, we prove convergence in capacity. Fix $l$. As for $l=0$, we have $u_{l,j}\le u_l$ everywhere. Use the same collar modification, and apply \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} with $u=u_{l,j}$ and $h=u_l$ to every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$; for $\delta>0$, Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{u_l-u_{l,j}>\delta\}\bigr)\le\frac{(n+1)!}{\delta^{n+1}}\int_B(u_l-u_{l,j})(\ddc u_{l,j})^n.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero: $\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_{l,j})^n\to\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_l)^n$ by the unweighted assertion and \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination} with the fixed quasi-continuous weight $u_l$, and $\int_Bu_{l,j}(\ddc u_{l,j})^n\to\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_l)^n$ by the weighted assertion with all factors and the weight equal to $u_{l,j}$. Since $u_{l,j}\le u_l$, the sets $\{u_{l,j}>u_l+\delta\}$ are empty. \Cref{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity}, applied to the bounded quasi-continuous function $u_l-u_{l,j}$, turns these estimates into the required bounds for outer capacity on compact subsets of the ball where the functions were not modified; a finite cover completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!increasing continuity}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty family of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ which is locally uniformly bounded above, and let $F=\sup_{v\in\mathcal F}v$ be its pointwise supremum and $F^*$ its upper semicontinuous regularization. Then $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{F<F^*\})=0$ for every compact set $K\subset D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} there is a countable subfamily with the same regularized supremum $u=F^*$. Let $v_j$ be the increasing finite maxima of its members; they are plurisubharmonic and converge almost everywhere to $u$. By \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4) and subadditivity it suffices to work on a ball $B\Subset D$, with $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ in place of $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. Fix an integer $m\ge1$. The functions $\max\{v_j,-m\}$ are uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $B$ and increase almost everywhere to $\max\{u,-m\}$, so by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity} they converge to it locally in capacity. For rational $\delta>0$, the set where $\sup_j\max\{v_j,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}-\delta$ is contained in $\{\max\{v_k,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}-\delta\}$ for every $k$, and the outer capacities of the intersections of the latter sets with a compact set tend to zero as $k\to\infty$. Taking the union over rational $\delta$, countable subadditivity shows that the set
\[
 E_m=\Bigl\{\sup_j\max\{v_j,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}\Bigr\}
\]
meets every compact set in a set of outer capacity zero.

If $\sup_jv_j(x)<u(x)$, then $u(x)>-\infty$, so $u(x)>-m$ for some integer $m$, and then $x\in E_m$. Hence $\{\sup_jv_j<u\}\subset\bigcup_mE_m$ also meets every compact set in a set of outer capacity zero. Finally, $\sup_jv_j\le F\le u$, so $\{F<F^*\}\subset\{\sup_jv_j<u\}$. (Since outer capacity is defined for all sets, we need not know whether $\{F<F^*\}$ is Borel.)
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!capacity-zero envelope discrepancy}

\section{The homogeneous Dirichlet problem and balayage}
\label{sec:bt-homogeneous-dirichlet}

A plurisubharmonic function is maximal if it cannot be increased on a compact set without losing plurisubharmonicity (\cref{def:maximal-plurisubharmonic}). We show in \cref{thm:bounded-psh-maximality} that, for locally bounded functions, maximality is equivalent to $(\ddc u)^n=0$. The proof replaces $u$ on a ball by a solution $H$ of the homogeneous equation $(\ddc H)^n=0$ with the same boundary values (\cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}); in \cref{chap:q03} the same replacement shows that relative extremal functions have vanishing Monge--Amp\`ere measure away from the sets on which they are constrained. We construct $H$ as an upper envelope; to show that it solves the equation, we bound its second differences by composing with automorphisms of the ball, which makes it twice differentiable almost everywhere.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be the unit ball. For $a\in B$ and $h\in\mathbb C^n$ with $|h|$ small, depending on $a$, there are biholomorphic automorphisms $S_{a,h}$ of $B$ such that $S_{a,0}$ is the identity and $S_{a,h}(a)=a+h$. They extend smoothly to a neighborhood of $\overline B$. For every compact set $K\subset B$ there are $h_0>0$ and $C$ such that, for $a\in K$, $|h|\le h_0$ and $z\in\overline B$, the first and second derivatives of $S_{a,h}(z)$ with respect to the real variable $h\in\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ are bounded by $C$.
\end{lemma}
\index{automorphisms of the ball}
\begin{proof}
Use the Hermitian product $\langle z,a\rangle=\sum z_j\bar a_j$, put $s_a=\sqrt{1-|a|^2}$, and define
\[
 T_a(z)=\frac{s_a z+\langle z,a\rangle a/(1+s_a)-a}
                       {1-\langle z,a\rangle}.
\]
This expression depends smoothly on $(a,z)$ for $|a|<1$ and $|z|<|a|^{-1}$, and $T_0$ is the identity. In unitary coordinates in which $a=(t,0,\ldots,0)$ with $t\ge0$, it reads
\[
 T_a(z)=\left(\frac{z_1-t}{1-tz_1},
           \frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}\,z_2}{1-tz_1},\ldots,
           \frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}\,z_n}{1-tz_1}\right).
\]
Direct substitution gives $T_a(a)=0$, $T_{-a}\circ T_a=\mathrm{id}$, and
\[
 1-|T_a(z)|^2=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^2}.
\]
Hence $T_a$ maps $B$ bijectively onto itself and the sphere $\partial B$ onto itself, with inverse $T_{-a}$. Set $S_{a,h}=T_{-(a+h)}\circ T_a$ for $|a+h|<1$. It maps $a$ to $T_{-(a+h)}(0)=a+h$ and is the identity for $h=0$. If $a$ ranges over a compact subset of $B$ and $|h|$ is small enough, the denominators $|1-\langle z,a\rangle|\ge1-|a|$ and $|1+\langle w,a+h\rangle|\ge1-|a+h|$ of both maps stay uniformly away from zero for $|z|,|w|\le1$, and the derivative bounds follow from the smoothness of the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a ball and $\varphi$ a real continuous function on $\partial B$. There is a unique $H\in\PSH(B)\cap C(\overline B)$ such that
\[
 (\ddc H)^n=0\quad\text{on }B,\qquad H|_{\partial B}=\varphi.
\]
It is the upper envelope of the class of all $v\in\PSH(B)$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le\varphi(x)$ for every $x\in\partial B$. If $\varphi$ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$, then $H\in C^{1,1}_{\loc}(B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After an affine change of coordinates, we may assume that $B$ is the unit ball. We call the functions $v$ in the statement the \emph{subsolutions for} $\varphi$ of the homogeneous equation, and we let $H$ denote their upper envelope.

\emph{Step 1: the envelope for $C^2$ data.} Suppose that $\varphi$ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$. Multiplying this function by a cutoff equal to $1$ near $\partial B$, we obtain a $C^2$ function $\tilde\varphi$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $\tilde\varphi=\varphi$ on $\partial B$. For $A$ large, $s=\tilde\varphi+A(|z|^2-1)$ is plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and $s=\varphi$ on $\partial B$; so $s$ is a subsolution. Let $P\varphi$ be the Poisson integral of $\varphi$, the harmonic function on $B$ with boundary values $\varphi$ given by \cref{thm:ball-dirichlet}. Every subsolution $v$ is subharmonic on $B$, so the comparison principle (\cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison} and the remark following it) gives $v\le P\varphi$. Hence $s\le H\le P\varphi$. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, the upper semicontinuous regularization $H^*$ is plurisubharmonic, and $s\le H^*\le P\varphi$ since $P\varphi$ is continuous. As $s$ and $P\varphi$ are continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $\varphi$, the function $H^*$ is a subsolution, so $H^*\le H$, and $H=H^*$ is plurisubharmonic. Moreover $H(z)\to\varphi(x)$ as $z\to x\in\partial B$, and $H$ is bounded.

\emph{Step 2: second differences.} Let $K\subset B$ be compact, and let $S_{a,h}$, $h_0$ and the derivative bounds be as in \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms}. For $x\in\partial B$, $a\in K$ and $|h|\le h_0$, Taylor's formula applied to $h\mapsto\tilde\varphi(S_{a,h}(x))$, whose second derivatives are bounded uniformly in $a$, $x$ and $h$, gives
\[
 \tfrac12\bigl(\varphi\circ S_{a,h}+\varphi\circ S_{a,-h}\bigr)
                 \le\varphi+C_K|h|^2\quad\text{on }\partial B.
\]
The function $\frac12(H\circ S_{a,h}+H\circ S_{a,-h})-C_K|h|^2$ is plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, and since $H$ is continuous at $\partial B$ and the maps $S_{a,\pm h}$ preserve $\partial B$, the preceding inequality shows that it is a subsolution. Evaluating the resulting inequality with $H$ at $z=a$ yields
\[
 H(a+h)+H(a-h)-2H(a)\le2C_K|h|^2\qquad(a\in K,\ |h|\le h_0).
\]
Apply this on a compact neighborhood $K'$ of a given compact set $K''\subset B$. For small $\varepsilon$, the regularization $H_\varepsilon=H*\rho_\varepsilon$ is an average of translates of $H$, so it satisfies the same inequality for $a\in K''$, with the constant $C_{K'}$. Dividing by $t^2$ for $h$ replaced by $th$ and letting $t\to0$ gives $D^2H_\varepsilon(a)[h,h]\le2C_{K'}|h|^2$. On the other hand $H_\varepsilon$ is plurisubharmonic, so
\[
 D^2H_\varepsilon[h,h]+D^2H_\varepsilon[\mathrm ih,\mathrm ih]\ge0,
\]
and hence $D^2H_\varepsilon[h,h]\ge-2C_{K'}|h|^2$ as well. Thus the real Hessians of the $H_\varepsilon$ are bounded on $K''$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$.

We cannot yet apply \cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor} directly, since $H$ is not yet known to be continuous in $B$, but its proof applies: the $H_\varepsilon$ are bounded, so their gradients are bounded and uniformly Lipschitz on compact sets, and by \cref{prop:arzela-ascoli} a sequence $H_{\varepsilon_k}$ converges locally uniformly to a $C^{1,1}$ function $G$. Since $H_\varepsilon$ decreases pointwise to $H$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, by \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}, we have $G=H$. Hence $H$ is continuous, \cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor} applies, and $H$ belongs to $C^{1,1}_{\loc}(B)$ and has a second-order Taylor expansion at almost every point.

\emph{Step 3: the equation.} The complex Hessian of $H$ has bounded measurable coefficients $H_{j\bar k}$, and the coefficients of the complex Hessian of $H_\varepsilon$ are their regularizations. These are bounded on compact sets uniformly in $\varepsilon$, and they converge to $H_{j\bar k}$ almost everywhere: if $x$ satisfies the conclusion of \cref{thm:lebesgue-differentiation} for a bounded function $f$, then $|f*\rho_\varepsilon(x)-f(x)|\le\sup\rho_1\cdot\varepsilon^{-2n}\int_{Q(x,\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(x)|\,\mathrm dy\to0$. Since $H_\varepsilon\to H$ locally uniformly, \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} and bounded convergence give
\[
 (\ddc H)^n=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\det(H_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
\]
The matrix $(H_{j\bar k})$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, as an almost everywhere limit of the complex Hessians of the plurisubharmonic functions $H_\varepsilon$. Suppose that $\det(H_{j\bar k})>0$ on a set of positive measure, and choose a point $a$ of this set at which $(H_{j\bar k}(a))$ is positive semidefinite and the second-order expansion of Step 2 holds, with Taylor polynomial $Q$. The complex Hessian of the real quadratic polynomial $Q$ is then positive definite, so there is $\eta>0$ such that $Q-\eta|z-a|^2$ is still plurisubharmonic. For sufficiently small $r>0$ the remainder satisfies $|H-Q|<\eta r^2/2$ on the sphere $|z-a|=r$. The polynomial $Q-\eta|z-a|^2+\eta r^2/4$ is then strictly smaller than $H$ on that sphere and strictly larger than $H$ at $a$. By \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, the function equal to the maximum of $H$ and this polynomial on the ball $|z-a|<r$ and to $H$ elsewhere is plurisubharmonic; it is a subsolution which exceeds $H$ at $a$, a contradiction. Hence $\det(H_{j\bar k})=0$ almost everywhere, and $(\ddc H)^n=0$. This proves the theorem for $C^2$ data, including the $C^{1,1}$ regularity.

\emph{Step 4: continuous data and uniqueness.} For continuous $\varphi$, extend it to an annular neighborhood of $\partial B$ by $\varphi(z/|z|)$ and regularize; this gives smooth functions $\varphi_j$ on a neighborhood of $\partial B$ converging uniformly to $\varphi$ on $\partial B$. If $\varphi\le\varphi'+c$ with a constant $c$, then every subsolution for $\varphi$ is at most $c$ plus a subsolution for $\varphi'$; hence the corresponding envelopes satisfy $\|H_j-H_k\|_{L^\infty(B)}\le\|\varphi_j-\varphi_k\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}$, and likewise $\|H_j-H\|_{L^\infty(B)}\le\|\varphi_j-\varphi\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}$ for the envelope $H$ of the subsolutions for $\varphi$. Therefore the $H_j$ converge uniformly on $\overline B$ to $H$, which is continuous on $\overline B$, plurisubharmonic, and equal to $\varphi$ on $\partial B$; by \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, $(\ddc H)^n=0$. Finally, if $H_1$ and $H_2$ are two solutions, the last assertion of \cref{thm:bt-comparison}, applied in both directions, gives $H_1=H_2$.
\end{proof}
\index{Dirichlet problem!homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}
Let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, and let $B\Subset\Omega$ be a ball. There is a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\widehat u$ on $\Omega$ which equals $u$ outside $B$, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$ on $B$, and $(\ddc\widehat u)^n=0$ on $B$. The function $\widehat u$ is increasing in $u$, and its restriction to $B$ is the largest plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for every $x\in\partial B$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $u_j$ be the radial regularizations of $u$, which are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ decreasing to $u$, and let $H_j$ be the solution of \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet} with boundary values $u_j|_{\partial B}$. Since $u_j$ is a subsolution for these boundary values, $u\le u_j\le H_j$ on $B$. Since $u_{j+1}\le u_j$ on $\partial B$, the envelopes decrease: $H_{j+1}\le H_j$. Let $H=\lim_jH_j$. It satisfies $\inf_{\overline B}u\le H\le\max_{\partial B}u_1$, so $H$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic, and \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} gives $(\ddc H)^n=0$. For $x\in\partial B$, since $H\le H_j$ and $H_j$ is continuous on $\overline B$ with $H_j=u_j$ on $\partial B$,
\[
 \limsup_{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u_j(x)\quad\text{for every }j,
 \qquad\text{hence}\quad\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u(x).
\]
By \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, the function $\widehat u$ equal to $\max\{u,H\}=H$ on $B$ and to $u$ outside $B$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$. It has the first three properties in the statement.

Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for $x\in\partial B$. Since $u\le u_j$, the function $v$ is a subsolution for the boundary values $u_j|_{\partial B}$, so $v\le H_j$ by \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}. Letting $j\to\infty$ gives $v\le H$. Conversely, $H$ belongs to this class by the displayed estimate, so $H$ is its largest member. If $u\le u'$, the class for $u$ is contained in the class for $u'$, which proves that $\widehat u\le\widehat{u'}$.
\end{proof}
\index{balayage!plurisubharmonic homogeneous replacement}

\begin{definition}\label{def:maximal-plurisubharmonic}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be open. A plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega$ is \emph{maximal} if every plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $\Omega$ with $v\le u$ outside a compact subset of $\Omega$ satisfies $v\le u$ everywhere.
\end{definition}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!maximal}\index{maximal plurisubharmonic function}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bounded-psh-maximality}
A locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u$ on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ is maximal if and only if $(\ddc u)^n=0$. In particular, maximality is a local property for locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions.
\end{theorem}
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!vanishing and maximality}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $(\ddc u)^n=0$, and let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ with $v\le u$ outside a compact set $K$. Choose a bounded open set $D$ with $K\subset D\Subset\Omega$. By upper semicontinuity, $v$ is bounded above on $\overline D$; choose $M$ with $-M<\inf_Du$. The function $\max\{v,-M\}$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $D$, and it is at most $u$ on $D\setminus K$, which contains a neighborhood of $\partial D$ in $D$. The last assertion of \cref{thm:bt-comparison} therefore gives $\max\{v,-M\}\le u$ on $D$. Hence $v\le u$ everywhere, and $u$ is maximal.

Conversely, suppose that $u$ is maximal, and let $B\Subset\Omega$ be a ball. The function $\widehat u$ of \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} is plurisubharmonic, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$, and equals $u$ outside the compact set $\overline B$. Maximality gives $\widehat u\le u$, so $\widehat u=u$ and $(\ddc u)^n=(\ddc\widehat u)^n=0$ on $B$. Since $B$ was arbitrary, $(\ddc u)^n=0$ on $\Omega$. The last assertion follows because the vanishing of a measure is a local property.
\end{proof}
