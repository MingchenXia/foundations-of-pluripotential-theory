\chapter{Bedford--Taylor products and local capacity}
\label{chap:q08}

Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^n$. By \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, $\ddc u$ is a positive closed $(1,1)$-current, but in general its coefficients are only measures, so its exterior powers cannot be formed by multiplying coefficients: products of measures have no general meaning. For $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ the top power is the Monge--Amp\`ere measure $(\ddc u)^n=n!\,\pi^{-n}\det(u_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}$, a fully nonlinear expression in the second derivatives of $u$, which plays in pluripotential theory the role that the Laplacian plays in classical potential theory. Bedford and Taylor showed that for locally bounded $u$ these powers, and more generally the mixed products $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ with a positive closed current $T$, can be defined by induction. Since $T$ has measure coefficients and $u$ is a locally bounded Borel function, the product $uT$ makes sense, and one puts $\ddc u\wedge T=\ddc(uT)$. This chapter develops the local theory of these products.

In the first two sections we construct the products and establish their basic properties: positivity, the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg mass estimates, continuity along decreasing sequences, symmetry, locality on sets of the form $\{u>v\}$, and the comparison principle. The products then define the Monge--Amp\`ere capacity, a complex analogue of the relative capacity of \cref{chap:p04}. Plurisubharmonic functions are quasi-continuous with respect to it, and convergence in capacity is strong enough to pass to the limit in the products; in particular the products are also continuous along increasing sequences. By solving the homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere equation on a ball, we finally show that a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function is maximal if and only if its Monge--Amp\`ere measure vanishes. In \cref{chap:q03} these results are used to characterize pluripolar sets by relative extremal functions.

The remaining sections study the equation $(\ddc u)^n=\mu$ with a prescribed measure $\mu$ on a ball, together with inequalities for mixed products; these are needed for the truncation and envelope arguments on compact manifolds in later chapters. Lower bounds for Monge--Amp\`ere densities are first characterized by smooth functions touching from above. This yields mixed inequalities such as $(\ddc u)^k\wedge(\ddc v)^{n-k}\geq f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}(\ddc|z|^2)^n$ whenever $(\ddc u)^n\geq f(\ddc|z|^2)^n$ and $(\ddc v)^n\geq g(\ddc|z|^2)^n$ with continuous $f,g\geq0$. We then solve the Dirichlet problem on a ball for continuous and for bounded measurable densities and, using local energy estimates for approximations by averages over cubes, for compactly supported measures dominated by the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded plurisubharmonic function. This leads to the mixed inequality $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n\geq(f_1\cdots f_n)^{1/n}\mu$ for a common measure $\mu$ in \cref{thm:bounded-multifactor-common-measure}. The chapter closes with the locality of complex Hessians on contact sets and a chain rule for gradients measured against positive currents. All potentials in this chapter are locally bounded; products of unbounded potentials are treated in \cref{chap:q09}. References and historical remarks are collected in \cref{sec:history-bounded-products}.

\section{Bounded potentials and recursive products}
\label{sec:bt-construction}

Throughout this section $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ is open, $n\ge1$, and $T$ is a positive closed current on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, where $0\le r\le n$ is an integer; thus $T$ has bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$. Let $\beta=(\mathrm{i}/2)\sum_j\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j$ be the form of \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}. For a compact set $K\subset\Omega$ we write
\[
 \|T\|_K=\int_KT\wedge\frac{\beta^r}{r!}
\]
for the mass of $T$ on $K$; it is $1/r!$ times the value on $K$ of the trace measure of $T$ (\cref{def:positive-current}). By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, $T$ is a current of order zero, and on every compact set the total variations of its coefficient measures are bounded by a constant multiple of its trace measure. All plurisubharmonic functions in this section are locally bounded; since plurisubharmonic functions are always bounded above on compact sets, this means that they are also bounded below on compact sets.

\begin{definition}\label{def:bedford-taylor-product}
Suppose that $r\ge1$, and let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. Let $uT$ be the current of order zero whose coefficient measures are those of $T$ multiplied by the locally bounded Borel function $u$, and define
\[
 \ddc u\wedge T=\ddc(uT).
\]
For an integer $1\le q\le r$ and locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$, define recursively
\[
 \ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 =\ddc\bigl(u_1\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\bigr).
\]
Here $\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ is a positive closed current, hence of order zero, by \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion} below applied to $q-1$ factors; so its product with $u_1$ is defined as before. The product with no factors is $T$. When $T=1$ (so that $r=n$), $q=n$ and $u_1=\cdots=u_n=u$, the resulting positive current of bidegree $(n,n)$ is a positive measure, the \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere measure} $(\ddc u)^n$ of $u$.
\end{definition}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product}\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!locally bounded potentials}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-positive-recursion}
Every product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ of \cref{def:bedford-taylor-product} is a positive closed current. If $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are smooth, it coincides with the exterior product of the smooth forms $\ddc u_j$ with $T$. The construction is local: if $u_j=v_j$ for every $j$ and $T=T'$ on an open set $U\subset\Omega$, then the two products agree on $U$. It is additive in each $u_j$ and in $T$, whenever the summands are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, respectively positive closed currents of bidimension $(r,r)$, and it is invariant under biholomorphic changes of coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first treat a single factor $u$. Its radial regularizations $u_\varepsilon=u*\rho_\varepsilon$ are smooth on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ and plurisubharmonic, since their complex Hessians are the regularizations of the positive current $\ddc u$; by \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative} and \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} they decrease to $u$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. On each compact set they are bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$, because $\inf u\le u_\varepsilon\le u_{\varepsilon_0}$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$. Dominated convergence with respect to the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ therefore gives $u_\varepsilon T\to uT$ weakly. Since $T$ is closed and of pure bidegree, $\partial T=\bar\partial T=0$, and the product rule for a smooth function gives
\[
 \ddc(u_\varepsilon T)=\ddc u_\varepsilon\wedge T.
\]
The right-hand side is the product of the positive current $T$ with the smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\ddc u_\varepsilon$, so it is positive; it is also closed. As $\ddc$ is continuous for weak convergence, $\ddc u_\varepsilon\wedge T\to\ddc(uT)$ weakly, and positivity and closedness pass to the limit. Thus $\ddc u\wedge T$ is positive and closed. Applying this with $T$ replaced by the positive closed current $\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$, we obtain the first assertion by induction on $q$.

If the $u_j$ are smooth, the same product rule shows at each step of the recursion that the product is the exterior product of smooth forms with $T$. Locality and additivity follow from the definition, which uses only multiplication of coefficient measures by functions and differentiation; both operations are local and linear, and no product of two measures occurs. Finally, let $F$ be a biholomorphism between open sets. Pullback by $F$ maps coefficient measures to coefficient measures and satisfies $F^*(uT)=(u\circ F)\,F^*T$. Pullback of smooth forms commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, and hence so does pullback of currents, which is defined by the change of variables on test forms. Consequently every step of the recursion commutes with $F^*$, and the products defined in overlapping holomorphic charts agree.
\end{proof}

For $u\in C^2(\Omega)$, expanding the determinant and using $\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j=2\,\mathrm{d}x_j\wedge\mathrm{d}y_j$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt-determinant-normalization}
 (\ddc u)^n=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\det(u_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\ddc|z|^2=\beta/\pi$. (In dimension $n=0$ the only product is the empty one, the constant $1$, and integration is against the unit mass $\mathrm{d}V_0$ on the point $\mathbb C^0$.)

\begin{theorem}[Chern--Levine--Nirenberg]\label{thm:cln-bounded}
Let $K,L\subset\Omega$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $0\le q\le r$ be an integer. There is a constant $C_{K,L,q}$, depending only on $K$, $L$, $n$ and $q$, such that
\[
 \|\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\|_K
 \le C_{K,L,q}\|T\|_L\prod_{j=1}^q\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}
\]
for every positive closed current $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ on $\Omega$ and all locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$. Here $\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}=\sup_L|u_j|$ is the supremum of the pointwise function $u_j$ on $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $q=0$ the estimate holds with constant $1$, since $K\subset L$. Let $q=1$ and write $u=u_1$. By \cref{lem:mollifier-density} there is a smooth function $\chi\ge0$ with compact support in $\Int L$ which equals $1$ on a neighborhood of $K$. Since $\ddc u\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ is a positive measure, the definition of $\ddc$ on currents gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_K\ddc u\wedge T\wedge\beta^{r-1}
 &\le\int\chi\,\ddc(uT)\wedge\beta^{r-1}\\
 &=\int uT\wedge\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}
 \le C\|u\|_{L^\infty(L)}\|T\|_L.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The form $\ddc\chi\wedge\beta^{r-1}$ is smooth with support in $L$, though in general not positive; the last inequality holds because the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ on $L$ are bounded by a constant multiple of $\|T\|_L$. Absorbing the factorials into the constant proves the case $q=1$.

For $q>1$, choose compact sets $K=L_0\subset\Int L_1\subset L_1\subset\cdots\subset\Int L_q\subset L_q=L$, depending only on $K$ and $L$. By \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion}, the currents $\ddc u_{j+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ are positive and closed. Applying the case $q=1$ to them and to the pairs $L_{j-1}\subset\Int L_j$, for $j=1,\ldots,q$, and using $\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L_j)}\le\|u_j\|_{L^\infty(L)}$, we obtain the estimate.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality}

For continuous potentials, uniform convergence is enough to pass to the limit in the products.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}
Let $T_k\to T$ weakly, where $T_k$ and $T$ are positive closed currents on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, and let $0\le q\le r$. For $0\le j\le q$, let $(u_{j,k})_{k\ge1}$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ converging locally uniformly, as $k\to\infty$, to a finite continuous plurisubharmonic function $u_j$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T,\\
 u_{0,k}\ddc u_{1,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly as $k\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since the $u_{j,k}$ converge locally uniformly to finite continuous functions, they are bounded on each compact set uniformly in $k$; and since $T_k\to T$ weakly, the masses $\|T_k\|_L$ are bounded for each compact $L$. By \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, the masses of all the products considered below are therefore bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and so are the total variations of their coefficient measures.

We claim: if $S_k\to S$ weakly, where $S_k$ and $S$ are positive closed currents whose masses are bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and if locally bounded plurisubharmonic $v_k$ converge locally uniformly to a continuous $v$, then $v_kS_k\to vS$ weakly. Applying this to $S_k=\ddc u_{2,k}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,k}\wedge T_k$ and $v_k=u_{1,k}$, and then $\ddc$, which is weakly continuous, proves the first assertion by induction on $q$; the case $q=0$ is the hypothesis. The second assertion is the claim with $v_k=u_{0,k}$.

To prove the claim, fix a smooth test form $\varphi$ with compact support $K$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{lem:mollifier-density} there is a smooth function $v_\varepsilon$ with $|v-v_\varepsilon|\le\varepsilon$ on $K$. Write
\[
 v_kS_k-vS=(v_k-v)S_k+(v-v_\varepsilon)S_k
             +v_\varepsilon(S_k-S)+(v_\varepsilon-v)S.
\]
Evaluated on $\varphi$, the first term tends to zero since $v_k\to v$ uniformly on $K$ and the masses of $S_k$ on $K$ are bounded; the second and fourth terms are bounded by a constant times $\varepsilon$; and the third tends to zero as $k\to\infty$, since $v_\varepsilon\varphi$ is a smooth test form. Letting first $k\to\infty$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Decreasing continuity and symmetry}
\label{sec:bt-monotone}

A bounded plurisubharmonic function need not be continuous, so in general it cannot be approximated uniformly by smooth plurisubharmonic functions; it is, however, the limit of the decreasing sequence of its regularizations. The main result of this section, \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, states that the products are continuous along decreasing sequences and symmetric in their factors. Upper semicontinuity bounds every weak limit of weighted products from above (\cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}). To prove equality, we compare total masses by an integration by parts, after modifying all functions so that they coincide with one fixed smooth function near the boundary of a ball (\cref{lem:bt-common-collar}).

We use the operator $\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}$ of \eqref{eq:dc-operator}, for which $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}=\ddc$. If $a$ and $b$ are smooth real functions, the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}b$ is $\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}(\partial a\wedge\bar\partial b+\partial b\wedge\bar\partial a)$, which is symmetric in $a$ and $b$. Only this part survives multiplication by a current $R$ of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, so
\[
 \mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R=\mathrm{d}b\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}a\wedge R.
\]

If $B$ is a ball, a \emph{collar} of $\partial B$ is an open neighborhood of the sphere $\partial B$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bt-common-collar}
Let $B$ be a ball and $R$ a positive closed current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Let $a$ be a smooth function and $b$ a bounded plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and suppose that $a$ and $b$ coincide with the same smooth function $\rho$ on a collar of $\partial B$. Then
\[
 \int_B a\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int_B b\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
In particular one may take $R=S\wedge\gamma$, where $S$ is a positive closed current, $\gamma$ is a smooth closed form, and $S\wedge\gamma$ is positive of bidimension $(1,1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both integrals are finite, since $a$ and $b$ are bounded on $B$ and $\ddc a\wedge R$ and $\ddc b\wedge R$ are measures of finite total variation near $\overline B$. The function $h=b-a$ is bounded and Borel, and it vanishes on a collar of $\partial B$; hence its restriction to $B$ vanishes outside a compact set $K_0\subset B$. On $B$ we have $bR=aR+hR$, and since $R$ is closed, $\ddc(aR)=\ddc a\wedge R$; thus
\[
 \ddc b\wedge R=\ddc a\wedge R+\ddc(hR)\quad\text{on }B,
\]
where $\ddc(hR)$ is a signed measure with support in $K_0$. Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of $K_0$. By the definition of $\ddc$ on currents,
\[
 \int_Ba\,\ddc(hR)=\int\chi a\,\ddc(hR)=\int h\,\ddc(\chi a)\wedge R=\int_Bh\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int_Ba\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int_Ba\,\ddc a\wedge R+\int_Bh\,\ddc a\wedge R=\int_Bb\,\ddc a\wedge R.
\]
The last assertion holds because $S\wedge\gamma$ is then a positive closed current of bidimension $(1,1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}
Let $\mu_j\to\mu$ weakly, where $\mu_j$ and $\mu$ are positive Radon measures on $\Omega$, and let $f_j$ be locally bounded real upper semicontinuous functions on $\Omega$ which decrease to a locally bounded function $f$. If $f_{j_k}\mu_{j_k}\to\nu$ weakly along a subsequence, then $\nu\le f\mu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\chi\ge0$ be a continuous function with compact support, and let $N$ be a compact neighborhood of $\Supp\chi$. Weak convergence bounds the masses $\mu_j(N)$, and $f\le f_j\le f_1$ bounds the functions $f_j$ on $N$ uniformly in $j$; hence the convergences below may be tested against continuous functions with compact support. Fix $l$. By \cref{lem:usc-majorants}, there are continuous functions $g_m$ on $N$ decreasing to $f_l$. For $j_k\ge l$ we have $f_{j_k}\le f_l\le g_m$ on $N$, so
\[
 \int\chi f_{j_k}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j_k}\le\int\chi g_m\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j_k}.
\]
Letting $k\to\infty$ gives $\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le\int\chi g_m\,\mathrm{d}\mu$. Letting $m\to\infty$ and then $l\to\infty$, bounded convergence gives $\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$. Since $\chi\ge0$ was arbitrary, $\nu\le f\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-maximum}
There are smooth convex functions $M_\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$, $\varepsilon>0$, increasing in each variable, such that $M_\varepsilon\downarrow\max$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $\max\le M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$, and $M_\varepsilon(s,t)=\max\{s,t\}$ when $|s-t|>2\varepsilon$. If $a$ and $b$ are smooth plurisubharmonic functions, so is $M_\varepsilon(a,b)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be the mollifier of \cref{sec:convolution} on $\mathbb{R}^2$; it is a nonnegative even radial probability density supported in the disc of radius $\varepsilon$. Put $M_\varepsilon=\max*\rho_\varepsilon$, where $\max(s,t)=\max\{s,t\}$. Convolution with a nonnegative kernel preserves convexity and monotonicity in each variable. Since $\rho_\varepsilon$ is even, its barycenter is the origin, and Jensen's inequality gives $M_\varepsilon\ge\max$; since $|\max(x-y)-\max(x)|\le|y|$, we have $M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$. If $|s-t|>2\varepsilon$, the same variable is the larger one throughout the disc of radius $\varepsilon$ about $(s,t)$; there $\max$ is linear, and its average equals its value at the center. For fixed $x\in\mathbb R^2$, the function $\varepsilon\mapsto M_\varepsilon(x)=\int\max(x-\varepsilon y)\rho_1(y)\,\mathrm{d}y$, defined for all real $\varepsilon$, is convex and even, hence increasing for $\varepsilon\ge0$. Therefore $M_\varepsilon\downarrow\max$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Finally, let $a$ and $b$ be smooth and plurisubharmonic, and write $(a_1,a_2)=(a,b)$. Denoting by $\partial_kM_\varepsilon$ and $\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon$ the partial derivatives evaluated at $(a,b)$, the chain rule gives
\[
 \mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial M_\varepsilon(a,b)
 =\sum_{k=1}^2\partial_kM_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial a_k
 +\mathrm{i}\sum_{k,l=1}^2\partial_k\partial_lM_\varepsilon\,\partial a_k\wedge\bar\partial a_l.
\]
The first sum is positive because $M_\varepsilon$ is increasing in each variable. The second is positive because the real Hessian of $M_\varepsilon$ is positive semidefinite and $\bar\partial a_l=\overline{\partial a_l}$ for real $a_l$. Hence $M_\varepsilon(a,b)$ is plurisubharmonic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bedford--Taylor]\label{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}
Let $T$ be a positive closed current on $\Omega$ of bidimension $(r,r)$, and let $0\le q\le r$ be an integer. For $0\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a decreasing sequence of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ whose limit $u_l$ is locally bounded (and therefore plurisubharmonic, by \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}). Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T,\\
 u_{0,j}\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly as $j\to\infty$. Moreover, the product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions is symmetric in $u_1,\ldots,u_q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the induction.} We prove the second (weighted) assertion by induction on $q$, simultaneously for all $r\ge q$, all positive closed currents $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ and all decreasing sequences as in the statement. For $q=0$ it states that $u_{0,j}T\to u_0T$; this follows from dominated convergence with respect to the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$, since $u_0\le u_{0,j}\le u_{0,1}$. Let $q\ge1$ and assume the weighted assertion for fewer than $q$ factors. Applying it to the $q-1$ factors $u_{2,j},\ldots,u_{q,j}$ with the weight $u_{1,j}$, and then applying the weakly continuous operator $\ddc$, we obtain the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. Next, let $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ be locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, and apply the unweighted assertion to their regularizations $u_l*\rho_{\varepsilon_k}$, where $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$; by locality of the products, this is legitimate on each $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$. It shows that $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T$ is the weak limit of exterior products of smooth forms with $T$, which are symmetric in the factors. Hence products with $q$ factors are symmetric. It remains to prove the weighted assertion for $q$ factors, and in doing so we may use the unweighted assertion and the symmetry for $q$ factors, as well as the weighted assertion for fewer factors.

\emph{Step 2: reduction to a common collar.} Weak convergence is a local property. Fix a ball $B=B(a,R)$, a larger ball $B_1$ with $\overline B\subset B_1\Subset\Omega$, and a compact set $K\subset B$; we prove the convergence on a neighborhood of $K$. Since $u_l\le u_{l,j}\le u_{l,1}$, all the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ are bounded on $B_1$ uniformly in $j$. Subtracting a constant from $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ changes neither the Hessians nor, since the unweighted assertion is already known, the validity of the weighted assertion. We may therefore assume that $-N\le u_{l,j},u_l\le-1$ on $B_1$ for all $l$ and $j$, where $N\ge1$. Put $\psi=|z-a|^2-R^2$, choose $\delta>0$ with $K\subset\{\psi<-\delta\}$, and set $A=N/\delta$. Replace every function $v$ among the $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ by $\max\{v,A\psi\}$, which is plurisubharmonic on $B_1$ by \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}. Since $A\psi<-N\le v$ on $\{\psi<-\delta\}$, the functions are unchanged there, and by locality so are their products. Since $v\le-1<A\psi$ on $V=\{z\in B_1:\psi(z)>-1/A\}$, which is a collar of $\partial B$, all modified functions coincide with $A\psi$ on $V$. The modified sequences still decrease to the modified limits, and on $B$ they take values in $[-N,0)$. Hence we may assume, in addition, that all the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ coincide with $A\psi$ on $V$ and are negative on $B$.

\emph{Step 3: smooth approximation with the same collar.} Fix $l$ and $j$, and let $\tilde u$ be the function $u_{l,j}$ before the modification of Step 2, so that $\tilde u\le-1$ on $B_1$ and $u_{l,j}=\max\{\tilde u,A\psi\}$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ so small that $\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon$ is defined near $\overline B$, put
\[
 u^\varepsilon_{l,j}=M_\varepsilon(\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon,A\psi),
\]
with $M_\varepsilon$ as in \cref{lem:smooth-maximum}. These are smooth plurisubharmonic functions near $\overline B$. Since $\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon\le-1$, on the collar $V'=\{z\in B_1:\psi(z)>-1/(2A)\}\subset V$ we have $A\psi-\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon>1/2>2\varepsilon$, and therefore $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}=A\psi$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $\tilde u*\rho_\varepsilon$ decreases to $\tilde u$ and $M_\varepsilon$ decreases to $\max$; since $M_\varepsilon$ is increasing in each variable and $\max\le M_\varepsilon\le\max+\varepsilon$, it follows that $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}$ decreases to $\max\{\tilde u,A\psi\}=u_{l,j}$. In particular $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}\ge u_{l,j}\ge u_l$, and all the functions $u_{l,j}$, $u_l$ and $u^\varepsilon_{l,j}$ coincide with $A\psi$ on $V'$.

\emph{Step 4: an upper bound for weak limits.} Put
\[
 S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge T,\qquad
 S=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T.
\]
By Step 1, $S_j\to S$ weakly. On $B$ the weights $u_{0,j}$ are negative, so the currents $-u_{0,j}S_j$ are positive there, and by \cref{thm:cln-bounded} their masses on compact subsets of $B$ are bounded uniformly in $j$. By the compactness part of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly on $B$. Let $\Theta$ be the limit of such a subsequence. For every smooth strongly positive test form $\varphi$ of complementary bidegree, the measures $S_j\wedge\varphi$ are positive and converge weakly to $S\wedge\varphi$; hence \cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}, applied with $f_j=u_{0,j}$, gives $\Theta\wedge\varphi\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$. Thus $u_0S-\Theta$ is a positive current on $B$. By locality, all the currents $u_{0,j}S_j$, and also $u_0S$, coincide on $V'$ with the fixed current $A\psi\,(A\ddc\psi)^q\wedge T$; hence so does $\Theta$, and $u_0S-\Theta$ has compact support in $B$.

\emph{Step 5: comparison of masses.} Set $\gamma=\beta^{r-q}$ and
\[
 I=\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma,
 \qquad I_j=\int_Bu_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
We claim that $I\le I_j$ for every $j$. Fix $j$ and parameters $\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_q$, and abbreviate $w_l=u^{\varepsilon_l}_{l,j}$. For $0\le k\le q$ put
\[
 J_k=\int_Bu_k\,\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma,
\]
so that $J_0=I$. The measure in $J_k$ is positive and $u_k\le w_k$, so $J_k$ does not decrease if $u_k$ is replaced by $w_k$. If $k<q$, symmetry of products with $q$ factors lets us write that measure as $\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge R_k$, where
\[
 R_k=\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge\ddc u_{k+2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma
\]
is a positive closed current of bidimension $(1,1)$; then \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, with $a=w_k$, $b=u_{k+1}$ and the common collar function $A\psi$, gives
\[
 J_k\le\int_Bw_k\,\ddc u_{k+1}\wedge R_k=\int_Bu_{k+1}\,\ddc w_k\wedge R_k=J_{k+1}.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation*}
 I=J_0\le J_1\le\cdots\le J_q\le\int_Bw_q\,\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{q-1}\wedge T\wedge\gamma.
\end{equation*}
For example, the first two steps read $I\le\int_Bw_0\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma=\int_Bu_1\,\ddc w_0\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T\wedge\gamma$.

Now let $\varepsilon_q\downarrow0$, then $\varepsilon_{q-1}\downarrow0$, and so on down to $\varepsilon_1\downarrow0$, keeping $\varepsilon_0$ fixed. We claim that the right-hand side of the last inequality then tends to
\[
 \int_Bu_{q,j}\,\ddc w_0\wedge\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T\wedge\gamma.
\]
When $\varepsilon_q\downarrow0$, the measure is fixed and the bounded weights $w_q$ decrease to $u_{q,j}$, so dominated convergence applies. When $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ for some $1\le k\le q-1$, the integrand is, after moving the smooth factors next to $T$ by symmetry,
\[
 u_{q,j}\,\ddc u^{\varepsilon_k}_{k,j}\wedge\ddc u_{k+1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T_k\wedge\gamma,
 \qquad T_k=\ddc w_0\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc w_{k-1}\wedge T.
\]
Here $T_k$ is a fixed positive closed current, and there are $q-k\le q-1$ Hessian factors with the fixed weight $u_{q,j}$; by the induction hypothesis these currents converge weakly. In each case the convergence holds for the integrals over $B$, and not only against compactly supported test forms, because all the currents involved coincide on the collar $V'$ with the same fixed current: if $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equals $1$ on $B\setminus V'$, the integrals against $\chi$ converge, and the integrals against $1-\chi$ do not depend on the parameters.

By \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, with $a=w_0$ and $b=u_{q,j}$, and by symmetry of products with $q$ factors, the limit integral equals
\[
 \int_Bw_0\,\ddc u_{q,j}\wedge\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q-1,j}\wedge T\wedge\gamma
 =\int_Bu^{\varepsilon_0}_{0,j}\,S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
Hence $I\le\int_Bu^{\varepsilon_0}_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma$ for every $\varepsilon_0$, and dominated convergence with respect to the finite measure $S_j\wedge\gamma$ on $B$ gives $I\le I_j$ as $\varepsilon_0\downarrow0$.

\emph{Step 6: conclusion.} Along the subsequence defining $\Theta$, splitting the integrals with a cutoff as in Step 5 shows that $I_j\to\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma$. Therefore
\[
 \int_B(u_0S-\Theta)\wedge\gamma=I-\lim_jI_j\le0.
\]
Since $u_0S-\Theta$ is positive with compact support in $B$, its trace measure $(u_0S-\Theta)\wedge\gamma$ is a positive measure of total mass at most zero, hence zero; by the coefficient bounds of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, $u_0S-\Theta=0$ on $B$. Thus every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly to $u_0S$ on $B$, so the whole sequence converges to $u_0S$ there. This completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bt-polynomial-weights}
Let $P$ be a real polynomial in $m$ variables and, for $1\le i\le m$, let $(v_{i,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a decreasing sequence of bounded plurisubharmonic functions with bounded limit $v_i$. Then in \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} the weight $u_{0,j}$ may be replaced by $P(v_{1,j},\ldots,v_{m,j})$, and the limit is then
\[
 P(v_1,\ldots,v_m)\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge T.
\] Moreover, the identity of \cref{lem:bt-common-collar} also holds when $a$ and $b$ are both bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ which coincide with the same smooth function on a collar of $\partial B$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Adding a constant to each $v_{i,j}$ and to $v_i$ replaces $P$ by another polynomial; since the $v_i$ are bounded and $v_{i,j}\ge v_i$, we may assume that all these functions are nonnegative. By linearity it suffices to treat a monomial. A constant weight is covered by the unweighted assertion of the theorem. A monomial of degree $N\ge1$ is a product $a_1\cdots a_N$, where each $a_l$ is one of the $v_i$, repetitions being allowed, and
\[
 \prod_{l=1}^N a_l=\frac{1}{N!}
   \sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}}(-1)^{N-|I|}
        \Bigl(\sum_{l\in I}a_l\Bigr)^N.
\]
To verify this identity, expand the right-hand side: a monomial of degree $N$ in which some $a_l$ does not occur appears with alternating signs that cancel, and the coefficient of $a_1\cdots a_N$ is $N!$. Each sum $s=\sum_{l\in I}a_l$ is a bounded nonnegative plurisubharmonic function, and so is $s^N$: for smooth $s$ this follows from $\ddc(s^N)=Ns^{N-1}\ddc s+N(N-1)s^{N-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}s$, and in general by applying this to the regularizations of $s$, which are nonnegative and decrease to $s$, and using \cref{thm:psh-decreasing-nets}. The corresponding sums $s_j$ of the $a_{l,j}$ are nonnegative and decrease to $s$, so $s_j^N$ are bounded plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to $s^N$. The weighted assertion of the theorem therefore applies to each term of the identity, and hence to the monomial.

For the second assertion, let $a$ and $b$ be bounded plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and equal on a collar $V$ of $\partial B$. Their regularizations $a_\varepsilon=a*\rho_\varepsilon$ and $b_\varepsilon=b*\rho_\varepsilon$, for $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$, are smooth and plurisubharmonic near $\overline B$, decrease to $a$ and $b$, and coincide on a smaller collar $V'$ independent of $\varepsilon$. By \cref{lem:bt-common-collar}, applied with the smooth common collar function $a_\varepsilon$,
\[
 \int_Ba_\varepsilon\,\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R=\int_Bb_\varepsilon\,\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R.
\]
Choose $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on $B\setminus V'$. On $V'$ the measures $a_\varepsilon\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R$ and $b_\varepsilon\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R$ coincide, by locality, so the identity is equivalent to $\int\chi a_\varepsilon\,\ddc b_\varepsilon\wedge R=\int\chi b_\varepsilon\,\ddc a_\varepsilon\wedge R$. By the weighted assertion of the theorem with one factor and the fixed base current $R$, this passes to the limit: $\int\chi a\,\ddc b\wedge R=\int\chi b\,\ddc a\wedge R$. Since $a=b$ on $V$, the measures $a\,\ddc b\wedge R$ and $b\,\ddc a\wedge R$ also coincide on $V'$, and adding the integrals against $1-\chi$ over $B$ gives the identity for $a$ and $b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-maximum-locality}
Let $u$ and $v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$, and let $0\le p\le r$ be an integer. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}(\ddc\max\{u,v\})^p\wedge T
 =\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}(\ddc u)^p\wedge T.
\]
Here, for a current $S$ of order zero and a Borel set $E$, $\mathbf1_ES$ denotes the current whose coefficient measures are those of $S$ restricted to $E$. The set $\{u>v\}$ is Borel, but in general not open.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The assertion is local. Let $u_j$ be smooth plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to $u$, for instance radial regularizations. Since $v$ is upper semicontinuous, the set $\{u_j>v\}$ is open, and on it $w_j=\max\{u_j,v\}$ equals $u_j$; by locality, $(\ddc w_j)^p\wedge T=(\ddc u_j)^p\wedge T$ there. The nonnegative Borel function $f_j=w_j-v$ vanishes outside $\{u_j>v\}$, so
\[
 f_j(\ddc w_j)^p\wedge T=f_j(\ddc u_j)^p\wedge T.
\]
The functions $w_j$ decrease to $w=\max\{u,v\}$. Applying \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} to both sides, once with the weights $w_j$ and once with the constant weight $v$, we obtain
$f(\ddc w)^p\wedge T=f(\ddc u)^p\wedge T$, where $f=w-v\ge0$. Multiply the coefficient measures of both sides by $1/(f+\varepsilon)$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Since $f/(f+\varepsilon)$ increases to the indicator of $\{f>0\}=\{u>v\}$, dominated convergence with respect to the locally finite total variations of the coefficient measures gives the assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!maximum locality}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-comparison}
Let $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ be a bounded open set, and let $u$ and $v$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ such that $\liminf_{D\ni z\to x}(u(z)-v(z))\ge0$ for every $x\in\partial D$. Then
\[
 \int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc v)^n\le\int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc u)^n,
\]
where both sides may be infinite. If $(\ddc u)^n=0$, then $u\ge v$ on $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Every $x\in\partial D$ has a neighborhood $U_x$ with $u>v-\varepsilon$ on $U_x\cap D$, and finitely many of them cover the compact set $\partial D$. Hence $w=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}$ equals $u$ outside a compact subset of $D$, which contains $\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}$. By symmetry and additivity of the products,
\[
 (\ddc w)^n-(\ddc u)^n
 =\ddc\Bigl((w-u)\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}
           (\ddc w)^l\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-1-l}\Bigr),
\]
because the sum on the right telescopes. The current in parentheses has compact support. Testing with a cutoff equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of that support shows that the signed measure $(\ddc w)^n-(\ddc u)^n$, which is supported in the same compact set, has total mass zero. Choose a relatively compact open set $D'\subset D$ containing this compact set. Then both measures are finite on $D'$, and
$\int_{D'}(\ddc w)^n=\int_{D'}(\ddc u)^n$. By \cref{prop:bt-maximum-locality}, $(\ddc w)^n$ coincides with $(\ddc v)^n$ on $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ and with $(\ddc u)^n$ on $\{u>v-\varepsilon\}$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{\{u<v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc v)^n
 &\le\int_{D'}(\ddc w)^n
       -\int_{D'\cap\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc u)^n\\
 &=\int_{\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}}(\ddc u)^n
 \le\int_{\{u<v\}}(\ddc u)^n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the sets $\{u<v-\varepsilon\}$ increase to $\{u<v\}$, and monotone convergence proves the comparison inequality.

For the last assertion choose $R$ with $D\subset B(0,R)$, and for $\eta>0$ put $v_\eta=v+\eta(|z|^2-R^2)$. Since $v_\eta\le v$, the boundary condition still holds for $u$ and $v_\eta$. Expanding $(\ddc v_\eta)^n$ by additivity and symmetry, all terms are positive, so $(\ddc v_\eta)^n\ge\eta^n(\beta/\pi)^n$. The comparison inequality gives
\[
 \eta^n\int_{\{u<v_\eta\}}(\beta/\pi)^n\le\int_{\{u<v_\eta\}}(\ddc u)^n=0,
\]
so $\{u<v_\eta\}$ is a Lebesgue null set. Hence $\max\{u,v_\eta\}=u$ almost everywhere. Both functions are plurisubharmonic, so the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives in \cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}, applied on each connected component, gives $\max\{u,v_\eta\}=u$ everywhere, that is, $u\ge v_\eta$. Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives $u\ge v$.
\end{proof}
\index{comparison principle!bounded plurisubharmonic functions}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bt-local-domination}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded domain, $n\geq1$, and let $u$ and $v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$. Suppose that
\[
 \liminf_{D\ni z\to x}(v(z)-u(z))\geq0
       \quad(x\in\partial D),\qquad
 u\leq v\quad\text{almost everywhere for }(\ddc v)^n.
\]
Then $u\leq v$ everywhere on $D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $R$ with $D\subset B(0,R)$, and for $\varepsilon,\delta>0$ put
$u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=u-\varepsilon+\delta(|z|^2-R^2)$. By the boundary assumption and compactness of $\partial D$, there is an open set $U\supset\partial D$ such that $v>u-\varepsilon/2$ on $U\cap D$. Then $K_0=D\setminus U$ is compact, and
$A=\{v<u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\}\subset\{v<u-\varepsilon\}$ lies in $K_0$. Choose a relatively compact open set $D'\subset D$ containing $K_0$; its boundary lies in $D\setminus K_0\subset U$. Both functions are bounded on $D'$, and $v-u_{\varepsilon,\delta}>\varepsilon/2$ on $U\cap D$, so the comparison inequality of \cref{thm:bt-comparison} applies on $D'$ and gives
\[
 \delta^n\int_A(\ddc|z|^2)^n
 \leq\int_A(\ddc u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^n
 \leq\int_A(\ddc v)^n=0.
\]
The first inequality follows by expanding $(\ddc u_{\varepsilon,\delta})^n$ as in the proof of \cref{thm:bt-comparison}, and the last equality holds because $A\subset\{v<u\}$. Thus $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq v$ almost everywhere, and the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives, applied to $\max\{u_{\varepsilon,\delta},v\}$ and $v$, gives $u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq v$ everywhere. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $u\le v$.
\end{proof}
\index{domination principle!locally bounded potentials}

\section{Weighted estimates and Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
\label{sec:bt-capacity}

We now use the products to define a capacity adapted to plurisubharmonic functions. The Monge--Amp\`ere capacity of a set $E$ is the largest mass that a measure $(\ddc v)^n$ can give to $E$ when $v$ ranges over the plurisubharmonic functions with values in $[0,1]$. To control exceptional sets with it, we need integral estimates that are uniform over this whole class. The first one is a version of the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality with an unbounded plurisubharmonic weight.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cln-weighted}
Let $K,L\subset\Omega$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. There is a constant $C_{K,L,q}$ such that for every $V\in\PSH(\Omega)$ and all locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ on $\Omega$,
\[
 \int_K |V|\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}
 \le C_{K,L,q}\|V\|_{L^1(L)}
            \prod_{l=1}^q\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)}.
\]
In particular, the function $V$, with its values $-\infty$ on the pole set $\{V=-\infty\}$, is locally integrable with respect to the measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}$.
\end{theorem}
Note that the base current here is $T=1$. For a general positive closed current $T$ no such estimate holds: if $T$ is the current of integration on $\{z_1=0\}$ and $V=\log|z_1|$, then $V=-\infty$ on the support of $T$.
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: reductions.} For $q=0$ the left-hand side equals $n!\int_K|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}\le n!\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$. Let $q\ge1$. If $\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)}=0$ for some $l$, then $u_l=0$ on $\Int L$, so the product vanishes on $\Int L\supset K$ by locality. Otherwise we replace each $u_l$ by $u_l/(2\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)})-3/2$, which takes values in $[-2,-1]$ on $L$; this multiplies the left-hand side by $\prod_l(2\|u_l\|_{L^\infty(L)})^{-1}$. Covering $K$ by finitely many balls, we see that it suffices to prove the following: for concentric balls $B'=B(a,R')$ and $B=B(a,R)$ with $R'<R$ and $\overline B\subset\Int L$, there is a constant $C$, depending only on $B'$, $B$ and $L$, such that
\[
 \int_{B'}|V|\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}\le C\|V\|_{L^1(L)}
\]
whenever $u_1,\ldots,u_q$ are plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $L$ with values in $[-2,-1]$ on $L$.

\emph{Step 2: a common collar.} Put $\psi=|z-a|^2-R^2$ and $b=\ddc\psi=\beta/\pi$, choose $\delta>0$ with $\overline{B'}\subset\{\psi<-\delta\}$, and set $A=2/\delta$. As in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, replacing each $u_l$ by $\max\{u_l,A\psi\}$ leaves it unchanged on $\{\psi<-\delta\}$ and makes it equal to $A\psi$ on a collar of $\partial B$ containing $\{-\delta/2<\psi\le0\}$; the modified functions take values in $[-2,0)$ on $B$. Let $S$ be the product $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ of the modified functions. With $\tau=\delta/4$ and $B_\tau=\{\psi<-\tau\}$, we have $B'\subset B_\tau$ and $S=A^qb^q$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B\setminus B_\tau$. Choose a smooth function $\chi\ge0$ with compact support in $B$ such that $\chi=-\psi$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_\tau}$.

\emph{Step 3: smooth $V$ and $q\le n-1$.} Suppose that $q\le n-1$ and that $V$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ with $V\le0$ on $B_\tau$. Since $\ddc\chi=-b$ near $\overline{B_\tau}$ and $S=A^qb^q$ on $B\setminus B_\tau$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{B_\tau}(-V)S\wedge b^{n-q}
 &=\int_B V S\wedge b^{n-q-1}\wedge\ddc\chi
       -A^q\int_{B\setminus B_\tau}V b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi\\
 &=\int_B\chi\,\ddc V\wedge S\wedge b^{n-q-1}
       -A^q\int_{B\setminus B_\tau}V b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality is the definition of $\ddc$ on currents, since $\ddc(VS\wedge b^{n-q-1})=\ddc V\wedge S\wedge b^{n-q-1}$ for smooth $V$. Choose a compact set $L'\subset B$ whose interior contains $\Supp\chi$. By \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, applied with the positive closed base current $\ddc V$ of bidimension $(n-1,n-1)$, the first term is at most $C\|\ddc V\|_{L'}$. Testing $\ddc V$ against a cutoff $\chi'\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on $L'$, as in the proof of \cref{thm:cln-bounded}, gives $\|\ddc V\|_{L'}\le\int V\,\ddc\chi'\wedge\beta^{n-1}/(n-1)!\le C\int_B|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}$. The collar term is bounded by the same quantity, because $b^{n-1}\wedge\ddc\chi$ has bounded smooth coefficients. Hence
\[
 \int_{B_\tau}(-V)S\wedge b^{n-q}\le C\int_B|V|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}.
\]

\emph{Step 4: general $V$ and $q\le n-1$.} Let $V\in\PSH(\Omega)$, and choose $r_0>0$ with $2r_0<\operatorname{dist}(\overline B,\mathbb C^n\setminus\Int L)$. By the submean inequality, $V(x)\le c_0\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$ for every $x$ within distance $r_0$ of $\overline B$, where $c_0=|B(0,r_0)|^{-1}$; put $c=c_0\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$. For $0<\varepsilon<r_0$ the regularization $V_\varepsilon=V*\rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic near $\overline B$, satisfies $V_\varepsilon\le c$ on $\overline B$, and $\int_B|V_\varepsilon|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}\le\|V\|_{L^1(L)}$ by Fubini's theorem. Applying Step 3 to $V_\varepsilon-c$ gives
\[
 \int_{B_\tau}(c-V_\varepsilon)\,S\wedge b^{n-q}\le C\bigl(\|V\|_{L^1(L)}+c|B|\bigr)\le C'\|V\|_{L^1(L)}.
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the nonnegative functions $c-V_\varepsilon$ increase to $c-V$, so monotone convergence gives the same bound for $\int_{B_\tau}(c-V)\,S\wedge b^{n-q}$. Since $V\le c$ on $B_\tau$, we have $|V|\le(c-V)+c$ there, and the mass of $S\wedge b^{n-q}$ on $B_\tau$ is bounded by \cref{thm:cln-bounded}. Since $S$ coincides with the original product on $B'\subset B_\tau$, this proves the estimate of Step 1 for $q\le n-1$.

\emph{Step 5: the case $q=n$.} Consider $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ with coordinates $(z,w)$, the unit disc $\Delta\subset\mathbb C$, and the projection $p(z,w)=z$. The functions $u_l\circ p$ and $V\circ p$ are plurisubharmonic on $\Omega\times\Delta$. Regularizing the $u_l$ in $z$ and using \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, we see that $\ddc(u_1\circ p)\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc(u_n\circ p)$ is the pullback by $p$ of $\mu=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$. Its trace with respect to the form $\beta_z+\beta_w$ of $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ equals $\mu\otimes\mathrm{d}V_2$: the term with $\beta_z$ has bidegree $(n+1,n+1)$ in $z$ and vanishes, and $\beta_w=\mathrm{d}V_2$. Applying the case $q=n\le(n+1)-1$ already proved to the compact sets $K\times\{|w|\le1/2\}$ and $L\times\{|w|\le3/4\}$, and using Fubini's theorem on both sides, we obtain the estimate in $\mathbb C^n$; the areas of the two discs only change the constant. Combining the finite cover of Step 1 with the normalizations proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
\index{Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality!weighted}

\begin{definition}\label{def:monge-ampere-capacity}
Let $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ be a bounded open set. The \emph{Monge--Amp\`ere capacity} of a Borel set $E\subset D$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D(E)=\sup\left\{\int_E(\ddc v)^n:
            v\in\PSH(D),\ 0\le v\le1\right\}.
\]
The functions $v$ in this supremum are called \emph{competitors}. The \emph{outer capacity} of an arbitrary set $A\subset D$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(A)=\inf\{\operatorname{Cap}_D(G):A\subset G\subset D,
                                                  \ G\text{ open}\}.
\]
\end{definition}
\index{capacity!Monge--Amp\`ere}\index{capacity!outer Monge--Amp\`ere}
On a complex manifold, capacities are computed in coordinate charts, with $D$ a bounded open set relatively compact in the chart, usually a ball.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-capacity-basic}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded open set. The set functions $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ and $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D$ are increasing with respect to inclusion and countably subadditive. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{Cap}_D(K)<\infty$ for every compact $K\subset D$, and $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)=\sup\{\operatorname{Cap}_D(K):K\subset E\text{ compact}\}$ for every Borel set $E\subset D$;
\item if $E_j\subset D$ are Borel sets increasing to $E$, then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(E_j)\to\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$;
\item there is a constant $C_D$ such that $|E|\le C_D\operatorname{Cap}_D(E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset D$, where $|E|$ is Lebesgue measure;
\item if $B\Subset D$ is a ball and $K\subset B$ is compact, there is a constant $C$ such that, for every Borel set $E\subset K$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D(E)\le\operatorname{Cap}_B(E)
                              \le C\operatorname{Cap}_D(E),
\]
and the same inequalities hold for the outer capacities of arbitrary subsets of $K$;
\item both capacities are invariant under biholomorphisms between bounded open sets.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each competitor $v$, the measure $(\ddc v)^n$ is increasing and countably subadditive on Borel sets; taking the supremum over $v$ gives the same properties for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. For outer capacity, given sets $A_j$ and $\varepsilon>0$, choose open sets $G_j\supset A_j$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_j)<\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(A_j)+\varepsilon2^{-j}$ and use their union. Finiteness on compact sets follows from \cref{thm:cln-bounded} with $T=1$, since $\|v\|_{L^\infty}\le1$ for every competitor. Inner regularity of each Radon measure $(\ddc v)^n$, followed by interchanging the two suprema, gives the approximation by compact sets in~(1). Similarly, monotone convergence for each $(\ddc v)^n$ and an interchange of suprema give~(2). For~(3), choose $a\in D$ and $d>0$ with $D\subset B(a,d)$; the function $|z-a|^2/d^2$ is a competitor, and by \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is a fixed positive multiple of Lebesgue measure.

For~(4), the first inequality holds because the restriction to $B$ of a competitor for $D$ is a competitor for $B$, with the same Monge--Amp\`ere measure on $B$. For the second, write $B=B(a,r)$ and choose $A>0$ such that $\rho=A(|z-a|^2-r^2)<-1$ on a neighborhood of $K$. Given a competitor $v$ for $B$, let $w$ be the function equal to $\max\{v-1,\rho\}$ on $B$ and to $\rho$ on $D\setminus B$. Since $v-1\le0=\rho$ on $\partial B$, \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing} shows that $w$ is plurisubharmonic on $D$. It equals $v-1$ near $K$, satisfies $w\ge-1$ on $D$, and is bounded above by $\Lambda=\max\{0,\sup_D\rho\}$, which does not depend on $v$. Thus $(w+1)/(\Lambda+1)$ is a competitor for $D$, and by locality its Monge--Amp\`ere measure near $K$ is $(\Lambda+1)^{-n}(\ddc v)^n$. This gives the second inequality with $C=(\Lambda+1)^n$. For outer capacities, choose a compact neighborhood $K'$ of $K$ in $B$ and the corresponding constant $C'$. Every open set $G$ containing $A\subset K$ may be replaced by $G\cap\Int K'$, and applying the Borel inequalities to these open sets and taking infima proves the inequalities for $\operatorname{Cap}^*$.

For~(5), let $F$ be a biholomorphism between bounded open sets. By \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, composition with $F$ is a bijection between the competitors, and by the invariance in \cref{prop:bt-positive-recursion} the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $v\circ F$ is the pullback of that of $v$. The change of variables formula gives equality of the capacities of Borel sets; since $F$ is a homeomorphism, it also maps open covers to open covers, so the outer capacities agree as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity}
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded open set, let $K,L\subset D$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $V\in\PSH(D)$. There is a constant $C_{K,L}$, depending only on $K$, $L$ and $n$, such that
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{V<-s\})
                 \le\frac{C_{K,L}}{s}\|V\|_{L^1(L)},\qquad s>0.
\]
Consequently, let $E\subset D$ be a set locally contained in pole sets of plurisubharmonic functions, that is, every point of $D$ has an open neighborhood $W\subset D$ and a plurisubharmonic function $V_W$ on $W$ with $E\cap W\subset\{V_W=-\infty\}$. (Such sets are called pluripolar in \cref{chap:q03}.) Then $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K)=0$ for every compact set $K\subset D$, and $E$ is contained in a Borel set which has measure zero for every measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$ with locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ on $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose an open neighborhood $U$ of $K$ with compact closure in $\Int L$. For every competitor $v$, \cref{thm:cln-weighted} with $T=1$ gives
\[
 \int_{U\cap\{V<-s\}}(\ddc v)^n\le s^{-1}\int_{\overline U}|V|\,(\ddc v)^n
\le C s^{-1}\|V\|_{L^1(L)}.
\]
Since $V$ is upper semicontinuous, $U\cap\{V<-s\}$ is an open set containing $K\cap\{V<-s\}$; taking the supremum over $v$ proves the estimate. Letting $s\to\infty$ gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{V=-\infty\})=0$.

Now let $E$ be as in the statement. Each $x\in D$ has a ball $B_x$ with $\overline{B_x}\subset W$ and $E\cap B_x\subset\{V_W=-\infty\}$, where $W$ is the neighborhood of $x$ in the hypothesis, and a compact neighborhood $K_x\subset B_x$. The estimate just proved, applied in $B_x$ to the restriction of $V_W$, gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_{B_x}(E\cap K_x)=0$; since every open set in $B_x$ is open in $D$, the first inequality of \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4) gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K_x)=0$. A compact set $K\subset D$ is covered by finitely many interiors $\Int K_x$, and subadditivity gives $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(E\cap K)=0$. Finally, by \cref{thm:cln-weighted} the Borel set $K_x\cap\{V_W=-\infty\}$ has measure zero for every measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_n$ as in the statement, since $V_W$ is integrable on $K_x$ for this measure. Countably many sets $\Int K_x$ cover $D$, and the union of the corresponding sets $K_x\cap\{V_W=-\infty\}$ is a Borel set with the required property.
\end{proof}

The next estimate controls, uniformly over all competitors, the set where two bounded plurisubharmonic functions with the same boundary values differ; it is the main tool of the next section.

\begin{proposition}[B\l{}ocki]\label{prop:blocki-difference-estimate}
Let $B$ be a ball, and let $u$ and $h$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ with $u\le h$ on $B$ and $u=h$ on a collar of $\partial B$. Let $v$ be a bounded plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and put $M=\sup_Bv-\inf_Bv$. Then
\[
 \int_B(h-u)^{n+1}(\ddc v)^n
       \le(n+1)!M^n\int_B(h-u)(\ddc u)^n.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that $u$, $h$ and $v$ are smooth. Subtracting $\sup_Bv$ from $v$, we may assume $-M\le v\le0$ on $B$. Put $w=h-u\ge0$; it vanishes on a collar of $\partial B$. Let $p\ge2$, and let $R$ be a smooth positive closed form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. Since $\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}w=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial w\wedge\bar\partial w$ is a positive $(1,1)$-form and $\ddc h\wedge R\ge0$, the chain rule gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc(w^p)\wedge R&=\bigl(p w^{p-1}(\ddc h-\ddc u)
               +p(p-1)w^{p-2}\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}w\bigr)\wedge R\\
 &\ge-pw^{p-1}\ddc u\wedge R.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since $w^p$ has compact support in $B$, integration by parts and $-M\le v\le0$ yield
\[
 \int_Bw^p\ddc v\wedge R
   =\int_Bv\,\ddc(w^p)\wedge R
   \le pM\int_Bw^{p-1}\ddc u\wedge R.
\]
Apply this first with $p=n+1$ and $R=(\ddc v)^{n-1}$, then with $p=n$ and $R=(\ddc v)^{n-2}\wedge\ddc u$, and so on; after $n$ steps, the last one with $p=2$ and $R=(\ddc u)^{n-1}$, we obtain the asserted inequality, since $(n+1)n\cdots2=(n+1)!$.

In general, choose a smaller concentric ball $B'$ whose boundary lies in the common collar and which contains the support of $(h-u)|_B$. Convolve $u$, $h$ and $v$ with the same radial kernel $\rho_\varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon$ small. The regularizations satisfy $u_\varepsilon\le h_\varepsilon$, and $h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$ vanishes on a fixed collar of $\partial B'$. The values of $v_\varepsilon$ on $B'$ are averages of values of $v$ on $B$, so the oscillation of $v_\varepsilon$ on $B'$ is at most $M$. Apply the smooth case on $B'$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The weights $(h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon)^{n+1}$ and $h_\varepsilon-u_\varepsilon$ are polynomials in decreasing sequences of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and they vanish outside a fixed compact subset of $B'$; so \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} lets both sides pass to the limit. Since $h-u$ vanishes on $B\setminus B'$, the limiting inequality on $B'$ is the asserted inequality on $B$.
\end{proof}
\index{B\l{}ocki's estimate}

\section{Quasi-continuity and convergence in capacity}
\label{sec:bt-quasicontinuity}

Throughout this section $D\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ is a bounded open set. Subharmonic functions are continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small classical capacity (\cref{thm:subharmonic-quasicontinuity}). We prove the analogous statement for plurisubharmonic functions and the Monge--Amp\`ere capacity, and then show that convergence in capacity can be passed through the products.

\begin{definition}\label{def:quasi-continuity}
A Borel function $f\colon D\to[-\infty,\infty]$ is \emph{quasi-continuous} (for $\operatorname{Cap}_D$) if for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ such that the restriction of $f$ to $K\setminus G$ is continuous. Here $[-\infty,\infty]$ carries its order topology.
\end{definition}
\index{quasi-continuity!Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
The same definition applies to a function defined on an open subset $W\subset D$, with compact sets $K\subset W$ and still with $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. A difference of two real quasi-continuous functions is quasi-continuous, by subadditivity of capacity.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}
Let $u\in\PSH(D)$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ which contains the pole set $\{u=-\infty\}$ and such that the restriction of $u$ to $D\setminus G$ is finite and continuous. In particular $u$ is quasi-continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: locally bounded $u$.} Let $u$ be locally bounded, $K\subset D$ compact and $\varepsilon>0$; we find an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$ such that $u$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$. Covering $K$ by finitely many balls and dividing $\varepsilon$ accordingly, we may assume that $K=\overline{B'}$, where $B'\Subset B\Subset B_1\Subset D$ are concentric balls. Subtracting a constant, we may assume that $-N\le u\le-1$ on $B_1$. With $\psi$, $\delta$ and $A$ chosen as in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} for the compact set $\overline{B'}$, put
\[
 g=\max\{u,A\psi\},\qquad g_j=\max\{u*\rho_{1/j},A\psi\},
\]
for $j$ large. The functions $g_j$ are continuous and plurisubharmonic near $\overline B$ and decrease to $g$; moreover $g=u$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B'}$, and $g_j=g=A\psi$ on a common collar of $\partial B$, since $u*\rho_{1/j}\le-1$. For $\delta'>0$ put $G_{j,\delta'}=\{z\in B:g_j(z)-g(z)>\delta'\}$; it is open, because $g_j$ is continuous and $g$ is upper semicontinuous. Every competitor $v$ for $D$ has oscillation at most $1$ on $B$, so \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate}, applied with $h=g_j$ and $u=g$, and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \int_{G_{j,\delta'}}(\ddc v)^n\le\frac{1}{\delta'^{n+1}}\int_B(g_j-g)^{n+1}(\ddc v)^n
 \le\frac{(n+1)!}{\delta'^{n+1}}\int_B(g_j-g)(\ddc g)^n.
\]
Taking the supremum over $v$, we obtain the same bound for $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_{j,\delta'})$. The right-hand side tends to zero as $j\to\infty$ by dominated convergence, because $g$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic near $\overline B$, so $(\ddc g)^n$ has finite mass on $B$, and $0\le g_j-g\le g_{j_0}-g$ decreases to zero. Choose $j_1<j_2<\cdots$ such that $G_l=G_{j_l,2^{-l}}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_l)<\varepsilon2^{-l}$, and let $G=\bigcup_lG_l$. Then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and on $B\setminus G$ we have $0\le g_{j_l}-g\le2^{-l}$ for every $l$. Thus $g$ is a uniform limit of continuous functions on $B\setminus G$, hence continuous there; in particular $u=g$ is continuous on $\overline{B'}\setminus G$.

\emph{Step 2: arbitrary $u$.} Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $\varepsilon>0$. By \cref{prop:bt-poles-zero-capacity} there is $s>0$ with $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{u<-s\})<\varepsilon/2$, and hence an open set $G_1\supset K\cap\{u<-s\}$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_1)<\varepsilon/2$. By Step 1, applied to the locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\max\{u,-s\}$, there is an open set $G_2$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G_2)<\varepsilon/2$ such that $\max\{u,-s\}$ is continuous on $K\setminus G_2$. On $K\setminus(G_1\cup G_2)$ we have $u\ge-s$, hence $u=\max\{u,-s\}$. Thus $G=G_1\cup G_2$ contains $K\cap\{u=-\infty\}$, $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and $u$ is finite and continuous on $K\setminus G$.

\emph{Step 3: a single exceptional set.} Choose compact sets $K_l$ with $K_l\subset\Int K_{l+1}$ and $\bigcup_lK_l=D$. By Step 2 there are open sets $G^{(l)}\supset K_l\cap\{u=-\infty\}$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G^{(l)})<\varepsilon2^{-l}$ such that $u$ is finite and continuous on $K_l\setminus G^{(l)}$. Put $G=\bigcup_lG^{(l)}$. Then $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\varepsilon$, and $G$ contains the pole set of $u$. Every $x\in D\setminus G$ lies in some $\Int K_l$, and on the neighborhood $\Int K_l\setminus G$ of $x$ in $D\setminus G$ the function $u$ is a restriction of the continuous function $u|_{K_l\setminus G^{(l)}}$. Hence $u|_{D\setminus G}$ is finite and continuous.
\end{proof}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!quasi-continuity}

In the proofs below we extend continuous functions from compact sets, using the following elementary case of Tietze's extension theorem: if $F\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ is compact and $f\colon F\to\mathbb R$ is continuous with $|f|\le a$, then $f$ has a continuous extension $g$ to $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|g|\le a$. For completeness we give a direct construction. If $F=\varnothing$ or $a=0$, take $g=0$. Otherwise, for $L>0$ put
\[
 f_L(x)=\max\Bigl\{-a,\min\bigl\{a,\inf_{y\in F}(f(y)+L|x-y|)\bigr\}\Bigr\}.
\]
This is a Lipschitz function on $\mathbb{R}^d$, and $f_L\to f$ uniformly on $F$ as $L\to\infty$. Indeed, $f_L\le f$ on $F$. Given $\eta>0$, uniform continuity gives $r>0$ with $|f(y)-f(x)|<\eta$ for $x,y\in F$ with $|x-y|<r$; if $Lr>2a$, then points $y$ with $|x-y|\ge r$ contribute values larger than $a\ge f(x)$, while the other points contribute values larger than $f(x)-\eta$, so $f_L(x)\ge f(x)-\eta$ for $x\in F$. Hence there is a continuous $g_1$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|g_1|\le a$ and $|f-g_1|\le a/2$ on $F$. Applying the same construction to $f-g_1$ on $F$, we find $g_2$ with $|g_2|\le a/2$ and $|f-g_1-g_2|\le a/4$ on $F$, and so on. The series $\sum_kg_k$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}^d$; its sum is continuous and equals $f$ on $F$, and clamping it to $[-a,a]$ does not change its values on $F$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity}
Let $K,L\subset D$ be compact sets with $K\subset\Int L$, and let $f$ be a real, bounded Borel function which is defined and quasi-continuous on a neighborhood of $L$. Then, for every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f|>\delta\})
       \le\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\eta>0$. Choose an open set $G\subset D$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_D(G)<\eta$ such that $f$ is continuous on $L\setminus G$, and let $g$ be a continuous extension of $f|_{L\setminus G}$ to $\mathbb{C}^n$, as constructed above. The open set $G\cup(\Int L\cap\{|g|>\delta/2\})$ contains $K\cap\{|f|>\delta\}$, since $g=f$ on $K\setminus G$. Its part outside $G$ is contained in $L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}$, because $g=f$ on $L\setminus G$. By subadditivity,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f|>\delta\})\le\eta+\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{|f|>\delta/2\}).
\]
Let $\eta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:local-capacity-convergence}
A net $(f_a)$ of Borel functions $f_a:D\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ \emph{converges locally in capacity} to a Borel function $f:D\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ if, for every compact set $K\subset D$ and every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{f_a>f+\delta\})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{f_a<f-\delta\})\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here $\pm\infty+\delta=\pm\infty$ and $\pm\infty-\delta=\pm\infty$.
\end{definition}
\index{convergence!Monge--Amp\`ere capacity}
For finite functions the two conditions together are equivalent to $\operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f_a-f|>\delta\})\to0$, by monotonicity and subadditivity of outer capacity. By the comparison of capacities in \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), convergence locally in capacity is a local property: it holds on $D$ if and only if, for every ball $B\Subset D$, it holds on $B$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ in place of $\operatorname{Cap}_D$. In particular it does not depend on the bounded open set containing the compact sets under consideration.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-decreasing-capacity}
Let $u_j$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ decreasing to a locally bounded function $u$. Then $u_j\to u$ locally in capacity. Moreover, a net of real Borel functions on $D$ which is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$ and converges locally in capacity to a locally bounded real Borel function also converges in $L^1_{\loc}(D)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $\delta>0$. Since $u_j\ge u$, the sets $\{u_j<u-\delta\}$ are empty, and we must show that $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{u_j-u>\delta\})\to0$. Covering $K$ by finitely many balls, we may assume that $K\subset\Int L$, where $L\subset B'$ is compact and $B'\Subset B\Subset D$ are concentric balls. As in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, there are bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\hat u_j$ and $\hat u$ near $\overline B$, with $\hat u_j\downarrow\hat u$, which coincide with $u_j$ and $u$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B'}$ and with a common function $A\psi$ on a collar of $\partial B$. For every competitor $v$, \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate} and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{\hat u_j-\hat u>\delta/2\}\bigr)
 \le(n+1)!\,(2/\delta)^{n+1}\int_B(\hat u_j-\hat u)(\ddc\hat u)^n,
\]
and the right-hand side tends to zero by dominated convergence. The function $w_j=u_j-u$ is bounded on a neighborhood of $L$ and quasi-continuous there, by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}. Hence \cref{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{w_j>\delta\})\le\operatorname{Cap}_D(L\cap\{w_j>\delta/2\})\le\operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{\hat u_j-\hat u>\delta/2\}\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]

For the second assertion, let $f_a\to f$ be as in the statement, let $K\subset D$ be compact, and let $M$ bound $|f_a|$ and $|f|$ on $K$. Splitting $K$ into the set where $|f_a-f|>\delta$ and its complement, and using \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(3), we get
\[
 \int_K|f_a-f|\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n}
 \le 2MC_D\operatorname{Cap}_D^*(K\cap\{|f_a-f|>\delta\})
          +\delta|K|.
\]
Taking the limit along the net and then letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves $L^1$ convergence on $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a ball, and let $\mathcal U$ be a family of plurisubharmonic functions on $B$ which is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $B$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every compact set $K\subset B$ there is a constant $C$ such that, for all integers $0\le q\le n$, all $u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathcal U$ and all Borel sets $E\subset K$, the trace measure $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}$, and the total variation of every coefficient measure of $\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$, give $E$ mass at most $C\operatorname{Cap}_B(E)$.
\item Let $S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ with $u_{l,j}\in\mathcal U$, and suppose that $S_j\to S$ weakly. Let $f_j$ be real Borel functions on $B$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $B$, which converge locally in capacity (for $\operatorname{Cap}_B$) to a real Borel function $f$ that is locally bounded and quasi-continuous. Then $f_jS_j\to fS$ weakly, and the same holds for the trace measures of $S_j$ and $S$. This applies in particular to a fixed weight $f_j=f$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Let $B=B(a,R)$, fix $K$, and choose $R'<R$ with $K\subset B'=B(a,R')$. Let $M\ge1$ be a common bound for $|u|$ on $B'$, $u\in\mathcal U$. For $u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathcal U$ put $v_l=(u_l+M)/(2M)$ and $\phi=|z-a|^2/R'^2$; these functions take values in $[0,1]$ on $B'$, and so does
\[
 F=\frac1n\bigl(v_1+\cdots+v_q+(n-q)\phi\bigr),
\]
which is therefore a competitor for $\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}$. Expand $(\ddc F)^n$ by additivity and symmetry. All terms are positive, and those in which each $\ddc v_l$ occurs once and $\ddc\phi$ occurs $n-q$ times add up to a positive multiple, depending only on $n$ and $q$, of $\ddc v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc v_q\wedge(\ddc\phi)^{n-q}$. Since $\ddc\phi=\beta/(\pi R'^2)$ and $\ddc u_l=2M\ddc v_l$, it follows that $\int_E\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\beta^{n-q}\le C\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}(E)$ for Borel $E\subset B'$. The bound for the coefficient measures follows from \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, and \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), applied to $B'\Subset B$ and $K$, replaces $\operatorname{Cap}_{B'}$ by $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ on subsets of $K$.

(2) Let $\sigma_j$ be either the trace measure or a coefficient measure of $S_j$, and $\sigma$ the corresponding measure of $S$; then $\sigma_j\to\sigma$ weakly. Fix a continuous function $\chi$ with compact support in $B$ and a compact set $K\subset B$ with $\Supp\chi\subset\Int K$, and let $C$ be the constant of~(1) for $K$. A similar bound holds for $\sigma$ on open subsets $U\subset\Int K$: for the trace measure, \cref{prop:vague-set-inequalities} gives $\sigma(U)\le\liminf_j\sigma_j(U)\le C\operatorname{Cap}_B(U)$, and the total variations of the coefficient measures of the positive current $S$ are bounded by a constant multiple of its trace measure.

Consider first the fixed weight $f$, and let $\eta>0$. Since $f$ is quasi-continuous, there is an open set $G\subset B$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_B(G)<\eta$ such that $f$ is continuous on $K\setminus G$; let $g$ be a continuous extension of $f|_{K\setminus G}$ with $\sup|g|\le\sup_K|f|$, as constructed above. Then
\[
 \Bigl|\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j-\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\Bigr|\le2\sup_K|f|\sup|\chi|\,|\sigma_j|(G\cap\Supp\chi)\le C'\eta,
\]
and similarly for $\sigma$, using the open set $G\cap\Int K$. Since $\chi g$ is continuous with compact support and the total variations of the $\sigma_j$ are bounded on compact sets, $\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\to\int\chi g\,\mathrm{d}\sigma$. Hence $\limsup_j|\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j-\int\chi f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma|\le2C'\eta$ for every $\eta>0$, which proves $f\sigma_j\to f\sigma$.

For varying weights, let $M_K$ bound $|f_j|$ and $|f|$ on $K$, and let $\delta>0$. By~(1),
\[
 \Bigl|\int\chi(f_j-f)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_j\Bigr|
 \le\sup|\chi|\Bigl(\delta\,C\operatorname{Cap}_B(K)+2M_KC\operatorname{Cap}^*_B\bigl(K\cap\{|f_j-f|>\delta\}\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
The second term tends to zero by convergence in capacity, and then $\delta\downarrow0$. Together with the fixed-weight case, this proves $f_j\sigma_j\to f\sigma$, and hence $f_jS_j\to fS$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-capacity-continuity}
Let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. For $1\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be a sequence of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converges locally in capacity to a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u_l$. Then
\[
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
             \longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q
\]
weakly. More generally, if $f_j$ are real Borel functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converge locally in capacity to a locally bounded, quasi-continuous real Borel function $f$, then $f_j\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}\to f\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ weakly.
\end{theorem}
Unlike \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, this theorem concerns only products with the base current $T=1$.
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!continuity under convergence in capacity}
\begin{proof}
Weak convergence is local. By \cref{prop:bt-capacity-basic}(4), the hypotheses hold with $\operatorname{Cap}_B$ in place of $\operatorname{Cap}_D$ on every ball $B\Subset D$, and the functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$, restricted to $B$, form a family which is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $B$. We may therefore work on such a ball and use \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}. We argue by induction on $q$. For $q=0$ the product is $1$, and the weighted assertion is the second part of the lemma with $S_j=1$. Let $q\ge1$, and assume that $S_j=\ddc u_{2,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ converges weakly to $S=\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$. The functions $u_{1,j}$ converge in capacity to $u_1$, which is quasi-continuous by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}; hence the second part of the lemma gives $u_{1,j}S_j\to u_1S$, and applying $\ddc$ proves the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. (For $q=1$ this is also a consequence of the $L^1_{\loc}$ convergence in \cref{prop:psh-decreasing-capacity}.) The weighted assertion then follows from the second part of the lemma once more.
\end{proof}

\section{Increasing limits and exceptional envelopes}
\label{sec:bt-increasing}

The supremum of an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions need not be upper semicontinuous; it agrees with its upper semicontinuous regularization only outside an exceptional set. We show that the products are nevertheless continuous along increasing sequences. The proof follows the pattern of the decreasing case: the inequality $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ bounds weak limits from above, while the quasi-continuity of the fixed weights, through \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}, supplies the lower bound for the masses. As a consequence, the exceptional set of an upper envelope has outer capacity zero.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bt-increasing-continuity}
Let $0\le q\le n$ be an integer. For $0\le l\le q$, let $(u_{l,j})_{j\ge1}$ be an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$, uniformly bounded on each compact subset of $D$, which converges almost everywhere to a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u_l$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
 &\longrightarrow\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q,\\
 u_{0,j}\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}
 &\longrightarrow u_0\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q
 \end{aligned}
\]
weakly. Moreover $u_{l,j}\to u_l$ locally in capacity for every $l$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the weighted assertion by induction on $q$, for all sequences as in the statement. For $q=0$ it follows from dominated convergence with respect to Lebesgue measure. Let $q\ge1$ and assume the weighted assertion for $q-1$ factors; applying $\ddc$ to it, with the weight $u_{1,j}$, gives the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors. Put $S_j=\ddc u_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{q,j}$ and $S=\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$, so that $S_j\to S$ weakly.

As in Step 2 of the proof of \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity}, we work on a ball $B\Subset D$ and may assume that all functions $u_{l,j}$ and $u_l$ coincide with a fixed function $A\psi$ on a collar $V$ of $\partial B$ and are negative on $B$, without changing them near a given compact subset of $B$; the maximum with $A\psi$ preserves increasing sequences and their limits almost everywhere. On $V$, all the currents considered below coincide with fixed currents, so integrals over $B$ may be split by a cutoff $\chi\in C^\infty_c(B)$ equal to $1$ on $B\setminus V$, as in Step 5 of that proof; we use this without further comment. By \cref{thm:cln-bounded} and the compactness part of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, every subsequence of $(u_{0,j}S_j)$ has a further subsequence converging weakly on $B$; let $\Theta$ be its limit.

Since $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ almost everywhere, the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives, applied to $\max\{u_{0,j},u_0\}$ and $u_0$, gives $u_{0,j}\le u_0$ everywhere. Hence $u_{0,j}S_j\le u_0S_j$. For a smooth strongly positive test form $\varphi$ of complementary bidegree, a further subsequence of $u_0S_j\wedge\varphi$ converges weakly to some $\nu$, and \cref{lem:usc-weight-weak-limit}, applied to the constant sequence $f_j=u_0$, gives $\nu\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$. Therefore $\Theta\wedge\varphi\le u_0S\wedge\varphi$, so $u_0S-\Theta$ is a positive current; it vanishes on $V$. Write $\gamma=\beta^{n-q}$.

For fixed $k$ and all $j\ge k$, positivity of $S_j\wedge\gamma$ and $u_{0,j}\ge u_{0,k}$ give
\[
 \int_Bu_{0,j}S_j\wedge\gamma\ge\int_Bu_{0,k}S_j\wedge\gamma.
\]
Let $j\to\infty$ along the subsequence defining $\Theta$. The left-hand side tends to $\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma$. The fixed weight $u_{0,k}$ is quasi-continuous by \cref{thm:psh-capacity-quasicontinuity}, so by \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination} the right-hand side tends to $\int_Bu_{0,k}S\wedge\gamma$. By the second assertion of \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} and symmetry,
\[
 \int_Bu_{0,k}S\wedge\gamma=\int_Bu_1\,\ddc u_{0,k}\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\gamma.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, the unweighted assertion for $q$ factors, already established, gives $\ddc u_{0,k}\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\to\ddc u_0\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q$ weakly, and \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination}, with the fixed quasi-continuous weight $u_1$, passes the integral to the limit. A second application of \cref{cor:bt-polynomial-weights} identifies the limit with $\int_Bu_0\,\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\wedge\gamma=\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma$. We have shown that $\int_B\Theta\wedge\gamma\ge\int_Bu_0S\wedge\gamma$. Since $u_0S-\Theta$ is positive with compact support in $B$, its trace measure vanishes, and $\Theta=u_0S$ by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}. As in the decreasing case, it follows that the whole sequence $u_{0,j}S_j$ converges to $u_0S$, which completes the induction.

Now that both assertions are known for every $q\le n$, we prove convergence in capacity. Fix $l$ and use the same collar modification, with $u=u_{l,j}\le h=u_l$ in \cref{prop:blocki-difference-estimate}. For every competitor $v$ and $\delta>0$, Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_D\bigl(B\cap\{u_l-u_{l,j}>\delta\}\bigr)\le\frac{(n+1)!}{\delta^{n+1}}\int_B(u_l-u_{l,j})(\ddc u_{l,j})^n.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero: $\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_{l,j})^n\to\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_l)^n$ by the unweighted assertion and \cref{lem:bounded-product-capacity-domination} with the fixed quasi-continuous weight $u_l$, and $\int_Bu_{l,j}(\ddc u_{l,j})^n\to\int_Bu_l(\ddc u_l)^n$ by the weighted assertion with all factors and the weight equal to $u_{l,j}$. Since $u_{l,j}\le u_l$, the sets $\{u_{l,j}>u_l+\delta\}$ are empty. \Cref{lem:quasi-continuous-level-capacity}, applied to the bounded quasi-continuous function $u_l-u_{l,j}$, turns these estimates into the required bounds for outer capacity on compact subsets of the ball where the functions were not modified; a finite cover completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\index{Bedford--Taylor product!increasing continuity}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:psh-envelope-zero-capacity}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty family of plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ which is locally uniformly bounded above, and let $F=\sup_{v\in\mathcal F}v$ be its pointwise supremum and $F^*$ its upper semicontinuous regularization. Then $\operatorname{Cap}^*_D(K\cap\{F<F^*\})=0$ for every compact set $K\subset D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes} there is a countable subfamily with the same regularized supremum $u=F^*$. Let $v_j$ be the increasing finite maxima of its members; they are plurisubharmonic and converge almost everywhere to $u$. By domain comparison and subadditivity it suffices to work on a ball $B\Subset D$. Fix an integer $m\ge1$. The functions $\max\{v_j,-m\}$ are uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $B$ and increase almost everywhere to $\max\{u,-m\}$, so by \cref{thm:bt-increasing-continuity} they converge to it locally in capacity. For rational $\delta>0$, the set where $\sup_j\max\{v_j,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}-\delta$ is contained in $\{\max\{v_k,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}-\delta\}$ for every $k$, and the outer capacities of the intersections of the latter sets with a compact set tend to zero as $k\to\infty$. Taking the union over rational $\delta$, countable subadditivity shows that the set
\[
 E_m=\Bigl\{\sup_j\max\{v_j,-m\}<\max\{u,-m\}\Bigr\}
\]
meets every compact set in a set of outer capacity zero.

If $\sup_jv_j(x)<u(x)$, then $u(x)>-\infty$, so $u(x)>-m$ for some integer $m$, and then $x\in E_m$. Hence $\{\sup_jv_j<u\}\subset\bigcup_mE_m$ also meets every compact set in a set of outer capacity zero. Finally, $\sup_jv_j\le F\le u$, so $\{F<F^*\}\subset\{\sup_jv_j<u\}$. Since outer capacity is defined for arbitrary sets, we do not need to know whether $\{F<F^*\}$ is a Borel set.
\end{proof}

\index{plurisubharmonic function!capacity-zero envelope discrepancy}

\section{The homogeneous Dirichlet problem and balayage}
\label{sec:bt-homogeneous-dirichlet}

A plurisubharmonic function is maximal if it cannot be increased on a compact set without losing plurisubharmonicity (\cref{def:maximal-plurisubharmonic}). This is how relative extremal functions behave away from the set on which they are constrained, and in \cref{chap:q03} the property must be converted into the vanishing of a Monge--Amp\`ere measure. We show in \cref{thm:bounded-psh-maximality} that, for locally bounded functions, maximality is equivalent to $(\ddc u)^n=0$. The proof requires replacing $u$ on a ball by a solution of the homogeneous equation $(\ddc H)^n=0$ with the same boundary values. We construct $H$ as an upper envelope; to show that it solves the equation, we bound its second differences by composing with automorphisms of the ball, which makes it twice differentiable almost everywhere.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be the unit ball. For $a\in B$ and $h\in\mathbb C^n$ with $|h|$ small, depending on $a$, there are biholomorphic automorphisms $S_{a,h}$ of $B$ such that $S_{a,0}$ is the identity and $S_{a,h}(a)=a+h$. They extend smoothly to a neighborhood of $\overline B$. For every compact set $K\subset B$ there are $h_0>0$ and $C$ such that, for $a\in K$, $|h|\le h_0$ and $z\in\overline B$, the first and second derivatives of $S_{a,h}(z)$ with respect to the real variable $h\in\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ are bounded by $C$.
\end{lemma}
\index{automorphisms of the ball}
\begin{proof}
Use the Hermitian product $\langle z,a\rangle=\sum z_j\bar a_j$, put $s_a=\sqrt{1-|a|^2}$, and define
\[
 T_a(z)=\frac{s_a z+\langle z,a\rangle a/(1+s_a)-a}
                       {1-\langle z,a\rangle}.
\]
This expression depends smoothly on $(a,z)$ for $|a|<1$ and $|z|<|a|^{-1}$, and $T_0$ is the identity. In unitary coordinates in which $a=(t,0,\ldots,0)$ with $t\ge0$, it reads
\[
 T_a(z)=\left(\frac{z_1-t}{1-tz_1},
           \frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}\,z_2}{1-tz_1},\ldots,
           \frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}\,z_n}{1-tz_1}\right).
\]
Direct substitution gives $T_a(a)=0$, $T_{-a}\circ T_a=\mathrm{id}$, and
\[
 1-|T_a(z)|^2=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^2}.
\]
Hence $T_a$ maps $B$ bijectively onto itself and the sphere $\partial B$ onto itself, with inverse $T_{-a}$. Set $S_{a,h}=T_{-(a+h)}\circ T_a$ for $|a+h|<1$. It maps $a$ to $T_{-(a+h)}(0)=a+h$ and is the identity for $h=0$. If $a$ ranges over a compact subset of $B$ and $|h|$ is small enough, the denominators $|1-\langle z,a\rangle|\ge1-|a|$ and $|1-\langle w,a+h\rangle|\ge1-|a+h|$ of both maps stay uniformly away from zero for $|z|,|w|\le1$, and the derivative bounds follow from the smoothness of the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a ball and $\varphi$ a real continuous function on $\partial B$. There is a unique $H\in\PSH(B)\cap C(\overline B)$ such that
\[
 (\ddc H)^n=0\quad\text{on }B,\qquad H|_{\partial B}=\varphi.
\]
It is the upper envelope of the class of all $v\in\PSH(B)$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le\varphi(x)$ for every $x\in\partial B$. If $\varphi$ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$, then $H\in C^{1,1}_{\loc}(B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After an affine change of coordinates, we may assume that $B$ is the unit ball. We call the functions $v$ in the statement \emph{competitors}, and let $H$ denote their upper envelope.

\emph{Step 1: the envelope for $C^2$ data.} Suppose that $\varphi$ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$. Multiplying this function by a cutoff equal to $1$ near $\partial B$, we obtain a $C^2$ function $\tilde\varphi$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$ with $\tilde\varphi=\varphi$ on $\partial B$. For $A$ large, $s=\tilde\varphi+A(|z|^2-1)$ is plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, and $s=\varphi$ on $\partial B$; so $s$ is a competitor. Let $P\varphi$ be the harmonic extension of $\varphi$ from \cref{thm:ball-dirichlet}. Every competitor is subharmonic, so \cref{thm:subharmonic-comparison} gives $v\le P\varphi$. Hence $s\le H\le P\varphi$. By \cref{thm:psh-upper-envelopes}, the upper semicontinuous regularization $H^*$ is plurisubharmonic, and $s\le H^*\le P\varphi$ since $P\varphi$ is continuous. As $s$ and $P\varphi$ are continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $\varphi$, the function $H^*$ is a competitor, so $H^*\le H$, and $H=H^*$ is plurisubharmonic. Moreover $H(z)\to\varphi(x)$ as $z\to x\in\partial B$, and $H$ is bounded.

\emph{Step 2: second differences.} Let $K\subset B$ be compact, and let $S_{a,h}$, $h_0$ and the derivative bounds be as in \cref{lem:ball-centered-automorphisms}. For $x\in\partial B$, $a\in K$ and $|h|\le h_0$, Taylor's formula applied to $h\mapsto\tilde\varphi(S_{a,h}(x))$, whose second derivatives are bounded uniformly in $a$, $x$ and $h$, gives
\[
 \tfrac12\bigl(\varphi\circ S_{a,h}+\varphi\circ S_{a,-h}\bigr)
                 \le\varphi+C_K|h|^2\quad\text{on }\partial B.
\]
The function $\frac12(H\circ S_{a,h}+H\circ S_{a,-h})-C_K|h|^2$ is plurisubharmonic by \cref{prop:psh-holomorphic-pullback}, and since $H$ is continuous at $\partial B$ and the maps $S_{a,\pm h}$ preserve $\partial B$, the preceding inequality shows that it is a competitor. Evaluating the resulting inequality with $H$ at $z=a$ yields
\[
 H(a+h)+H(a-h)-2H(a)\le2C_K|h|^2\qquad(a\in K,\ |h|\le h_0).
\]
Apply this on a compact neighborhood $K'$ of a given compact set $K''\subset B$. For small $\varepsilon$, the regularization $H_\varepsilon=H*\rho_\varepsilon$ is an average of translates of $H$, so it satisfies the same inequality for $a\in K''$, with the constant $C_{K'}$. Dividing by $t^2$ for $h$ replaced by $th$ and letting $t\to0$ gives $D^2H_\varepsilon(a)[h,h]\le2C_{K'}|h|^2$. On the other hand $H_\varepsilon$ is plurisubharmonic, so
\[
 D^2H_\varepsilon[h,h]+D^2H_\varepsilon[\mathrm ih,\mathrm ih]\ge0,
\]
and hence $D^2H_\varepsilon[h,h]\ge-2C_{K'}|h|^2$ as well. Thus the real Hessians of the $H_\varepsilon$ are bounded on $K''$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$.

We cannot yet apply \cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor} directly, since $H$ is not yet known to be continuous in $B$, but its proof applies: the $H_\varepsilon$ are bounded, so their gradients are bounded and uniformly Lipschitz on compact sets, and by \cref{prop:arzela-ascoli} a sequence $H_{\varepsilon_k}$ converges locally uniformly to a $C^{1,1}$ function $G$. Since $H_\varepsilon\to H$ in $L^1_{\loc}$, we have $G=H$ almost everywhere; $G$ is plurisubharmonic as a locally uniform limit of plurisubharmonic functions, so the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives (\cref{thm:psh-distribution-representative}) gives $G=H$. Hence $H$ is continuous, \cref{prop:bounded-hessian-taylor} applies, and $H$ belongs to $C^{1,1}_{\loc}(B)$ and has a second-order Taylor expansion at almost every point.

\emph{Step 3: the equation.} The complex Hessian of $H$ has bounded measurable coefficients $H_{j\bar k}$, and the coefficients of the complex Hessian of $H_\varepsilon$ are their regularizations. These are bounded on compact sets uniformly in $\varepsilon$, and by \cref{thm:lebesgue-differentiation} they converge to $H_{j\bar k}$ at every common Lebesgue point. Since $H_\varepsilon\to H$ locally uniformly, \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, \eqref{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} and bounded convergence give
\[
 (\ddc H)^n=\frac{n!}{\pi^n}\det(H_{j\bar k})\,\mathrm dV_{2n}.
\]
The matrix $(H_{j\bar k})$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere. Suppose that $\det(H_{j\bar k})>0$ on a set of positive measure, and choose a point $a$ of this set at which the second-order expansion of Step 2 holds, with Taylor polynomial $Q$. The complex Hessian of the real quadratic polynomial $Q$ is then positive definite, so there is $\eta>0$ such that $Q-\eta|z-a|^2$ is still plurisubharmonic. For sufficiently small $r>0$ the remainder satisfies $|H-Q|<\eta r^2/2$ on the sphere $|z-a|=r$. The polynomial
$Q-\eta|z-a|^2+\eta r^2/4$ is then strictly smaller than $H$ on that sphere and strictly larger than $H$ at $a$. By \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, the function equal to the maximum of $H$ and this polynomial on the ball $|z-a|<r$ and to $H$ elsewhere is plurisubharmonic; it is a competitor which exceeds $H$ at $a$, a contradiction. Hence $\det(H_{j\bar k})=0$ almost everywhere, and $(\ddc H)^n=0$. This proves the theorem for $C^2$ data, including the $C^{1,1}$ regularity.

\emph{Step 4: continuous data and uniqueness.} For continuous $\varphi$, extend it to an annular neighborhood of $\partial B$ by $\varphi(z/|z|)$ and regularize; this gives smooth functions $\varphi_j$ on a neighborhood of $\partial B$ converging uniformly to $\varphi$ on $\partial B$. If $\varphi\le\varphi'+c$ with a constant $c$, then every competitor for $\varphi$ is at most $c$ plus a competitor for $\varphi'$; hence the corresponding envelopes satisfy
$\|H_j-H_k\|_{L^\infty(B)}\le\|\varphi_j-\varphi_k\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}$, and likewise $\|H_j-H\|_{L^\infty(B)}\le\|\varphi_j-\varphi\|_{L^\infty(\partial B)}$ for the envelope $H$ of the competitors for $\varphi$. Therefore the $H_j$ converge uniformly on $\overline B$ to $H$, which is continuous on $\overline B$, plurisubharmonic, and equal to $\varphi$ on $\partial B$; by \cref{prop:bt-uniform-continuity}, $(\ddc H)^n=0$. Finally, if $H_1$ and $H_2$ are two solutions, the last assertion of \cref{thm:bt-comparison}, applied in both directions, gives $H_1=H_2$.
\end{proof}
\index{Dirichlet problem!homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage}
Let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$, and let $B\Subset\Omega$ be a ball. There is a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\widehat u$ on $\Omega$ which equals $u$ outside $B$, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$ on $B$, and $(\ddc\widehat u)^n=0$ on $B$. The function $\widehat u$ is increasing in $u$, and its restriction to $B$ is the largest plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for every $x\in\partial B$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $u_j$ be the radial regularizations of $u$, which are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ decreasing to $u$, and let $H_j$ be the solution of \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet} with boundary values $u_j|_{\partial B}$. Since $u_j$ is a competitor for these boundary values, $u\le u_j\le H_j$ on $B$. Since $u_{j+1}\le u_j$ on $\partial B$, the envelopes decrease: $H_{j+1}\le H_j$. Let $H=\lim_jH_j$. It satisfies $\inf_{\overline B}u\le H\le\max_{\partial B}u_1$, so $H$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic, and \cref{thm:bt-decreasing-continuity} gives $(\ddc H)^n=0$. For $x\in\partial B$, since $H\le H_j$ and $H_j$ is continuous on $\overline B$ with $H_j=u_j$ on $\partial B$,
\[
 \limsup_{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u_j(x)\quad\text{for every }j,
 \qquad\text{hence}\quad\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u(x).
\]
By \cref{prop:psh-max-gluing}, the function $\widehat u$ equal to $\max\{u,H\}=H$ on $B$ and to $u$ outside $B$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$. It has the first three properties in the statement.

Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $\limsup_{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for $x\in\partial B$. Since $u\le u_j$, the function $v$ is a competitor for the boundary values $u_j|_{\partial B}$, so $v\le H_j$ by \cref{thm:homogeneous-ball-dirichlet}. Letting $j\to\infty$ gives $v\le H$. Conversely, $H$ belongs to this class by the displayed estimate, so $H$ is its largest member. If $u\le u'$, the class for $u$ is contained in the class for $u'$, which proves that $\widehat u\le\widehat{u'}$.
\end{proof}
\index{balayage!plurisubharmonic homogeneous replacement}

\begin{definition}\label{def:maximal-plurisubharmonic}
A plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega$ is \emph{maximal} if every plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $\Omega$ with $v\le u$ outside a compact subset of $\Omega$ satisfies $v\le u$ everywhere.
\end{definition}
\index{plurisubharmonic function!maximal}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bounded-psh-maximality}
A locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega$ is maximal if and only if $(\ddc u)^n=0$. In particular, maximality is a local property for locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions.
\end{theorem}
\index{Monge--Amp\`ere measure!vanishing and maximality}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $(\ddc u)^n=0$, and let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ with $v\le u$ outside a compact set $K$. Choose a bounded open set $D$ with $K\subset D\Subset\Omega$. By upper semicontinuity, $v$ is bounded above on $\overline D$; choose $M$ with $-M<\inf_Du$. The function $\max\{v,-M\}$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $D$, and it is at most $u$ on $D\setminus K$, which contains a neighborhood of $\partial D$ in $D$. The last assertion of \cref{thm:bt-comparison} therefore gives $\max\{v,-M\}\le u$ on $D$. Hence $v\le u$ everywhere, and $u$ is maximal.

Conversely, suppose that $u$ is maximal, and let $B\Subset\Omega$ be a ball. The function $\widehat u$ of \cref{cor:homogeneous-psh-balayage} is plurisubharmonic, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$, and equals $u$ outside the compact set $\overline B$. Maximality gives $\widehat u\le u$, so $\widehat u=u$ and $(\ddc u)^n=(\ddc\widehat u)^n=0$ on $B$. Since $B$ was arbitrary, $(\ddc u)^n=0$ on $\Omega$. The last assertion follows because the vanishing of a measure is a local property.
\end{proof}
