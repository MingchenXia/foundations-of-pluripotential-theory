\chapter{Complex differential forms and positive currents}
\label{chap:complex-currents}

This chapter sets up the calculus on which Part II is built. The central object of the following chapters is the complex Hessian $(\partial^2u/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k)$ of a function $u$ on an open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$, or rather the $(1,1)$-form $\ddc u$ that it defines. Two features distinguish it from the Laplacian of Part I: its positivity is a condition in every complex direction, and it transforms naturally under holomorphic changes of coordinates. Complex differential forms express both features, and currents, that is, differential forms with distribution coefficients, allow us to apply $\ddc$ to locally integrable functions such as $\log|f|^2$ with $f$ holomorphic.

We begin with complex coordinates, complex manifolds and the operators $\partial$, $\bar\partial$, $\ddc$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$. Positivity of currents is defined by duality from positivity of forms, a question of linear algebra that we treat in \cref{sec:positive-currents}, together with a pointwise Hodge index inequality for mixed discriminants (\cref{lem:mixed-discriminant-signature}). The basic analytic fact is \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}: a positive current has measure coefficients, all dominated by a single positive measure, its trace measure, so that families of positive currents with bounded trace measures are weakly compact.

For closed positive $(1,1)$-currents we then construct local potentials, functions $u$ with $\ddc u=T$ that are unique up to pluriharmonic functions (\cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential}), and show that positivity of $\ddc u$ survives composition with holomorphic maps. The fundamental example is the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula $\ddc\log|f|^2=[\Div(f)]$ of \cref{thm:poincare-lelong}, which identifies the complex Hessian of $\log|f|^2$ with the current of integration over the zero set of $f$, counted with multiplicities. The chapter closes with pushforward and pullback of currents and with trace measures defined by a Hermitian metric.

\section{Complex coordinates and exterior differentiation}
\label{sec:complex-exterior-calculus}

Let $n\geq0$ be an integer. On $\mathbb{C}^n$ we write $z_j=x_j+\mathrm{i}y_j$ for $1\leq j\leq n$, and we use the complex partial derivatives and differentials
\[
 \partial_{z_j}=\tfrac12(\partial_{x_j}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{y_j}),\qquad
 \partial_{\bar z_j}=\tfrac12(\partial_{x_j}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{y_j}),\qquad
 \mathrm{d}z_j=\mathrm{d}x_j+\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}y_j.
\]
A smooth function on an open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$ is holomorphic if all its derivatives $\partial_{\bar z_j}f$ vanish. Let $n\geq1$, and let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed polydisc $\{|z_j|\leq r_j,\ 1\leq j\leq n\}$, where $r_j>0$. Applying the one-variable Cauchy formula in each variable in turn, we obtain for $|z_j|<r_j$
\[
 f(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^n}
 \int_{|\zeta_1|=r_1}\cdots\int_{|\zeta_n|=r_n}
 \frac{f(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_1\cdots\mathrm{d}\zeta_n}
 {\prod_j(\zeta_j-z_j)}.
\]
Expanding each factor $(\zeta_j-z_j)^{-1}$ in a geometric series, we see that $f$ is the sum of a normally convergent power series on every smaller concentric polydisc, and differentiation under the integral sign gives integral formulas for all derivatives of $f$. In particular the identity theorem holds: a holomorphic function on a domain that vanishes on a nonempty open subset vanishes identically. Indeed, the set of points at which all derivatives of the function vanish is nonempty, closed because all derivatives are continuous, and open because the function is the sum of its Taylor series near each point; hence it is the whole domain.

\begin{definition}\label{def:complex-manifold}
A complex manifold of dimension $n$ is a paracompact Hausdorff space with an atlas of homeomorphisms onto open subsets of $\mathbb{C}^n$, whose coordinate changes are holomorphic with holomorphic inverses. A map between complex manifolds is holomorphic if its coordinate expressions are holomorphic. A complex submanifold is a closed subset that is locally a coordinate plane in suitable holomorphic coordinates.
\end{definition}
\index{complex manifold}\index{holomorphic map}
The underlying real manifold has dimension $2n$. A holomorphic map whose derivative is invertible at a point has a holomorphic inverse near the image of that point: the real inverse function theorem gives a smooth local inverse, and its derivative, being the inverse of a complex-linear map, is complex linear.

Recall that paracompactness means that every open cover has a locally finite open refinement. Every connected component of a complex manifold $X$ is open, since manifolds are locally connected, and it has a countable basis of its topology. To see this, refine the cover of $X$ by all relatively compact coordinate balls to a locally finite open cover $(U_a)$. Each $U_a$ lies in a coordinate ball, hence in a single connected component, and its closure is compact, so it meets only finitely many members of the cover. Fix a member $U_{a_0}$, and call a member reachable if it is joined to $U_{a_0}$ by a finite chain of members, each meeting the next. By the finiteness just noted, there are only countably many reachable members. The union of the reachable members and the union of the remaining ones are disjoint open sets covering $X$. Hence the first union is open and closed; it contains the connected component $C$ of $U_{a_0}$, and it is contained in $C$ because each member lies in a single component. Thus $C$ is covered by countably many coordinate balls, and therefore has a countable basis. In particular connected manifolds and compact manifolds have a countable basis, whereas a general complex manifold may have uncountably many components. We state the countability of a basis as a hypothesis whenever a global subsequence or another countable selection requires it.

A differential $k$-form is a smooth field of alternating $k$-linear forms on the real tangent spaces, with complex values. In holomorphic coordinates every $k$-form $\alpha$ decomposes uniquely as
\[
 \alpha=\sum_{p+q=k}\alpha^{p,q},\qquad
 \alpha^{p,q}=\sum_{|I|=p,\,|J|=q}\alpha_{I,J}\,\mathrm{d}z_I\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_J,
\]
where, for an increasing multi-index $I=(i_1<\cdots<i_p)$, we write $\mathrm{d}z_I=\mathrm{d}z_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{i_p}$, and similarly $\mathrm{d}\bar z_J$. The form $\alpha^{p,q}$ is the component of bidegree, or type, $(p,q)$ of $\alpha$. A holomorphic change of coordinates transforms the $\mathrm dz_j$ among themselves and the $\mathrm d\bar z_j$ among themselves, so this decomposition does not depend on the chart. The exterior derivative is defined in real coordinates by differentiating the coefficients and placing the coordinate differential on the left; by the chain rule it does not depend on the coordinates. It satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\alpha\wedge\gamma)=\mathrm{d}\alpha\wedge\gamma+(-1)^k\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\gamma,
 \qquad \mathrm{d}^2=0
\]
for a $k$-form $\alpha$ and every form $\gamma$. In holomorphic coordinates $\mathrm d(f\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J)=\sum_l(\partial_{z_l}f\,\mathrm dz_l+\partial_{\bar z_l}f\,\mathrm d\bar z_l)\wedge\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$. Thus, for a form $\alpha$ of type $(p,q)$, the form $\mathrm d\alpha$ is the sum of a form $\partial\alpha$ of type $(p+1,q)$ and a form $\bar\partial\alpha$ of type $(p,q+1)$. Extending $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ linearly to all forms, we have $\mathrm{d}=\partial+\bar\partial$. Comparing types in $\mathrm d^2=0$ gives
\[
 \partial^2=\bar\partial^2=0,\qquad
 \partial\bar\partial=-\bar\partial\partial.
\]
Complex conjugation exchanges the types $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$, and a form is real if it equals its conjugate. We orient a complex manifold by the form $\mathrm{d}x_1\wedge\mathrm{d}y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_n\wedge\mathrm{d}y_n$ in each holomorphic chart; these orientations agree on overlapping charts, because a complex-linear map has real determinant $|\det_{\mathbb C}|^2>0$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$. Then $X$ admits smooth partitions of unity subordinate to every open cover. If $n\geq1$ and $\alpha$ is a smooth $(2n-1)$-form with compact support on $X$, then $\int_X\mathrm{d}\alpha=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since the connected components of $X$ are open, it suffices to construct partitions of unity on each component; the union of the families so obtained is locally finite on $X$. So let $X$ be connected. By the preceding discussion, $X$ is covered by a sequence of coordinate balls with compact closures. Taking finite unions of their closures, enlarged at each stage so that the new set contains the previous one in its interior, we obtain compact sets $K_1\subset\Int K_2\subset K_2\subset\Int K_3\subset\cdots$ with union $X$; put $K_j=\varnothing$ for $j\leq0$. Let an open cover of $X$ be given. For $j\geq1$, the compact set $K_j\setminus\Int K_{j-1}$ lies in the open set $\Int K_{j+1}\setminus K_{j-2}$. Cover it by finitely many coordinate balls $B'$ whose concentric enlargements $B$ have compact closures contained in a member of the given cover and in $\Int K_{j+1}\setminus K_{j-2}$. For each such pair choose, in the chart, a smooth nonnegative function supported in $B$ and equal to one on $B'$ (\cref{lem:mollifier-density}). The resulting family is locally finite, since a point of $\Int K_i$ has the neighborhood $\Int K_i$, which meets the supports chosen at stage $j$ only if $j\leq i+1$. Its sum is positive, because the balls $B'$ cover $X$. Dividing each function by this sum gives the required partition of unity.

For the second assertion, recall that the integral of a compactly supported $2n$-form over $X$ is computed with a partition of unity subordinate to a cover by charts. If $\gamma$ is a compactly supported $(2n-1)$-form in a single chart, then $\mathrm{d}\gamma$ is a sum of terms $\pm\partial_{t_l}g\,\mathrm dt_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dt_{2n}$, where $t_1,\ldots,t_{2n}$ are the real coordinates and $g$ has compact support; the integral of each term vanishes by Fubini's theorem and the fundamental theorem of calculus on lines parallel to the $t_l$-axis. Now let $(\chi_a)$ be a partition of unity subordinate to a cover by charts. Only finitely many $\chi_a$ are nonzero on the compact set $\Supp\alpha$, and $\sum_a\mathrm{d}\chi_a=0$ there. Hence $\mathrm d\alpha=\sum_a\mathrm d(\chi_a\alpha)$ is a finite sum, and $\int_X\mathrm d\alpha=\sum_a\int_X\mathrm d(\chi_a\alpha)=0$.
\end{proof}
\index{partition of unity}\index{Stokes' theorem}

For computations in $\mathbb{C}^n$ we use the Euclidean K\"ahler form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form}
 \beta=\frac{\mathrm{i}}2\sum_{j=1}^n\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j,
 \qquad \frac{\beta^n}{n!}=\mathrm{d}V_{2n}.
\end{equation}
\index{Euclidean K\"ahler form}
Here $\mathrm dV_{2n}=\mathrm dx_1\wedge\mathrm dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dx_n\wedge\mathrm dy_n$ is the Euclidean volume form, which we identify with Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{C}^n=\mathbb{R}^{2n}$; for $n=0$, $\mathrm dV_0$ is the unit mass on the singleton $\mathbb C^0$. The second identity holds because $(\mathrm{i}/2)\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j=\mathrm dx_j\wedge\mathrm dy_j$ and these $2$-forms commute.
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
The operator $\ddc$ is normalized as in the Conventions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}
 \ddc u=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial u
 =\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}u_{j\bar k}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_k,
 \qquad \Delta u=4\sum_j u_{j\bar j}.
\end{equation}
\index{ddc@$\ddc$ operator}
Here $u_{j\bar k}=\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}u$, and $\Delta$ is the Laplacian of $\mathbb R^{2n}$; the matrix $(u_{j\bar k})$ is the complex Hessian of $u$. The same formulas define $\ddc u$ for a distribution $u$ on an open subset of $\mathbb C^n$, with distributional derivatives $u_{j\bar k}$; then $\ddc u$ is a current in the sense of the next section. We also use the real operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dc-operator}
 \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}(\bar\partial-\partial)=\frac{\partial-\bar\partial}{4\pi\mathrm{i}},
 \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
 \mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial=\ddc.
\end{equation}
\index{dc@$\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ operator}
Indeed, since $\partial^2=\bar\partial^2=0$ and $\bar\partial\partial=-\partial\bar\partial$, we have $(\partial+\bar\partial)(\bar\partial-\partial)=\partial\bar\partial-\bar\partial\partial=2\partial\bar\partial$. Moreover $\overline{\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\alpha}=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\bar\alpha$, because conjugation exchanges $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ and changes the sign of $\mathrm{i}$; hence $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ maps real forms to real forms.

\section{Currents and positivity}
\label{sec:positive-currents}

Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n$. A test form on $X$ is a smooth complex differential form with compact support. In a chart with real coordinates $t_1,\ldots,t_{2n}$ we write test forms as $\varphi=\sum_J\varphi_J\,\mathrm dt_J$ and measure them by the seminorms $p_{K,m}$ of \cref{def:distribution} applied to their coefficients.
\index{test form}

For an integer $0\leq k\leq2n$, a current of degree $k$ on $X$ is a complex-linear functional $T$ on the test forms of degree $2n-k$ with the following continuity property: for every coordinate chart and every compact set $K$ in it, there are $C>0$ and $m\in\mathbb N$ such that $|T(\varphi)|\leq C\sum_Jp_{K,m}(\varphi_J)$ for all test forms $\varphi$ of degree $2n-k$ supported in $K$. Equivalently, in every chart and for every multi-index $J$ with $|J|=2n-k$, the functional $\chi\mapsto T(\chi\,\mathrm dt_J)$ is a distribution. In a chart there are therefore unique distributions $T_J$, $|J|=k$, such that
\[
 T(\varphi)=\sum_J\langle T_J,g_J\rangle,\qquad\text{where }\mathrm dt_J\wedge\varphi=g_J\,\mathrm dt_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dt_{2n};
\]
we write $T=\sum_JT_J\,\mathrm dt_J$ and call the $T_J$ the coefficients of $T$. In holomorphic coordinates we use the basis $\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ in the same way. Thus locally a current is a differential form with distribution coefficients. A locally integrable $k$-form $\alpha$, that is, a form with locally integrable coefficients, defines the current $T_\alpha(\varphi)=\int_X\alpha\wedge\varphi$ of degree $k$, which we usually denote again by $\alpha$.
\index{current}\index{current!bidegree and bidimension}

A current of degree $k$ has bidegree $(p,q)$, where $p+q=k$, if it vanishes on all test forms of bidegree other than $(n-p,n-q)$; in holomorphic coordinates it is then $T=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}T_{IJ}\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$. Its bidimension is $(n-p,n-q)$. Every current is the sum of its components of pure bidegree. Forms and currents of a degree or bidegree outside the admissible range are taken to be zero. The conjugate of a current $T$ is $\overline T(\varphi)=\overline{T(\bar\varphi)}$, and $T$ is real if $\overline T=T$, that is, $T(\bar\varphi)=\overline{T(\varphi)}$ for every test form $\varphi$. A sequence, or more generally a net, of currents $T_i$ of degree $k$ converges weakly to $T$ if $T_i(\varphi)\to T(\varphi)$ for every test form $\varphi$ of degree $2n-k$.
\index{current!real}\index{current!weak convergence}

The support $\Supp T$ of a current $T$ is the complement of the largest open set on which $T$ vanishes, that is, the set of points having no open neighborhood $W$ such that $T(\varphi)=0$ for all test forms $\varphi$ supported in $W$. Such a largest open set exists: if $T$ vanishes on each member of a family of open sets, it vanishes on their union, because a partition of unity subordinate to the cover formed by these sets and by $X\setminus\Supp\varphi$ (\cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}) writes every test form $\varphi$ supported in the union as a finite sum of test forms supported in single members. In particular $T(\varphi)=0$ whenever $\Supp\varphi\cap\Supp T=\varnothing$.
\index{current!support}

Let $T$ be a current of degree $k$ and $\alpha$ a smooth form. We define
\[
 (\mathrm{d}T)(\varphi)=(-1)^{k+1}T(\mathrm{d}\varphi),\qquad
 (T\wedge\alpha)(\varphi)=T(\alpha\wedge\varphi),
\]
and, in the same way, $(\partial T)(\varphi)=(-1)^{k+1}T(\partial\varphi)$ and $(\bar\partial T)(\varphi)=(-1)^{k+1}T(\bar\partial\varphi)$. For a smooth form $T$ these definitions agree with the exterior derivative and the exterior product: by the Leibniz rule and \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes}, $\int_X\mathrm dT\wedge\varphi=(-1)^{k+1}\int_XT\wedge\mathrm d\varphi$, and the analogous identities for $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ follow by comparing types. In a chart, these operations act on the distribution coefficients exactly as on the coefficients of smooth forms, and evaluating both sides on a test form proves the Leibniz rule $\mathrm d(T\wedge\alpha)=\mathrm dT\wedge\alpha+(-1)^kT\wedge\mathrm d\alpha$, together with its analogues for $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. Note that only multiplication by smooth forms is defined in this way; two general currents cannot be multiplied. We put $\ddc T=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi)\partial\bar\partial T$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}T=(\mathrm{i}/4\pi)(\bar\partial T-\partial T)$. A short computation from the definitions gives
\[
 (\ddc T)(\varphi)=T(\ddc\varphi),\qquad \mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}T=\ddc T.
\]
All these operations are continuous for weak convergence. A current $T$ is closed if $\mathrm{d}T=0$. For a current of pure bidegree this is equivalent to $\partial T=\bar\partial T=0$, since $\partial T$ and $\bar\partial T$ are the components of $\mathrm dT$ of two different bidegrees.

Positivity of currents is defined by duality from positivity of forms, which is a question of linear algebra. Let $V$ be a complex vector space of dimension $n$, identified with $\mathbb C^n$ by linear coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, and let $\beta$ be the form \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form} in these coordinates; the forms below are constant forms on $V$. For an integer $0\leq p\leq n$, a real $(p,p)$-form is \emph{strongly positive} if it is a finite sum, with nonnegative coefficients, of the forms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-positive-generator}
 \mathrm{i}^{p^2}\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_p
 \wedge\bar\theta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\bar\theta_p
 =\bigwedge_{j=1}^p(\mathrm{i}\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j),
\end{equation}
where $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_p\in V^*$ are complex-linear forms, that is, forms of type $(1,0)$. We call the forms \eqref{eq:strong-positive-generator} generators. The identity in \eqref{eq:strong-positive-generator} holds because rearranging the factors of the right-hand side produces the sign $(-1)^{p(p-1)/2}=\mathrm{i}^{p^2-p}$, and the generators are real because each factor $\mathrm i\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j$ is real. A real $(p,p)$-form is \emph{weakly positive} if its exterior product with every strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form $\beta^n/n!$. In degree zero both notions mean a nonnegative real number. A form on a complex manifold is strongly or weakly positive if it is so at every point, as a form on the tangent space. Finally, the restriction of a $(p,p)$-form to a complex subspace $W$ of dimension $p$ is a form of top degree on $W$; we call it nonnegative if it is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form of $W$ in its complex orientation.
\index{positivity!strong}\index{positivity!weak}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positivity-cones}
The strongly positive $(p,p)$-forms on $V$ form a closed convex cone, which spans the real vector space of real $(p,p)$-forms and contains $\beta^p$ in its interior. A real $(p,p)$-form is weakly positive if and only if its restriction to every complex $p$-dimensional subspace of $V$ is nonnegative. Pullback by complex-linear maps, and hence by holomorphic maps between complex manifolds, preserves strong and weak positivity. The product of strongly positive forms is strongly positive, and the product of a weakly positive form with a strongly positive form is weakly positive when its bidegree is at most $(n,n)$. In particular, strongly positive forms are weakly positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We use the Hermitian inner product on $V^*$ for which $\mathrm dz_1,\ldots,\mathrm dz_n$ are orthonormal, and the induced inner product on $\Lambda^{p,0}V^*$, for which the $\mathrm dz_I$ are orthonormal. The form $\beta$ is invariant under unitary changes of coordinates. For $\theta\in\Lambda^{p,0}V^*$ put $g(\theta)=\mathrm i^{p^2}\theta\wedge\bar\theta$, so that $g(c\theta)=|c|^2g(\theta)$ and the generators are the forms $g(\theta)$ with $\theta$ decomposable.

\emph{Closedness.} Every nonzero generator is a positive multiple of a normalized generator $g(\theta)$, with $\theta$ decomposable and $|\theta|=1$. By the Gram--Schmidt process such a $\theta$ equals $c\,e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_p$ for an orthonormal family $e_1,\ldots,e_p$ in $V^*$ and a complex number $c$ of modulus one, and $g(\theta)$ does not depend on $c$. Hence the set $G$ of normalized generators is the continuous image of the compact set of orthonormal $p$-frames, and $G$ is compact. Define a linear functional $\tau$ on real $(p,p)$-forms by $u\wedge\beta^{n-p}=\tau(u)\beta^n/n!$. If $e_1,\ldots,e_p$ is orthonormal, complete it to an orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V^*$ and use the unitary coordinates $w_j$ with $\mathrm dw_j=e_j$. Then $\beta=(\mathrm{i}/2)\sum_j\mathrm dw_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_j$, only one term of $\beta^{n-p}$ contributes, and $\tau(g(e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_p))=2^p(n-p)!$. Thus $\tau$ is a positive constant $c_0$ on $G$. Let $H$ be the convex hull of $G$ in the real vector space of real $(p,p)$-forms, of dimension $N$ say. Then $H$ is compact. Indeed, if a point is a convex combination of more than $N+1$ points of $G$, these points are affinely dependent, and subtracting a suitable multiple of an affine dependence relation makes one coefficient zero while the others stay nonnegative; repeating this, we see that $H$ is the continuous image of the compact set $G^{N+1}\times\Delta_N$, where $\Delta_N$ is the standard simplex of dimension $N$. Every strongly positive form can be written as $u=sh$ with $h\in H$ and $s=\tau(u)/c_0\geq0$. If strongly positive forms $u_k=s_kh_k$ converge to $u$, then $s_k=\tau(u_k)/c_0$ converges to some $s\geq0$, and a subsequence of $(h_k)$ converges to some $h\in H$; hence $u=sh$ is strongly positive. The cone is convex by definition.

\emph{Spanning.} Let $\lambda$ be a real-linear functional on the real $(p,p)$-forms that vanishes on all generators; we show that $\lambda=0$. Every $(p,p)$-form is uniquely $u+\mathrm iv$ with $u,v$ real, so $\lambda$ extends complex-linearly to all $(p,p)$-forms. The form $B(a,b)=\lambda(\mathrm i^{p^2}a\wedge\bar b)$ on $\Lambda^{p,0}V^*$ is sesquilinear, and $B(a,a)=0$ for every decomposable $a$. We prove by induction on $r=0,\ldots,p$ that $B(a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge a_p,b_1\wedge\cdots\wedge b_p)=0$ whenever $a_j=b_j$ for all $j>r$. For $r=0$ this is the hypothesis. Assume it for $r-1$, and fix all vectors except the $r$th ones. The resulting form $G_r(x,y)=B(\cdots\wedge x\wedge\cdots,\cdots\wedge y\wedge\cdots)$, with $x$ and $y$ in the $r$th slots, is sesquilinear and vanishes for $x=y$ by the induction hypothesis. By the polarization identity $G_r(x,y)=\frac14\sum_{k=0}^3\mathrm{i}^kG_r(x+\mathrm{i}^ky,x+\mathrm{i}^ky)$, valid for every sesquilinear form, $G_r$ vanishes identically. For $r=p$ we obtain $B=0$ on decomposable vectors, which span $\Lambda^{p,0}V^*$, so $B=0$. Since the forms $a\wedge\bar b$ with $a,b\in\Lambda^{p,0}V^*$ span all $(p,p)$-forms, $\lambda=0$. Hence the generators span the real $(p,p)$-forms over $\mathbb R$.

\emph{Interior.} Choose finitely many normalized generators $q_1,\ldots,q_M$ spanning the real $(p,p)$-forms. For a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and signs $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^n$, the linear map $z\mapsto(\varepsilon_1z_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\varepsilon_nz_{\sigma(n)})$ is unitary, so its pullback maps normalized generators to normalized generators. Let $\bar q_r$ be the average of the pullbacks of $q_r$ under all these maps. Write $q_r=\mathrm i^{p^2}\sum_{I,J}c_{IJ}\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$. The sign changes multiply $c_{IJ}$ by $\prod_{i\in I}\varepsilon_i\prod_{j\in J}\varepsilon_j$, whose average over $\varepsilon$ vanishes unless $I=J$; the permutations then make all diagonal coefficients equal. Since $q_r=g(\theta)$ with $|\theta|=1$, we have $\sum_Ic_{II}=|\theta|^2=1$, and therefore
\[
 \bar q_r=\binom np^{-1}\mathrm{i}^{p^2}\sum_{|I|=p}\mathrm{d}z_I\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_I
 =\frac{2^p}{p!\binom np}\beta^p.
\]
Consequently $\beta^p$ is a positive multiple of $\sum_r\bar q_r$, which is a combination of generators with strictly positive coefficients in which every $q_r$ occurs. A real $(p,p)$-form $v$ of small norm can be written as $\sum_rc_r(v)q_r$ with small coefficients depending linearly on $v$. Adding these coefficients to the positive coefficients of the $q_r$ keeps all of them positive, so $\beta^p+v$ is strongly positive. Thus $\beta^p$ is an interior point.

\emph{Restriction criterion.} Let $W$ be a complex subspace of dimension $p$, and choose linear coordinates in which $W=\{z_{p+1}=\cdots=z_n=0\}$. For a real $(p,p)$-form $u$, the product $u\wedge\bigwedge_{j>p}(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_j)$ involves only the coefficient of $\mathrm dz_{(1,\ldots,p)}\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_{(1,\ldots,p)}$ in $u$, which is also the coefficient of the restriction $u|_W$ in the coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_p$ of $W$. Hence this product is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form if and only if $u|_W$ is nonnegative. Conversely, every generator $\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-p}(\mathrm{i}\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j)$ of bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ is zero if the $\theta_j$ are linearly dependent, and otherwise it is of the above form, with $z_{p+j}=\theta_j$ and $W=\{\theta_1=\cdots=\theta_{n-p}=0\}$. Since weak positivity need only be tested on generators, the restriction criterion follows.

\emph{Pullbacks and products.} Let $F\colon V'\to V$ be complex linear. The pullback of the generator $\bigwedge_j(\mathrm i\theta_j\wedge\bar\theta_j)$ is the generator $\bigwedge_j(\mathrm iF^*\theta_j\wedge\overline{F^*\theta_j})$, so $F^*$ preserves strong positivity. Let $u$ be weakly positive on $V$ and let $W'\subset V'$ be a complex subspace of dimension $p$. If $F|_{W'}$ is injective, it is a complex-linear isomorphism onto the $p$-dimensional subspace $F(W')$ and preserves the complex orientations, so $(F^*u)|_{W'}=(F|_{W'})^*(u|_{F(W')})$ is nonnegative. Otherwise $F(W')$ has dimension less than $p$, and $(F^*u)|_{W'}=0$ because it factors through the $(p,p)$-forms on $F(W')$. By the restriction criterion, $F^*u$ is weakly positive. For a holomorphic map, apply this to its derivative at each point. A product of generators is a generator, so products of strongly positive forms are strongly positive. If $u$ is a weakly positive $(p,p)$-form and $s$ a strongly positive $(r,r)$-form with $p+r\leq n$, then $s\wedge t$ is strongly positive for every strongly positive $(n-p-r,n-p-r)$-form $t$, so $u\wedge s\wedge t$ is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form; hence $u\wedge s$ is weakly positive. Finally, an $(n,n)$-generator equals $|\det(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)|^2\bigwedge_j(\mathrm i\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j)$, a nonnegative multiple of the volume form, so strongly positive forms are weakly positive.
\end{proof}

In bidegree $(1,1)$ the two notions coincide. Write a real $(1,1)$-form as $u=\mathrm{i}\sum a_{j\bar k}\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_k$, where reality means that the matrix $A=(a_{j\bar k})$ is Hermitian. The restriction of $u$ to the complex line spanned by a vector $\xi$ is $(\sum_{j,k}a_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k)\,\mathrm i\mathrm dt\wedge\mathrm d\bar t$ in the coordinate $t$ of the line $\{t\xi:t\in\mathbb C\}$. Hence $u$ is weakly positive if and only if $A$ is positive semidefinite. In that case a unitary diagonalization of $A$ writes $u$ as $\sum_r\lambda_r\mathrm{i}\theta_r\wedge\bar\theta_r$ with $\lambda_r\geq0$, a sum of generators. In general the two notions differ. Let $n=4$ and $\theta=\mathrm{d}z_1\wedge\mathrm{d}z_2+\mathrm{d}z_3\wedge\mathrm{d}z_4$. The $(2,2)$-form $\theta\wedge\bar\theta=\mathrm i^4\theta\wedge\bar\theta$ is weakly positive: if the restriction of $\theta$ to a complex $2$-plane $W$ is $c\,\mathrm dw_1\wedge\mathrm dw_2$, then the restriction of $\theta\wedge\bar\theta$ is $|c|^2\bigwedge_{j=1}^2(\mathrm i\mathrm dw_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_j)\geq0$. It is not strongly positive. To see this, identify a real $(p,p)$-form $\mathrm i^{p^2}\sum_{I,J}c_{IJ}\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ with the Hermitian matrix $(c_{IJ})$. A generator $\mathrm i^{p^2}\eta\wedge\bar\eta$, with $\eta=\sum_I\eta_I\mathrm dz_I$ decomposable, corresponds to the rank-one matrix $(\eta_I\bar\eta_J)$. If $\theta\wedge\bar\theta$ were a sum of generators $\mathrm i^4\eta_r\wedge\bar\eta_r$ with nonzero decomposable $\eta_r$, the corresponding matrices would satisfy $(\theta_I\bar\theta_J)=\sum_r(\eta_{r,I}\bar\eta_{r,J})$. For every $v\in\Lambda^{2,0}V^*$ orthogonal to $\theta$, this gives $0=|\langle\theta,v\rangle|^2=\sum_r|\langle\eta_r,v\rangle|^2$, so every $\eta_r$ is orthogonal to $v$; hence every $\eta_r$ is a multiple of $\theta$. This is impossible: $\theta\wedge\theta=2\,\mathrm dz_1\wedge\mathrm dz_2\wedge\mathrm dz_3\wedge\mathrm dz_4\ne0$, whereas $\eta\wedge\eta=0$ for every decomposable $2$-form $\eta$.

If $\omega_3,\ldots,\omega_n$ are fixed positive $(1,1)$-forms, the exterior product $\omega\wedge\omega'\wedge\omega_3\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$ is a multiple of the volume form that depends symmetrically and bilinearly on the real $(1,1)$-forms $\omega$ and $\omega'$. We now describe this quadratic form in terms of matrices. For Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices $A_1,\ldots,A_n$, the \emph{mixed discriminant}\index{mixed discriminant} $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ is $1/n!$ times the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_n$ in the polynomial $\det(\sum_jt_jA_j)$. Expanding the determinant column by column, we see that $D$ is real, symmetric and real-multilinear, and $D(A,\ldots,A)=\det A$. For a Hermitian matrix $A$ put $\omega_A=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j,k}A_{j\bar k}\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_k$; thus $\omega_I=2\beta$ for the identity matrix $I$. Since $\omega_A^n=n!\det A\bigwedge_j(\mathrm i\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j)=\det A\,\omega_I^n$, polarization of both sides, which are symmetric and multilinear in $A$, gives
\[
 \omega_{A_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{A_n}
                  =D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\omega_I^n.
\]
By \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\geq0$ when all $A_j$ are positive semidefinite. If all $A_j$ are positive definite, then $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)>0$: choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that every $A_j-\varepsilon I$ is positive semidefinite, and expand $D(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ by multilinearity; all terms are nonnegative, and one of them is $\varepsilon^nD(I,\ldots,I)=\varepsilon^n$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mixed-discriminant-signature}
Let $n\geq2$, and let $A_3,\ldots,A_n$ be positive definite Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices. The real symmetric bilinear form
\[
 q(M,N)=D(M,N,A_3,\ldots,A_n)
\]
on the real vector space of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, which has dimension $n^2$, has signature $(1,n^2-1)$. For every positive definite $H$, the restriction of $q$ to the hyperplane $\{M:q(M,H)=0\}$ is negative definite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first show that, for a given tuple $A_3,\ldots,A_n$, the second assertion follows from the first. Let $H$ be positive definite. Then $q(H,H)>0$, so the space of Hermitian matrices is the $q$-orthogonal direct sum of $\mathbb RH$ and $H^\perp=\{M:q(M,H)=0\}$. The restriction of $q$ to $H^\perp$ is nondegenerate, since a vector of $H^\perp$ orthogonal to $H^\perp$ would be orthogonal to the whole space. By Sylvester's law of inertia it has signature $(0,n^2-1)$; that is, it is negative definite.

We prove the signature assertion by induction on $n$. For $n=2$ there are no fixed matrices, and expanding $\det(t_1M+t_2N)$ gives $q(M,N)=(\operatorname{tr}M\operatorname{tr}N-\operatorname{tr}(MN))/2$. The decomposition of a matrix into a scalar matrix and a trace-free matrix is $q$-orthogonal, $q(I,I)=1$, and $q(M,M)=-\operatorname{tr}(M^2)/2<0$ for a nonzero trace-free $M$, since $\operatorname{tr}(M^2)$ is the sum of the squares of the real eigenvalues of $M$. Hence the signature is $(1,3)$.

Let $n\geq3$, and assume both assertions in dimension $n-1$. We first show that $q$ is nondegenerate for every tuple of positive definite matrices. Replacing every matrix $X$ by $C^*XC$ multiplies $D$ by $|\det C|^2$, by the definition of $D$ through the determinant, and preserves positive definiteness; since $X\mapsto C^*XC$ is a linear automorphism of the Hermitian matrices, we may take $C=A_n^{-1/2}$ and assume that $A_n=I$. For a Hermitian matrix $X$, let $X^{(i)}$ be the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix obtained by deleting the $i$th row and column, and let $P_i$ be the diagonal matrix with entry $1$ in position $(i,i)$ and zeros elsewhere. Expanding along the $i$th row, $\det(tP_i+Y)=\det Y+t\det Y^{(i)}$; comparing coefficients of $t_1\cdots t_n$ in $\det(t_1P_i+\sum_{j\geq2}t_jB_j)$ gives
\[
 D(P_i,B_2,\ldots,B_n)=\frac1nD_{n-1}(B_2^{(i)},\ldots,B_n^{(i)}),
\]
where the factor $1/n=(n-1)!/n!$ comes from the normalizations of $D$ and of the mixed discriminant $D_{n-1}$ of size $n-1$. Put
\[
 q_i(B,C)=D_{n-1}(B,C,A_3^{(i)},\ldots,A_{n-1}^{(i)});
\]
the matrices $A_j^{(i)}$ and $I^{(i)}$ are positive definite. Using the last formula, and writing $I=\sum_iP_i$ in the last slot of $q(M,M)$, we obtain
\[
 q(M,P_i)=\frac1nq_i(M^{(i)},I^{(i)}),\qquad
 q(M,M)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nq_i(M^{(i)},M^{(i)}).
\]
Now let $M$ lie in the kernel of $q$. Then $q(M,P_i)=0$, so $M^{(i)}$ lies in the hyperplane $\{q_i(\,\cdot\,,I^{(i)})=0\}$, on which $q_i$ is negative definite by the induction hypothesis. Since also $q(M,M)=0$, every term of the sum vanishes, and $M^{(i)}=0$ for every $i$. As $n\geq3$, every entry of $M$ lies in some $M^{(i)}$, so $M=0$.

Finally, the set of tuples $(A_3,\ldots,A_n)$ of positive definite matrices is convex, hence connected. In a fixed basis of the Hermitian matrices, $q$ is represented by a real symmetric matrix depending continuously on the tuple. Its eigenvalues depend continuously on the tuple and, by nondegeneracy, never vanish, so the number of positive eigenvalues is constant. It therefore suffices to compute the signature at $A_3=\cdots=A_n=I$. With $s=t_3+\cdots+t_n$, the coefficient of $s^{n-2}$ in $\det(t_1M+t_2N+sI)$ is the sum of the principal $2\times2$ minors of $t_1M+t_2N$, that is, $((\operatorname{tr}X)^2-\operatorname{tr}(X^2))/2$ for $X=t_1M+t_2N$, in which $t_1t_2$ has the coefficient $\operatorname{tr}M\operatorname{tr}N-\operatorname{tr}(MN)$; moreover, the coefficient of $t_3\cdots t_n$ in $s^{n-2}$ is $(n-2)!$. Hence
\[
 q(M,N)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}M\operatorname{tr}N-\operatorname{tr}(MN)}{n(n-1)},
\]
and, as in the case $n=2$, the decomposition into scalar and trace-free matrices shows that the signature is $(1,n^2-1)$.
\end{proof}
\index{Hodge index theorem!pointwise mixed form}

\begin{definition}\label{def:positive-current}
Let $0\leq p\leq n$. A current $T$ of bidegree $(p,p)$ on $X$ is positive if $T(\varphi)\ge0$ for every test form $\varphi$ of bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ that is strongly positive at every point. We then write $T\geq0$, and $T\geq S$ if $T-S\geq0$. If $T$ is positive and $U$ is a coordinate chart, the trace measure of $T$ in $U$ is the positive Radon measure
\[
 \mu_T=T\wedge\beta^{n-p},\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad \mu_T(\chi)=T(\chi\beta^{n-p})\quad\text{for test functions }\chi\text{ on }U,
\]
where $\beta$ is the form \eqref{eq:euclidean-hermitian-form} in the coordinates of $U$.
\end{definition}
\index{positive current}\index{trace measure!of a positive current}
The functional $\chi\mapsto T(\chi\beta^{n-p})$ is indeed a Radon measure. It is nonnegative on nonnegative test functions, because $\chi\beta^{n-p}$ is then strongly positive, and such a distribution is real: a real test function $\chi$ is the difference of the nonnegative test functions $\chi+\|\chi\|_\infty\eta$ and $\|\chi\|_\infty\eta$, where $\eta\geq0$ is a cutoff equal to one on $\Supp\chi$. Hence it is a positive distribution, and \cref{thm:positive-distribution-measure} applies. The trace measure depends on the chart; a version defined by a Hermitian metric is introduced at the end of \cref{sec:current-maps}. A smooth $(p,p)$-form $\alpha$, regarded as a current, is positive if and only if it is weakly positive at every point. Indeed, if $\alpha$ is weakly positive, then $\alpha\wedge\varphi$ is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form for every strongly positive test form $\varphi$. Conversely, testing $\alpha$ against $\chi s$, with $s$ a constant strongly positive form in a chart and $\chi\geq0$ a test function concentrated near a point, shows that $\alpha\wedge s$ is nonnegative at that point.

Positive currents have measure coefficients. To state this precisely we need measures with complex values. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ be open. A complex Radon measure on $\Omega$ is a linear functional on $C_c(\Omega)$ of the form $\sigma=\sigma_1-\sigma_2+\mathrm{i}(\sigma_3-\sigma_4)$ with positive Radon measures $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_4$; it assigns a complex number $\sigma(A)$ to every relatively compact Borel set $A\subset\Omega$. Its total variation $|\sigma|$ is defined, as for real signed measures in \cref{chap:p01}, by
\[
 |\sigma|(A)=\sup\sum_{i=1}^m|\sigma(A_i)|,
\]
the supremum being taken over all finite Borel partitions of the relatively compact Borel set $A$. The refinement argument of \cref{chap:p01} shows that $|\sigma|$ is countably additive on relatively compact Borel sets, and $|\sigma|\leq\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3+\sigma_4$. Exhausting $\Omega$ by relatively compact open sets, we obtain a Borel measure $|\sigma|$ on $\Omega$ that is finite on compact sets, hence a positive Radon measure by \cref{prop:borel-regularity}. Approximation by simple functions gives $|\int f\,\mathrm d\sigma|\leq\int|f|\,\mathrm d|\sigma|$ for bounded Borel functions $f$ with compact support.
\index{measure!complex Radon}\index{measure!total variation}

A current $T$ on $X$ has \emph{order zero} if, in every chart, its coefficients are complex Radon measures; we call them the coefficient measures of $T$. A change of coordinates transforms the coefficient measures by a diffeomorphism and by multiplication with smooth functions, so it suffices to check this condition on an atlas, and the total variation of the coefficient measures of $T$ on a compact subset of the intersection of two charts is bounded in one chart by a constant times that in the other. A family $(T_i)$ of currents of order zero has locally uniformly bounded total variation if the total variations of the coefficient measures of the $T_i$ are uniformly bounded on every compact subset of every chart, and $T_i\to T$ in total variation on compact sets if the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T_i-T$ tend to zero on every such compact set. A current of order zero extends, by the formula $T(\varphi)=\sum_J\int g_J\,\mathrm dT_J$ in charts and a partition of unity, to forms $\varphi$ with bounded Borel coefficients and compact support. If $f$ is a locally bounded Borel function on $X$, multiplying all coefficient measures by $f$ defines a current $fT$ of order zero; this is compatible with coordinate changes. In particular $\mathbf 1_ET$ is defined for every Borel set $E\subset X$. We say that $T$ gives $E$ zero mass, or does not charge $E$, if $\mathbf 1_ET=0$, that is, if all coefficient measures of $T$ vanish on $E$ in every chart.
\index{current!order zero}\index{current!coefficient measures}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-current-order-zero}
Let $T$ be a positive current of bidegree $(p,p)$ on a complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$. Then $T$ is real and of order zero. More precisely, in every coordinate chart the coefficient measures of $T=\sum_{|I|=|J|=p}T_{IJ}\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ satisfy
\[
 |T_{IJ}|\leq C_{n,p}\,\mu_T,
\]
where $\mu_T$ is the trace measure and $C_{n,p}$ depends only on $n$ and $p$. Moreover, suppose that $X$ has a countable basis, and let $(T_j)$ be a sequence of positive currents of bidegree $(p,p)$ whose trace measures are locally uniformly bounded: $\sup_j\mu_{T_j}(K)<\infty$ for every compact subset $K$ of every chart. Then a subsequence converges weakly to a positive current, which is closed if all $T_j$ are closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a chart and put $q=n-p$. By \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, $\beta^q$ is an interior point of the cone of strongly positive $(q,q)$-forms. Hence for every constant real $(q,q)$-form $\tau$ there is $C_\tau>0$ such that $C_\tau\beta^q+\tau$ and $C_\tau\beta^q-\tau$ are strongly positive. For a nonnegative test function $\chi$, the forms $\chi(C_\tau\beta^q\pm\tau)$ are strongly positive test forms, so positivity of $T$ gives $T(\chi(C_\tau\beta^q\pm\tau))\geq0$. Therefore $T(\chi\tau)$ is real and
\[
 |T(\chi\tau)|\le C_\tau\mu_T(\chi).
\]
The distributions $C_\tau\mu_T\pm T(\,\cdot\,\tau)$ are nonnegative on nonnegative test functions, hence positive Radon measures by the remark after \cref{def:positive-current} and \cref{thm:positive-distribution-measure}. Consequently $S_\tau=T(\,\cdot\,\tau)$ is a real signed Radon measure, the difference of the positive measures $(C_\tau\mu_T\pm S_\tau)/2$, and $|S_\tau(A)|\leq C_\tau\mu_T(A)$ for every relatively compact Borel set $A$, so that $|S_\tau|\leq C_\tau\mu_T$.

Choose real constant $(q,q)$-forms $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N$ forming a basis of the real $(q,q)$-forms; they also form a complex basis of all constant $(q,q)$-forms, and they can be chosen depending only on $n$ and $p$. Up to a constant factor depending only on $n$, $I$ and $J$, the coefficient $T_{IJ}$ is the distribution $\chi\mapsto T(\chi\,\mathrm dz_{I^c}\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_{J^c})$, where $I^c$ and $J^c$ are the complementary multi-indices. Expanding $\mathrm dz_{I^c}\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_{J^c}=\sum_rc_r\tau_r$, we see that $T_{IJ}$ is a fixed complex linear combination of the measures $S_{\tau_r}$, and the coefficient bound follows from $|S_{\tau_r}|\leq C_{\tau_r}\mu_T$. To prove that $T$ is real, let $\varphi$ be a test form; only its $(q,q)$-component contributes, and we write it as $\sum_rf_r\tau_r$ with complex test functions $f_r$. Since $T(f\tau_r)=\int f\,\mathrm dS_{\tau_r}$ is real for real $f$, we obtain $T(\bar\varphi)=\overline{T(\varphi)}$.

Now let $X$ have a countable basis, and let $(T_j)$ be as in the statement. Since $X$ has a countable basis, it is covered by countably many charts $U_1,U_2,\ldots$. On $U_1$ the positive Radon measures $C_{\tau_r}\mu_{T_j}\pm S^j_{\tau_r}$, where $S^j_\tau=T_j(\,\cdot\,\tau)$, have mass at most $2C_{\tau_r}\sup_j\mu_{T_j}(K)$ on every compact $K\subset U_1$. Applying \cref{thm:local-measure-selection} successively to these finitely many sequences, we find a subsequence along which all of them converge vaguely on $U_1$; then each $S^j_{\tau_r}$ converges vaguely, and $T_j(\varphi)$ converges for every test form $\varphi$ supported in $U_1$. Repeating the extraction for $U_2,U_3,\ldots$ and passing to the diagonal subsequence, we may assume that $T_j(\varphi)$ converges for every test form $\varphi$ supported in some $U_k$. A partition of unity subordinate to $(U_k)$ writes every test form as a finite sum of such forms, so $T(\varphi)=\lim_jT_j(\varphi)$ exists for every test form $\varphi$. The functional $T$ is linear. It is a current: if $\varphi=\sum_rf_r\tau_r$ is supported in a compact set $K\subset U_k$, then $|T_j(\varphi)|\leq\sum_rC_{\tau_r}\|f_r\|_\infty\sup_i\mu_{T_i}(K)$ for all $j$, and this bound passes to the limit; covering a compact subset of an arbitrary chart by finitely many $U_k$ and using a partition of unity, we obtain the estimate in the definition of a current, with $m=0$. Thus $T_j\to T$ weakly. If $\varphi$ is a strongly positive test form, then $T(\varphi)=\lim_jT_j(\varphi)\geq0$; and if all $T_j$ are closed, then $T(\mathrm d\psi)=\lim_jT_j(\mathrm d\psi)=0$ for every test form $\psi$.
\end{proof}

Positive currents can be regularized in coordinates. Let $T$ be a current on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ with coefficients $T_{IJ}$, and let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be the mollifiers of \cref{sec:convolution}. The smooth form $T_\varepsilon=\sum_{I,J}(T_{IJ})_\varepsilon\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ satisfies $T_\varepsilon(\varphi)=T(\varphi*\rho_\varepsilon)$ for test forms $\varphi$ supported in $\Omega_\varepsilon$, where $\varphi*\rho_\varepsilon$ is obtained by mollifying the coefficients of $\varphi$; this is \eqref{eq:distribution-convolution-pairing}, applied to each coefficient. By \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}, $T_\varepsilon\to T$ weakly as $\varepsilon\to0$, in the sense that $T_\varepsilon(\varphi)\to T(\varphi)$ for every test form $\varphi$ on $\Omega$; here $T_\varepsilon(\varphi)$ is defined as soon as $\Supp\varphi\subset\Omega_\varepsilon$. If $T$ is positive, so is $T_\varepsilon$ on $\Omega_\varepsilon$. Indeed, let $\varphi$ be a strongly positive test form. Since the frame $\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ is invariant under translations, the translates $x\mapsto\varphi(x-h)$ are strongly positive, and $(\varphi*\rho_\varepsilon)(x)=\int\rho_\varepsilon(h)\varphi(x-h)\,\mathrm dV_{2n}(h)$ is a limit of nonnegative combinations of strongly positive forms; it is strongly positive because the cone is closed (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}). Hence $T_\varepsilon(\varphi)=T(\varphi*\rho_\varepsilon)\geq0$. If $T$ is closed, so is $T_\varepsilon$, since derivatives with respect to the coordinates commute with convolution. Finally, if $T$ is positive of bidegree $(p,p)$ and $\alpha$ is a smooth strongly positive $(s,s)$-form with $p+s\leq n$, then $T\wedge\alpha$ is positive, because $\alpha\wedge\varphi$ is a strongly positive test form for every strongly positive test form $\varphi$ (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}). The trace measure $T\wedge\beta^{n-p}$ is an example.

\begin{example}\label{ex:submanifold-current}
Let $Z$ be a complex submanifold of $X$ of dimension $r$, with its complex orientation. Then
\[
 [Z](\varphi)=\int_Z\varphi|_Z
\]
defines a positive closed current of bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$ on $X$, the current of integration over $Z$. Since $Z$ is closed, $Z\cap\Supp\varphi$ is compact for every test form $\varphi$, so the integral is defined. In a chart in which $Z=\{z_{r+1}=\cdots=z_n=0\}$, it is the integral of one coefficient of $\varphi$ over a coordinate plane; hence $[Z]$ is a current of order zero. If $\varphi$ is strongly positive, so is its pullback $\varphi|_Z$ to $Z$, which is therefore a nonnegative multiple of the volume form of $Z$ at each point (\cref{prop:positivity-cones}); thus $[Z](\varphi)\geq0$. Finally $[Z](\mathrm d\psi)=\int_Z\mathrm d(\psi|_Z)=0$ by \cref{prop:manifold-cutoffs-stokes} applied on $Z$ when $r\geq1$; when $r=0$ the current $[Z]$ has degree $2n$, and $\mathrm d[Z]=0$ because currents of degree $2n+1$ are zero.
\end{example}
\index{integration current}

\section{Local potentials for closed \texorpdfstring{$(1,1)$}{(1,1)}-currents}
\label{sec:local-complex-potentials}

Coordinate computations in this section take place on an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ with $n\geq1$, for instance a coordinate domain. By \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, a positive $(1,1)$-current on $\Omega$ is real and has measure coefficients. We normalize them as
\[
 T=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}t_{j\bar k}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_k,
\]
so that $T=\ddc u$ means $t_{j\bar k}=u_{j\bar k}$, by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}. Positivity of $T$ is expressed by the following matrix criterion. The coefficient matrix $(t_{j\bar k})$ is Hermitian, that is, $t_{k\bar j}=\overline{t_{j\bar k}}$, because $T$ is real, and it is positive semidefinite in the distributional sense: $\sum_{j,k}t_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$ is a positive measure for every $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$. To see this, we may assume that $|\xi|=1$ and choose a unitary matrix $U$ whose first row is $\bar\xi$. In the coordinates $w=Uz$ the coefficient of $\mathrm dw_1\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_1$ in $T$ is $(\mathrm i/2\pi)\sum_{j,k}t_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$, and evaluating $T$ on the strongly positive test forms $\chi\bigwedge_{j\geq2}(\mathrm i\mathrm dw_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_j)$ with $\chi\geq0$ gives a positive constant times $\langle\sum_{j,k}t_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k,\chi\rangle$, which is therefore nonnegative. Conversely, a real $(1,1)$-current whose coefficient matrix is positive semidefinite in this sense is positive. For smooth coefficients this is the $(1,1)$-case discussed after \cref{prop:positivity-cones}. In general the mollified currents $T_\varepsilon$ have pointwise positive semidefinite coefficient matrices, because mollifications of positive measures are nonnegative (\cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}); hence they are positive, and $T_\varepsilon\to T$ weakly. In particular the trace $\mu=\sum_jt_{j\bar j}$ is a positive measure; expanding $\beta^{n-1}$ shows that the trace measure of $T$ is $T\wedge\beta^{n-1}=((n-1)!/\pi)\,\mu$. Since $\partial T$ and $\bar\partial T$ are computed by differentiating the coefficients, $T$ is closed if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-hessian-coefficients}
 \partial_{z_l}t_{j\bar k}=\partial_{z_j}t_{l\bar k},\qquad
 \partial_{\bar z_l}t_{j\bar k}=\partial_{\bar z_k}t_{j\bar l}
 \qquad\text{for all }j,k,l.
\end{equation}
\index{positive current!matrix criterion}

The construction of local potentials uses the following smooth local exactness results, which we prove by explicit integral formulas.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}
Let $\Delta\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, be a polydisc, and let $g$ be a smooth $(0,1)$-form on $\Delta$ with $\bar\partial g=0$. Then for every polydisc $\Delta'\Subset\Delta$ with the same center there is a smooth function $f$ on $\Delta'$ with $\bar\partial f=g$. Moreover, every smooth real closed $(1,1)$-form $R$ on an open subset of $\mathbb C^n$ is locally of the form $\ddc v$: each point has a neighborhood on which $R=\ddc v$ for a smooth real function $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: one variable.} The function $K(z)=1/(\pi z)$ on $\mathbb C$ is locally integrable, and $\partial_{\bar z}K=\delta_0$ in the sense of distributions. Indeed, for a test function $\varphi$ we have $\langle\partial_{\bar z}K,\varphi\rangle=-\pi^{-1}\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_{|z|>\varepsilon}(\partial_{\bar z}\varphi/z)\,\mathrm dV_2$. On $\{z\neq0\}$, $\mathrm d(\varphi\,\mathrm dz/z)=(\partial_{\bar z}\varphi/z)\,\mathrm d\bar z\wedge\mathrm dz$ and $\mathrm d\bar z\wedge\mathrm dz=2\mathrm i\,\mathrm dV_2$. Stokes' formula on an annulus $\{\varepsilon<|z|<R\}$ containing $\Supp\varphi\setminus\{|z|\leq\varepsilon\}$ therefore gives
\[
 2\mathrm i\int_{|z|>\varepsilon}\frac{\partial_{\bar z}\varphi}{z}\,\mathrm dV_2=-\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\varphi\,\frac{\mathrm dz}{z}=-2\pi\mathrm i\,\varphi(0)+o(1),
\]
with the circle oriented counterclockwise. Hence the integral tends to $-\pi\varphi(0)$, and $\langle\partial_{\bar z}K,\varphi\rangle=\varphi(0)$. Consequently, for a smooth function $a$ with compact support in $\mathbb C$, the function
\[
 f(z)=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}\frac{a(\zeta)}{z-\zeta}\,\mathrm dV_2(\zeta)=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}\frac{a(z-w)}{w}\,\mathrm dV_2(w)
\]
is smooth, since in the second expression all derivatives may be taken under the integral sign, and $\partial_{\bar z}f(z)=\int K(w)(\partial_{\bar z}a)(z-w)\,\mathrm dV_2(w)=\langle\partial_{\bar w}K,a(z-\cdot)\rangle=a(z)$. If $a$ depends smoothly, or holomorphically, on further parameters, then so does $f$, again by differentiation under the integral sign.

\emph{Step 2: the $\bar\partial$-equation on a polydisc.} Write $\Delta=\Delta_1\times\cdots\times\Delta_n$ and $\Delta'=\Delta'_1\times\cdots\times\Delta'_n$, and write $g=\sum_ja_j\,\mathrm d\bar z_j$. For each $j$ choose a smooth function $\chi_j$ with compact support in $\Delta_j$ that equals one on an open disc $D_j\supset\Delta'_j$, and put $P_k=D_1\times\cdots\times D_k\times\Delta_{k+1}\times\cdots\times\Delta_n$. We construct smooth functions $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ such that, for $k=0,\ldots,n$, the $\bar\partial$-closed form $g^{(k)}=g-\bar\partial(f_1+\cdots+f_k)=\sum_ja^{(k)}_j\mathrm d\bar z_j$ satisfies $a^{(k)}_j=0$ on $P_k$ for $j\leq k$. For $k=0$ there is nothing to prove. Let $k\geq1$. Since $g^{(k-1)}$ is $\bar\partial$-closed, $\partial_{\bar z_i}a^{(k-1)}_j=\partial_{\bar z_j}a^{(k-1)}_i=0$ on $P_{k-1}$ for $i<k$; thus the coefficients of $g^{(k-1)}$ are holomorphic in $z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1}$ there. Define
\[
 f_k(z)=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}\frac{\chi_k(\zeta)\,a^{(k-1)}_k(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1},\zeta,z_{k+1},\ldots,z_n)}{z_k-\zeta}\,\mathrm dV_2(\zeta).
\]
By Step 1, $f_k$ is smooth, holomorphic in $z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1}$, and $\partial_{\bar z_k}f_k=\chi_ka^{(k-1)}_k=a^{(k-1)}_k$ on $P_k$. Hence $g^{(k)}=g^{(k-1)}-\bar\partial f_k$ has the required property. For $k=n$ we obtain $g=\bar\partial(f_1+\cdots+f_n)$ on $P_n\supset\Delta'$.

\emph{Step 3: real closed $(1,1)$-forms.} In this step we write points of $\mathbb C^n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ as $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_{2n})$, and we may assume that $R$ is defined on a ball $B$ centered at $0$. For a smooth $k$-form $\omega$ on $B$, $k\geq1$, define the $(k-1)$-form $H\omega$ by
\[
 (H\omega)_x(v_1,\ldots,v_{k-1})
 =\int_0^1t^{k-1}\omega_{tx}(x,v_1,\ldots,v_{k-1})\,\mathrm{d}t,
\]
where $x$ is regarded as a tangent vector at $tx$. Then $\mathrm{d}H\omega+H\mathrm{d}\omega=\omega$. Since both sides are linear in $\omega$, it suffices to verify this for $\omega=f\,\mathrm dx_I$ with $|I|=k$. Let $\iota_x$ denote contraction with $x$, and write $\partial_l=\partial/\partial x_l$. Then $H\omega=F\,\iota_x\mathrm dx_I$ with $F(x)=\int_0^1t^{k-1}f(tx)\,\mathrm dt$, and $H\mathrm d\omega=\sum_l\partial_lF\,\iota_x(\mathrm dx_l\wedge\mathrm dx_I)$, because $\partial_lF(x)=\int_0^1t^k(\partial_lf)(tx)\,\mathrm dt$. Using $\mathrm d(\iota_x\mathrm dx_I)=k\,\mathrm dx_I$ and $\iota_x(\mathrm dx_l\wedge\mathrm dx_I)=x_l\,\mathrm dx_I-\mathrm dx_l\wedge\iota_x\mathrm dx_I$, we find
\[
 \mathrm dH\omega+H\mathrm d\omega=\Big(kF+\sum_lx_l\partial_lF\Big)\mathrm dx_I=\int_0^1\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\big(t^kf(tx)\big)\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx_I=\omega,
\]
where $k\geq1$ was used at $t=0$. Applied to the closed real $2$-form $R$, this gives $R=\mathrm d\alpha$ with the real $1$-form $\alpha=HR$. Since $\alpha$ is real, $\alpha^{1,0}=\overline{\alpha^{0,1}}$. The $(0,2)$-component of $\mathrm d\alpha=R$ is $\bar\partial\alpha^{0,1}=0$, so by Step 2, applied to a polydisc in $B$ centered at $0$, there is a smooth function $f$ with $\alpha^{0,1}=\bar\partial f$ on a smaller polydisc; then $\alpha^{1,0}=\partial\bar f$. Hence
\[
 R=\mathrm d\alpha=\bar\partial\partial\bar f+\partial\bar\partial f=\partial\bar\partial(f-\bar f)=2\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial\Img f
   =\ddc(4\pi\Img f),
\]
and $v=4\pi\Img f$ has the required properties.
\end{proof}
\index{Dolbeault lemma!smooth local}\index{Poincare lemma!smooth local}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-positive-current-potential}
Let $T$ be a positive closed $(1,1)$-current on a complex manifold of positive dimension. Every point has a coordinate neighborhood $U$ on which $T=\ddc u$ for a real function $u\in L^1_{\loc}(U)$. The class of $u$ has a canonical subharmonic representative in the coordinates of $U$, which we call a canonical local potential of $T$. Two canonical local potentials $u$ and $\widetilde u$ of $T$ on the same coordinate neighborhood differ by a smooth pluriharmonic function $h$, that is, a real smooth function with $\ddc h=0$: we have $\widetilde u=u+h$ at every point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point and a coordinate chart around it, identified with an open set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, and write $T$ as above with trace $\mu=\sum_jt_{j\bar j}$, a positive Radon measure on $\Omega$. Choose a test function $\chi$ on $\Omega$ with $0\leq\chi\leq1$ that equals one on a neighborhood $W$ of the point, and let $E_{2n}$ be the fundamental solution of \cref{prop:laplacian-fundamental-solution}. By \cref{prop:fundamental-potential}, the function $u_0=4E_{2n}*(\chi\mu)$ is locally integrable and subharmonic, and $\Delta u_0=4\chi\mu$; in particular $\Delta u_0=4\mu$ on $W$. The current $R=T-\ddc u_0$ on $W$ is real and closed, and its coefficients $r_{j\bar k}=t_{j\bar k}-(u_0)_{j\bar k}$ have trace $\sum_lr_{l\bar l}=\mu-\Delta u_0/4=0$, by \eqref{eq:complex-hessian-normalization}. Using both relations \eqref{eq:closed-hessian-coefficients} for $R$, we find
\[
 \tfrac14\Delta r_{j\bar k}
 =\sum_l\partial_{z_l}\partial_{\bar z_l}r_{j\bar k}
 =\sum_l\partial_{\bar z_k}\partial_{z_l}r_{j\bar l}
 =\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_k}\sum_l r_{l\bar l}=0.
\]
By Weyl's lemma (\cref{thm:weyl}) every coefficient $r_{j\bar k}$ is smooth, so $R$ is a smooth real closed $(1,1)$-form on $W$. By \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare}, $R=\ddc v$ on a coordinate neighborhood $U\subset W$ of the point, with $v$ smooth and real. Then $u=u_0+v$ satisfies $\ddc u=T$ on $U$. Moreover $\Delta v=4\sum_lr_{l\bar l}=0$, so $v$ is harmonic and $u_0+v$ is a locally integrable subharmonic function; by the uniqueness in \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution}, it is the canonical subharmonic representative of the class of $u$.

Now let $u$ and $\widetilde u$ be canonical local potentials of $T$ on the same coordinate neighborhood $U$. Their classes in $L^1_{\loc}(U)$ differ by a real distribution with vanishing complex Hessian, hence with vanishing Laplacian. By Weyl's lemma, this difference is a smooth harmonic function $h$, and $\ddc h=0$. The function $u+h$ is locally integrable and subharmonic, and it agrees almost everywhere with $\widetilde u$. By the uniqueness in \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution}, $\widetilde u=u+h$ at every point of $U$, including the points where $u=-\infty$.
\end{proof}
\index{local potential!of a positive current}\index{local potential!canonical}\index{pluriharmonic function}

A smooth real function $h$ is pluriharmonic if and only if it is locally the real part of a holomorphic function. If $h=\Real g$ with $g$ holomorphic, then $\bar\partial g=0$ and $\partial\bar g=\overline{\bar\partial g}=0$, so $\partial\bar\partial h=\frac12(\partial\bar\partial g-\bar\partial\partial\bar g)=0$. Conversely, let $\ddc h=0$ on a ball $B$ centered at $0$. The $(1,0)$-form $\partial h=\sum_jh_{z_j}\mathrm dz_j$ has holomorphic coefficients, since $\partial_{\bar z_k}h_{z_j}=h_{j\bar k}=0$, and it is closed, since $\partial_{z_k}h_{z_j}$ is symmetric in $j$ and $k$. The function
\[
 g(z)=2\int_0^1\sum_j z_jh_{z_j}(tz)\,\mathrm{d}t
\]
is holomorphic on $B$, and the symmetry gives
\begin{align*}
 \partial_{z_k}g(z)&=2\int_0^1\Big(h_{z_k}(tz)+t\sum_jz_j(\partial_{z_j}h_{z_k})(tz)\Big)\mathrm dt\\
 &=2\int_0^1\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\big(th_{z_k}(tz)\big)\,\mathrm dt=2h_{z_k}(z).
\end{align*}
Hence $\partial(\Real g-h)=\frac12\partial g-\partial h=0$. Since $\Real g-h$ is real, also $\bar\partial(\Real g-h)=0$, so $\Real g-h$ is constant on $B$, and subtracting this constant from $g$ gives $h=\Real g$ on $B$. In particular, if $f$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, then $\log|f|^2$ is pluriharmonic: near each point $z_0$, a branch of the logarithm on a disc around $f(z_0)$ not containing $0$ gives a holomorphic function $L$ with $\mathrm e^L=f$, and $\log|f|^2=2\Real L$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ be a domain, and let $u$ be the canonical subharmonic representative of a real function in $L^1_{\loc}(\Omega)$ with $\ddc u\ge0$. Let $D\subset\mathbb{C}^m$, $m\ge1$, be open and let $F\colon D\to\Omega$ be holomorphic. Then on each connected component of $D$ the composition $u\circ F$ is either identically $-\infty$, or locally integrable and subharmonic with $\ddc(u\circ F)\geq0$. In particular, the restriction of $u$ to each connected component of the intersection of $\Omega$ with a complex line has this alternative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first work near a point. Fix $w_0\in D$, a ball $B\Subset\Omega$ centered at $F(w_0)$, and a connected open neighborhood $W$ of $w_0$ with $F(W)\subset B$. The radial mollifications $u_\varepsilon=u*\rho_\varepsilon$ of \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution} are defined on $B$ for small $\varepsilon$, and by \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic} they decrease pointwise to $u$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Their complex Hessians are positive semidefinite at every point: for $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$, the function $\sum_{j,k}(u_\varepsilon)_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$ is the mollification of the positive measure $\sum_{j,k}u_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$ (matrix criterion), hence nonnegative by \cref{prop:distribution-smoothing}. Since $F$ is holomorphic, the chain rule gives
\[
 (u_\varepsilon\circ F)_{a\bar b}
 =\sum_{j,k}(u_\varepsilon)_{j\bar k}(F)\,
   \frac{\partial F_j}{\partial w_a}\,
   \overline{\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial w_b}},
\]
so the complex Hessian of $u_\varepsilon\circ F$ is positive semidefinite, and in particular its Laplacian is nonnegative. Thus, along a sequence $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$, the smooth subharmonic functions $u_{\varepsilon_k}\circ F$ decrease to $u\circ F$ on $W$. By \cref{thm:subharmonic-decreasing}, $u\circ F$ is subharmonic on $W$, and it is either identically $-\infty$ there or locally integrable, in which case $u_{\varepsilon_k}\circ F\to u\circ F$ in $L^1_{\loc}(W)$. In the second case the complex Hessians converge as distributions, and the limit is positive, so $\ddc(u\circ F)\geq0$ on $W$.

It remains to pass from these neighborhoods to the connected components of $D$. Let $D_0$ be a component and let $A$ be the set of points of $D_0$ having a neighborhood on which $u\circ F\equiv-\infty$. The set $A$ is open. It is also closed in $D_0$: if $w_0\in D_0$ lies in the closure of $A$, the connected neighborhood $W$ of $w_0$ constructed above meets $A$, so $u\circ F=-\infty$ on a nonempty open subset of $W$; since a locally integrable function is finite almost everywhere, the alternative on $W$ forces $u\circ F\equiv-\infty$ on $W$, and $w_0\in A$. Hence either $A=D_0$, or $A=\varnothing$; in the latter case $u\circ F$ is locally integrable and subharmonic with positive complex Hessian near every point of $D_0$, hence on $D_0$.
\end{proof}

Applied to holomorphic changes of coordinates, \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition} shows that the canonical subharmonic representative of a class with positive complex Hessian does not depend on the chart. Indeed, let $F$ be a biholomorphic map between open subsets of $\mathbb C^n$. Since $u$ is finite almost everywhere and biholomorphic maps preserve Lebesgue null sets, $u\circ F$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any component; hence it is locally integrable and subharmonic, and it represents the composition of the class of $u$ with $F$. By the uniqueness in \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution}, it is the canonical representative in the new coordinates. In particular, canonical local potentials of a positive closed $(1,1)$-current are well defined on open subsets of the manifold, and by \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential} any two of them differ by a smooth pluriharmonic function on the intersection of their domains.

\section{Holomorphic zeros and the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula}
\label{sec:poincare-lelong}

Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a domain in $\mathbb C^n$, not identically zero. Away from the zero set of $f$, the function $\log|f|^2$ is pluriharmonic, so the current $\ddc\log|f|^2$ is supported on the zero set. We show that it is the current of integration over the zero set, each smooth sheet counted with the vanishing order of $f$ along it. The main difficulty is the singular part of the zero set, where several sheets may meet. Using the Weierstrass preparation theorem in several directions, we show that in suitable linear coordinates this part lies over a Lebesgue null set in every coordinate projection, which is enough to see that it carries no mass.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}
Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\ge1$, that is not identically zero. Then the zero set of $f$ has Lebesgue measure zero. Moreover, for every zero $a$ of $f$ there is a complex linear coordinate system centered at $a$ such that $f$ does not vanish identically on any of the coordinate axes through $a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove the first assertion by induction on $n$. For $n=1$, every zero of $f$ is isolated: at a zero $a$, the Taylor expansion has a first nonzero term $c(z-a)^m$, since otherwise $f$ would vanish near $a$ and hence on $\Omega$ by the identity theorem; so $f(z)=(z-a)^mg(z)$ with $g(a)\neq0$. A closed discrete subset of $\Omega$ is countable, hence null. Let $n\geq2$, and let $P=P'\times\Delta$ be a polydisc with closure in $\Omega$, where $P'\subset\mathbb C^{n-1}$ and $\Delta\subset\mathbb C$ has center $c$. The functions $c_k(z')=\partial_w^kf(z',c)/k!$ are holomorphic on $P'$, and $w\mapsto f(z',w)$ vanishes identically on $\Delta$ if and only if all $c_k(z')$ vanish. Since $f$ does not vanish identically on $P$ by the identity theorem, some $c_k$ is not identically zero, and its zero set $N\subset P'$ is null by the induction hypothesis. For $z'\notin N$, the zero set of $w\mapsto f(z',w)$ in $\Delta$ is null by the case $n=1$. By Fubini's theorem the zero set of $f$ in $P$ is null, and countably many such polydiscs cover $\Omega$.

For the second assertion, translate $a$ to the origin and let $P_m$ be the first nonzero homogeneous term of the Taylor expansion of $f$ at $0$; thus $m\geq1$. The set $\{P_m\neq0\}$ is open and dense in $\mathbb C^n$, because a polynomial vanishing on a nonempty open set has all its coefficients equal to zero, as one sees by differentiating at a point of that set. Choose $v_1\in\{P_m\neq0\}$, and inductively $v_j\in\{P_m\neq0\}$ outside the linear span of $v_1,\ldots,v_{j-1}$; this is possible because the complement of a proper linear subspace is open and dense. Then $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ is a basis, and $f(tv_j)=t^mP_m(v_j)+O(t^{m+1})$ does not vanish identically near $t=0$. Taking $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ as coordinate axes proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weierstrass preparation]\label{thm:local-weierstrass-preparation}
Let $f$ be holomorphic near $0$ in $\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}$, $n\geq1$, and suppose that $w\mapsto f(0,w)$ has a zero of order $m\ge1$ at $w=0$. Then on a polydisc centered at $0$
\[
 f(z',w)=g(z',w)W(z',w),\qquad
 W=w^m+a_1(z')w^{m-1}+\cdots+a_m(z'),
\]
where $g$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, and the coefficients $a_j$ are holomorphic with $a_j(0)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since the zeros of $f(0,\cdot)$ are isolated, there is $\rho>0$ such that $f(0,w)\neq0$ for $0<|w|\leq\rho$. Choose $\rho_1<\rho<\rho_2$ such that $f(0,w)\neq0$ on the closed annulus $\{\rho_1\leq|w|\leq\rho_2\}$. Since $|f(0,\cdot)|$ has a positive minimum on this compact annulus and $f$ is uniformly continuous on a compact neighborhood of $\{0\}\times\{\rho_1\leq|w|\leq\rho_2\}$, there is a polydisc $P'\ni0$ such that $f(z',w)\neq0$ for $z'\in P'$ and $\rho_1\leq|w|\leq\rho_2$. By the argument principle, the number of zeros of $f(z',\cdot)$ in $\{|w|<\rho\}$, counted with multiplicity, is
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|w|=\rho}\frac{f_w(z',w)}{f(z',w)}\,\mathrm{d}w,
\]
a continuous integer-valued function of $z'$ on the connected set $P'$; hence it equals its value $m$ at $z'=0$. Denote these zeros by $\lambda_1(z'),\ldots,\lambda_m(z')$, in any order; they lie in $\{|w|<\rho_1\}$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
By the residue theorem, their power sums are
\[
 S_k(z')=\sum_{l=1}^m\lambda_l(z')^k=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|w|=\rho}
 w^k\frac{f_w(z',w)}{f(z',w)}\,\mathrm{d}w,\qquad 1\leq k\leq m,
\]
which are holomorphic functions on $P'$, by differentiation under the integral sign. The elementary symmetric functions $e_k$ of the roots satisfy Newton's identities
\[
 ke_k=\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}e_{k-j}S_j,\qquad e_0=1.
\]
To prove them, put $E(t)=\prod_l(1+t\lambda_l)=\sum_ke_kt^k$ and compare the coefficients of $t^{k-1}$ in $E'(t)=E(t)\sum_{j\geq1}(-1)^{j-1}S_jt^{j-1}$, which is the expansion of the logarithmic derivative $E'/E=\sum_l\lambda_l/(1+t\lambda_l)$. By induction on $k$, every $e_k$ is holomorphic on $P'$. Hence $W(z',w)=\prod_l(w-\lambda_l(z'))$ is a monic polynomial in $w$ with holomorphic coefficients $a_k=(-1)^ke_k$, and $a_k(0)=0$ because all $\lambda_l(0)=0$.

It remains to show that $f/W$ extends to a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function on $P'\times\{|w|<\rho\}$. For fixed $z'\in P'$, the functions $f(z',\cdot)$ and $W(z',\cdot)$ have the same zeros, with the same multiplicities, in $\{|w|<\rho_2\}$; so $f/W$ and $W/f$ extend to holomorphic functions of $w$ there. On the annulus $\{\rho_1<|w|<\rho_2\}$ both quotients are holomorphic in $(z',w)$. The Cauchy integral
\[
 G(z',w)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}\int_{|\zeta|=\rho}\frac{(f/W)(z',\zeta)}{\zeta-w}\,\mathrm d\zeta,\qquad|w|<\rho,
\]
is holomorphic in $(z',w)$, by differentiation under the integral sign, and equals $f/W$ by Cauchy's formula in $w$. Defining $\widetilde G$ in the same way from $W/f$, we obtain $G\widetilde G=1$. Thus $g=G$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, and $f=gW$.
\end{proof}
\index{Weierstrass preparation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:holomorphic-logarithm-hessian}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, and not identically zero, and put $\log|f|^2=-\infty$ on the zero set of $f$. Then $\log|f|^2$ is locally integrable, it is the canonical subharmonic representative of its class, and $\ddc\log|f|^2\geq0$. Moreover, let $P\subset\Omega$ be a polydisc, let $1\leq j\leq n$, and write points of $P$ as $(z',z_j)$, where $z'$ consists of the coordinates other than $z_j$. Then for every test function $\varphi$ on $P$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithm-coordinate-slices}
 \big\langle(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j},\varphi\big\rangle
 =\pi\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n-1}}\sum_{a:\,f(z',a)=0}
       m(z',a)\varphi(z',a)\,\mathrm{d}V_{2n-2}(z'),
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the zeros $a$ of $z_j\mapsto f(z',z_j)$ in the fiber of $P$ over $z'$, and $m(z',a)$ is the order of that zero; the integrand is defined outside the null set of those $z'$ over which $f$ vanishes identically on the fiber. In particular, if $\pi_j$ is the projection of $P$ omitting $z_j$, the positive measure $(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ gives zero mass to $\pi_j^{-1}(N)$ for every Lebesgue null set $N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Local integrability.} Near a point where $f\neq0$, the function $\log|f|^2$ is smooth. Let $a$ be a zero of $f$. After a translation and a complex-linear change of coordinates, which preserve local integrability, we may assume that $a=0$ and that $f(0,w)$ does not vanish identically in the last variable $w$ (\cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}). By \cref{thm:local-weierstrass-preparation}, $f=gW$ on a polydisc $P'\times\{|w|<r\}$, where $g$ is a holomorphic unit and $W(z',w)=\prod_{l=1}^m(w-\lambda_l(z'))$ with all roots in a fixed disc $\{|w|\leq\rho_1\}$. Shrinking the polydisc, we may assume that $\log|g|^2$ is bounded. For $|\lambda|\leq\rho_1$ we have
\[
 \int_{|w|<r}\big|\log|w-\lambda|\big|\,\mathrm{d}V_2(w)\le C,
\]
with $C$ independent of $\lambda$: the positive part of $\log|w-\lambda|$ is at most $\log^+(r+\rho_1)$, and after the translation $\zeta=w-\lambda$ the integral of its negative part is at most $\int_{|\zeta|<1}(-\log|\zeta|)\,\mathrm dV_2(\zeta)$. Since $|\log|W(z',w)||\leq\sum_l|\log|w-\lambda_l(z')||$, it follows that $\int_{|w|<r}|\log|W(z',w)||\,\mathrm dV_2(w)\leq mC$ for every $z'\in P'$. (The estimate holds for each fiber separately, so we need not choose the roots as measurable functions of $z'$.) By Tonelli's theorem $\log|W|^2$, and hence $\log|f|^2$, is integrable on the polydisc.

\emph{The slice formula.} For $n=1$, \cref{ex:subharmonic-convex-log} gives $\Delta\log|f|^2=4\pi\sum_am(a)\delta_a$ locally, that is, $(\log|f|^2)_{z\bar z}=\pi\sum_am(a)\delta_a$; this is \eqref{eq:logarithm-coordinate-slices} with $\mathbb C^0$ a point. In general, write $P=P'\times\Delta$ with $\Delta$ a disc in the variable $z_j$. As in the proof of \cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}, the set $N_0$ of points $z'\in P'$ over which $f$ vanishes identically on $\Delta$ is contained in the zero set of a holomorphic function on $P'$ that is not identically zero, so $N_0$ is null. By Fubini's theorem,
\begin{align*}
 \big\langle(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j},\varphi\big\rangle&=\int_P\log|f|^2\,\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_j}\varphi\,\mathrm dV_{2n}\\
 &=\int_{P'}\Big(\int_\Delta\log|f(z',w)|^2\,\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}\varphi(z',w)\,\mathrm dV_2(w)\Big)\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z'),
\end{align*}
the inner integral being an integrable function of $z'$. For $z'\notin N_0$, the one-variable case applied to $f(z',\cdot)$ on $\Delta$ shows that the inner integral equals $\pi\sum_am(z',a)\varphi(z',a)$. This proves \eqref{eq:logarithm-coordinate-slices}; the integrand on the right is measurable and integrable, being equal almost everywhere to the inner integral.

\emph{Positivity.} By \eqref{eq:logarithm-coordinate-slices}, $(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ is nonnegative on nonnegative test functions, in every system of linear coordinates. Applying this after a unitary change of coordinates, as in the matrix criterion of \cref{sec:local-complex-potentials}, we see that $\sum_{j,k}(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$ is a positive measure for every $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$. By the matrix criterion, $\ddc\log|f|^2\geq0$, and by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero} its coefficients are measures.

\emph{Null sets.} Let $\nu=(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ on $P=P'\times\Delta$, and let $\eta\geq0$ be a test function on $\Delta$. By \eqref{eq:logarithm-coordinate-slices}, for every nonnegative test function $\psi$ on $P'$,
\[
 \int_P\psi(z')\eta(z_j)\,\mathrm d\nu=\int_{P'}\psi\,h_\eta\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2},\qquad h_\eta(z')=\pi\sum_am(z',a)\eta(a)\geq0.
\]
Both sides define positive Radon measures on $P'$ in $\psi$, so by the uniqueness in \cref{thm:positive-distribution-measure} the image of the measure $\eta\nu$ under $\pi_j$ is $h_\eta\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}$. Hence $\int_{\pi_j^{-1}(N)}\eta\,\mathrm d\nu=\int_Nh_\eta\,\mathrm dV_{2n-2}=0$ for a null set $N$. Letting $\eta$ increase to $1$ on $\Delta$, we obtain $\nu(\pi_j^{-1}(N))=0$ by monotone convergence.

\emph{The canonical representative.} The trace of $\ddc\log|f|^2$ is positive, so by \cref{thm:subharmonic-distribution} the class of $\log|f|^2$ has a canonical subharmonic representative $v$, given by the limits of ball averages in \eqref{eq:canonical-subharmonic}. At a point where $f\neq0$, the function $\log|f|^2$ is continuous, so $v$ agrees with it there. At a zero $a$, the Taylor expansion gives $m\geq1$ and $C$ with $|f(z)|\leq C|z-a|^m$ near $a$; hence the average of $\log|f|^2$ over $B(a,r)$ is at most $\log C^2+2m\log r$, which tends to $-\infty$ as $r\to0$. Thus $v(a)=-\infty=\log|f(a)|^2$, and $v=\log|f|^2$ everywhere.
\end{proof}
\index{holomorphic function!logarithm of modulus}

To describe $\ddc\log|f|^2$ we introduce the regular part of the zero set $V=f^{-1}(0)$, independently of the logarithm. Let $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ be the set of points $p\in V$ having a neighborhood on which
\[
 f=g\,a^m,\qquad g\ne0,\quad \mathrm{d}a\ne0,\quad m\ge1,
\]
with $a$ and $g$ holomorphic. By the holomorphic inverse function theorem, $a$ is then the first coordinate of a holomorphic chart near $p$, in which $V=\{a=0\}$ is a coordinate hyperplane. The integer $m$ depends only on $f$ and $p$: if $\gamma$ is a holomorphic disc through $p=\gamma(0)$ whose tangent vector $\gamma'(0)$ does not lie in the tangent space $T_pV=\ker\mathrm da_p$, then $a\circ\gamma$ has a simple zero at $0$, and $m$ is the order of vanishing of $f\circ\gamma$ at $0$; since any other representation $f=\widetilde g\,\widetilde a^{\widetilde m}$ defines the same hypersurface, and hence $\ker\mathrm d\widetilde a_p=T_pV$, it gives $\widetilde m=m$. We call $m$ the multiplicity, or transverse vanishing order, of $f$ along $V$ at $p$; it is locally constant on $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. The set $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is open in $V$, so $S=V\setminus V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is closed, and $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a complex hypersurface, that is, a submanifold of dimension $n-1$, of the open set $\Omega\setminus S$. These definitions are local and invariant under holomorphic changes of coordinates, so they apply equally to holomorphic functions on complex manifolds.
\index{divisor!multiplicity}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:divisor-exceptional-projections}
Let $f$ be as in \cref{prop:holomorphic-logarithm-hessian}, and let $a$ be a zero of $f$. There are complex linear coordinates centered at $a$ and a polydisc $P$ centered at $a$ such that, for every $j=1,\ldots,n$, the projection $\pi_j$ of $P$ omitting the coordinate $z_j$ satisfies $S\cap P\subset\pi_j^{-1}(N_j)$ for a Lebesgue null set $N_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose coordinates as in \cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}, so that $f$ does not vanish identically on any coordinate axis through $a=0$. Fix $j$. By \cref{thm:local-weierstrass-preparation} in the variable $w=z_j$, there is a polydisc $P_j=P'_j\times\Delta_j$ on which $f=gW$, with $g$ a holomorphic unit and $W$ a monic polynomial in $w$ whose coefficients are holomorphic on $P'_j$. We shall find a holomorphic function $h$ on $P'_j$, not identically zero, such that every zero of $f$ in $P_j$ lying over $\{h\neq0\}$ belongs to $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Then $S\cap P_j\subset\pi_j^{-1}(N_j)$ with $N_j=\{h=0\}$, which is null by \cref{lem:holomorphic-zero-null}, and any polydisc $P$ centered at $0$ and contained in $P_1\cap\cdots\cap P_n$ has the required property. (For $n=1$ the base is a point, and near $a$ the zero set is $\{a\}$ with $f=g\,(z-a)^m$, so $S$ does not meet a neighborhood of $a$.)

By the identity theorem, the ring of holomorphic functions on the connected polydisc $P'_j$ is an integral domain; let $K$ be its field of fractions. In the polynomial ring $K[w]$, every monic polynomial is a product of monic irreducible polynomials, as one sees by splitting reducible factors into factors of lower degree as long as possible. Since $K[w]$ has division with remainder, the Euclidean algorithm shows that any two polynomials $p_1,p_2$ have a greatest common divisor of the form $Ap_1+Bp_2$. In particular, if an irreducible $p$ does not divide $x$, then $Ap+Bx=1$ for some $A,B$, so $p$ divides $y$ whenever it divides $xy$. Write $W=\prod_{l=1}^sP_l^{m_l}$ with distinct monic irreducible polynomials $P_l$, and put $P=P_1\cdots P_s$. Since $K$ has characteristic zero, the derivative $P_l'$ is a nonzero polynomial of smaller degree, so $P_l$ does not divide $P_l'$, nor the factors $P_k$ with $k\neq l$. In the expansion $P'=\sum_kP_k'\prod_{i\neq k}P_i$, the polynomial $P_l$ divides every term with $k\neq l$ but not the term with $k=l$; hence $P_l$ does not divide $P'$. Since every irreducible factor of $P$ is a constant multiple of some $P_l$, the polynomials $P$ and $P'$ have no common irreducible factor, and $AP+BP'=1$ for some $A,B\in K[w]$.

Let $h$ be the product of the denominators of all coefficients of $P_1,\ldots,P_s$, $A$ and $B$. On the open set $\{h\neq0\}\subset P'_j$ these coefficients are holomorphic functions, and the identities $W=\prod_lP_l^{m_l}$ and $AP+BP'=1$ hold pointwise. Let $h(z'_0)\neq0$ and let $(z'_0,w_0)\in P_j$ be a zero of $f$. Then $W(z'_0,w_0)=0$, and $w_0$ is a simple root of $P(z'_0,\cdot)$ because $AP+BP'=1$; in particular $w_0$ is a root of exactly one $P_l(z'_0,\cdot)$. Choose a circle $C$ around $w_0$ enclosing no other root of $P(z'_0,\cdot)$. For $z'$ near $z'_0$, the polynomial $P(z',\cdot)$ does not vanish on $C$ and has exactly one root inside it, namely
\[
 \alpha(z')=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_Cw\,\frac{P_w(z',w)}{P(z',w)}\,\mathrm{d}w,
\]
which is a holomorphic function of $z'$; by the argument principle applied to $P_l$, it is a root of $P_l(z',\cdot)$. Dividing $P_l$ by the monic linear factor $w-\alpha(z')$, we get $P_l=(w-\alpha(z'))Q$ with $Q$ a polynomial in $w$ whose coefficients are holomorphic near $z'_0$. Hence $W=(w-\alpha(z'))^{m_l}U$ with $U=Q^{m_l}\prod_{k\neq l}P_k^{m_k}$, and $U(z'_0,w_0)\neq0$ because $w_0$ is a simple root of $P$. Thus $f=(gU)(w-\alpha(z'))^{m_l}$ near $(z'_0,w_0)$, where $gU$ is a holomorphic unit and $\mathrm d(w-\alpha(z'))\neq0$; that is, $(z'_0,w_0)\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Poincar\'e--Lelong]\label{thm:poincare-lelong}
Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a complex manifold $X$ that is not identically zero on any connected component of $X$, and let $V$, $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and $S$ be as above. Then the current $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $X\setminus S$, the integration over $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ weighted by the multiplicity $m$, has locally finite mass near $S$: its coefficient measures have finite total variation on $K\setminus S$ for every compact subset $K$ of a chart. Extending its coefficient measures by zero on $S$, we obtain a positive closed $(1,1)$-current $[\Div(f)]$ on $X$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poincare-lelong-normalized}
 \ddc\log|f|^2=[\Div(f)].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $X$ has dimension zero, then $f$ has no zeros and both sides vanish. Let $X$ have dimension $n\geq1$. By the identity theorem $f$ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open set, so by \cref{prop:holomorphic-logarithm-hessian} the function $\log|f|^2$ is locally integrable and $T=\ddc\log|f|^2$ is a positive current with measure coefficients; it is closed because $\mathrm d\ddc=\mathrm d\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=0$.

\emph{Step 1: $T$ gives $S$ zero mass.} Let $a\in S$, and choose coordinates and a polydisc $P$ centered at $a$ as in \cref{lem:divisor-exceptional-projections}. For each $j$, the last assertion of \cref{prop:holomorphic-logarithm-hessian} shows that the diagonal coefficient $(\log|f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ vanishes on $S\cap P\subset\pi_j^{-1}(N_j)$. By the matrix criterion of \cref{sec:local-complex-potentials}, the sum of these diagonal coefficients is a positive multiple of the trace measure of $T$ in these coordinates, which therefore vanishes on $S\cap P$; by \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero} so do all coefficient measures of $T$.

\emph{Step 2: $T=m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $X\setminus S$.} Since $\ddc$ acts on currents by duality, $(\ddc u)(\varphi)=\int_Xu\,\ddc\varphi$ for locally integrable $u$, the current $T$ does not depend on the coordinates used to compute it. Away from $V$ the function $\log|f|^2$ is pluriharmonic, so $T=0$ there. Near a point $p\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ write $f=g\,a^m$ as above, and use holomorphic coordinates $(t,x')$ with $t=a$. Then $\log|f|^2=\log|g|^2+m\log|t|^2$, where $\log|g|^2$ is pluriharmonic. In one variable, $\partial_t\partial_{\bar t}\log|t|^2=\pi\delta_0$ by \cref{ex:subharmonic-convex-log}, and $\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}t\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar t=2\,\mathrm{d}V_2$; hence $\ddc\log|t|^2=(\mathrm i/2\pi)\pi\delta_0\,\mathrm dt\wedge\mathrm d\bar t=\delta_0\,\mathrm dV_2$, the current $[0]$ of evaluation at the origin. Since $\log|t|^2$ does not depend on $x'$, Fubini's theorem shows that the only nonzero coefficient of $\ddc\log|t|^2$ in the coordinates $(t,x')$ is $(\log|t|^2)_{t\bar t}=\pi\delta_0(t)\otimes\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(x')$, and comparison with \cref{ex:submanifold-current} gives $\ddc\log|t|^2=[\{t=0\}]$, the hypersurface carrying its complex orientation. Therefore $T=m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ near $p$.

\emph{Step 3: extension across $S$.} Let $U$ be a chart and $K\subset U$ compact. By Step 2, the trace measure of the positive current $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $U\setminus S$ is the restriction of $\mu_T$, so its mass on $K\setminus S$ is at most $\mu_T(K)<\infty$. By the coefficient bound of \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, applied on $U\setminus S$, the coefficient measures of $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ have total variation at most $C_{n,n-1}\mu_T(K)$ on $K\setminus S$. Hence they extend by zero on $S$ to complex Radon measures on $U$, and these extensions define a current $[\Div(f)]$ of order zero on $X$. Its coefficient measures agree with those of $T$ on $X\setminus S$ by Step 2, and both vanish on $S$, by Step 1 and by construction. Hence $[\Div(f)]=T$, which proves \eqref{eq:poincare-lelong-normalized}; in particular $[\Div(f)]$ is positive and closed.
\end{proof}
\index{Poincare--Lelong formula}\index{divisor!integration current}

\begin{example}\label{ex:poincare-lelong-products}
Let $1\leq r\leq n$ and $f(z)=z_1^{a_1}\cdots z_r^{a_r}$ on $\mathbb C^n$, with positive integers $a_j$. Then $\log|f|^2=\sum_ja_j\log|z_j|^2$ everywhere, both sides being $-\infty$ on the zero set, and \cref{thm:poincare-lelong} applied to each $z_j$ gives $\ddc\log|f|^2=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j[z_j=0]$ on all of $\mathbb C^n$, including the intersections of the coordinate hyperplanes. More generally, let $g$ and $h$ be holomorphic and not identically zero on a connected coordinate neighborhood. The function $\log|g|^2-\log|h|^2$, defined outside the null set where $g$ or $h$ vanishes, is locally integrable, and its complex Hessian is $\ddc(\log|g|^2-\log|h|^2)=[\Div(g)]-[\Div(h)]$. This current depends only on the quotient $g/h$: if $g',h'$ is another such pair with $gh'=g'h$, the two logarithmic differences agree almost everywhere, hence as distributions. Note that the difference is an element of $L^1_{\loc}$ and is assigned no values at the common zeros of $g$ and $h$.
\end{example}

\section{Maps of currents and local mass}
\label{sec:current-maps}

Let $F\colon X\to Y$ be a holomorphic map between complex manifolds, with $\dim_{\mathbb C}X=m$ and $\dim_{\mathbb C}Y=n$. Smooth forms on $Y$ can be pulled back to $X$ by means of the derivative of $F$; the pullback commutes with $\mathrm d$, and also with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, because the derivative is complex linear and therefore preserves types. By duality, currents on $X$ can be pushed forward to $Y$ when $F$ is proper on their support, and currents on $Y$ can be pulled back when $F$ is a submersion. Positive closed $(1,1)$-currents can also be pulled back through their local potentials, as long as these do not become identically $-\infty$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:proper-current-pushforward}
Let $T$ be a current of bidegree $(p,q)$ on $X$, and assume that the restriction of $F$ to $\Supp T$ is proper, that is, $\Supp T\cap F^{-1}(L)$ is compact for every compact set $L\subset Y$. Then the pushforward $F_*T$ defined below is a current of bidegree $(p+n-m,q+n-m)$ on $Y$; it is the zero current if this bidegree lies outside the range $0,\ldots,n$. The pushforward commutes with $\mathrm{d}$, $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, and if $p=q$ and $T$ is positive, then $F_*T$ is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varphi$ be a test form on $Y$. By properness, the set $\Supp T\cap F^{-1}(\Supp\varphi)$ is compact. Choose a smooth function $\chi\geq0$ with compact support on $X$ that equals one on a neighborhood of this set, and define
\[
 (F_*T)(\varphi)=T(\chi F^*\varphi).
\]
The value does not depend on $\chi$: if $\chi'$ is another choice, then $(\chi-\chi')F^*\varphi$ is a test form whose support is disjoint from $\Supp T$, since $\chi=\chi'$ near $\Supp T\cap F^{-1}(\Supp\varphi)$ and $F^*\varphi=0$ outside $F^{-1}(\Supp\varphi)$. For test forms supported in a fixed compact set $L\subset Y$ a single cutoff serves, and the coefficients of $\chi F^*\varphi$ and their derivatives are bounded by those of $\varphi$, up to constants depending on $\chi$ and $L$; hence $F_*T$ is continuous. Pullback preserves bidegrees, and $T$ acts on test forms of bidegree $(m-p,m-q)$, so $F_*T$ has bidegree $(n-(m-p),n-(m-q))$. For differentiation, note that $\mathrm d(\chi F^*\varphi)=\chi F^*\mathrm d\varphi+\mathrm d\chi\wedge F^*\varphi$, and $T$ vanishes on the last term because its support is disjoint from $\Supp T$. Since the degrees of $T$ and $F_*T$ differ by the even integer $2(m-n)$, the signs in the definitions of $\mathrm dT$ and $\mathrm dF_*T$ agree, and $\mathrm d(F_*T)=F_*(\mathrm dT)$; the same argument applies to $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. If $\varphi$ is strongly positive, so is $\chi F^*\varphi$ by \cref{prop:positivity-cones}, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
\index{current!proper pushforward}
For example, the current $[Z]$ of \cref{ex:submanifold-current} is the pushforward of the constant function $1$, regarded as a current of degree zero on $Z$, under the inclusion $Z\hookrightarrow X$, which is proper. A current with compact support can be pushed forward by every holomorphic map.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:submersion-current-pullback}
Assume that $F$ is a submersion, that is, its complex derivative has rank $n$ at every point of $X$. Then every current $T$ on $Y$ has a pullback $F^*T$, a current on $X$ of the same bidegree, which agrees with the pullback of forms when $T$ is smooth. The map $T\mapsto F^*T$ is continuous for weak convergence, commutes with $\mathrm d$, $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, and maps positive currents to positive currents.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Local construction.} Let $x\in X$, and choose holomorphic coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$ near $F(x)$ and holomorphic coordinates near $x$. Some $n\times n$ minor of the derivative of $z\circ F$ at $x$ is invertible. Completing the components of $z\circ F$ by the $m-n$ source coordinates not involved in this minor, we obtain, by the inverse function theorem, holomorphic coordinates $(z,w)\in\mathbb C^n\times\mathbb C^{m-n}$ near $x$ in which $F$ is the projection $(z,w)\mapsto z$; shrinking, we may assume that the coordinate domain is a product $U\times W$. For a distribution $a$ on $U$, define the distribution $F^*a$ on $U\times W$ by
\[
 \langle F^*a,\psi\rangle
 =\left\langle a,\int_{W}\psi(\cdot,w)\,\mathrm{d}V_{2m-2n}(w)\right\rangle.
\]
Integration in $w$ maps test functions on $U\times W$ continuously to test functions on $U$, so $F^*a$ is a distribution, and if $a$ is locally integrable, then $F^*a$ is the function $(z,w)\mapsto a(z)$, by Fubini's theorem. For $T=\sum T_{IJ}\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$ on $U$ put $F^*T=\sum F^*T_{IJ}\,\mathrm dz_I\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_J$. This is a linear map from currents on $U$ to currents on $U\times W$ preserving bidegrees, continuous for weak convergence, and equal to the pullback of forms for smooth $T$. It commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, since $\partial_{z_l}F^*a=F^*\partial_{z_l}a$ and $\partial_{w_l}F^*a=0$, and similarly for the conjugate derivatives.

\emph{Independence and gluing.} Two such local constructions near $x$ agree. Indeed, mollifying the coefficients of $T$ in a chart near $F(x)$, we obtain smooth forms $T_\varepsilon\to T$ weakly on a neighborhood of $F(x)$, as in the discussion following \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}. Both constructions are weakly continuous and agree with the pullback of forms on each $T_\varepsilon$, so they agree on $T$ near $x$. By a partition of unity, the local pullbacks therefore glue to a current $F^*T$ on $X$, and the stated properties, being local, hold for it.

\emph{Positivity.} If $T$ is positive, the forms $T_\varepsilon$ are positive near $F(x)$, hence weakly positive at every point. By \cref{prop:positivity-cones} their pullbacks are weakly positive at every point, hence positive currents, and positivity passes to the weak limit $F^*T$.
\end{proof}
\index{current!submersion pullback}

Positive closed $(1,1)$-currents can also be pulled back by holomorphic maps that are not submersions, provided their local potentials do not become identically $-\infty$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:potential-current-pullback}
Let $T$ be a positive closed $(1,1)$-current on $Y$, and let $F\colon X\to Y$ be holomorphic. Suppose that every point of $X$ has a neighborhood $W$ such that $F(W)$ lies in the domain of a canonical local potential $u$ of $T$ and $u\circ F$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any connected component of $W$. Then the currents $\ddc(u\circ F)$ on these neighborhoods glue to a positive closed current
\[
 F^*T=\ddc(u\circ F)
\]
on $X$, which does not depend on the chosen potentials. It agrees with the pullback of forms if $T$ is smooth, and with the pullback of \cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback} if $F$ is a submersion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $X$ or $Y$ has dimension zero, then $F^*T=0$: in the first case every $(1,1)$-current on $X$ vanishes, and in the second $F$ is locally constant, so each $u\circ F$ is locally constant. Assume that both dimensions are positive, and let $W$ and $u$ be as in the hypothesis, with $W$ in a chart. By \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition}, $u\circ F$ is locally integrable on $W$ and $\ddc(u\circ F)\geq0$. Let $\widetilde u$ be another canonical local potential of $T$, defined on a neighborhood of $F(\widetilde W)$. On the intersection of the domains, $\widetilde u=u+h$ with $h$ smooth and pluriharmonic, by \cref{thm:local-positive-current-potential} and the remark following \cref{prop:positive-hessian-holomorphic-composition}. Locally $h=\Real g$ with $g$ holomorphic, so $h\circ F=\Real(g\circ F)$ is pluriharmonic, and $\widetilde u\circ F=u\circ F+h\circ F$ at every point of $W\cap\widetilde W$. Consequently $\widetilde u\circ F$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any component on which $u\circ F$ is not, and $\ddc(\widetilde u\circ F)=\ddc(u\circ F)$ on $W\cap\widetilde W$. Hence the local currents glue to a current $F^*T$ on $X$, which is independent of the choices, positive, and closed because $\mathrm d\ddc=0$.

If $T$ is smooth, \cref{lem:smooth-dbar-poincare} gives smooth local potentials, which are their own canonical representatives; every other canonical local potential differs from them by a smooth function. For a smooth potential $u$ the chain rule gives $\ddc(u\circ F)=F^*\ddc u=F^*T$. If $F$ is a submersion, write it locally as the projection $(z,w)\mapsto z$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:submersion-current-pullback}. Then $u\circ F$ is the function $(z,w)\mapsto u(z)$, which represents the distribution $F^*u$, and since the submersion pullback commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$, we obtain $\ddc(u\circ F)=\ddc F^*u=F^*\ddc u=F^*T$.
\end{proof}
\index{current!pullback by local potentials}

\begin{example}\label{ex:undefined-pole-pullback}
On $\mathbb{C}^2$, the current $T=[z_1=0]$ has the canonical potential $\log|z_1|^2$ by \cref{thm:poincare-lelong}. For $F(w)=(0,w)$, the composition $\log|z_1|^2\circ F$ is identically $-\infty$, and so is the composition of every canonical local potential, since these differ from $\log|z_1|^2$ by smooth functions; thus \cref{prop:potential-current-pullback} does not define $F^*T$. For $G(w)=(w,0)$, the composition is $\log|w|^2$, and $G^*T=[0]$ is the Dirac mass at the origin. Thus the current of integration over a line can be restricted to a transverse line, but not to the line itself.
\end{example}

We finally compare trace measures in different charts by means of a Hermitian metric. A Hermitian metric on $X$ is a smooth field of positive definite Hermitian quadratic forms on its tangent spaces. In coordinates we write it as $h=\sum h_{j\bar k}\mathrm{d}z_j\otimes\mathrm{d}\bar z_k$, with $(h_{j\bar k})$ a positive definite Hermitian matrix at each point, and we associate with it the real $(1,1)$-form $\omega=(\mathrm{i}/2)\sum h_{j\bar k}\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z_k$, which is strongly positive; for the Euclidean metric $\sum_j\mathrm dz_j\otimes\mathrm d\bar z_j$ we obtain $\omega=\beta$. Every complex manifold carries Hermitian metrics: a partition of unity combines the Euclidean metrics of charts into a locally finite convex combination, which is positive definite. In general $\mathrm d\omega\neq0$.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
For a positive current $T$ of bidegree $(p,p)$ on $X$, its trace measure with respect to $\omega$ is
\[
 \sigma_{T,\omega}=T\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-p}}{(n-p)!}.
\]
Since $\omega^{n-p}$ is a smooth strongly positive form, $T\wedge\omega^{n-p}$ is a positive current of degree $2n$, that is, a positive distribution in every chart; hence $\sigma_{T,\omega}$ is a positive Radon measure on $X$, and unlike $\mu_T$ it does not depend on a chart. It is comparable with the trace measures in charts. Let $K$ be a compact subset of a chart, choose a compact neighborhood $K'$ of $K$ in the chart, and let $0<c\leq C$ be lower and upper bounds for the eigenvalues of $(h_{j\bar k})$ on $K'$. At a point of $K'$, a unitary change of coordinates $w$ puts $\omega$ in the form $(\mathrm i/2)\sum_j\lambda_j\,\mathrm dw_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_j$ with $c\leq\lambda_j\leq C$, while $\beta$ keeps its form. Expanding,
\[
 \frac{\omega^{n-p}}{(n-p)!}=\sum_{|L|=n-p}\Big(\prod_{l\in L}\lambda_l\Big)\bigwedge_{l\in L}\Big(\frac{\mathrm i}2\mathrm dw_l\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_l\Big),
\]
\[
 \frac{\beta^{n-p}}{(n-p)!}=\sum_{|L|=n-p}\bigwedge_{l\in L}\Big(\frac{\mathrm i}2\mathrm dw_l\wedge\mathrm d\bar w_l\Big).
\]
Hence $\omega^{n-p}-c^{n-p}\beta^{n-p}$ and $C^{n-p}\beta^{n-p}-\omega^{n-p}$ are strongly positive at every point of $K'$, and positivity of $T$ gives
\[
 c^{n-p}\mu_T\leq(n-p)!\,\sigma_{T,\omega}\leq C^{n-p}\mu_T\qquad\text{on }\Int K'\supset K.
\]
Consequently, for a family of positive currents, locally uniform bounds for the trace measures in charts, as in \cref{thm:positive-current-order-zero}, are equivalent to uniform bounds for $\sigma_{T,\omega}(K)$ on every compact set $K\subset X$, for any Hermitian metric.
\index{Hermitian metric}\index{trace measure!metric trace}

For a complex submanifold $Z$ of $X$ of dimension $r$, the restriction of $\omega^r/r!$ to $Z$ is the volume form of the Riemannian metric $\Real h$ restricted to $Z$. Indeed, at a point of $Z$ choose a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ of the complex tangent space of $Z$ that is orthonormal for $h$. Then $e_1,\mathrm ie_1,\ldots,e_r,\mathrm ie_r$ is a positively oriented orthonormal basis for $\Real h$, and in the dual real coordinates the restriction of $\omega$ is $\sum_{j=1}^r\mathrm{d}x_j\wedge\mathrm{d}y_j$, whose $r$th power divided by $r!$ is $\mathrm dx_1\wedge\mathrm dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dx_r\wedge\mathrm dy_r$. Consequently the trace measure $\sigma_{[Z],\omega}$ is the Riemannian volume measure of $Z$. Finally, in dimension zero all forms of positive degree and all $(1,1)$-currents vanish, and integration currents and currents of degree zero are sums over the discrete points of the manifold.
